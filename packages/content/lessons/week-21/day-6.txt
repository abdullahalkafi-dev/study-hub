@section math | 2 · Part A: Fourier series and applications (14 marks, about 55 min) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 10
P: (1 mark) Find b₁ and b₃ for the square wave that equals 3 on (0, π) and −3 on (−π, 0). => For height 1, bₙ = 4 ÷ (nπ). Times 3: **b₁ = 12 ÷ π = 3.82** and **b₃ = 4 ÷ π = 1.27**.
P: (3 marks) Find the Fourier series of f (x) = x² on (−π, π), and use x = π to find Σ 1 ÷ n². => f is even, so bₙ = 0 and a₀ ÷ 2 = (1 ÷ π) ∫₀^π x² dx = **π² ÷ 3** (1 mark). aₙ = (2 ÷ π) ∫₀^π x² cos nx dx; by parts twice ∫₀^π x² cos nx dx = 2π (−1)^n ÷ n², so **aₙ = 4 (−1)^n ÷ n²** (1 mark). Hence x² = π² ÷ 3 + 4 Σ (−1)^n cos nx ÷ n². At x = π: π² = π² ÷ 3 + 4 Σ 1 ÷ n², so **Σ 1 ÷ n² = π² ÷ 6** (1 mark).
P: (1 mark) Find the complex coefficients c₂ and c₋₂ of sin 2x. => sin 2x = (e^(2ix) − e^(−2ix)) ÷ 2i, so **c₂ = 1 ÷ 2i = −i ÷ 2** and **c₋₂ = i ÷ 2**.
P: (1 mark) The unit square wave has (1 ÷ π) ∫ f² dx = 2. Find the share of the energy in the first harmonic. => b₁² = 16 ÷ π², so the share is (16 ÷ π²) ÷ 2 = 8 ÷ π² ≈ **0.81 (81%)**.
P: (1 mark) What value do the partial sums of the square wave from −1 to 1 overshoot to near the jump as the number of terms grows? => About **1.18** (the Gibbs overshoot: about 9% of the jump height 2, so 1 + 0.179).
P: (2 marks) A pulse train of height 1 has duty 0.2, so cₙ = sin (0.2 nπ) ÷ (nπ). Find c₀ and c₁ (use sin 36° ≈ 0.588) and name the first missing harmonic. => **c₀ = 0.2** and **c₁ = 0.588 ÷ π ≈ 0.187** (1 mark). The first missing harmonic is where 0.2 n is a whole number: **n = 5** (1 mark).
P: (1 mark) A square wave of frequency f_c passes through an RC low-pass with cut-off f_c. Find the gain applied to its 3rd harmonic. => 1 ÷ √(1 + 3²) = **1 ÷ √10 ≈ 0.316**.
P: (2 marks) A full-wave rectifier makes |10 sin ωt| (peak 10 V). Find the DC value and the amplitude of the first ripple harmonic (at twice the mains frequency) before smoothing. => DC = 2 × 10 ÷ π ≈ **6.37 V** (1 mark). The ripple is 4 × 10 ÷ (3π) ≈ **4.24 V** (1 mark).
P: (1 mark) In the heat equation for a rod with cold ends, by what factor does mode 3 decay faster than mode 1? => The exponent is n² α t, so **9 times** faster.
P: (1 mark) A string has c = 400 m/s and L = 0.5 m and is plucked at its middle. Find f₁ and say which harmonics are absent. => f₁ = c ÷ 2L = **400 Hz**; the **even** harmonics are absent.

CHECK: math

@section physics | 3 · Part B: magnetic fields and motors (6 marks, about 35 min) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 11 to 16
P: (1 mark) A proton moves at 2 × 10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 0.4 T field. Find the force and the radius of its path. => F = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2 × 10⁶ × 0.4 = **1.28 × 10⁻¹³ N**; r = m v ÷ (q B) = 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ × 2 × 10⁶ ÷ (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.4) = **5.22 cm**.
P: (1 mark) A velocity selector has E = 2 × 10⁵ V/m and B = 0.5 T. Find the speed it passes. => v = E ÷ B = **4 × 10⁵ m/s**.
P: (1 mark) A 0.8 m wire carries 15 A in a 0.25 T field. Find the force when the wire is perpendicular to the field and when it makes 30° with it. => F = B I L = **3.0 N**, and at 30° **1.5 N**.
P: (1 mark) Find the field 8 cm from a wire carrying 40 A, and the force per metre between two parallel wires of 20 A each, 5 cm apart. => B = 2 × 10⁻⁷ × 40 ÷ 0.08 = **100 μT**. F ÷ L = 2 × 10⁻⁷ × 20 × 20 ÷ 0.05 = **1.6 mN/m** (attraction, same direction).
P: (1 mark) A solenoid has 800 turns on 0.4 m and carries 2.5 A. Find B inside and the energy density. => n = 2000 per m; B = 4π × 10⁻⁷ × 2000 × 2.5 = **6.28 mT**; u = B² ÷ 2μ₀ = **15.7 J/m³**.
P: (1 mark) A DC motor has V = 24 V, R = 2 Ω and k = 0.05 V s/rad. Find the stall current and the no-load speed in rpm, and the torque at 300 rad/s. => Stall I = **12 A**; ω₀ = 24 ÷ 0.05 = 480 rad/s = **4584 rpm**; at 300 rad/s the back EMF is 15 V, I = 4.5 A and τ = k I = **0.225 N m**.

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | The sum of fourth powers from Parseval
P: Apply Parseval to f (x) = x² on (−π, π) (a₀ ÷ 2 = π² ÷ 3, aₙ = 4 (−1)^n ÷ n²) to find Σ 1 ÷ n⁴. => (1 ÷ π) ∫ x⁴ dx over (−π, π) = 2π⁴ ÷ 5. Parseval: a₀² ÷ 2 + Σ aₙ² = 2π⁴ ÷ 9 + 16 Σ 1 ÷ n⁴. So 16 Σ 1 ÷ n⁴ = 2π⁴ (1 ÷ 5 − 1 ÷ 9) = 2π⁴ × 4 ÷ 45, and **Σ 1 ÷ n⁴ = π⁴ ÷ 90**. A sum nobody could do by hand before Fourier.

LVL: Challenge | Measure an unknown field with a Hall probe
P: A semiconductor probe (n = 10²² m⁻³, thickness 0.1 mm) carries 2 mA and shows a Hall voltage of 4.0 mV. Find the field. => V_H = I B ÷ (n q t), so B = V_H n q t ÷ I = 4 × 10⁻³ × 10²² × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 10⁻⁴ ÷ (2 × 10⁻³) = **0.32 T**, about the field of a fridge magnet array or a small loudspeaker magnet.

### What Week 22 looks like
**The Fourier transform, and electromagnetic induction.** In maths, Fourier series for waves that do not repeat: the Fourier transform, the idea that a short signal has a wide spectrum, and a first look at sampling. In physics, Faraday's law and Lenz's law (a changing magnetic field makes an electric field), generators and transformers, and Maxwell's four equations in overview. The story is the power station, from spinning turbine to the socket in your wall.
