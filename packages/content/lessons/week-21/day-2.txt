# Symmetry, convergence and the Gibbs overshoot. Then: particle machines
lead: Fourier series come in two flavours: smooth waves, whose harmonics fade quickly, and waves with jumps, whose harmonics fade slowly and leave a stubborn overshoot called the Gibbs phenomenon. You also meet Parseval's identity, which says how a wave's energy is shared among its harmonics. In physics, you put the circular path of a charged particle to work in four machines: the velocity selector, the mass spectrometer, the cyclotron and the Hall sensor.

@section math | 2 · Math: symmetry, smoothness and energy | 55 min

### The triangle wave
Take f (x) = |x| on (−π, π), repeated. It is **even**, so all bₙ = 0 and aₙ = (2 ÷ π) ∫₀^π x cos nx dx. By parts: ∫₀^π x cos nx dx = [x sin nx ÷ n]₀^π − ∫₀^π sin nx ÷ n dx = (cos nπ − 1) ÷ n². So **aₙ = (2 ÷ π)(cos nπ − 1) ÷ n² = −4 ÷ (π n²) for odd n** and 0 for even n. Also a₀ ÷ 2 = π ÷ 2 (the average height). Therefore

EQ: |x| = π ÷ 2 − (4 ÷ π) (cos x + cos 3x ÷ 9 + cos 5x ÷ 25 + …)

**Checks:** at x = 0 the left side is 0, and the right side is π ÷ 2 − (4 ÷ π)(1 + ⅑ + 1 ÷ 25 + …) = π ÷ 2 − (4 ÷ π)(π² ÷ 8) = **0** ✓. At x = π: π ÷ 2 + (4 ÷ π)(π² ÷ 8) = **π** ✓.

### Smooth waves converge fast
| Wave | Harmonics fall like | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Square wave (jumps) | 1 ÷ n | the wave jumps |
| Triangle wave (corners, no jumps) | 1 ÷ n² | continuous, only the slope jumps |
| A smooth wave with continuous slope | 1 ÷ n³ or faster | every extra smoothness speeds it up |

Three terms of the triangle series at x = 0 give 0.105 (target 0), six terms give 0.053. The square wave's three terms at x = π ÷ 2 give 1.103 (target 1).

BN: |x| এর ধারা = π/2 − (4/π)(cos x + cos 3x/9 + cos 5x/25 + …); জোড় ফাংশন তাই শুধু cos। মসৃণতা বাড়লে হারমোনিক দ্রুত কমে: লাফ → 1/n, কোণ → 1/n², আরও মসৃণ → 1/n³।

### The Gibbs overshoot
Near a jump, however many terms you add, the partial sum **overshoots the jump** by about **9% of the jump's height**. For a square wave from −1 to +1 (jump 2), the partial sum's maximum is:

| Terms | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 8 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum | 1.273 | 1.200 | 1.188 | 1.184 | 1.180 | 1.179 |

The limit is **1.179**: an overshoot of 0.179, which is 8.95% of the jump 2. More terms squeeze the overshoot closer to the jump (its width falls as 1 ÷ n) but never lower it. This is real: the ringing near an edge in a JPEG image or in a poorly filtered audio square wave is the Gibbs effect.

### Energy and Parseval's identity
Since the harmonics are orthogonal, their energies add. For a wave of period 2π:

EQ: (1 ÷ π) ∫ f² dx = a₀² ÷ 2 + Σ (aₙ² + bₙ²)

For the square wave of height 1: the left side is (1 ÷ π) × 2π = **2**, and the right side is Σ over odd n of 16 ÷ (π² n²) = (16 ÷ π²)(π² ÷ 8) = **2** ✓. The share of the energy in the first harmonic is (16 ÷ π²) ÷ 2 = **81.1%**, in the first two (n = 1, 3) **90.1%**, in the first three **93.3%**, in the first five **96.0%** and in the first twelve **98.3%**. So a good recording of the square wave needs only a few harmonics for most of its energy, even though many are needed for its sharp corners.

BN: পার্সেভাল: (1/π)∫f² dx = a₀²/2 + Σ(aₙ² + bₙ²)। বর্গ তরঙ্গে প্রথম হারমোনিকে 81%, প্রথম দুটোতে 90%, তিনটাতে 93%। গিবস: লাফের কাছে যতই পদ যোগ করো, প্রায় 9% ওভারশুট থেকে যায় (সীমা 1.179), শুধু সরু হয়।

### A different period
For period 2L, replace x by πx ÷ L: f = a₀ ÷ 2 + Σ (aₙ cos (nπx ÷ L) + bₙ sin (nπx ÷ L)) with the coefficients (1 ÷ L) ∫ from −L to L. Example: a 50 Hz square wave of amplitude 1 has period 0.02 s, and its components are at **50 Hz (1.273), 150 Hz (0.424), 250 Hz (0.255), …**

EX: How many harmonics does an 8 V square wave need?
A square wave of amplitude 8 V at 1 kHz (jump 16 V).
1. **Harmonics:** amplitudes (4 ÷ π) × 8 ÷ n = 10.19 V at 1 kHz, 3.40 V at 3 kHz, 2.04 V at 5 kHz, 1.46 V at 7 kHz.
2. **Energy:** the first three components (1, 3, 5 kHz) carry 93.3% of the energy.
3. **Gibbs:** with an amplifier cut off at 9 kHz (5 harmonics), the top overshoots to about 8 × 1.18 = **9.4 V** for a moment on each edge.
4. **Bandwidth rule:** an amplifier for square waves needs a bandwidth of at least 10 to 20 times the wave's frequency, which is why a good oscilloscope specification says so.

TRAP: The Gibbs overshoot is **not** an error that vanishes with more terms: it stays near 9% of the jump. But the area of the overshoot shrinks, so the series still converges in the energy sense. For Parseval use the **squares** of the coefficients (aₙ² + bₙ²), not their sum. And check the symmetry first: a function that is even about x = 0 has no sines, but a function that is odd about x = 0 has no cosines and no constant.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: velocity selector, mass spectrometer, cyclotron, Hall sensor | 35 min

### Four machines from one equation
**Velocity selector.** A beam passes between crossed electric and magnetic fields, E and B at right angles. Only particles with q E = q v B go straight: **v = E ÷ B**, independent of charge and mass.

**Mass spectrometer.** Ions of charge q and mass m are accelerated through a voltage V (q V = ½ m v²), then bent in a field B: r = m v ÷ (q B). So **r = (1 ÷ B) √(2 m V ÷ q)**: for the same q and V, **r ∝ √m**. Landing positions sort the ions by mass.

**Cyclotron.** Charges go round in two D-shaped hollow electrodes; the voltage between them flips at the frequency **f = q B ÷ (2π m)**, so the particle is pushed forward at every crossing. The radius grows, the final speed is **v = q B R ÷ m** at the outer radius R.

**Hall sensor.** A current I flows along a thin strip of thickness t. A field B across the strip pushes the charge carriers sideways, building up a voltage **V_H = I B ÷ (n q t)**, where n is the density of carriers. It measures magnetic fields, and tells you whether the carriers are positive or negative.

BN: বেগ নির্বাচক: qE = qvB, v = E/B। ভর বর্ণালীমাপক: V দিয়ে ত্বরণ, তারপর B তে বাঁক: r = (1/B)√(2mV/q), r ∝ √m। সাইক্লোট্রন: f = qB/(2πm), শেষ বেগ v = qBR/m। হল সেন্সর: V_H = IB/(nqt), পাতলা পাতে কারেন্ট আর আড়াআড়ি B থেকে পাশে ভোল্টেজ।

EX: Sorting carbon isotopes
Singly charged ions ¹²C⁺ and ¹⁴C⁺ are accelerated through 2000 V and enter B = 0.5 T.
1. **Speed of ¹²C⁺:** v = √(2 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2000 ÷ (12 × 1.6605 × 10⁻²⁷)) = **1.79 × 10⁵ m/s**. For ¹⁴C⁺ it is **1.66 × 10⁵ m/s**.
2. **Radii:** r = m v ÷ (q B): **4.46 cm** for ¹²C⁺ and **4.82 cm** for ¹⁴C⁺. The ratio is √(14 ÷ 12) = **1.080** ✓.
3. **Separation:** the two spots are **3.6 mm** apart. A mass spectrometer in an archaeology lab counts ¹⁴C atoms in a sample this way, which gives the age of ancient wood or bone.
/EX

EX: A velocity selector, a cyclotron and a Hall strip
1. **Velocity selector:** E = 5 × 10⁴ V/m and B = 0.1 T: v = **5 × 10⁵ m/s**. Faster particles feel more magnetic force and bend one way, slower ones the other way.
2. **Cyclotron for protons in 1.5 T:** f = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1.5 ÷ (2π × 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷) = **22.9 MHz**. With a dee radius R = 0.5 m the final speed is v = q B R ÷ m = **7.2 × 10⁷ m/s** (0.24 c), the kinetic energy is about **26.9 MeV** (relativistically 26.5 MeV, as γ = 1.03). With 50 kV between the dees, each turn adds 100 keV, so about **269 turns** are needed. The frequency stays fixed because the period does not depend on the speed, until the mass increase at high speed (relativity, Week 31) spoils the match.
3. **Hall strip:** copper (n = 8.5 × 10²⁸ m⁻³), t = 0.1 mm, I = 10 A and B = 1 T: V_H = 10 × 1 ÷ (8.5 × 10²⁸ × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 10⁻⁴) = **7.3 μV**: tiny, since a metal has a huge n. A semiconductor with n = 10²² m⁻³ and I = 1 mA gives V_H = **6.2 mV**: this is why Hall sensors are made of semiconductors. They sit in phones (compass), in cars (wheel speed, crankshaft position) and in current clamps.

TRAP: A velocity selector selects speed only: charge and mass cancel. In a mass spectrometer r ∝ √m, not m, because the speed also depends on m. The cyclotron frequency does not depend on the radius or the speed, only on q B ÷ m. And the Hall voltage is larger when n is smaller, so use a semiconductor for a sensor.
