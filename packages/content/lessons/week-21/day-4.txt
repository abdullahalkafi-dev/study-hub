# Fourier series at work: sound, rectifiers and driven springs. Then: Ampère's law, solenoids and toroids
lead: Fourier series are not only a formula, they are the way engineers think about signals. A musical note is a list of harmonics, a power supply is a rectifier whose ripple you can predict from its series, and a spring pushed by a square wave resonates with the harmonic that matches its own frequency. In physics, Ampère's law does for currents what Gauss's law did for charges: it gives the magnetic field of a symmetric current in a single line, and it explains the solenoid, the toroid and the heart of an electromagnet.

BN: ফুরিয়ে ধারা শুধু সূত্র নয়, প্রকৌশলীর সংকেত ভাবার ভাষা। সুর হলো হারমোনিকের তালিকা, পাওয়ার সাপ্লাই হলো রেকটিফায়ার যার রিপল ধারা থেকে বলা যায়, আর বর্গ তরঙ্গে ঠেলা স্প্রিং সেই হারমোনিকের সাথে অনুনাদ করে যা তার নিজের কম্পাঙ্কের সমান। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে অ্যাম্পিয়ারের সূত্র কারেন্টের জন্য তাই করে যা গাউসের সূত্র চার্জের জন্য করেছিল: প্রতিসম কারেন্টের চৌম্বক ক্ষেত্র এক লাইনে। সলিনয়েড, টরয়েড ও ইলেকট্রোম্যাগনেট এখান থেকেই।

@section math | 2 · Math: three applications of Fourier series | 55 min

### 1. Sound: a note is a list of harmonics
A musical instrument playing A at 440 Hz produces components at 440, 880, 1320, 1760, 2200 Hz, …, which are the multiples n f₀. The **recipe of amplitudes** is the **timbre**:
- **Flute:** almost a pure fundamental.
- **Clarinet:** mostly odd harmonics (like a square wave, with a closed tube at one end).
- **Violin:** all harmonics, falling slowly (like a sawtooth), so it sounds bright.
Octaves are factors of 2 (880 Hz is the octave above 440 Hz) and the third harmonic is a "perfect fifth" above the octave. A single sine sounds pure and dull. The ear splits sound into its harmonics (the cochlea is a mechanical Fourier analyser).

### 2. The full-wave rectifier
A diode bridge turns v = V_p sin ωt into |V_p sin ωt|, a wave of period π. Its Fourier series is

EQ: |sin x| = 2 ÷ π − (4 ÷ π) (cos 2x ÷ 3 + cos 4x ÷ 15 + cos 6x ÷ 35 + …) = 2 ÷ π − (4 ÷ π) Σ cos (2kx) ÷ (4k² − 1)

(check at x = 0: Σ 1 ÷ (4k² − 1) telescopes to ½, so the right side is 2 ÷ π − 2 ÷ π = 0 ✓).
- **DC part:** 2 V_p ÷ π = 0.637 V_p. This is what you want.
- **Ripple:** the largest harmonic is at **twice the mains frequency (100 Hz)** with amplitude 4 V_p ÷ (3π) = **0.424 V_p**, which is 67% of the DC value. A smoothing circuit (a capacitor) is an RC low-pass that kills the harmonics.

EX: A 12 V power supply
Peak V_p = 12 V, a smoothing capacitor C = 1000 μF and a load R = 100 Ω.
1. **DC:** 2 × 12 ÷ π = **7.64 V**.
2. **Unsmoothed ripple:** 4 × 12 ÷ (3π) = **5.09 V** at 100 Hz (67% of the DC).
3. **The smoothing filter:** f_c = 1 ÷ (2π × 100 × 10⁻³) = **1.59 Hz**. At 100 Hz the gain is 1 ÷ √(1 + (100 ÷ 1.59)²) = **0.0159**.
4. **Smoothed ripple:** 5.09 × 0.0159 = **0.081 V**, which is **1.06%** of the DC output. The next harmonic (200 Hz, amplitude 4 V_p ÷ (15π) = 1.02 V) is cut much harder, to 0.0081 V: a tenth of the first one.
5. **Why a bigger C:** doubling C halves the ripple. This is how a 12 V supply for a router stays steady.
/EX

### 3. A spring driven by a square wave
Take m x″ + b x′ + k x = F (t) with a square wave F of amplitude 1 and angular frequency ω. Each harmonic n of the force (amplitude 4 ÷ (nπ), frequency nω) drives its own steady response, with the amplitude from Week 19:

EQ: Aₙ = (4 ÷ (nπ)) ÷ √((k − m n² ω²)² + (b n ω)²)

EX: Resonance with the third harmonic
m = 1, k = 900 (so ω₀ = 30 rad/s), b = 2, and the drive is ω = 10 rad/s.
1. **n = 1** (10 rad/s): A₁ = 1.273 ÷ √(800² + 20²) = **0.00159**.
2. **n = 3** (30 rad/s, exactly ω₀!): A₃ = 0.424 ÷ √(0² + 60²) = **0.00707**. The third harmonic is only a third as strong as the first, yet its response is **4.4 times larger**, because it hits resonance.
3. **n = 5** (50 rad/s): A₅ = 0.2546 ÷ √(1600² + 100²) = **0.000159**.
4. **The lesson:** a spring pushed by a non-sinusoidal force at one-third of its natural frequency vibrates mostly at its natural frequency. A bridge, a floor or a washing machine can resonate with a harmonic of the footsteps or the drum, even if the main drive frequency is far from resonance. This is why engineers check harmonics.
/EX

TRAP: In the rectifier the ripple frequency is **twice** the mains frequency (100 Hz for 50 Hz mains), because both half-cycles are positive. The ripple fraction depends on the **product R C**: a bigger load resistance (smaller current) also means less ripple. In the driven spring, the response of the nth harmonic uses the frequency **n ω**, not ω. And do not confuse the resonance of the force at ω = ω₀ with the one at ω = ω₀ ÷ 3.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: Ampère's law, solenoids and toroids | 35 min

### The law
For any closed loop, the line integral of **B** around it equals μ₀ times the current passing through it:

EQ: ∮ **B** · d**l** = μ₀ I_enc

For symmetric currents, choose the loop where B is constant along it. It is the magnetic twin of Gauss's law.

- **Long straight wire:** a circle of radius r: B (2π r) = μ₀ I, so **B = μ₀ I ÷ (2π r)**, which agrees with Biot-Savart.
- **Inside a thick wire** of radius a with uniform current: I_enc = I r² ÷ a², so **B = μ₀ I r ÷ (2π a²)**: it rises linearly from the axis up to the surface, then falls as 1 ÷ r outside.
- **Solenoid** (n turns per metre, long): a rectangle that goes inside and comes back outside (where B ≈ 0): B L = μ₀ n L I, so **B = μ₀ n I** inside, uniform. Zero outside.
- **Toroid** (N turns on a ring): a circle of radius r inside the ring: B (2π r) = μ₀ N I, so **B = μ₀ N I ÷ (2π r)**. The field is trapped inside the ring.
- **Energy:** a magnetic field stores an energy per volume **u = B² ÷ (2 μ₀)**. With a magnetic core (iron), μ₀ is replaced by μ = μ_r μ₀ and the field is μ_r times larger.

BN: ∮B·dl = μ₀I_enc (গাউসের সূত্রের চৌম্বক যমজ)। সোজা তার: B = μ₀I/(2πr)। মোটা তারের ভেতরে B = μ₀Ir/(2πa²)। সলিনয়েড: B = μ₀nI (ভেতরে সমান, বাইরে ≈ 0)। টরয়েড: B = μ₀NI/(2πr)। শক্তি ঘনত্ব B²/(2μ₀)। লোহার কোরে B μ_r গুণ।

EX: Solenoids and toroids
1. **A solenoid:** N = 400 turns on ℓ = 20 cm (n = 2000 per metre) with I = 1.5 A: B = 4π × 10⁻⁷ × 2000 × 1.5 = **3.77 mT**, which is about 75 times Earth's field.
2. **With an iron core** (μ_r = 1000): the field would be 3.77 T in theory. In practice iron **saturates** at about 1.5 to 2 T, so a real electromagnet reaches about 2 T. That is why a scrapyard magnet can lift cars.
3. **Energy density:** B²/(2μ₀) = (3.77 × 10⁻³)² ÷ (2.513 × 10⁻⁶) = **5.65 J/m³**. In an MRI (B = 3 T) it is 3 ÷ 2μ₀: **3.58 × 10⁶ J/m³**, so a bore of 0.3 m³ stores about 1 MJ.
4. **A toroid:** N = 500 turns, I = 2 A, mean radius 10 cm: B = 4π × 10⁻⁷ × 500 × 2 ÷ (2π × 0.1) = **2.0 mT** inside, and zero outside. Transformers and chokes use toroids to keep the field inside.
5. **Inductance check:** for the solenoid, B = μ₀ n I and the flux through N turns of area A is N B A, so L = N B A ÷ I = μ₀ n N A = μ₀ N² A ÷ ℓ: **Week 20's formula**.
6. **A coaxial cable:** the inner conductor carries I and the outer sheath carries −I. A loop outside both has I_enc = 0, so B = 0 outside: no field leaks out, and no outside field gets in. That is why coax is used for antennas and cable TV.
/EX

TRAP: Ampère's law needs a **symmetric** loop where B is constant along the path. The enclosed current is the current **through the loop**, not all the current in the system. The solenoid formula B = μ₀ n I uses n, the turns per **metre** (N ÷ ℓ), not N. And the field outside an ideal solenoid is zero, but the field outside a bar magnet is not: a short coil is not a long solenoid.
