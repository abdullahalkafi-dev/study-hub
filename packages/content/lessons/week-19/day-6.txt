@section math | 2 · Part A: Second-order differential equations (14 marks, about 55 min) | e⁻² ≈ 0.135, e⁻³ ≈ 0.050

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 10
P: (2 marks) Solve y″ + 5y′ + 6y = 0 with y (0) = 1 and y′ (0) = 0, and find y (1). => r² + 5r + 6 = 0 gives r = −2, −3 (1 mark). C₁ + C₂ = 1 and −2C₁ − 3C₂ = 0: C₂ = −2 and C₁ = 3, so y = 3 e^(−2x) − 2 e^(−3x) and **y (1) = 0.406 − 0.100 = 0.306** (1 mark).
P: (1 mark) Solve y″ + 4y = 0 with y (0) = 0 and y′ (0) = 6, and find y (π ÷ 4). => y = C sin 2x with 2C = 6: **y = 3 sin 2x**, y (π ÷ 4) = **3**.
P: (1 mark) A mass of 0.5 kg hangs on a spring with k = 200 N/m. Find ω, f and T. => ω = √400 = **20 rad/s**, f = 20 ÷ 2π = **3.18 Hz**, T = **0.314 s**.
P: (2 marks) Solve y″ + 4y′ + 13y = 0 with y (0) = 2 and y′ (0) = 0. => r = −2 ± 3i (1 mark). y = e^(−2x) (2 cos 3x + C₂ sin 3x) and y′ (0) = −4 + 3C₂ = 0: C₂ = 4 ÷ 3, so **y = e^(−2x) (2 cos 3x + (4 ÷ 3) sin 3x)** (1 mark).
P: (1 mark) For m = 2 kg, b = 8 N s/m and k = 50 N/m find ζ and the damped frequency. => ω₀ = 5 rad/s; ζ = 8 ÷ (2 × 10) = **0.4**; ω_d = 5 √(1 − 0.16) = **4.58 rad/s**.
P: (1 mark) What damping b makes m = 2 kg and k = 50 N/m critically damped? => b = 2 √(m k) = 2 √100 = **20 N s/m**.
P: (2 marks) Solve y″ − y = 6 e^(2x) (general solution). => Homogeneous: r = ±1, so C₁ e^x + C₂ e^(−x) (1 mark). Try A e^(2x): (4 − 1) A = 6, A = 2 (1 mark): **y = C₁ e^x + C₂ e^(−x) + 2 e^(2x)**.
P: (1 mark) For y″ + 9y = cos 3x find the form of the particular solution and say why. => The simple guess A cos 3x + B sin 3x solves the homogeneous equation (resonance), so use **y_p = (x ÷ 6) sin 3x** (the amplitude grows linearly).
P: (2 marks) A car with m = 300 kg, k = 20,000 N/m and b = 3000 N s/m is pushed by F₀ = 1500 N at ω = 10 rad/s. Find the steady amplitude. => A = F₀ ÷ √[(k − mω²)² + (bω)²] = 1500 ÷ √[(−10,000)² + (30,000)²] = 1500 ÷ 31,623 = **0.0474 m**.
P: (1 mark) A swing has ζ = 0.02. Find Q and the fraction of energy lost per cycle. => Q = 1 ÷ 0.04 = **25**; the fraction is 2π ÷ 25 = **25%**.

P: (2 marks) In the two-battery circuit (ε₁ = 10 V with R₁ = 1 Ω; ε₂ = 4 V with R₂ = 2 Ω; middle R₃ = 4 Ω) find all the currents. => Loop 1: 10 − I₁ − 4 (I₁ + I₂) = 0 gives 5 I₁ + 4 I₂ = 10. P: (1 mark) A house wire has a one-way length 15 m of 1.5 mm² copper (ρ = 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m, loop = 30 m). Find the heat in it at 10 A and the voltage drop. => R = 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ × 30 ÷ 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ = **0.34 Ω**; P = I² R = **34 W**; drop = **3.4 V**.
P: (1 mark) A battery with ε = 12 V and r = 0.4 Ω drives a 3.6 Ω load. Find I, the terminal voltage, the load power and the efficiency. => I = 12 ÷ 4 = **3 A**; V = 12 − 1.2 = **10.8 V**; P = **32.4 W**; efficiency **90%**.
P: (1 mark) For ε = 9 V and r = 1.5 Ω, which load gets the most power and what is that power? => R = r = **1.5 Ω**; P_max = 81 ÷ 6 = **13.5 W**.
P: (1 mark) A charger has I₀ = 2 A in the CV phase with τ = 15 min and a cut-off of 0.2 A. Find the CV time and the charge added. => t = 15 ln 10 = **34.5 min**; charge = (2 − 0.2) × (15 ÷ 60) = **0.45 Ah = 450 mAh**.

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | Does a bigger push at resonance always mean more power?
P: A driven oscillator with m = 1 kg, k = 100 N/m and b = 2 N s/m is pushed by F₀ = 10 N at its natural frequency. Find the steady amplitude and the average power absorbed by the damper. => ω₀ = 10 rad/s; A = F₀ ÷ (b ω₀) = 10 ÷ 20 = **0.5 m**. The average power is ½ b ω² A² = 0.5 × 2 × 100 × 0.25 = **25 W**, which equals ½ F₀ ω A = 0.5 × 10 × 10 × 0.5 = **25 W** (the power the force puts in). At resonance, the force is in phase with the velocity, so all of its work goes into the damper.

LVL: Challenge | Charging two batteries
P: A 12 V charger with 0.5 Ω internal resistance charges a 9 V battery with r = 0.5 Ω, and a lamp (R = 2 Ω) is connected in parallel with the battery. Find the charger current, the lamp current and the battery current. => Let V be the common voltage. Charger: I = (12 − V) ÷ 0.5. Lamp: V ÷ 2. Battery (charging): I_b = (V − 9) ÷ 0.5. Junction: (12 − V) ÷ 0.5 = V ÷ 2 + (V − 9) ÷ 0.5, so 24 − 2V = 0.5 V + 2V − 18 and 42 = 4.5 V: **V = 9.33 V**. The charger gives **5.33 A**, the lamp takes **4.67 A** and the battery is charged at **0.67 A** (4.67 + 0.67 = 5.33 ✓).
