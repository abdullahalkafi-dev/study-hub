# Damping: under, critical and over. Then: Kirchhoff's rules
lead: Real springs lose energy to friction. Damping changes the solution of m x″ + b x′ + k x = 0 in three different ways, depending on a single number, the discriminant. A car's shock absorbers are tuned to sit right at the best compromise. In physics, you learn Kirchhoff's two rules, which solve any circuit, however tangled: charge does not pile up at a junction, and the voltage gains and drops around a loop add to zero.

BN: বাস্তব স্প্রিং ঘর্ষণে শক্তি হারায়। m x″ + b x′ + k x = 0 এর সমাধান অবমন্দনের কারণে তিন রকম হয়, একটাই সংখ্যার (discriminant) উপর নির্ভর করে। গাড়ির শক অ্যাবজরবার সেরা সমঝোতার ঠিক কাছে বসানো হয়। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে কার্শফের দুটো নিয়ম শিখবে, যা যেকোনো জটিল বর্তনী সমাধান করে: জংশনে চার্জ জমে না, আর লুপে ভোল্টেজ বৃদ্ধি ও পতনের যোগফল শূন্য।

@section math | 2 · Math: the three kinds of damping | 55 min

### Three cases
For m x″ + b x′ + k x = 0 the roots are r = [−b ± √(b² − 4mk)] ÷ 2m. Let ω₀ = √(k ÷ m) and define the **damping ratio** ζ = b ÷ (2√(m k)) (so that **b_c = 2√(m k)** is the critical damping):

- **Underdamped (ζ < 1):** r = −ζω₀ ± i ω_d with ω_d = ω₀ √(1 − ζ²). x = e^(−ζω₀ t) (C₁ cos ω_d t + C₂ sin ω_d t): oscillates with a decaying envelope.
- **Critically damped (ζ = 1):** double root r = −ω₀. x = (C₁ + C₂ t) e^(−ω₀ t): the fastest return to zero **without** overshoot.
- **Overdamped (ζ > 1):** two real negative roots, x = C₁ e^(r₁ t) + C₂ e^(r₂ t): slow creep back.

BN: ζ = b/(2√(mk)); b_c = 2√(mk) সংকট অবমন্দন। ζ < 1: দুলে দুলে কমে (ω_d = ω₀√(1 − ζ²)); ζ = 1: দোল ছাড়াই সবচেয়ে দ্রুত ফেরা, x = (C₁ + C₂t)e^(−ω₀t); ζ > 1: দুটো বাস্তব ঋণাত্মক মূল, ধীরে ফেরা।

EX: A car suspension
A quarter of a car: m = 300 kg and a spring k = 20,000 N/m.
1. **Natural frequency:** ω₀ = √(20000 ÷ 300) = **8.165 rad/s** (1.30 Hz). **Critical damping:** b_c = 2√(300 × 20000) = **4899 N s/m**.
2. **b = 3000 N s/m:** ζ = 0.612 (underdamped). ω_d = 8.165 × √(1 − 0.375) = **6.45 rad/s**; the amplitude decays as e^(−5t) (ζω₀ = 5.0 per second). The **overshoot** after one swing is e^(−πζ ÷ √(1 − ζ²)) = **8.8%**. The settling time (to about 2%) is ≈ 4 ÷ (ζω₀) = **0.80 s**. This is a typical good tuning, with a bit of overshoot.
3. **b = 1500 N s/m:** ζ = 0.306: overshoot **36%**, settling 1.6 s: a bouncy ride that feels "floaty".
4. **b = 4899 (critical):** no overshoot, settling about 0.49 s.
5. **b = 8000 N s/m:** ζ = 1.63, the roots are r = −2.79 and −23.9: a very slow creep (time constant 1 ÷ 2.79 = 0.36 s for the slow mode): the car feels "stiff" and gives a hard ride over bumps.
6. **Critical damping example:** y″ + 4y′ + 4y = 0, y (0) = 1, y′ (0) = 0. Double root r = −2: y = (C₁ + C₂ x) e^(−2x), C₁ = 1 and y′ (0) = −2C₁ + C₂ = 0 gives C₂ = 2: **y = (1 + 2x) e^(−2x)**, y (1) = 3 e⁻² = **0.406**.
7. **Quality factor:** Q = 1 ÷ (2ζ): ζ = 0.306 gives Q = 1.63. A low ζ (high Q) means a sharp resonance (Day 4).
/EX

TRAP: Do not mix up b² − 4mk with b: heavy damping is **not** "better": the best return is at critical damping, and an overdamped system is slower. The frequency of a damped oscillation is ω_d, which is below ω₀. And the double root needs the extra factor **t** (C₁ + C₂ t), not just C e^(rt) twice.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: Kirchhoff's rules | 35 min

### The two rules
1. **Junction rule** (conservation of charge): the currents entering a junction equal the currents leaving: **Σ I = 0**.
2. **Loop rule** (conservation of energy, the field is conservative): around any closed loop the potential changes add to zero: **Σ ΔV = 0**. Going through a battery from − to + is a **gain** +ε; going through a resistor **in the direction of the current** is a **drop** −I R.

Method: label the currents with assumed directions (a negative answer means the opposite direction), write the junction equation and as many loop equations as needed to reach as many equations as unknowns, and solve.

BN: ১) জংশন নিয়ম: ΣI = 0 (চার্জ সংরক্ষণ)। ২) লুপ নিয়ম: বদ্ধ লুপে ΣΔV = 0 (শক্তি সংরক্ষণ)। ব্যাটারি − থেকে + এ গেলে +ε; রোধ প্রবাহের দিকে গেলে −IR। প্রবাহের দিক অনুমান করো; উত্তর ঋণাত্মক হলে দিক উল্টো।

EX: A two-battery circuit
Branch 1 has ε₁ = 12 V and R₁ = 2 Ω, branch 2 has ε₂ = 6 V and R₂ = 3 Ω, and R₃ = 6 Ω is the middle branch. Let I₁ and I₂ flow up through the batteries into the top junction and I₃ flow down through R₃.
1. **Junction:** I₃ = I₁ + I₂.
2. **Loop 1:** 12 − 2 I₁ − 6 I₃ = 0. **Loop 2:** 6 − 3 I₂ − 6 I₃ = 0.
3. Substituting I₃: 12 = 8 I₁ + 6 I₂ and 6 = 6 I₁ + 9 I₂. Solving: **I₁ = 2 A**, **I₂ = −0.667 A**, **I₃ = 1.333 A**.
4. **Meaning:** I₂ is negative, so the 6 V battery is being **charged** by the 12 V battery (the current flows into its + terminal).
5. **Check with energy:** ε₁ supplies 12 × 2 = 24 W. ε₂ absorbs 6 × 0.667 = 4 W. The resistors dissipate 2² × 2 + 0.667² × 3 + 1.333² × 6 = 8 + 1.33 + 10.67 = **20 W**, and 24 − 4 = 20 ✓.
/EX

EX: Smaller cases
1. **A battery charger:** a 14 V charger charges a 12 V battery with a total resistance 0.1 Ω: I = (14 − 12) ÷ 0.1 = **20 A** at the start. Chargers limit the current.
2. **A single loop with two batteries opposed:** 9 V and 5 V in series opposition with 4 Ω total: I = (9 − 5) ÷ 4 = **1 A**.
3. **Two batteries in parallel with unequal EMF** (12 V and 11.8 V, each with r = 0.05 Ω, no load): the circulating current is 0.2 ÷ 0.1 = **2 A**, wasting 0.4 W: never connect unequal batteries in parallel.
/EX

TRAP: Choose the **direction** of each current and keep it: a negative result is not a mistake. In the loop rule the sign depends on the direction **you travel**: crossing a resistor against the current gives +I R. The number of **independent** equations must equal the number of unknowns (do not write all three loops: the third is redundant).
