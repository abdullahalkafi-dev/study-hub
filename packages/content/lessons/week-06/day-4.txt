# Implicit differentiation and related rates. Then: forces from flowing momentum
lead: Some curves, like a circle, are not a single function y = f(x). Implicit differentiation finds their slopes anyway, and it lets you handle quantities that change together, like a sliding ladder. In physics you use F = dp/dt on streams of water, bullets and rocket exhaust, and see why a rocket works in empty space.

BN: কিছু বক্ররেখা, যেমন বৃত্ত, y = f(x) আকারে লেখা যায় না। অন্তর্নিহিত (implicit) অন্তরকলন দিয়ে তবুও ঢাল বের করা যায়, আর একসঙ্গে বদলানো রাশি, যেমন পিছলে পড়া মই, সামলানো যায়। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে F = dp/dt ব্যবহার করব পানির ধারা, গুলি আর রকেটের নিঃসরণে, আর দেখব মহাশূন্যে রকেট কীভাবে চলে।

@section math | 2 · Math: implicit differentiation | 55 min

### The problem
The circle x² + y² = 25 is not one function: for each x there are two y values. But near any point it behaves like a function y(x), so it has a slope. We can find that slope without solving for y.

**The method.** Differentiate **both sides with respect to x**, remembering that y depends on x. Whenever you differentiate something with y, the chain rule adds a factor y′ (for example d/dx y² = 2y y′). Then solve for y′.

EX: The circle
1. x² + y² = 25. Differentiate: 2x + 2y y′ = 0.
2. Solve: **y′ = −x ÷ y**.
3. At (3, 4): y′ = −3/4. This agrees with the tangent we found in Week 4 using the radius.
4. At (0, 5) the slope is 0 (the top of the circle), and at (5, 0) the denominator is 0: a vertical tangent ✓.
/EX

EX: An ellipse
1. x²/9 + y²/4 = 1. Differentiate: 2x/9 + 2y y′/4 = 0, so y y′/2 = −2x/9.
2. **y′ = −4x ÷ (9y)**. At (3 cos t, 2 sin t) this is a clear slope without solving for y.
/EX

EX: The folium of Descartes
1. x³ + y³ = 6xy. Differentiate (product rule on the right): 3x² + 3y² y′ = 6y + 6x y′.
2. Collect y′: y′ (3y² − 6x) = 6y − 3x², so **y′ = (2y − x²) ÷ (y² − 2x)**.
3. The point (3, 3) is on the curve (27 + 27 = 54 = 6 × 9). There y′ = (6 − 9) ÷ (9 − 6) = **−1**.
/EX

EX: Using it for a tangent line
1. At (3, 3) on the folium: y − 3 = −1 (x − 3), so **y = −x + 6**.
/EX

### Related rates
Two quantities linked by an equation change together. Differentiate the equation with respect to **time** t, then substitute.

EX: The sliding ladder
1. A 5 m ladder leans against a wall. The foot is pulled away at 0.5 m/s. How fast does the top slide down when the foot is 3 m from the wall?
2. x² + y² = 25. Differentiate with respect to t: 2x (dx/dt) + 2y (dy/dt) = 0, so dy/dt = −(x ÷ y)(dx/dt).
3. When x = 3, y = 4, so dy/dt = −(3/4)(0.5) = **−0.375 m/s**: the top slides down at 0.375 m/s.
4. As the foot approaches 5 m, y → 0 and the top falls faster and faster (the lab shows this).
/EX

EX: A ripple
1. A circular ripple has radius increasing at 3 cm/s. How fast is the area growing when r = 10 cm?
2. A = π r², so dA/dt = 2π r dr/dt = 2π × 10 × 3 = **188.5 cm²/s**.
/EX

### Steps for related-rates problems
1. Draw and name the quantities that change.
2. Write one equation connecting them (valid at all times).
3. Differentiate **both sides with respect to t**, using the chain rule.
4. **Only now** substitute the numbers for the moment asked about.

BN: অন্তর্নিহিত অন্তরকলনে দুই পাশকে x এর সাপেক্ষে অন্তরকলন করো; y এর যেকোনো পদে চেইন রুল দিয়ে y′ গুণ হয়। বৃত্ত x² + y² = 25 থেকে y′ = −x/y। সম্পর্কিত হার (related rates) এ সমীকরণকে সময় t এর সাপেক্ষে অন্তরকলন করো, তারপর সংখ্যা বসাও, আগে নয়।

TRAP: Do not substitute numbers **before** differentiating: if you put x = 3 first, the variable disappears and the derivative becomes 0. Also do not forget the y′ factor on every y term: d/dx y³ = 3y² y′, not 3y².

@section physics | 3 · Physics: forces from flowing momentum | 35 min

### Streams and rates
When a steady stream of mass hits a surface, the force is the **rate of change of momentum**:

EQ: F = (dm/dt) Δv

where dm/dt is the mass per second and Δv the change in velocity of each piece.

EX: A fire hose
1. A hose delivers 20 kg of water per second at 15 m/s onto a wall, and the water stops. F = 20 × 15 = **300 N**.
2. If the water bounces straight back at the same speed, Δv = 30 m/s, so **600 N**.
/EX

EX: Machine gun
1. A gun fires 10 bullets per second, each 0.02 kg at 600 m/s. The average recoil force: 10 × 0.02 × 600 = **120 N**.
/EX

### Variable mass and the product rule
Newton's law in the form F = dp/dt = d(mv)/dt. If the mass changes, the product rule from Monday applies:

EQ: F = m dv/dt + v dm/dt

EX: A conveyor belt
1. Sand falls from a hopper onto a belt moving at a constant 2 m/s, at 5 kg/s. The belt's speed does not change, so dv/dt = 0 and F = v dm/dt = 2 × 5 = **10 N**.
2. Power from the motor = F v = 10 × 2 = **20 W**.
3. The kinetic energy gained by the sand per second is ½ × 5 × 4 = 10 W. **Half the power is lost** as heat in the sliding of the sand when it first lands on the belt.
/EX

### Rockets: why they work in empty space
A rocket throws mass backward at speed v_e (relative to the rocket). Momentum conservation means the rocket gains forward momentum. The thrust is

EQ: thrust = (dm/dt) v_e

and the rocket's acceleration at the launch pad is a = thrust ÷ m − g.

EX: A launch
1. Burn rate 100 kg/s, exhaust speed 2500 m/s: thrust = 250,000 N.
2. For a 20,000 kg rocket, a = 250,000 ÷ 20,000 − 9.8 = 12.5 − 9.8 = **2.7 m/s²** upward.
3. As fuel burns, m falls while the thrust stays the same, so the acceleration **rises**. After 100 s, m = 10,000 kg and a = 25 − 9.8 = **15.2 m/s²**.
/EX

### Kinetic energy and momentum together
Since KE = ½ m v² and p = m v, we get

EQ: KE = p² ÷ (2m)

Two bodies with the same momentum have different kinetic energies: the lighter one has more. A 0.02 kg bullet and a 4 kg rifle have equal and opposite momenta, but the bullet has the energy 200 times larger (the mass ratio).

EX: The rifle and bullet
1. The bullet: p = 12 kg·m/s, KE = 144 ÷ 0.04 = **3600 J**.
2. The rifle: p = 12, KE = 144 ÷ 8 = **18 J**. The light bullet takes 99.5% of the energy.
/EX

TRAP: In F = (dm/dt) Δv, Δv is the change in velocity of the **mass that hits** the surface (a bounce doubles it). The rocket's thrust uses the exhaust speed **relative to the rocket**, not relative to the ground. And remember that g is subtracted for a vertical launch.
