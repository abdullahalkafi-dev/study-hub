@section math | 2 · Math: product and quotient rules | 55 min

### Why the derivative of a product is not the product of derivatives
Try x² × x³ = x⁵. The derivative of x⁵ is 5x⁴. But the product of the derivatives is 2x × 3x² = 6x³, which is wrong. So we need a real rule.

**A picture.** Think of a rectangle with width f and height g, so the area is f g. When x increases a little, the width grows by Δf and the height by Δg. The area grows by three pieces: f Δg (a strip on top), g Δf (a strip on the side) and the tiny corner Δf Δg. Divide by Δx and let it shrink: the corner vanishes (it is a product of two small things), leaving two strips.

EQ: (f g)′ = f′ g + f g′

**Proof with limits.** f(x + h) g(x + h) − f(x) g(x) = f(x + h) [g(x + h) − g(x)] + g(x) [f(x + h) − f(x)]. Divide by h and let h → 0. Since f is continuous, f(x + h) → f(x), so we get f g′ + g f′. ∎

BN: গুণফলের অন্তরকলজ (fg)′ = f′g + fg′। ছবিতে ভাবো: আয়তক্ষেত্রের দৈর্ঘ্য f ও প্রস্থ g বাড়লে ক্ষেত্রফল বাড়ে দুটি ফালি (fΔg ও gΔf) আর একটা ক্ষুদ্র কোণা (ΔfΔg), শেষেরটা হারিয়ে যায়।

EX: Check the rule
1. (x² · x³)′ = 2x · x³ + x² · 3x² = 2x⁴ + 3x⁴ = **5x⁴** ✓, matching the derivative of x⁵.
/EX

EX: Use the rule
1. d/dx (x² sin x) = 2x sin x + x² cos x.
2. d/dx (x eˣ) = 1 · eˣ + x · eˣ = **eˣ (1 + x)**.
3. d/dx ((3x + 1)(x² − 2)) = 3(x² − 2) + (3x + 1)(2x) = 3x² − 6 + 6x² + 2x = **9x² + 2x − 6**. Check by expanding first: 3x³ + x² − 6x − 2 has derivative 9x² + 2x − 6 ✓.
/EX

### The quotient rule
Write f ÷ g = f × (1 ÷ g). First, (1 ÷ g)′ = −g′ ÷ g² (from the chain rule you learn tomorrow, or directly from the definition). Then the product rule gives:

EQ: (f ÷ g)′ = (f′ g − f g′) ÷ g²

A memory trick: "low d-high minus high d-low, over low squared", where low is the denominator g and high is the numerator f.

EX: tan x
1. tan x = sin x ÷ cos x. Then (cos x · cos x − sin x · (−sin x)) ÷ cos²x = (cos²x + sin²x) ÷ cos²x = **1 ÷ cos²x = sec²x**.
/EX

EX: A rational function
1. y = x ÷ (x² + 1). y′ = ((x² + 1) · 1 − x · 2x) ÷ (x² + 1)² = **(1 − x²) ÷ (x² + 1)²**.
2. It is zero at x = ±1. At x = 1, y = 1/2, the highest value of the function. Between x = −1 and 1, y′ > 0, so y rises, then falls.
/EX

EX: Exponential over x
1. y = eˣ ÷ x. y′ = (eˣ · x − eˣ · 1) ÷ x² = **eˣ (x − 1) ÷ x²**.
2. Zero at x = 1, and y(1) = e = 2.718. For x > 0 this is the minimum of eˣ ÷ x: no matter how you slice it, eˣ ÷ x ≥ e.
/EX

TRAP: Do not differentiate a product factor by factor, and do not swap the order in the quotient rule: the numerator is f′g **minus** fg′, not the other way round. When a product is simple to expand, expanding first and then using the power rule is often faster, and it is a good way to check your answer.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: momentum and impulse | 35 min

### Momentum
The **momentum** of a body is its mass times its velocity:

EQ: p = m v     unit: kg·m/s (also N·s)

It is a **vector**: it points the way the body moves. A heavy slow truck and a light fast bullet can have the same momentum. Newton's original second law was written for momentum:

EQ: F_net = dp/dt

The net force is the **rate of change of momentum**. For constant mass this gives F = m dv/dt = m a, the form you know. (Notice the derivative: you now understand where F = ma really comes from.)

### Impulse
If a force F acts for a time Δt, the **impulse** is J = F Δt, and from F = Δp ÷ Δt:

EQ: J = F Δt = Δp = m v_final − m v_initial

An impulse changes the momentum. If the force changes with time, the impulse is the **area under the force-time graph**.

BN: ভরবেগ p = mv (ভেক্টর)। নিউটনের দ্বিতীয় সূত্রের মূল রূপ: F = dp/dt, অর্থাৎ বলের মান ভরবেগের পরিবর্তনের হার। ঘাত J = FΔt = Δp: কোনো বল যত বেশি সময় ধরে কাজ করে, ভরবেগের পরিবর্তন তত বেশি। বল-সময় লেখচিত্রের নিচের ক্ষেত্রফলই ঘাত।

EX: Hitting a ball back
1. A 0.16 kg cricket ball arrives at 30 m/s and is hit straight back at 40 m/s. Take the outgoing direction as positive: v_i = −30 and v_f = +40.
2. Δp = 0.16 × (40 − (−30)) = 0.16 × 70 = **11.2 N·s**.
3. If the bat touches the ball for 5 ms (0.005 s), the average force is 11.2 ÷ 0.005 = **2240 N**, about 230 kg of weight.
/EX

EX: Area under a force-time graph
1. A football (0.5 kg) is kicked. The force rises from 0 to 800 N and falls back to 0 over 10 ms, a triangle.
2. Impulse = ½ × 0.01 × 800 = **4 N·s**. The ball leaves at v = 4 ÷ 0.5 = **8 m/s** (from rest).
/EX

### The same impulse, a gentler force
Because F = Δp ÷ Δt, a fixed change of momentum can come from a **large force for a short time** or a **small force for a long time**. Every safety device uses this: airbags, crumple zones, helmets, bending your knees when you land, catching a ball by pulling your hands back.

EX: Crash comparison
1. A 1200 kg car at 15 m/s stops. Δp = 1200 × 15 = 18,000 N·s.
2. Hitting a rigid wall in 0.1 s: F = 18,000 ÷ 0.1 = **180,000 N**, and the deceleration is 150 m/s², or 15 g.
3. With a crumple zone that lengthens the crash to 0.4 s: F = **45,000 N**, 3.8 g. The momentum change is the same, but the force on the passengers is four times smaller.
/EX

### Conservation of momentum
For two bodies that exert forces on each other (and nothing else pushes them), Newton's third law says the forces are equal and opposite, so the two impulses are equal and opposite, and the momentum changes cancel:

EQ: m₁ v₁ + m₂ v₂ = m₁ v₁′ + m₂ v₂′     (total momentum before = total momentum after)

This holds for any collision or explosion in an isolated system, including ones where energy is lost.

EX: Recoil
1. A 80 kg skater at rest on smooth ice throws a 2 kg ball at 10 m/s. The total momentum was 0, so 2 × 10 + 80 v = 0.
2. **v = −0.25 m/s**: the skater slides backwards at 0.25 m/s.
/EX

EX: A rifle
1. A 4 kg rifle fires a 0.02 kg bullet at 600 m/s. The bullet's momentum is 12 kg·m/s forward, so the rifle gets 12 kg·m/s backward.
2. Recoil speed = 12 ÷ 4 = **3 m/s**. This is why firing a gun pushes your shoulder.
/EX

TRAP: Momentum is a vector. Choose a positive direction and give **negative** values to velocities in the other direction (as in the cricket ball example). And do not mix up momentum with kinetic energy: momentum depends on v, kinetic energy on v², and kinetic energy is not conserved in most collisions.
