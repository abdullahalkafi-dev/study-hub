# The chain rule. Then: collisions in one dimension
lead: Most useful functions are built by plugging one function into another, like sin(x²) or e^(−3x). The chain rule differentiates them by multiplying the rates of the pieces. In physics, you use conservation of momentum to predict what happens when two bodies collide, from billiard balls to car crashes.

BN: বেশিরভাগ কাজের ফাংশন একটা আরেকটার ভেতরে বসিয়ে বানানো, যেমন sin(x²) বা e^(−3x)। চেইন রুল (chain rule) প্রতিটা অংশের পরিবর্তনের হার গুণ করে অন্তরকলজ বের করে। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে ভরবেগের সংরক্ষণ দিয়ে দুটি বস্তুর সংঘর্ষের ফল আগে থেকে বলা যায়, বিলিয়ার্ড বল থেকে গাড়ির দুর্ঘটনা পর্যন্ত।

@section math | 2 · Math: the chain rule | 55 min

### The idea: rates multiply
Suppose y depends on u, and u depends on x. If y changes 3 times as fast as u, and u changes 2 times as fast as x, then y changes 3 × 2 = 6 times as fast as x. Like a chain of gears: the speeds multiply.

EQ: dy/dx = dy/du × du/dx     or     (f(g(x)))′ = f′(g(x)) · g′(x)

In words: **differentiate the outside function (leaving the inside alone), then multiply by the derivative of the inside.**

**Why it works.** The change in y over a small Δx is (Δy ÷ Δu) × (Δu ÷ Δx). Let Δx → 0, and each ratio becomes its derivative. (A careful proof handles the case Δu = 0, but the idea is exactly this.)

BN: চেইন রুল: dy/dx = (dy/du) × (du/dx)। বাইরের ফাংশনের অন্তরকলজ নাও (ভেতরেরটা যেমন আছে তেমন রেখে), তারপর ভেতরের ফাংশনের অন্তরকলজ দিয়ে গুণ করো। গিয়ারের মতো: গতির অনুপাতগুলো গুণ হয়।

EX: Powers of a bracket
1. y = (3x + 1)⁵. Outside: u⁵, derivative 5u⁴. Inside: 3x + 1, derivative 3.
2. y′ = 5 (3x + 1)⁴ × 3 = **15 (3x + 1)⁴**.
/EX

EX: Trig and exponential with an inner function
1. d/dx sin(x²) = cos(x²) × 2x = **2x cos(x²)**.
2. d/dx e^(−x²) = e^(−x²) × (−2x) = **−2x e^(−x²)** (the bell curve, with a peak at x = 0).
3. d/dx cos(5x) = −sin(5x) × 5 = **−5 sin(5x)**.
4. d/dx √(1 + x²) = (1 ÷ (2√(1 + x²))) × 2x = **x ÷ √(1 + x²)**.
/EX

### More examples
EX: Nested chains
1. y = sin(cos 3x). Outside sin, then cos, then 3x. Multiply three derivatives: y′ = cos(cos 3x) × (−sin 3x) × 3 = **−3 sin(3x) cos(cos 3x)**.
/EX

EX: A trap with sin²x
1. sin²x means (sin x)². Outside: u², inside: sin x. y′ = 2 sin x × cos x = **sin 2x**, using the double angle formula from Week 3.
2. Check from cos 2x = 1 − 2 sin²x: sin²x = (1 − cos 2x) ÷ 2, whose derivative is (2 sin 2x) ÷ 2 = sin 2x ✓.
/EX

EX: Chain with product and quotient
1. y = x² e^(3x). Product rule, with the chain rule inside: y′ = 2x e^(3x) + x² × 3 e^(3x) = **x e^(3x) (2 + 3x)**.
2. y = 1 ÷ (x² + 1)³ = (x² + 1)⁻³. Chain: −3 (x² + 1)⁻⁴ × 2x = **−6x ÷ (x² + 1)⁴**.
/EX

EX: Exponentials with any base
1. Write 2ˣ = e^(x ln 2). By the chain rule the derivative is e^(x ln 2) × ln 2 = **(ln 2) 2ˣ**. This confirms the formula you found with limits last week.
/EX

EX: The logarithm
1. Start with e^(ln x) = x, differentiate both sides with the chain rule: e^(ln x) × (ln x)′ = 1, so x × (ln x)′ = 1.
2. **d/dx (ln x) = 1/x**. We use this properly tomorrow.
/EX

### Rates that depend on each other
EX: A growing sphere
1. A balloon's radius grows at 0.2 cm/s. How fast does the volume grow when r = 5 cm?
2. V = (4/3) π r³. By the chain rule dV/dt = (dV/dr)(dr/dt) = 4π r² × 0.2.
3. At r = 5: 4π × 25 × 0.2 = **62.8 cm³/s**. The same growth of the radius adds more volume when the balloon is bigger.
/EX

TRAP: Forgetting the inner derivative is the most common mistake: the derivative of sin(3x) is 3 cos(3x), not cos(3x). Also do not mix up (sin x)² and sin(x²): the first has outside u² and inside sin x, the second has outside sin u and inside x².

@section physics | 3 · Physics: collisions | 35 min

### Three kinds of collision
In every collision of an isolated system, **momentum is conserved**. Kinetic energy may or may not be.

| Type | Momentum | Kinetic energy | Example |
| Elastic | conserved | conserved | billiard balls, atoms in a gas |
| Inelastic | conserved | partly lost | car crashes, a dropped ball |
| Perfectly inelastic | conserved | maximum loss, bodies stick together | a bullet in wood, coupling train carriages |

The lost kinetic energy turns into heat, sound and deformation.

### Perfectly inelastic (sticking)
EQ: m₁ u₁ + m₂ u₂ = (m₁ + m₂) v

EX: A car crash
1. A 1000 kg car at 20 m/s hits a stationary 1500 kg car, and they lock together.
2. v = 20,000 ÷ 2500 = **8 m/s**.
3. KE before: ½ × 1000 × 400 = 200,000 J. After: ½ × 2500 × 64 = 80,000 J. **120,000 J (60%) is lost** to deformation and heat.
/EX

### Elastic collisions
Two equations hold: momentum, and kinetic energy. Combining them gives an elegant fact: **the relative speed of approach equals the relative speed of separation.**

EQ: u₁ − u₂ = −(v₁ − v₂)

**Why.** m₁(u₁ − v₁) = m₂(v₂ − u₂) from momentum. m₁(u₁² − v₁²) = m₂(v₂² − u₂²) from energy. Divide the second by the first: u₁ + v₁ = v₂ + u₂, so u₁ − u₂ = v₂ − v₁ ∎.

The final velocities are:

EQ: v₁ = ((m₁ − m₂) u₁ + 2 m₂ u₂) ÷ (m₁ + m₂)     v₂ = ((m₂ − m₁) u₂ + 2 m₁ u₁) ÷ (m₁ + m₂)

EX: Heavy hits light, and the reverse
1. A 2 kg ball at 6 m/s hits a 4 kg ball at rest, elastically. v₁ = ((2 − 4) × 6) ÷ 6 = **−2 m/s** (it bounces back). v₂ = (2 × 2 × 6) ÷ 6 = **4 m/s**.
2. Check momentum: 12 = 2(−2) + 4(4) = 12 ✓. Check energy: 36 = ½ × 2 × 4 + ½ × 4 × 16 = 4 + 32 = 36 ✓.
3. **Equal masses:** v₁ = u₂ and v₂ = u₁. They exchange velocities (Newton's cradle).
4. **Heavy hits a tiny stationary one:** the tiny one flies off at nearly 2u (a truck hitting a ball).
/EX

BN: সংঘর্ষে ভরবেগ সবসময় সংরক্ষিত। স্থিতিস্থাপক (elastic) সংঘর্ষে গতিশক্তিও সংরক্ষিত, আর কাছে আসার আপেক্ষিক বেগ = দূরে যাওয়ার আপেক্ষিক বেগ। অস্থিতিস্থাপকে গতিশক্তির কিছুটা তাপ ও বিকৃতিতে যায়। সম্পূর্ণ অস্থিতিস্থাপকে বস্তু দুটি আটকে যায়। সমান ভর হলে বেগ বিনিময় হয়।

### The coefficient of restitution
In real collisions, the speed of separation is a fraction e of the speed of approach: v₂ − v₁ = e (u₁ − u₂), with e = 1 for elastic and e = 0 for sticking.

EX: A bouncing ball
1. A ball dropped from 1.0 m bounces back to 0.64 m. The speeds just before and after the bounce are √(2 g h), so e = √(0.64 ÷ 1.0) = **0.8**.
2. After n bounces the height is 0.64ⁿ metres. After 3 bounces: 0.262 m.
/EX

### Measuring a bullet: the ballistic pendulum
EX: Bullet speed from a swing
1. A 0.01 kg bullet embeds in a 2 kg block hanging on strings. The block swings up by 0.1 m.
2. After the collision (perfectly inelastic), energy conservation gives V = √(2 g h) = √1.96 = 1.4 m/s.
3. Before the collision, momentum: 0.01 v = 2.01 × 1.4 = 2.814, so **v = 281 m/s**.
4. Two different laws in two stages: momentum for the collision (energy is lost), energy for the swing.
/EX

TRAP: Do not use energy conservation for the collision stage of a sticking or deforming collision: energy is lost there. Use momentum for the collision, then energy for what happens after it. Also remember signs: a body moving left has a negative velocity.
