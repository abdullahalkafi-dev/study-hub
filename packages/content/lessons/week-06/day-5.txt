@section math | 2 · Math: logarithmic differentiation and higher derivatives | 55 min

### When is logarithmic differentiation useful?
There are two situations the other rules handle badly: a variable raised to a variable power (like x^x), and a product or quotient of many factors. The logarithm turns powers into products and products into sums, which are easy to differentiate.

**The method.**
1. Take ln of both sides and simplify with the log laws.
2. Differentiate both sides with respect to x (implicit differentiation: the left side gives y′ ÷ y).
3. Multiply by y to isolate y′, then replace y by the original expression.

EX: x to the power x
1. y = xˣ. Then ln y = x ln x.
2. y′ ÷ y = ln x + 1 (product rule on the right).
3. **y′ = xˣ (ln x + 1)**.
4. It is zero at x = 1/e, so xˣ has its minimum at x = 1/e = 0.368, where the value is (1/e)^(1/e) = 0.692.

EX: A power with a variable in both places
1. y = x^(sin x) for x > 0. ln y = sin x ln x.
2. y′ ÷ y = cos x ln x + sin x ÷ x.
3. **y′ = x^(sin x) (cos x ln x + sin x ÷ x)**.
/EX

EX: A big product and quotient
1. y = (x² + 1)³ (x − 2)⁴ ÷ (x + 5)². ln y = 3 ln(x² + 1) + 4 ln(x − 2) − 2 ln(x + 5).
2. y′ ÷ y = 6x ÷ (x² + 1) + 4 ÷ (x − 2) − 2 ÷ (x + 5).
3. Multiply by y. Notice how much simpler this was than three nested product and quotient rules.
4. This is also a clean way to see the **relative rate of change**, y′ ÷ y. If a measurement is a product of factors, the percentage errors add, as the sum shows.
/EX

EX: The general power rule
1. For any real n and x > 0, y = xⁿ. ln y = n ln x, so y′ ÷ y = n ÷ x and **y′ = n xⁿ⁻¹**. This proves the power rule for every real exponent.
/EX

### Higher derivatives
The derivative of f′ is f″ (the **second derivative**), then f‴ (third), and so on. In Leibniz notation, d²y/dx², d³y/dx³.

EX: Position, velocity, acceleration, jerk
1. s(t) = t³ − 6t² + 9t. Velocity v = 3t² − 12t + 9. Acceleration a = 6t − 12. The **jerk** (rate of change of acceleration) is **6 m/s³**, a constant.
2. Jerk matters for comfort: lifts and trains are designed to limit it, since sudden changes in acceleration feel like jolts.
/EX

EX: Patterns
1. (eᵏˣ)⁽ⁿ⁾ = kⁿ eᵏˣ.
2. sin x has derivatives cos x, −sin x, −cos x, sin x: repeats every four.
3. xⁿ differentiated n times gives n! (for example, x⁴ gives 24 after four derivatives), and then 0.
4. Always check simple cases: for f = x⁴, f‴ = 24x and f⁗ = 24.
/EX

### Concavity and inflection points
The second derivative tells you how the graph bends. If f″ > 0, the graph is **concave up** (like a cup); if f″ < 0, **concave down**. A point where the bending changes is an **inflection point**.

EX: Finding inflection
1. f(x) = x³ − 3x². f′ = 3x² − 6x and f″ = 6x − 6.
2. f″ = 0 at x = 1, where it changes from negative to positive. The inflection point is **(1, −2)**.
3. For x < 1 the graph bends down, for x > 1 up. The flat spots (f′ = 0) are at x = 0 (a local maximum, since f″ = −6 < 0) and x = 2 (a local minimum, since f″ = 6 > 0).
/EX

### Choosing a rule
| What you see | Rule |
| A sum or a constant multiple | Differentiate term by term |
| One function times another | Product rule |
| One function divided by another | Quotient rule (or write it as a product with a negative power) |
| A function inside a function | Chain rule |
| y mixed up with x in an equation | Implicit differentiation |
| A variable in the exponent, or many factors | Logarithmic differentiation |

Most real expressions need two or three of these in layers. Work from the outside in.

TRAP: In logarithmic differentiation, take ln only where the expression is positive (or use the absolute value). And remember the last step: multiply by the original y, because the left side was y′ ÷ y, not y′.

### The two-stage strategy
Many problems have two stages, and each stage has its own law.

| Stage | Law to use | Why |
| A collision or explosion (very short) | Conserve **momentum** | External forces are negligible over the short time |
| What happens after (swinging, sliding, compressing) | Conserve or account for **energy** | Forces like gravity and friction act over distance |

Never apply energy conservation across a sticking collision, because kinetic energy is lost.

EX: A ballistic pendulum, symbolically
1. Bullet m, speed v, embeds in a block M. Stage 1 (momentum): m v = (m + M) V.
2. Stage 2 (energy): ½ (m + M) V² = (m + M) g h.
3. So V = m v ÷ (m + M) and h = V² ÷ (2g). The fraction of energy lost in stage 1 is M ÷ (m + M), nearly all of it for a heavy block.
/EX

EX: Maximum compression of a spring between colliding blocks
1. A 2 kg block moving at 3 m/s hits a 3 kg block at rest. A spring (k = 300 N/m) is attached to the front of the second block. What is the maximum compression?
2. The spring is compressed most when both blocks move at the same velocity: 6 = 5 V, so V = 1.2 m/s.
3. Energy: ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J before. After: ½ × 5 × 1.44 = 3.6 J of kinetic energy. The spring holds 9 − 3.6 = 5.4 J.
4. ½ × 300 × x² = 5.4, so x² = 0.036 and **x = 0.19 m**.
/EX

EX: A crash investigation
1. A 1500 kg car hits a stationary 1000 kg car and they lock together and skid 12 m on a road with μ = 0.8 until they stop.
2. Stage 2 (energy): ½ V² = μ g d, so V = √(2 × 0.8 × 9.8 × 12) = √188.2 = **13.7 m/s**.
3. Stage 1 (momentum): 1500 u = 2500 × 13.7, so **u = 22.9 m/s** (about 82 km/h). This is how skid marks reveal the speed before the crash.
4. Energy lost in the crash: ½ × 1500 × 22.9² − ½ × 2500 × 13.7² = 393,000 − 235,000 = **158,000 J**.
/EX

EX: An explosion on a slope
1. A 6 kg shell, moving at 10 m/s, splits into 2 kg and 4 kg parts that both move along the original line. The 2 kg part moves at 4 m/s forward. Find the other part's speed.
2. Momentum: 60 = 2 × 4 + 4 w, so w = **13 m/s** forward.
3. Energy gained: ½ × 2 × 16 + ½ × 4 × 169 − ½ × 6 × 100 = 16 + 338 − 300 = **54 J**, supplied by the explosive.
/EX

### The kinetic energy lost in a sticking collision
For a mass m₁ hitting a stationary mass m₂ and sticking, the fraction of the kinetic energy lost is **m₂ ÷ (m₁ + m₂)**. (Check: the car crash from Tuesday had 1500 ÷ 2500 = 60% ✓.) A heavy target absorbs most of the energy: a bullet loses almost all its energy in a big block.

BN: দুই ধাপের সমস্যায় সংঘর্ষের সময় ভরবেগ সংরক্ষণ করো (বাইরের বল অতি সামান্য), তারপর পরের ধাপে (দোলা, পিছলানো, স্প্রিং সংকোচন) শক্তির হিসাব করো। আটকে যাওয়া সংঘর্ষে শক্তি সংরক্ষিত নয়। স্থির লক্ষ্যে ধাক্কা দিলে হারানো গতিশক্তির ভগ্নাংশ = m₂/(m₁ + m₂)।

TRAP: The most common mistake is conserving kinetic energy through a collision that sticks or deforms. Another is to use the skid distance with the **initial** speed of the first car: the skid happens after the collision, at the common speed.
