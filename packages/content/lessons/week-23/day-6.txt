@section math | 2 · Part A: mathematics (28 marks, about 80 minutes) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 11
P: **Q1 (2 marks).** Write 1 + i√3 in polar form and find (1 + i√3)⁶. => r = 2, θ = 60°: **2 e^(iπ/3)** (1 mark). The sixth power is 2⁶ e^(i2π) = **64** (1 mark).
P: **Q2 (3 marks).** Find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of B = [[1, 2], [2, 1]], and the top-left entry of B⁴. => det (B − λ I) = (1 − λ)² − 4 = 0, so **λ = 3 and λ = −1** (1 mark). Eigenvectors **(1, 1)** and **(1, −1)** (1 mark). B⁴ has top-left entry (3⁴ + (−1)⁴) ÷ 2 = **41** (1 mark).
P: **Q3 (3 marks).** Solve 2x + y − z = 1, x − y + 2z = 5 and 3x + 2y + z = 10. => From the first, z = 2x + y − 1. In the second: 5x + y = 7. In the third: 5x + 3y = 11 (1 mark). Subtract: 2y = 4, **y = 2**, then **x = 1** (1 mark), and **z = 3** (1 mark). Check in the third: 3 + 4 + 3 = 10 ✓.
P: **Q4 (3 marks).** f (x, y) = x e^y. Find ∇f at (2, 0), the rate of change in the direction (3, 4) ÷ 5, and the greatest possible rate. => ∇f = (e^y, x e^y) = **(1, 2)** at (2, 0) (1 mark). Directional derivative: (3 + 8) ÷ 5 = **2.2** (1 mark). Greatest rate |∇f| = **√5 ≈ 2.24** (1 mark).
P: **Q5 (3 marks).** Find the maximum of x y on the circle x² + y² = 8. => ∇(xy) = (y, x) = λ (2x, 2y) (1 mark). Then y = 2λx and x = 2λy give x² = y² (1 mark). On the circle x = y = ±2, so the **maximum is 4** (at (2, 2) and (−2, −2)); the minimum is −4 (1 mark).
P: **Q6 (3 marks).** Evaluate ∭ z dV over the upper half of the ball x² + y² + z² ≤ 4 (z ≥ 0). => Spherical: z = r cos φ, dV = r² sin φ dr dφ dθ (1 mark). ∫₀² r³ dr = 4, ∫₀^{π/2} cos φ sin φ dφ = 1/2, ∫₀^{2π} dθ = 2π (1 mark). The result is 4 × 1/2 × 2π = **4π** (1 mark).
P: **Q7 (3 marks).** A tank of 100 L holds pure water. Brine of 0.5 kg/L flows in at 3 L/min and the well-mixed liquid flows out at 3 L/min. Write the equation for the salt S (kg), solve it, and give the final amount and the time constant. => dS/dt = 1.5 − 0.03 S (1 mark). S = 50 (1 − e^(−0.03 t)) (1 mark). It tends to **50 kg** (0.5 kg/L × 100 L) with a time constant of **1 ÷ 0.03 = 33.3 min** (1 mark).
P: **Q8 (3 marks).** Solve y″ + y = cos t. Say what happens to the amplitude for large t and why. => The complementary solution is **A cos t + B sin t** (1 mark). A particular solution is **y = (t ÷ 2) sin t** (check: y″ + y = cos t) (1 mark). The amplitude **grows linearly with t**: the driving frequency equals the natural frequency, which is resonance (1 mark).
P: **Q9 (2 marks).** Find the inverse Laplace transform of Y (s) = (3s + 5) ÷ ((s + 1)(s + 2)). => Partial fractions: A ÷ (s + 1) + B ÷ (s + 2) with A = (−3 + 5) ÷ 1 = 2 and B = (−6 + 5) ÷ (−1) = 1 (1 mark). So **y = 2 e^(−t) + e^(−2t)** (1 mark). Check: y (0) = 3, and Y → 3 ÷ s for large s ✓.
P: **Q10 (2 marks).** f (x) = x on (−π, π) has bₙ = 2 (−1)^(n+1) ÷ n. Use Parseval to find Σ 1 ÷ n². => (1 ÷ π) ∫ x² dx = 2π² ÷ 3 (1 mark). This equals Σ bₙ² = 4 Σ 1 ÷ n², so **Σ 1 ÷ n² = π² ÷ 6** (1 mark).
P: **Q11 (1 mark).** A recording of N = 1000 samples is taken at 8 kHz. Find the bin spacing of its DFT. => f_s ÷ N = 8000 ÷ 1000 = **8 Hz**.

@section physics | 3 · Part B: physics (12 marks, about 40 minutes) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 12 to 15
P: **Q12 (3 marks).** A refrigerator keeps its inside at 255 K in a room at 300 K. Assuming the best possible (Carnot) performance, find the coefficient of performance, the electric power needed to remove 500 W of heat and the heat given to the room. => COP = T_c ÷ (T_h − T_c) = 255 ÷ 45 = **5.67** (1 mark). Power = 500 ÷ 5.67 = **88.2 W** (1 mark). Heat to the room = 500 + 88.2 = **588 W** (1 mark).
P: **Q13 (3 marks).** A 220 μF capacitor is charged from a 12 V supply through 10 kΩ. Find the time constant, the voltage after 3.0 s and the time to reach 9.0 V. => τ = R C = **2.2 s** (1 mark). V = 12 (1 − e^(−3 ÷ 2.2)) = **8.93 V** (1 mark). 9 V is 75%: t = τ ln 4 = **3.05 s** (1 mark).
P: **Q14 (3 marks).** A solenoid has 600 turns on a length of 0.30 m and carries 4.0 A. Find B inside, the force on a 5.0 cm straight wire carrying 3.0 A perpendicular to the field inside it, and the energy density of the field. => n = 2000 per m: B = μ₀ n I = **10.05 mT** (1 mark). F = B I L = 0.01005 × 3 × 0.05 = **1.51 mN** (1 mark). u = B² ÷ (2 μ₀) = **40.2 J/m³** (1 mark).
P: **Q15 (3 marks).** A rod of length 0.80 m slides at 2.5 m/s along rails in a field of 0.35 T. The circuit has a resistance of 1.4 Ω. Find the EMF, the current and the force needed to keep the speed constant, and the power supplied. => ε = B ℓ v = 0.35 × 0.8 × 2.5 = **0.70 V** and I = 0.70 ÷ 1.4 = **0.50 A** (1 mark). F = B I ℓ = 0.35 × 0.5 × 0.8 = **0.14 N** (1 mark). P = F v = **0.35 W** = I² R ✓ (1 mark).

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | The eigenvalue of a vibrating system
P: Two equal masses m joined to walls and to each other by three identical springs k obey m x₁″ = −2k x₁ + k x₂ and m x₂″ = k x₁ − 2k x₂. Find the two normal-mode frequencies. => Try x = v cos ωt: the matrix [[2, −1], [−1, 2]] k ÷ m has eigenvalues 1 and 3 times k ÷ m (eigenvectors (1, 1) and (1, −1)). So **ω₁ = √(k ÷ m)** (both move together) and **ω₂ = √(3k ÷ m)** (they move in opposition), a ratio of 1.73.

LVL: Challenge | A Fourier integral from Maxwell's world
P: A rectangular current pulse of height I₀ and duration τ flows in an antenna. Show that the first zero of the radiated spectrum is at f = 1 ÷ τ, and find the bandwidth needed for 1 ns pulses (ultra-wideband radar). => The spectrum is I₀ τ sinc (π f τ), whose first zero is at **f = 1 ÷ τ**. For τ = 1 ns the zero is at **1 GHz**, so the system needs a bandwidth of about 1 GHz: duration and bandwidth are inversely related.

### What comes next: Phase 4
Phase 4 (Weeks 24 to 31) turns to chance and to the modern physics of light, relativity and the atom. It begins next week with **counting and probability** and with **electromagnetic waves, light and polarisation**: the story is why sunglasses work. Over the phase you will meet random variables and statistics, Bayes and Markov chains, logic and proof, graphs and number theory, optimisation, optics, interference, special relativity, quantum mechanics and semiconductors. You already have the tools: Maxwell's waves from Week 22 are the start of optics, and Fourier is the heart of quantum mechanics.
