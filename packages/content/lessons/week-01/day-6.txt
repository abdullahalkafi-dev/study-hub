@section math | 2 · Part A: Algebra (13 marks, about 55 min) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 12
P: (1 mark) Simplify (2a²b)³ ÷ (4ab²). => 8a⁶b³ ÷ 4ab² = **2a⁵b**.
P: (1 mark) Evaluate 16^(3/4) − 2⁻³. => 8 − 1/8 = **63/8** (7⅞).
P: (1 mark) Simplify √75 − √12. => 5√3 − 2√3 = **3√3**.
P: (1 mark) Expand and simplify (x + 3)² − (x − 3)². => (x² + 6x + 9) − (x² − 6x + 9) = **12x**.
P: (1 mark) Factor 6x² + 11x − 10. => 6 × (−10) = −60, so use 15 and −4: 6x² + 15x − 4x − 10 = 3x(2x + 5) − 2(2x + 5) = **(2x + 5)(3x − 2)**.
P: (1 mark) Solve (2x − 1)/3 − (x + 2)/4 = 1. => Multiply by 12: 4(2x − 1) − 3(x + 2) = 12, so 8x − 4 − 3x − 6 = 12, so 5x = 22 and **x = 22/5 = 4.4**.
P: (1 mark) Make b the subject of A = ½(a + b)h. => 2A ÷ h = a + b, so **b = 2A/h − a**.
P: (1 mark) Solve 3x + 2y = 12 and 5x − 2y = 4. => Add: 8x = 16, so x = 2. Then 6 + 2y = 12 and y = 3. **(2, 3)**.
P: (1 mark) Solve −2(x − 1) ≥ 6. => x − 1 ≤ −3 (flip), so **x ≤ −2**.
P: (1 mark) Solve |3x + 1| = 8. => 3x + 1 = 8 gives x = 7/3. 3x + 1 = −8 gives x = −3. **x = 7/3 or x = −3**.
P: (2 marks) Solve x² − x − 12 = 0, and then solve x² − x − 12 < 0. => (x − 4)(x + 3) = 0 gives **x = 4 or x = −3**. The curve is below zero between the roots: **−3 < x < 4**.
P: (1 mark) Simplify (x² − x − 6) ÷ (x² − 9). => (x − 3)(x + 2) ÷ [(x − 3)(x + 3)] = **(x + 2) ÷ (x + 3)** for x ≠ 3, −3.

@section physics | 3 · Part B: Motion (7 marks, about 35 min) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 13 to 18
P: (1 mark) Convert 108 km/h to m/s. => 108 ÷ 3.6 = **30 m/s**.
P: (2 marks) A v–t graph goes in a straight line from (0 s, 0 m/s) to (8 s, 24 m/s), stays at 24 m/s until 12 s, then falls in a straight line to 0 m/s at 16 s. Find the acceleration in the first part and the total distance. => a = 24 ÷ 8 = **3 m/s²**. Distance = areas: ½ × 8 × 24 = 96, plus 4 × 24 = 96, plus ½ × 4 × 24 = 48. Total **240 m**.
P: (1 mark) A cyclist with u = 3 m/s accelerates at 0.5 m/s² for 10 s. Find v and s. => v = 3 + 5 = **8 m/s**. s = 30 + ½ × 0.5 × 100 = **55 m**.
P: (1 mark) A car brakes uniformly from 30 m/s and stops after 75 m. Find the deceleration and the time to stop. => 0 = 900 + 2a(75), so **a = −6 m/s²**. t = 30 ÷ 6 = **5 s**.
P: (1 mark) A car needs 20 m to stop from 10 m/s. With the same brakes, what distance from 25 m/s? => Braking distance ∝ u²: 20 × (25 ÷ 10)² = 20 × 6.25 = **125 m**.
P: (1 mark) Car B is 75 m ahead of car A and moves at a constant 10 m/s. At the same moment car A starts from rest with a = 2 m/s². When does A catch B? => Positions: x_A = t², x_B = 75 + 10t. Set equal: t² − 10t − 75 = 0, so (t − 15)(t + 5) = 0 and **t = 15 s** (reject −5). Check: 225 = 75 + 150 ✓.

### Challenge of the week
LVL: Challenge | The sprinter
P: A 100 m sprinter accelerates uniformly for the first 4 s, then runs at constant speed for the rest of the race. Her total time is 10 s. Find her acceleration and her top speed. => Let the acceleration be a. Top speed v = 4a. Distance in the first phase: ½ × a × 16 = 8a. Distance in the last 6 s: 4a × 6 = 24a. Total: 32a = 100, so **a = 3.125 m/s²** and **top speed = 12.5 m/s** (45 km/h). Sprinters really do accelerate for a few seconds, then hold speed, which is why the first 30 m matters so much.
