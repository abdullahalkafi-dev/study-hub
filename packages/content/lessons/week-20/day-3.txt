# Transfer functions, impulses and the step response. Then: LC and RLC oscillations
lead: A system is a machine: you push, it responds. The Laplace transform gives the machine a single formula, the transfer function, whose poles tell you everything about its behaviour before you solve anything. Two standard pushes, the sudden switch-on (step) and the sudden hit (impulse), give you the system's personality. In physics, you connect an inductor to a capacitor and get an electrical oscillator, the circuit behind every clock, radio and wireless charger, with exactly the same mathematics as the spring from Week 19.

BN: একটা সিস্টেম একটা যন্ত্র: ধাক্কা দিলে সাড়া দেয়। ল্যাপলাস রূপান্তর তাকে একটাই সূত্র দেয়: ট্রান্সফার ফাংশন, যার মেরুগুলো সমাধানের আগেই আচরণ বলে দেয়। দুটো আদর্শ ধাক্কা: হঠাৎ চালু (ধাপ) আর হঠাৎ আঘাত (ইমপালস)। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে ইনডাক্টর + ক্যাপাসিটর = বৈদ্যুতিক দোলক, ঘড়ি, রেডিও আর ওয়্যারলেস চার্জারের ভিত্তি; গণিত ঠিক Week 19 এর স্প্রিং।

@section math | 2 · Math: the transfer function | 55 min

### One formula for the machine
For m y″ + b y′ + k y = f (t) with zero starting values, the transform gives (m s² + b s + k) Y = F, so

EQ: Y (s) = H (s) F (s), with H (s) = 1 ÷ (m s² + b s + k)

**H (s)** is the **transfer function**: output over input. The roots of the denominator are the **poles**. Everything about stability and oscillation sits in the poles. The input f only chooses which of the system's behaviours you see.

- **Impulse response.** A sudden hit δ (t) has L{δ} = 1, so Y = H and the response is **h (t) = L⁻¹{H}**.
- **Step response.** Switching on a constant: F = 1 ÷ s, so Y = H ÷ s.
- **Any input.** The answer is the **convolution** y = h ∗ f = ∫₀ᵗ h (τ) f (t − τ) dτ, and L{h ∗ f} = H F. (The input is a chain of tiny hits, and each one rings the system.)
- **Final value:** if the system is stable, y (∞) = lim as s → 0 of s Y (s). **Initial value:** y (0⁺) = lim as s → ∞ of s Y (s).

BN: Y(s) = H(s)·F(s), H = 1/(ms² + bs + k) (ট্রান্সফার ফাংশন); হরের মূল = মেরু। ইমপালসে L{δ} = 1, তাই Y = H, সাড়া h(t) = L⁻¹{H}। ধাপে Y = H/s। যেকোনো ইনপুটে কনভোলিউশন y = h∗f, আর L{h∗f} = HF। চূড়ান্ত মান: s→0 এ sY।

EX: Impulse and step of one system
Take y″ + 2y′ + 5y with zero start: H = 1 ÷ (s² + 2s + 5) = 1 ÷ ((s + 1)² + 4), poles at −1 ± 2i.
1. **Impulse response:** h (t) = **½ e^(−t) sin 2t**. A damped sine, starting at 0 and ringing.
2. **Step response (input 5 u (t), the target is y = 1):** Y = 5 ÷ (s (s² + 2s + 5)) = 1 ÷ s − (s + 2) ÷ (s² + 2s + 5), which gives **y = 1 − e^(−t) (cos 2t + ½ sin 2t)**.
3. **Overshoot:** y′ = (5 ÷ 2) e^(−t) sin 2t = 0 at 2t = π, so the first peak is at **t = π ÷ 2 = 1.571** and y = 1 − e^(−π/2) cos π = **1.208**: an overshoot of **20.8%**. The formula e^(−π ζ ÷ √(1 − ζ²)) with ζ = 2 ÷ (2√5) = 0.447 gives e^(−π/2) = 0.208 ✓.
4. **Final value check:** s Y at s → 0 is 5 ÷ 5 = 1 ✓.
5. **Convolution check:** with h = e^(−t) and a unit step input, y = ∫₀ᵗ e^(−τ) dτ = **1 − e^(−t)**, which is the same as the step response of y′ + y = 1.
6. **A stability test:** y″ + 4y′ + 13y: poles at −2 ± 3i, so h (t) = ⅓ e^(−2t) sin 3t: decays, rings 3 radians per second.

TRAP: The transfer function assumes **zero starting values**. If there are initial conditions, they add terms to the numerator. Poles in the right half-plane (positive real part) mean growth: the final value theorem only works when the system is stable. And a step response is not the impulse response: the step response is the integral of the impulse response.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: LC and RLC oscillations | 35 min

### An electrical spring
Charge a capacitor C to Q₀ and connect it across an inductor L. The loop rule gives L dI/dt + q ÷ C = 0 with I = dq/dt, which is **L q″ + q ÷ C = 0**, the mass-spring equation with ω₀² = 1 ÷ (L C):

EQ: q = Q₀ cos ω₀ t, ω₀ = 1 ÷ √(L C), f₀ = 1 ÷ (2π √(L C))

| Mechanics | Electric circuit |
|---|---|
| mass m | inductance L |
| spring constant k | 1 ÷ C |
| position x | charge q |
| velocity v | current I |
| friction b | resistance R |
| ½ m v² and ½ k x² | ½ L I² and q² ÷ 2C |

The energy sloshes between the capacitor (electric field) and the inductor (magnetic field). Add a resistor and you get the damped version L q″ + R q′ + q ÷ C = 0, with **decay rate α = R ÷ (2L)**, damped frequency ω_d = √(ω₀² − α²), **Q = ω₀ L ÷ R** and critical damping at **R = 2√(L ÷ C)**.

BN: চার্জিত ক্যাপাসিটরকে ইনডাক্টরে যুক্ত করলে Lq″ + q/C = 0: স্প্রিংয়ের সমীকরণ, ω₀ = 1/√(LC)। শক্তি ক্যাপাসিটর (বৈদ্যুতিক ক্ষেত্র) আর ইনডাক্টর (চৌম্বক ক্ষেত্র) এর মধ্যে ঘুরে। রোধ যোগ করলে অবমন্দন: α = R/2L, Q = ω₀L/R, সংকট R = 2√(L/C)। মিল: m↔L, k↔1/C, x↔q, v↔I, b↔R।

EX: A radio-sized LC circuit
L = 10 mH, C = 100 nF, charged to 5 V.
1. **Frequency:** ω₀ = 1 ÷ √(10⁻² × 10⁻⁷) = **31,623 rad/s**, f₀ = **5.03 kHz**, period **199 μs**.
2. **Charge and current:** Q₀ = C V₀ = 0.5 μC, and the peak current is I_max = Q₀ ω₀ = **15.8 mA**.
3. **Energy:** ½ C V₀² = **1.25 μJ**, and when all of it is in the coil, ½ L I_max² = 0.5 × 0.01 × (0.0158)² = **1.25 μJ** ✓.
4. **Add R = 20 Ω:** α = 20 ÷ 0.02 = **1000 s⁻¹**, ω_d = **31,607 rad/s**, Q = 31623 × 0.01 ÷ 20 = **15.8**. The amplitude halves in ln 2 ÷ 1000 = **0.69 ms**, which is 3.5 cycles.
5. **Critical resistance:** R = 2√(0.01 ÷ 10⁻⁷) = 2 × 316.2 = **632 Ω**. Above it the circuit does not oscillate.
6. **Where you meet it:** a wireless phone charger and a radio both use L and C tuned to a chosen f₀, and a quartz clock uses a crystal that behaves like a very high Q version.

TRAP: ω₀ = 1 ÷ √(L C): a bigger L or C slows the oscillation. Units are the common slip: mH and nF must be converted to H and F (10 mH is 0.01 H, 100 nF is 10⁻⁷ F). The critical resistance 2√(L ÷ C) does **not** use ω₀. And in the RLC circuit the resistor, not the inductor, is the source of the damping: only R turns energy into heat.

### Recap
- Transfer function H = 1 ÷ (m s² + b s + k): poles decide the behaviour. Impulse response h = L⁻¹{H}; step response from H ÷ s; any input by convolution. Left half-plane poles mean stability. Overshoot e^(−πζ ÷ √(1 − ζ²)).
- LC: ω₀ = 1 ÷ √(LC), energy sloshes. RLC: α = R ÷ 2L, Q = ω₀ L ÷ R, critical R = 2√(L ÷ C). Mapping: L ↔ m, 1 ÷ C ↔ k, R ↔ b, q ↔ x, I ↔ v.
