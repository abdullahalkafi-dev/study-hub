# Frequency response and numerical modelling. Then: series RLC on AC, resonance and tuning a radio
lead: This is the day the week's story comes together. A radio picks one station out of thousands because a circuit made of a coil, a capacitor and a resistor responds sharply to one frequency and ignores the others. In maths, the tool that describes this is the frequency response: put s = iω into the transfer function and read off the gain. You also see numerical methods at work on a real problem, a ball moving through air, which has no formula answer, and discover that the best throwing angle is not 45°.

BN: আজ সপ্তাহের গল্প মেলে। রেডিও হাজারটা স্টেশন থেকে একটাকে বেছে নেয় কারণ কয়েল, ক্যাপাসিটর ও রোধের একটা বর্তনী এক কম্পাঙ্কে তীব্রভাবে সাড়া দেয়, বাকিগুলোতে নয়। গণিতে এর বর্ণনা কম্পাঙ্ক সাড়া: ট্রান্সফার ফাংশনে s = iω বসিয়ে গেইন পড়ো। আর বাতাসে বলের গতি, যার সূত্র নেই, সংখ্যায় সমাধান করে দেখবে সেরা কোণ 45° নয়।

@section math | 2 · Math: frequency response and a numerical experiment | 50 min

### From the transfer function to the frequency response
Push a stable system with a sine of frequency ω: after the start-up has died away, the output is also a sine of the same frequency. Its size and delay are given by **H (iω)**: the **gain** is |H (iω)| and the **phase shift** is the angle of H (iω). Gain is often written in **decibels: 20 log₁₀ |H|**; a drop of 3 dB means the amplitude fell to 1 ÷ √2 = 0.707.

**First example: the RC low-pass filter** (the resistor then the capacitor): H (s) = 1 ÷ (1 + R C s). Put s = iω:

EQ: |H (iω)| = 1 ÷ √(1 + (ω R C)²), phase = −atan (ω R C)

The **cut-off frequency** is where ω R C = 1: **f_c = 1 ÷ (2π R C)**. There the gain is 0.707 (−3 dB) and the phase is −45°. Well below f_c the gain is 1 (the signal passes), well above it the gain falls as 1 ÷ f (−20 dB per decade).

BN: স্থির অবস্থায় সাইনের উত্তর একই কম্পাঙ্কের সাইন; আকার = |H(iω)| (গেইন), দেরি = H(iω) এর কোণ। dB = 20 log₁₀|H|; −3 dB মানে 0.707। RC লো-পাস: |H| = 1/√(1 + (ωRC)²), কাটঅফ f_c = 1/(2πRC), সেখানে গেইন 0.707 ও দশা −45°।

EX: A low-pass filter
R = 1.6 kΩ and C = 0.1 μF.
1. **Cut-off:** f_c = 1 ÷ (2π × 1600 × 10⁻⁷) = **995 Hz**.
2. **Gain at 100 Hz:** 1 ÷ √(1 + 0.1²) = **0.995** (−0.04 dB): passes almost unchanged.
3. **At 1 kHz:** **0.705** (−3.03 dB): half the power gone. The phase is about −45°.
4. **At 10 kHz:** 1 ÷ √(1 + 10.05²) = **0.099** (−20.1 dB): a tenfold drop for a tenfold frequency, which is the −20 dB per decade rule.
5. **Why it works:** the capacitor lets fast changes through to ground (its reactance is small at high f) and blocks slow ones, so the output across it keeps the slow part. Audio equipment uses this to remove hiss, and your phone's touchscreen uses it to remove noise.
/EX

### A numerical experiment: a baseball with air drag
A ball of mass 0.145 kg, radius 3.66 cm, leaves the bat at 40 m/s at 45°. Air drag is F = k v² against the velocity with k = ½ ρ C_d A = 0.5 × 1.2 × 0.4 × 4.2 × 10⁻³ = **0.00101 kg/m**. The equations x′ = v_x, y′ = v_y, v_x′ = −(k ÷ m) |v| v_x and v_y′ = −g − (k ÷ m) |v| v_y have no formula solution, so you step them with RK4 (four slopes for all four unknowns each step).

EX: What the numbers say
1. **Without air** the range is v² sin 2θ ÷ g = **163.1 m** and the flight time is 5.77 s.
2. **With drag**, RK4 with h = 0.001 s gives a range of only **91.42 m**, a time of **4.83 s** and a maximum height of **28.87 m**. Air takes away 44% of the range.
3. **Euler check:** with h = 0.1 s the Euler range is 92.26 m (error 0.84 m), with h = 0.01 it is 91.51 m and with h = 0.001 it is 91.43 m: the error shrinks in proportion to h. The RK4 value with h = 0.1 is already 91.415 m, within 0.007 m of the best answer.
4. **The best angle is not 45°.** Ranges with RK4: 35° gives **90.85 m**, 40° gives **92.24 m**, 45° gives **91.42 m**, 50° gives **88.48 m**. The best is near **40°** because the fast rising phase loses a lot to drag, so a flatter throw wins.
5. **Terminal velocity** for a falling object under the same law: m g = k v², so v_t = √(m g ÷ k). For a skydiver (m = 80 kg, k = 0.25) v_t = **56 m/s** (202 km/h) and the speed v (t) = v_t tanh (g t ÷ v_t), which is 39.5 m/s after 5 s and 52.7 m/s after 10 s.
/EX

TRAP: The gain is |H (iω)|, not H (s) at a real s. A −3 dB point means 0.707 of the **amplitude**, which is half the **power**. A numerical method does not give "the" answer: always repeat with a smaller step and see that the result stops changing (here h = 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001 agree to 1%). And a model with drag has a different best angle: do not use v² sin 2θ ÷ g when air matters.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: series RLC on AC, resonance and tuning a radio | 40 min

### The series RLC circuit
A source of RMS voltage V and frequency f drives R, L and C in series. All three carry the same current, and the impedance is

EQ: Z = √(R² + (X_L − X_C)²), I = V ÷ Z, tan φ = (X_L − X_C) ÷ R

At the **resonant frequency f₀ = 1 ÷ (2π √(LC))**, X_L = X_C. The two reactances cancel, Z = R is the smallest possible value, the current is the largest, **I = V ÷ R**, and the phase is zero. Away from f₀ the current drops. The sharpness is measured by

**Q = ω₀ L ÷ R = 1 ÷ (ω₀ R C)**, and the bandwidth (the width between the half-power points) is **Δf = f₀ ÷ Q**.

The relative current at the frequency f is I ÷ I_max = 1 ÷ √(1 + Q² (f ÷ f₀ − f₀ ÷ f)²). At resonance the voltages across L and C are each **Q times the source voltage**, and they cancel each other.

BN: শ্রেণি RLC: Z = √(R² + (X_L − X_C)²), I = V/Z। অনুনাদে f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)), X_L = X_C, Z = R (সর্বনিম্ন), I = V/R (সর্বোচ্চ), দশা 0। Q = ω₀L/R, ব্যান্ডউইডথ Δf = f₀/Q। অনুনাদে L ও C এ ভোল্টেজ = Q × উৎস, কিন্তু একে অপরকে কাটে।

EX: A circuit tuned near the mains
R = 10 Ω, L = 0.1 H, C = 100 μF on 230 V.
1. **Resonance:** ω₀ = 1 ÷ √(0.1 × 10⁻⁴) = **316.2 rad/s**, f₀ = **50.3 Hz**. Q = 316.2 × 0.1 ÷ 10 = **3.16** and Δf = 50.3 ÷ 3.16 = **15.9 Hz**.
2. **At resonance:** I = 230 ÷ 10 = **23 A**. The voltage across the coil (and also the capacitor) is Q × 230 = **727 V**: bigger than the supply! This is real, and it is why high-voltage lab equipment is dangerous.
3. **At exactly 50 Hz:** X_L = 31.4 Ω, X_C = 31.8 Ω, Z = √(10² + 0.4²) = **10.01 Ω**, I = 22.98 A. The circuit is almost at resonance.
4. **At 100 Hz:** X_L = 62.8, X_C = 15.9, Z = √(100 + 46.9²) = 47.9 Ω, I = **4.8 A**: far off resonance the current drops to a fifth.
/EX

EX: Tuning an AM radio
A coil of L = 25 μH and a variable capacitor.
1. **To tune to 1 MHz:** C = 1 ÷ (ω₀² L) = **1.013 nF** (1013 pF).
2. **Selectivity:** a neighbouring station is 10 kHz away (1.01 MHz). The relative current there is 1 ÷ √(1 + Q² (1.01 − 1 ÷ 1.01)²), so for **Q = 100 the neighbour gets through at 0.449** (45% of the wanted amplitude) and for **Q = 300 at 0.165** (17%).
3. **The trade-off:** a higher Q rejects the neighbour better but its bandwidth Δf = f₀ ÷ Q (3.3 kHz at Q = 300) is narrower than the music (up to about 5 kHz), so the treble is lost. A radio designer picks Q to balance them.
4. **Typical R:** Q = 100 needs R = ω₀ L ÷ Q = 157 ÷ 100 = **1.57 Ω**: this resistance is only the coil's wire and losses.
/EX

### Real power and the power factor
In an AC circuit with phase angle φ between voltage and current: **P = V I cos φ**, where **cos φ is the power factor**. The apparent power is S = V I (volt-amperes). Only P (watts) does work; the rest sloshes back and forth.

EX: A motor
A motor behaves like R = 30 Ω in series with X_L = 40 Ω on 230 V, 50 Hz.
1. **Z = 50 Ω, I = 4.6 A, φ = 53.1°, cos φ = 0.6.** S = 1058 VA and **P = 230 × 4.6 × 0.6 = 635 W**.
2. **The wires carry 4.6 A** although only 2.76 A (I cos φ) does useful work.
3. **Correction:** a capacitor in parallel supplies the reactive current I sin φ = 3.68 A. Its size is C = 3.68 ÷ (2π × 50 × 230) = **51 μF**. Now the line current is only **2.76 A**, the real power is unchanged (635 W) and the heat loss in the supply wires falls by (4.6 ÷ 2.76)² = **2.8 times**. Factories pay for a good power factor, and this is why.
/EX

TRAP: At resonance the **source** current is largest, but the voltages across L and C can be far larger than the supply. The bandwidth is Δf = f₀ ÷ Q: high Q means narrow and selective. A power factor of 0.6 does not mean 40% waste of energy, it means the current is bigger than it needs to be; the real power is P = V I cos φ. And correct a power factor with a **parallel** capacitor: a series one would change the circuit.
