### Networks: counting walks
A network with n nodes has an **adjacency matrix** A with a_ij = 1 if nodes i and j are joined, otherwise 0. The big fact:

EQ: (Aᵏ)_ij = the number of walks of length k from node i to node j

EX: A small graph
1. Nodes 1 to 4. Edges: 1–2, 2–3, 1–3, 3–4 (a triangle with a tail). Rows of A: (0, 1, 1, 0), (1, 0, 1, 0), (1, 1, 0, 1), (0, 0, 1, 0).
2. A² row i is the sum of the rows of A for the neighbours of i. Row 1: row 2 + row 3 = (1, 0, 1, 0) + (1, 1, 0, 1) = (2, 1, 1, 1). Row 2: row 1 + row 3 = (1, 2, 1, 1). Row 3: rows 1 + 2 + 4 = (1, 1, 3, 0). Row 4: row 3 = (1, 1, 0, 1).
3. So A² = [[2, 1, 1, 1], [1, 2, 1, 1], [1, 1, 3, 0], [1, 1, 0, 1]]. The diagonal gives the **degrees** (2, 2, 3, 1), and the trace 8 equals 2 × the 4 edges.
4. (A²)₁₄ = 1: the only 2-step walk from 1 to 4 is 1 → 3 → 4 ✓.
5. (A³)₁₃ = (row 1 of A²) · (column 3 of A) = (2, 1, 1, 1) · (1, 1, 0, 1) = 4. The four walks are 1-2-1-3, 1-3-1-3, 1-3-2-3 and 1-3-4-3 ✓.
6. The trace of A³ is 6 = 6 × (number of triangles), and there is 1 triangle.
/EX

BN: গ্রাফের adjacency matrix A: নোড i ও j যুক্ত হলে a_ij = 1। Aᵏ এর (i, j) ঘর হলো i থেকে j তে k ধাপের হাঁটার সংখ্যা। A² এর কর্ণ = নোডের ডিগ্রি; tr(A³) = 6 × ত্রিভুজের সংখ্যা। সামাজিক নেটওয়ার্ক, ওয়েব লিংক ও রাস্তার জালে এটা ব্যবহার হয়।

### An economy: the input-output model (Leontief)
An economy has two sectors, agriculture (1) and manufacturing (2). To produce one unit of sector j's output, the economy uses c_ij units of sector i. Let x be the output vector and d the outside demand. Then

EQ: x = C x + d     so     (I − C) x = d     and     x = (I − C)⁻¹ d

EX: Two sectors
1. C = [[0.2, 0.3], [0.4, 0.1]] and d = (50, 30).
2. I − C = [[0.8, −0.3], [−0.4, 0.9]], det = 0.72 − 0.12 = 0.60. The inverse is (1 ÷ 0.6) [[0.9, 0.3], [0.4, 0.8]] = [[1.5, 0.5], [0.667, 1.333]].
3. x = (1.5 × 50 + 0.5 × 30, 0.667 × 50 + 1.333 × 30) = (75 + 15, 33.33 + 40) = **(90, 73.33)**.
4. Check: C x = (0.2 × 90 + 0.3 × 73.33, 0.4 × 90 + 0.1 × 73.33) = (40, 43.33). Plus d = (90, 73.33) ✓.
5. To deliver 50 and 30 units to consumers, the economy must produce 90 and 73.3: the rest is eaten by the production process itself.
/EX

### Fitting a line to data: least squares
Three measurements at x = 0, 1, 2 give y₀, y₁, y₂. No straight line passes through all three in general, so we pick the line y = a + b x that minimises the sum of squared residuals. In matrix form, with X = [[1, 0], [1, 1], [1, 2]] and β = (a, b), the best fit solves the **normal equations**:

EQ: (Xᵀ X) β = Xᵀ y     so     β = (Xᵀ X)⁻¹ Xᵀ y

EX: Fitting
1. Points (0, 1), (1, 3), (2, 4). Xᵀ X = [[3, 3], [3, 5]] and Xᵀ y = (8, 11) (that is Σ y = 8 and Σ x y = 3 + 8 = 11).
2. det = 15 − 9 = 6, and (Xᵀ X)⁻¹ = (1 ÷ 6) [[5, −3], [−3, 3]].
3. β = (1 ÷ 6) (5 × 8 − 3 × 11, −3 × 8 + 3 × 11) = (7/6, 9/6) = **(1.167, 1.5)**: the line is y = 1.167 + 1.5 x.
4. Residuals: at x = 0 the line gives 1.167 (residual −0.167), at x = 1 it gives 2.667 (+0.333), at x = 2 it gives 4.167 (−0.167). The residuals sum to **0**, as always for this method.
5. For x = 0, 1, 2 in general: **b = (Σ x y − Σ y) ÷ 2** and **a = (5 Σ y − 3 Σ x y) ÷ 6**.
/EX

TRAP: In (Aᵏ)_ij the order of the indices matters for directed graphs: row i is the **start**. In the Leontief model, do not forget that the production x is larger than the demand d. And least squares minimises the **squares** of the vertical errors, so one wild point can pull the whole line.

### Convection
In **convection**, heat is carried by the bulk motion of a fluid. Hot fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises, and cooler fluid sinks. That is **natural** convection (a radiator warming a room, sea breezes, ocean currents, boiling water). **Forced** convection uses a fan or pump. The heat flow between a surface and a fluid is approximately

EQ: P = h A (T_surface − T_fluid)

where the **convection coefficient** h depends on the flow: about 5 to 25 W/(m²·K) for still air near a wall, 10 to 200 for forced air, and 500 to 10,000 for flowing water.

EX: Convection
1. **Bare skin:** A = 1.8 m², T_skin = 33 °C in still air at 20 °C with h = 8: P = 8 × 1.8 × 13 = **187 W** (clothing greatly reduces this).
2. **Wind chill:** in a strong wind h could be 40: P = 40 × 1.8 × 13 = **936 W**, five times more. The air temperature is the same, but you cool faster. That is "wind chill".
3. **A radiator:** a panel of area 2 m² at 60 °C in 20 °C air with h = 10 delivers 10 × 2 × 40 = **800 W** by convection (and some more by radiation). It is placed under a window so that the rising warm air circulates.
/EX

### Radiation
Every object emits electromagnetic radiation, with power depending steeply on its absolute temperature (**Stefan–Boltzmann law**):

EQ: P = ε σ A T⁴     σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴)

ε (between 0 and 1) is the **emissivity**: a perfect "black body" has ε = 1. An object also absorbs radiation from its surroundings at T_env, so the **net** power is P_net = ε σ A (T⁴ − T_env⁴). The wavelength of the peak emission follows **Wien's law**:

EQ: λ_max T = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m·K

BN: পরিচলনে তরলের চলাচলে তাপ যায়: P = hA(T_s − T_f)। বিকিরণে P = εσAT⁴, যেখানে σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸। তাপমাত্রা দ্বিগুণ হলে বিকিরণ ১৬ গুণ। ভিন সূত্র: λ_max T = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m·K, তাই গরম বস্তু ছোট তরঙ্গদৈর্ঘ্যে আলো দেয়। নিট বিকিরণ = εσA(T⁴ − T_env⁴), এবং তাপমাত্রা অবশ্যই কেলভিনে।

EX: Radiation examples
1. **The Sun:** T = 5800 K, R = 6.96 × 10⁸ m, so A = 4π R² = 6.09 × 10¹⁸ m². P = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 6.09 × 10¹⁸ × 5800⁴ = 3.45 × 10¹¹ × 1.13 × 10¹⁵ = **3.9 × 10²⁶ W** (the measured value is 3.83 × 10²⁶ W).
2. **A body:** A = 1.8 m², ε = 0.97, skin 306 K, surroundings 293 K: P_net = 0.97 × 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 1.8 × (306⁴ − 293⁴) = 9.90 × 10⁻⁸ × (8.768 × 10⁹ − 7.370 × 10⁹) = **138 W**. So radiation is the main way we lose heat at rest.
3. **Colours:** the Sun peaks at λ = 2.898 × 10⁻³ ÷ 5800 = **500 nm** (green). A person at 310 K peaks at **9.35 μm** (infrared, which is what thermal cameras see). A light-bulb filament at 2800 K peaks at 1035 nm, in the infrared, which is why incandescent bulbs waste most of their energy as heat.
4. **A hot plate:** 0.5 m² black body at 500 K in a 300 K room: P_net = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 0.5 × (500⁴ − 300⁴) = 2.835 × 10⁻⁸ × 5.44 × 10¹⁰ = **1542 W**.
/EX

### The temperature of the Earth
In equilibrium, the power absorbed by the Earth equals the power radiated. Sunlight at the Earth has intensity S = 1361 W/m². The Earth intercepts it over a disc (π R²) but radiates from the whole sphere (4π R²), so the average absorbed intensity is S (1 − a) ÷ 4, where a is the **albedo** (reflected fraction, about 0.30).

EQ: S (1 − a) ÷ 4 = σ T⁴     so     T = (S (1 − a) ÷ (4 σ))^(1/4)

EX: Earth in balance
1. a = 0.30: absorbed = 1361 × 0.70 ÷ 4 = 238.2 W/m². T = (238.2 ÷ 5.67 × 10⁻⁸)^(1/4) = (4.20 × 10⁹)^(1/4) = **254.6 K = −18.6 °C**.
2. The actual mean surface temperature is **288 K (15 °C)**. The 33 K difference is the **greenhouse effect**: gases such as H₂O and CO₂ absorb infrared and send part of it back down.
3. **Ice-albedo feedback:** if ice spreads and the albedo rises to 0.50: absorbed = 1361 × 0.50 ÷ 4 = 170.1 W/m², T = **234.0 K**: 20 K colder.
4. **A one-layer atmosphere** that absorbs all infrared and re-emits half up and half down gives σ T_s⁴ = 2 × 238.2, so T_s = 2^(1/4) × 254.6 = **302.8 K**, about 30 °C. This is too warm, because the real atmosphere absorbs only part of the radiation.
/EX

TRAP: Use **kelvin** for T⁴: 20 °C is 293.15 K, not 20. Radiation depends on **T⁴**, so a 10% rise in kelvin temperature raises the power by 46%. In the Earth's balance, do not forget the factor 4 between the disc and the sphere.
