# Independence, rank and row operations. Then: heat conduction
lead: When are two vectors really giving you two directions, and when is one a waste? Linear independence and rank answer that question, and row operations are the machine that computes it (and finds inverses). In physics, you study the first of three ways heat travels: conduction, where energy passes from molecule to molecule through a material. It explains why a woollen jumper keeps you warm and why a metal spoon in hot soup burns your fingers.

BN: দুটো ভেক্টর সত্যিই দুটো দিক দিচ্ছে নাকি একটা বাড়তি? রৈখিক স্বাধীনতা ও র‍্যাঙ্ক এই প্রশ্নের উত্তর দেয়, আর সারি-প্রক্রিয়া (row operations) হলো তা বের করার এবং বিপরীত ম্যাট্রিক্স খোঁজার যন্ত্র। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে তাপ চলার তিন পথের প্রথমটি পড়বে: পরিবহন (conduction), যেখানে শক্তি অণু থেকে অণুতে যায়। এ কারণেই পশমি সোয়েটার গরম রাখে আর গরম স্যুপে ধাতব চামচ হাত পোড়ায়।

### Linear combinations and span
A **linear combination** of vectors v₁, v₂ is a v₁ + b v₂ for numbers a and b. The set of all of them is their **span**.

Vectors are **linearly independent** if none of them is a linear combination of the others. Equivalently, the only way to get a v₁ + b v₂ = 0 is a = b = 0.

BN: v₁, v₂ এর রৈখিক সমাবেশ a v₁ + b v₂। সব সমাবেশের সেট হলো span। কোনো ভেক্টর অন্যগুলোর সমাবেশ না হলে তারা রৈখিকভাবে স্বাধীন। দুটো স্বাধীন ভেক্টর সমতলের যেকোনো বিন্দুতে পৌঁছাতে পারে, কিন্তু সমান্তরাল দুটো ভেক্টর শুধু একটা রেখায় আটকে থাকে।

EX: Two vectors in the plane
1. v₁ = (1, 2), v₂ = (2, 4). Since v₂ = 2 v₁, they are **dependent**. Their span is just the line through (1, 2). det [[1, 2], [2, 4]] = 0.
2. v₁ = (1, 2), v₂ = (3, 1). det [[1, 3], [2, 1]] = 1 − 6 = −5 ≠ 0, so they are **independent** and span the whole plane.
3. Write (7, 4) = a (1, 2) + b (3, 1): a + 3b = 7 and 2a + b = 4. From the second b = 4 − 2a, so a + 12 − 6a = 7, **a = 1**, **b = 2**. Check: (1 + 6, 2 + 2) = (7, 4) ✓.
/EX

EX: Three vectors in space
1. u = (1, 0, 1), v = (0, 1, 1), w = (1, 1, 2). Since w = u + v, they are **dependent** (the three lie in one plane). The determinant of the matrix with these rows: 1 (2 − 1) − 0 + 1 (0 − 1) = **0** ✓.
2. Replace w by (1, 1, 0): det = 1 (0 − 1) − 0 + 1 (0 − 1) = **−2**. Now they are **independent** and span all of 3D space.
3. **Rule:** n vectors in n dimensions are independent exactly when the determinant is not zero.
/EX

### Row operations and rank
Three **row operations** do not change the solutions of a linear system, nor the rank:
1. Swap two rows. 2. Multiply a row by a non-zero number. 3. Add a multiple of one row to another.

Use them to make an **echelon form** (zeros below each leading entry). The **rank** is the number of non-zero rows left, which equals the maximum number of independent rows (or columns).

EX: Finding the rank
1. Rows (1, 2, 3), (2, 4, 6), (1, 0, 1). R₂ − 2R₁ = (0, 0, 0). R₃ − R₁ = (0, −2, −2). The echelon form has two non-zero rows, so **rank = 2**.
2. Rows (1, 2, 1), (2, 5, 3), (1, 1, 0). R₂ − 2R₁ = (0, 1, 1). R₃ − R₁ = (0, −1, −1). R₃ + R₂ = (0, 0, 0). **Rank = 2**, and the determinant is 1 (0 − 3) − 2 (0 − 3) + 1 (2 − 5) = −3 + 6 − 3 = 0 ✓.
3. Rows (1, 2, 3), (0, 1, 4), (5, 6, 0): the determinant is 1, so **rank = 3** (full).
4. **Rank 1:** the matrix [[1, 2], [2, 4], [3, 6]] is the product of the column (1, 2, 3) and the row (1, 2). Every row is a multiple of (1, 2), so the rank is 1. A rank-1 image of 1000 × 1000 pixels needs only 1000 + 1000 numbers instead of a million, which is the idea behind image compression with low-rank matrices.
/EX

### The inverse by row operations (Gauss–Jordan)
Write the augmented matrix [A | I] and use row operations to turn the left half into I. The right half then becomes A⁻¹.

EX: Inverting [[2, 1], [5, 3]]
1. Start: rows (2, 1 | 1, 0) and (5, 3 | 0, 1).
2. R₁ ÷ 2: (1, 0.5 | 0.5, 0). R₂ − 5R₁: (0, 0.5 | −2.5, 1).
3. R₂ × 2: (0, 1 | −5, 2). R₁ − 0.5 R₂: (1, 0 | 0.5 + 2.5, 0 − 1) = (1, 0 | 3, −1).
4. So A⁻¹ = **[[3, −1], [−5, 2]]**. The formula gives the same: det = 6 − 5 = 1, and (1 ÷ 1) [[3, −1], [−5, 2]] ✓.
5. If a row of zeros appears on the left, the matrix has **no inverse**.
/EX

BN: [A | I] থেকে সারি-প্রক্রিয়ায় বাম পাশকে I বানালে ডান পাশে A⁻¹ পাওয়া যায়। বামে শূন্য সারি এলে বিপরীত নেই। র‍্যাঙ্ক = অশূন্য সারির সংখ্যা। n × n ম্যাট্রিক্স উল্টানো যায় ⇔ র‍্যাঙ্ক n ⇔ det ≠ 0 ⇔ স্তম্ভগুলো স্বাধীন।

TRAP: Row operations change the **determinant** (swapping a row flips its sign, scaling multiplies it), so do not read the determinant off the echelon form unless you track every operation. And independence is about **all** the vectors together: three vectors in the plane are **always** dependent, even if every pair is independent.

### Heat flowing through a material
In **conduction**, faster-vibrating molecules pass their energy to their slower neighbours, and in metals free electrons carry energy quickly too. The rate of heat flow through a slab of area A and thickness L, with temperature difference ΔT across it:

EQ: P = Q ÷ t = k A ΔT ÷ L

k is the **thermal conductivity** (W/(m·K)).

| Material | k (W/(m·K)) |
| Copper | 400 |
| Aluminium | 240 |
| Steel | 50 |
| Ice | 2.2 |
| Glass | 1.0 |
| Concrete | 1.0 |
| Water | 0.6 |
| Brick | 0.7 |
| Wood | 0.12 |
| Fibreglass insulation | 0.04 |
| Polystyrene foam | 0.033 |
| Air (still) | 0.026 |

BN: পরিবহনে দ্রুত কাঁপা অণু তার ধীর প্রতিবেশীকে শক্তি দেয় (ধাতুতে মুক্ত ইলেকট্রনও)। P = kAΔT/L, k হলো তাপ পরিবাহিতা। তামার k = 400, বায়ুর k = 0.026: তাই আটকে থাকা বায়ু ভালো অন্তরক, আর পশম বা ফোম মূলত বায়ু আটকে রাখে।

EX: Heat flow
1. **A single window:** glass 4 mm thick, area 1.5 m², ΔT = 20 K: P = 1.0 × 1.5 × 20 ÷ 0.004 = **7500 W** (an ideal figure: real windows lose less because of air films on both sides).
2. **A copper pan:** bottom 3 mm thick, area 0.03 m², ΔT = 5 K: P = 400 × 0.03 × 5 ÷ 0.003 = **20,000 W**, so the heat spreads evenly.
3. **Why a metal feels cold:** a metal spoon and a wooden spoon at 20 °C. The metal carries heat away from your skin far faster than the wood does, so your skin cools more and the metal feels "cold". The temperature is the same.
/EX

### Layers in series: thermal resistance
For a slab, define the **thermal resistance per unit area** R = L ÷ k (m²·K/W). Layers in series add their resistances, like electrical resistors:

EQ: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + …     P = A ΔT ÷ R_total

EX: Walls and windows
1. **A wall:** brick 0.2 m (k = 0.7) gives R = 0.2857. Add fibreglass 0.1 m (k = 0.04) giving R = 2.5. Total R = **2.786 m²·K/W**.
2. For A = 12 m² and ΔT = 25 K: P = 12 × 25 ÷ 2.786 = **107.7 W**. Without the insulation: 12 × 25 ÷ 0.2857 = **1050 W**. Insulation cuts the loss by a factor of nearly **10**.
3. **Temperature drops:** the heat flux is 25 ÷ 2.786 = 8.97 W/m². Drop across the brick: 8.97 × 0.2857 = **2.6 K**. Drop across the insulation: 8.97 × 2.5 = **22.4 K**. Almost all the temperature drop is in the insulation.
4. **Double glazing:** two 4 mm glass sheets with a 12 mm air gap. R = 0.004 + 0.4615 + 0.004 = 0.4695. Compared with a single sheet (R = 0.004), the loss is cut by a factor of about **117** in this idealised model (real windows improve by a factor of about 2, because of air films and convection in the gap).
/EX

### Ice on a pond: a differential equation
Ice grows downward from the surface. Heat flows through the ice of thickness h (air at −T_a above, water at 0 °C below). The heat freezing a new layer dh of ice (density ρ, latent heat L_f) is carried by conduction: ρ L_f dh/dt = k ΔT ÷ h.

EQ: h dh = (k ΔT ÷ (ρ L_f)) dt     so     h = √(2 k ΔT t ÷ (ρ L_f))

EX: Skating weather
1. Take an air temperature of −10 °C (ΔT = 10 K), k = 2.2, ρ = 917, L_f = 334,000. Then 2 k ΔT ÷ (ρ L_f) = 44 ÷ 3.063 × 10⁸ = 1.437 × 10⁻⁷ m²/s.
2. After 1 day (86,400 s): h² = 0.01241, **h = 11.1 cm**.
3. After 7 days: h² = 0.0869, h = **29.5 cm**. After 10 days: **35.2 cm**. The thickness grows like √t: the thicker the ice, the slower it grows, since it insulates the water below.
/EX

TRAP: In P = k A ΔT ÷ L the thickness L is in **metres**: convert centimetres first. And do not add conductivities in series: add the **resistances** L ÷ k. Finally, remember that "cold" is not a thing that flows: heat flows from hot to cold.

### Recap
- Span, independence (det ≠ 0 for n vectors in n-D), row operations (swap, scale, add), echelon form, rank = number of non-zero rows. Gauss–Jordan: [A | I] → [I | A⁻¹]. Rank 1 matrices = column × row. Three vectors in the plane are always dependent.
- Conduction P = k A ΔT ÷ L. Series R_total = Σ L ÷ k. A good insulator traps still air. Ice thickness h = √(2 k ΔT t ÷ (ρ L_f)).
