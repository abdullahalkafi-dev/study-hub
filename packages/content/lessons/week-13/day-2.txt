# Determinants and inverses. Then: heat, specific heat and calorimetry
lead: The determinant is a single number that tells you whether a matrix can be undone and how much it stretches area or volume. The inverse matrix is how you "divide" by a matrix. In physics, you separate two ideas that are easy to confuse, temperature and heat, and learn to calculate how much energy it takes to warm something up.

BN: নির্ণায়ক (determinant) একটা সংখ্যা যা বলে দেয় ম্যাট্রিক্সটা উল্টানো যাবে কি না এবং ক্ষেত্রফল বা আয়তন কতটা বাড়ায়। বিপরীত ম্যাট্রিক্স হলো ম্যাট্রিক্স দিয়ে "ভাগ" করার উপায়। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে তাপমাত্রা ও তাপ নামের দুটো সহজে গুলিয়ে ফেলা ধারণা আলাদা করবে এবং কোনো কিছু গরম করতে কত শক্তি লাগে তা হিসাব করবে।

@section math | 2 · Math: determinants and inverses | 55 min

### The 2 × 2 determinant and inverse
EQ: det [[a, b], [c, d]] = a d − b c

If the determinant is not zero, the inverse exists:

EQ: A⁻¹ = (1 ÷ det A) × [[d, −b], [−c, a]]     and     A A⁻¹ = A⁻¹ A = I

Swap the diagonal, negate the off-diagonal, divide by the determinant.

BN: 2 × 2 নির্ণায়ক = ad − bc। বিপরীত ম্যাট্রিক্স: কর্ণের সংখ্যা দুটো অদলবদল করো, অপর দুটোর চিহ্ন উল্টাও, তারপর নির্ণায়ক দিয়ে ভাগ করো। নির্ণায়ক 0 হলে বিপরীত ম্যাট্রিক্স নেই (singular)।

EX: An inverse
1. A = [[1, 2], [3, 4]]. det A = 1×4 − 2×3 = **−2**.
2. A⁻¹ = (−1/2) [[4, −2], [−3, 1]] = **[[−2, 1], [1.5, −0.5]]**.
3. Check: row 1 of A times column 1 of A⁻¹ = 1×(−2) + 2×1.5 = 1 ✓. Row 1 times column 2: 1×1 + 2×(−0.5) = 0 ✓. Row 2 times column 1: 3×(−2) + 4×1.5 = 0 ✓. Row 2 times column 2: 3×1 + 4×(−0.5) = 1 ✓.
4. **Solve a system:** x + 2y = 5 and 3x + 4y = 11 means A (x, y) = (5, 11). So (x, y) = A⁻¹ (5, 11) = (−2×5 + 1×11, 1.5×5 − 0.5×11) = **(1, 2)**.
/EX

### What the determinant means
The columns of A tell you where the unit vectors go: (1, 0) → (a, c) and (0, 1) → (b, d). The unit square becomes a parallelogram with area **abs(det A)**. The sign tells you whether the picture was flipped (negative: a reflection).

EX: Geometric meaning
1. [[2, 0], [0, 3]] stretches x by 2 and y by 3: det = 6, area × 6.
2. A rotation [[cos θ, −sin θ], [sin θ, cos θ]] has det = cos²θ + sin²θ = **1**: area is kept.
3. A reflection in the x-axis [[1, 0], [0, −1]] has det **−1**.
4. [[1, 2], [2, 4]] has det 4 − 4 = **0**: the columns (1, 2) and (2, 4) are parallel, so the whole plane is **squashed onto a line**. Information is lost, so no inverse exists. The matrix is **singular**.
5. A triangle with vertices (0, 0), (2, 0), (0, 2) has area 2. Under A = [[2, 1], [0, 3]] (det 6) its area becomes 2 × 6 = **12**. The image vertices are (0, 0), (4, 0), (2, 6): area = ½ × 4 × 6 = 12 ✓.
/EX

### 3 × 3 determinants
Expand along the first row. Each entry multiplies the determinant of the 2 × 2 matrix left when you delete its row and column, with signs + − +:

EQ: det = a (e i − f h) − b (d i − f g) + c (d h − e f)     for the matrix with rows (a, b, c), (d, e, f), (g, h, i)

EX: Computing 3 × 3 determinants
1. Rows (2, 1, 0), (1, 3, 2), (0, 1, 1): det = 2 (3×1 − 2×1) − 1 (1×1 − 2×0) + 0 = 2 × 1 − 1 × 1 = **1**.
2. Rows (1, 2, 3), (0, 1, 4), (5, 6, 0): det = 1 (0 − 24) − 2 (0 − 20) + 3 (0 − 5) = −24 + 40 − 15 = **1**. Check with Sarrus' rule (sum of the three "down" diagonals minus the three "up" diagonals): (1×1×0 + 2×4×5 + 3×0×6) − (3×1×5 + 1×4×6 + 2×0×0) = 40 − 39 = 1 ✓.
3. This second matrix has the inverse with rows (−24, 18, 5), (20, −15, −4), (−5, 4, 1). Check one entry: row 1 times column 1 = 1×(−24) + 2×20 + 3×(−5) = 1 ✓, and row 1 times column 2 = 18 − 30 + 12 = 0 ✓.
4. **Volume:** the columns (1, 0, 0), (1, 2, 0), (1, 1, 3) span a parallelepiped. The matrix is triangular, so the determinant is the product of the diagonal: 1 × 2 × 3 = **6**, which is the volume.
/EX

### Rules to remember
1. det (A B) = det A × det B. 2. det (Aᵀ) = det A. 3. Swapping two rows changes the sign. 4. A row of zeros, or two equal rows, gives det = 0. 5. (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹ A⁻¹ (reversed order).

EX: Checking the product rule
1. A = [[1, 2], [3, 4]] (det −2), B = [[0, 1], [1, 0]] (det −1). AB = [[2, 1], [4, 3]], with det 6 − 4 = **2** = (−2) × (−1) ✓.
2. The inverse of a product: (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹ A⁻¹. Here B⁻¹ = B, so (AB)⁻¹ = B A⁻¹ = [[0, 1], [1, 0]] [[−2, 1], [1.5, −0.5]] = [[1.5, −0.5], [−2, 1]]. Check: AB times this = [[2×1.5 + 1×(−2), 2×(−0.5) + 1×1], [4×1.5 + 3×(−2), 4×(−0.5) + 3×1]] = [[1, 0], [0, 1]] ✓.
/EX

BN: নির্ণায়ক ক্ষেত্রফল (বা আয়তন) এর গুণক। det = 0 মানে সমতল একটা রেখায় চেপ্টে যায়, তথ্য হারায়, তাই বিপরীত নেই। det(AB) = det A · det B। (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹ (উল্টো ক্রম)। ঘূর্ণনের det = 1, প্রতিফলনের det = −1।

TRAP: Do not forget to **divide by the determinant** when inverting. The signs in the 3 × 3 expansion alternate + − +. And (AB)⁻¹ is B⁻¹ A⁻¹, **not** A⁻¹ B⁻¹. A matrix with det = 0 has no inverse, so never write 1 ÷ A.

### Heat versus temperature
**Temperature** describes the state of an object. **Heat** Q is the energy that **flows** between objects because of a temperature difference. It is measured in **joules** (or the older calorie: 1 cal = 4.186 J, and a food "Calorie" is 1 kcal = 4186 J).

The amount of heat to change the temperature of a mass m by ΔT is

EQ: Q = m c ΔT

where c is the **specific heat capacity**, the energy needed to warm 1 kg by 1 K.

| Substance | c (J/(kg·K)) |
| Water | 4186 |
| Ice | 2090 |
| Steam | 2010 |
| Aluminium | 900 |
| Iron, steel | 450 |
| Copper | 385 |
| Lead | 130 |
| Air (constant pressure) | 1005 |

Water has a very high specific heat, so it stores a lot of energy and changes temperature slowly. This is why coastal climates are mild.

BN: তাপমাত্রা অবস্থা বোঝায়, আর তাপ Q হলো তাপমাত্রার পার্থক্যের কারণে প্রবাহিত শক্তি (জুল)। Q = mcΔT, c হলো আপেক্ষিক তাপ: ১ kg কে ১ K গরম করতে প্রয়োজনীয় শক্তি। পানির c = 4186 J/(kg·K), অনেক বেশি, তাই পানি ধীরে গরম বা ঠান্ডা হয়।

EX: Heating water
1. Heat 1.5 kg of water from 20 °C to 100 °C: Q = 1.5 × 4186 × 80 = **502,320 J ≈ 502 kJ**.
2. A 2000 W kettle (energy per second 2000 J): time = 502,320 ÷ 2000 = **251 s ≈ 4.2 min** if there are no losses. At 80% efficiency: 251 ÷ 0.8 = **314 s**.
3. **Food:** a 250 kcal snack = 250 × 4186 = **1,046,500 J ≈ 1.05 MJ**, enough to warm 1 kg of water by 250 K, if it could be used that way.
4. **Joule's experiment:** a 10 kg weight falls 2 m, 100 times, turning a paddle in 0.5 kg of water: energy = 10 × 9.8 × 2 × 100 = 19,600 J, so ΔT = 19,600 ÷ (0.5 × 4186) = **9.4 K**. Work and heat are both forms of energy.
/EX

### Calorimetry: heat exchanged is heat gained
When objects at different temperatures are mixed in an insulated container, **heat lost by the hot one = heat gained by the cold one** (conservation of energy). The final temperature T_f satisfies Σ m c (T_f − T_initial) = 0.

EX: Mixing
1. **Water with water:** 200 g at 80 °C mixed with 300 g at 20 °C. T_f = (200 × 80 + 300 × 20) ÷ 500 = 22,000 ÷ 500 = **44 °C**.
2. **Hot copper into water:** 0.3 kg copper at 100 °C into 0.5 kg water at 20 °C. 0.3 × 385 × (100 − T) = 0.5 × 4186 × (T − 20). So 11,550 − 115.5 T = 2093 T − 41,860, giving 53,410 = 2208.5 T and **T = 24.2 °C**. Copper's low specific heat means it barely warms the water.
3. **Find an unknown c:** a 0.15 kg metal sample at 100 °C is dropped into 0.4 kg of water at 20 °C and the final temperature is 26.0 °C. Heat lost = heat gained: 0.15 × c × 74 = 0.4 × 4186 × 6, so c = 10,046 ÷ 11.1 = **905 J/(kg·K)**. This matches **aluminium**.
/EX

### Cooling: Newton's law
A hot object loses heat at a rate proportional to the temperature difference with its surroundings: dT/dt = −k (T − T_env). This separable equation (you know the method from integration) has the solution

EQ: T(t) = T_env + (T₀ − T_env) e^(−k t)

EX: Coffee
1. Coffee at 90 °C in a 20 °C room cools to 60 °C in 10 min. e^(−10k) = 40 ÷ 70 = 4/7, so k = ln (7/4) ÷ 10 = 0.5596 ÷ 10 = **0.0560 per min**.
2. Time to reach 40 °C: 20 = 70 e^(−k t), so t = ln 3.5 ÷ k = 1.2528 ÷ 0.0560 = **22.4 min**.
3. The cooling is fast at first and slows down as the difference gets small, which is why the last degrees take forever.
/EX
