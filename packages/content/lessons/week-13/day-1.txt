### What a matrix is
A **matrix** is a rectangular array of numbers. An m × n matrix has m rows and n columns. The entry in row i, column j is written a_ij.

A = [[1, 2], [3, 4]] is a 2 × 2 matrix: row 1 is (1, 2), row 2 is (3, 4). A vector is a matrix with one column.

BN: ম্যাট্রিক্স হলো সংখ্যার আয়তাকার ছক। m × n ম্যাট্রিক্সে m টা সারি (row) ও n টা স্তম্ভ (column)। i-তম সারি ও j-তম স্তম্ভের সংখ্যা a_ij। ভেক্টর হলো এক স্তম্ভের ম্যাট্রিক্স।

### Addition and scalar multiplication
Add matrices of the **same size** entry by entry. Multiply by a number c entry by entry.

EX: Entry-by-entry rules
1. A = [[1, 2], [3, 4]], B = [[0, 1], [1, 0]]. A + B = [[1, 3], [4, 4]].
2. 3A = [[3, 6], [9, 12]].
3. A − 2B = [[1, 0], [1, 4]]: since 2B = [[0, 2], [2, 0]].
4. The **zero matrix** O has all entries 0, and A + O = A.
/EX

### Matrix multiplication
To multiply an m × n matrix A by an n × p matrix B, the result AB is m × p. The entry (AB)_ij is the **dot product of row i of A with column j of B**:

EQ: (AB)_ij = a_i1 b_1j + a_i2 b_2j + … + a_in b_nj

The number of columns of A must equal the number of rows of B.

EX: A first product
1. A = [[1, 2], [3, 4]], B = [[0, 1], [1, 0]].
2. (AB)₁₁ = 1 × 0 + 2 × 1 = 2. (AB)₁₂ = 1 × 1 + 2 × 0 = 1. (AB)₂₁ = 3 × 0 + 4 × 1 = 4. (AB)₂₂ = 3 × 1 + 4 × 0 = 3.
3. So AB = [[2, 1], [4, 3]]: B has **swapped the columns** of A.
4. BA = [[3, 4], [1, 2]]: here the **rows** are swapped. So AB ≠ BA.
/EX

PREDICT: If A is 2 × 3 and B is 3 × 2, what size are AB and BA? Can both exist? Are they equal? => AB is **2 × 2** and BA is **3 × 3**. Both exist, but they have different sizes, so they can never be equal.

EX: A rectangular product
1. C = [[1, 0, 2], [−1, 3, 1]] (2 × 3) and D = [[3, 1], [2, 1], [1, 0]] (3 × 2).
2. CD: row 1 · col 1 = 1×3 + 0×2 + 2×1 = 5. Row 1 · col 2 = 1×1 + 0×1 + 2×0 = 1. Row 2 · col 1 = −3 + 6 + 1 = 4. Row 2 · col 2 = −1 + 3 + 0 = 2. So CD = [[5, 1], [4, 2]].
3. DC is 3 × 3: DC = [[2, 3, 7], [1, 3, 5], [1, 0, 2]]. For instance (DC)₁₃ = 3 × 2 + 1 × 1 = 7.
/EX

### Special matrices and the transpose
- The **identity** I has 1s on the diagonal and 0s elsewhere. IA = AI = A. For 2 × 2: I = [[1, 0], [0, 1]].
- The **transpose** Aᵀ swaps rows and columns: (Aᵀ)_ij = a_ji. For C above, Cᵀ = [[1, −1], [0, 3], [2, 1]].
- **Reversal rule:** (AB)ᵀ = Bᵀ Aᵀ.

EX: Checking the rule
1. With A and B as before, (AB)ᵀ = [[2, 4], [1, 3]].
2. Bᵀ = B = [[0, 1], [1, 0]] and Aᵀ = [[1, 3], [2, 4]]. BᵀAᵀ = [[2, 4], [1, 3]] ✓.
3. **Matrix times vector:** A (x, y) = (x + 2y, 3x + 4y). For (1, 1): **(3, 7)**.
/EX

EX: A real-world product
1. Three shops sell two products. Units sold (rows are shops, columns are products): S = [[10, 5], [8, 12], [6, 9]]. Prices per unit: p = (4, 3) in euros.
2. Revenue per shop = S p = (10×4 + 5×3, 8×4 + 12×3, 6×4 + 9×3) = **(55, 68, 54)** euros.
3. Total revenue = 55 + 68 + 54 = **177** euros. The matrix did three dot products at once.
/EX

BN: (AB)_ij = A এর i-তম সারি ও B এর j-তম স্তম্ভের ডট গুণফল। A এর স্তম্ভ সংখ্যা = B এর সারি সংখ্যা হতে হবে। AB ≠ BA সাধারণভাবে। (AB)ᵀ = BᵀAᵀ (উল্টো ক্রম)। I দিয়ে গুণ করলে কিছু বদলায় না।

TRAP: Matrix multiplication is **not commutative**: AB ≠ BA in general, so (A + B)² ≠ A² + 2AB + B². Check the sizes before multiplying: (m × n)(n × p). And you cannot "cancel" a matrix: AB = AC does not always imply B = C.

### Temperature
**Temperature** measures how hot something is: physically, it is related to the average kinetic energy of the molecules. If two objects at different temperatures touch, heat flows until they reach **thermal equilibrium**. The **zeroth law**: if A and B are each in equilibrium with C, then A and B are in equilibrium with each other. That is why a thermometer works.

EQ: T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15     T(°F) = 1.8 T(°C) + 32

The **kelvin** scale starts at **absolute zero**, 0 K = −273.15 °C, where molecular motion is minimal. Differences are the same in kelvin and °C: ΔT(K) = ΔT(°C).

BN: তাপমাত্রা হলো অণুর গড় গতিশক্তির পরিমাপ। দুই বস্তু ছুঁলে তাপ প্রবাহিত হয় যতক্ষণ না তাপীয় সাম্য আসে। শূন্যতম সূত্র: A ও B আলাদাভাবে C এর সাথে সাম্যে থাকলে A ও B পরস্পর সাম্যে। T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15। পার্থক্য কেলভিন ও °C এ একই।

EX: Conversions
1. Body temperature 37 °C = 37 × 1.8 + 32 = **98.6 °F** = **310.15 K**.
2. Water boils at 100 °C = 212 °F = 373.15 K.
3. −40 °C = −40 °F: solve T = 1.8 T + 32, so −0.8 T = 32, T = −40 ✓.
4. Absolute zero: −273.15 °C = 1.8 × (−273.15) + 32 = **−459.67 °F**.
5. A warming of 20 °C is a warming of **20 K** and of 36 °F.
/EX

### Thermal expansion
Almost everything expands when heated, because the molecules vibrate harder and push apart. For a small temperature change ΔT:

EQ: ΔL = α L₀ ΔT     ΔA = 2 α A₀ ΔT     ΔV = β V₀ ΔT with β = 3 α (solids)

α is the **coefficient of linear expansion** (per kelvin).

| Material | α (10⁻⁶ per K) |
| Invar (a nickel-steel alloy) | 1.2 |
| Glass | 9 |
| Steel | 12 |
| Concrete | 12 |
| Copper | 17 |
| Aluminium | 23 |

EX: Expansion in the real world
1. **Rail:** a steel rail 30 m long warms by 40 K: ΔL = 12 × 10⁻⁶ × 30 × 40 = 0.0144 m = **14.4 mm**. Railways leave gaps or use welded rails held under stress.
2. **Bridge:** an aluminium bridge span 100 m, ΔT = 50 K: ΔL = 23 × 10⁻⁶ × 100 × 50 = **11.5 cm**. Bridges have expansion joints.
3. **Area:** a steel plate of 2 m² heated by 100 K: ΔA = 2 × 12 × 10⁻⁶ × 2 × 100 = **0.0048 m²**. A hole in the plate expands as well, as if it were made of the same material.
4. **Liquids:** petrol has β = 9.5 × 10⁻⁴ per K. A 50 L tank filled at 10 °C and warmed to 30 °C gains 9.5 × 10⁻⁴ × 50 × 20 = **0.95 L** which overflows.
5. **Thermal stress:** a steel rod clamped at both ends cannot expand. The stress is σ = E α ΔT = 200 × 10⁹ × 12 × 10⁻⁶ × 40 = **96 MPa**. On a cross-section of 10 cm² this is a force of **96 kN**, enough to buckle a rail.
/EX

### The strange case of water
Most liquids shrink as they cool, but water is densest at **4 °C** (999.97 kg/m³). Below that it expands, and ice (917 kg/m³) is less dense than liquid water. So a lake cools from the top, the 4 °C water sinks, and ice forms at the surface and insulates the water below, which lets fish survive winters.

### The bimetallic strip
Two metals are bonded together, for example brass (α = 19 × 10⁻⁶) and steel (12 × 10⁻⁶). When heated, brass expands more, so the strip **bends** toward the steel side. This is the heart of a simple thermostat: the strip bends until it breaks a circuit.

BN: প্রসারণ: ΔL = αL₀ΔT, ΔA = 2αA₀ΔT, ΔV = βV₀ΔT (β = 3α)। ইস্পাতের α = 12 × 10⁻⁶/K, অ্যালুমিনিয়ামের 23 × 10⁻⁶/K। আটকানো দণ্ডে তাপীয় পীড়ন σ = EαΔT। পানি 4 °C এ সবচেয়ে ঘন, তাই হ্রদ ওপর থেকে জমে। দ্বিধাতব পাত গরমে বাঁকে এবং থার্মোস্ট্যাটে ব্যবহৃত হয়।

TRAP: Temperature **differences** are the same in °C and K, but the **values** are not: never use °C in gas laws (Week 14). Also do not use the linear formula α for volume without multiplying by 3, and remember that a **hole** gets bigger when the plate is heated.
