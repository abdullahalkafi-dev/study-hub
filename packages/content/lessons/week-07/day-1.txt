@section math | 2 · Math: optimisation | 55 min

### Critical points
Where can a smooth function have a maximum or a minimum? Only where the tangent is horizontal (f′ = 0), or at a point where the derivative does not exist, or at the **ends** of the interval. A point in the interior where f′ = 0 or f′ does not exist is called a **critical point**.

**The Extreme Value Theorem.** A continuous function on a closed interval [a, b] always has both an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum. (Last week's continuity pays off here: no jumps means no skipped values.)

### The closed-interval method
1. Find all critical points inside (a, b).
2. Evaluate f at each critical point **and at both endpoints**.
3. The largest value is the absolute maximum. The smallest is the absolute minimum.

EX: A cubic on an interval
1. f(x) = x³ − 3x² − 9x + 5 on [−2, 6]. f′ = 3x² − 6x − 9 = 3 (x − 3)(x + 1). Critical points: x = −1 and x = 3.
2. Values: f(−2) = −8 − 12 + 18 + 5 = 3. f(−1) = −1 − 3 + 9 + 5 = 10. f(3) = 27 − 27 − 27 + 5 = −22. f(6) = 216 − 108 − 54 + 5 = 59.
3. **Absolute maximum 59 at x = 6** (an endpoint!). **Absolute minimum −22 at x = 3.** The local maximum 10 at x = −1 is not the absolute one.
/EX

### Classifying a critical point
**First derivative test.** If f′ changes from positive to negative at c, it is a local maximum. From negative to positive, a local minimum. No sign change means neither (like x³ at 0).

**Second derivative test.** At a critical point c (f′(c) = 0): if f″(c) > 0 the graph is a cup, so a local minimum. If f″(c) < 0, a cap, so a local maximum. If f″(c) = 0, the test fails: use the first derivative test.

EX: Both tests
1. f(x) = x³ − 3x² − 9x + 5: f″ = 6x − 6. At x = −1: f″ = −12 < 0, so a **local maximum**. At x = 3: f″ = 12 > 0, so a **local minimum**.
2. f(x) = x⁴: f′ = 4x³ = 0 at 0, and f″(0) = 0, so the second test fails. The first derivative test: f′ < 0 before and > 0 after, so a **minimum**.
/EX

BN: সর্বোচ্চ বা সর্বনিম্ন থাকতে পারে যেখানে f′ = 0, বা f′ নেই, বা বন্ধ ব্যবধানের প্রান্তে। বন্ধ ব্যবধান পদ্ধতি: ক্রান্তি বিন্দু ও দুই প্রান্তে মান বের করো; সবচেয়ে বড়টা সর্বোচ্চ, ছোটটা সর্বনিম্ন। দ্বিতীয় অন্তরকলজ পরীক্ষা: f″ > 0 হলে ন্যূনতম (কাপ), f″ < 0 হলে সর্বোচ্চ (টুপি)।

### A method for word problems
1. **Read and draw.** Name the quantities.
2. **Objective:** write the quantity to maximise or minimise.
3. **Constraint:** write the given fixed relation.
4. **Reduce to one variable** with the constraint, and state its domain.
5. **Differentiate**, set to zero, solve.
6. **Check** it is the max or min (endpoints, or the second derivative).
7. **Answer the question** in words with units.

EX: The best rectangular pen
1. 100 m of fence encloses a rectangular pen against a straight river (so only three sides need fencing). Maximise the area.
2. Let the two sides perpendicular to the river be x, and the side parallel be y = 100 − 2x. Area A = x (100 − 2x) = 100x − 2x², with 0 < x < 50.
3. A′ = 100 − 4x = 0 gives x = 25. A″ = −4 < 0, so it is a maximum.
4. Dimensions **25 m by 50 m**, area **1250 m²**. Notice the parallel side is twice each other side.
/EX

EX: The open box
1. From a square sheet of side 12 cm, cut equal squares of side x from the corners and fold up to make an open box. Maximise the volume.
2. V = x (12 − 2x)², with 0 < x < 6.
3. V′ = (12 − 2x)² + x × 2 (12 − 2x)(−2) = (12 − 2x)(12 − 2x − 4x) = (12 − 2x)(12 − 6x). Zero at x = 6 (volume 0) and **x = 2**.
4. V(2) = 2 × 8² = **128 cm³**. At the ends V = 0, so this is the maximum.
/EX

EX: The cheapest can
1. A cylindrical can holds 1000 cm³ (one litre). Minimise the metal used (surface area S = 2π r² + 2π r h).
2. Constraint: π r² h = 1000, so h = 1000 ÷ (π r²). Then S = 2π r² + 2000 ÷ r.
3. S′ = 4π r − 2000 ÷ r² = 0, so r³ = 500 ÷ π = 159.2 and **r = 5.42 cm**.
4. h = 1000 ÷ (π × 29.4) = **10.8 cm = 2r**. S = 184.5 + 369.1 = **553.6 cm²**.
5. The best can has height equal to its diameter. Real cans are often taller because of other costs, such as the seams and what fits a hand or a shelf.
/EX

EX: Maximum profit
1. A factory's profit from selling x items is P(x) = −2x² + 120x − 500. P′ = −4x + 120 = 0 gives **x = 30**.
2. P(30) = −1800 + 3600 − 500 = **1300**. P″ = −4 < 0, so a maximum.
/EX

TRAP: Do not forget the endpoints: in the cubic example the true maximum was at an endpoint. Also state the **domain** of the variable (x between 0 and 6 for the box), and make sure the final answer answers the question asked, with units, not just the value of x.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: torque and the lever | 35 min

### Turning effects
A force can change straight-line motion (Newton's second law), and it can also change **rotation**. The turning effect depends on the force, on how far from the pivot it acts, and on the direction. You push a door near the hinge and it barely moves, near the handle and it swings easily.

EQ: τ = r F sin θ = F × d⊥     unit: N·m

- r is the distance from the pivot (the axis) to the point where the force acts.
- θ is the angle between the force and the line from the pivot to the point.
- **d⊥ = r sin θ is the lever arm**: the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of the force.

In vector form, τ = r × F, from Week 4. The direction (by the right-hand rule) is along the axis: counterclockwise is usually positive.

LAB: Torque and angle | Choose the force and the distance from the pivot. The graph shows how the torque changes as the force direction rotates from along the wrench (0°) to straight across (90°) and back. | F:Force (N):10:200:5:100 ;; r:Distance from pivot (m):0.1:1:0.05:0.3 ;; a:Angle θ (degrees):0:180:5:90 | r*F*sin(a*pi/180):Torque (N·m):1 ;; r*sin(a*pi/180):Lever arm (m):3 ;; 20/(r*sin(a*pi/180)+0.0001):Force needed for 20 N·m (N):0 | r*F*sin(x*pi/180) | 0,180 | 0,320

EX: A wrench
1. A force of 100 N at the end of a 0.3 m wrench, applied at 90°: τ = 0.3 × 100 × 1 = **30 N·m**.
2. At 30° to the wrench: τ = 0.3 × 100 × 0.5 = **15 N·m**. Pulling along the wrench (0°) gives zero.
3. To get 30 N·m with a shorter 0.15 m wrench you need 200 N: a longer handle means less force.
/EX

BN: টর্ক τ = rF sin θ = F × (লম্ব দূরত্ব)। লম্ব দূরত্ব d⊥ = r sin θ কে বলে বাহু (lever arm)। বল আর অক্ষের দূরত্ব যত বেশি, ঘোরানোর প্রভাব তত বেশি; বল ঘূর্ণন-ব্যাসার্ধ বরাবর হলে টর্ক শূন্য। একক N·m, কিন্তু এটা জুল নয় (শক্তি নয়, একটা ভেক্টর)।

### Balance: the lever and the seesaw
A body does not rotate if the **net torque is zero**. Take torques about the same pivot, with clockwise and counterclockwise having opposite signs. For a lever with forces F₁ and F₂ at distances d₁ and d₂ on opposite sides:

EQ: F₁ d₁ = F₂ d₂

EX: A seesaw
1. A 30 kg child sits 2 m from the pivot. Where must a 60 kg adult sit to balance?
2. Weights: 30g × 2 = 60g × d, so d = **1 m**. The heavier person sits closer.
/EX

EX: Prising a nail
1. A claw hammer: the effort arm (from the pivot to your hand) is 30 cm, the load arm (from the pivot to the nail) is 3 cm.
2. Mechanical advantage = 30 ÷ 3 = **10**. A 20 N effort produces a 200 N pull on the nail.
3. A lever trades force for distance: your hand moves 10 times farther than the nail.
/EX

EX: A uniform plank
1. A uniform 6 kg plank of length 3 m rests on a pivot 1 m from its left end. A child stands on the left end to balance it. Find the child's mass.
2. The plank's weight acts at its centre of mass, 1.5 m from the left end, which is 0.5 m to the right of the pivot. Torque from the plank: 6 × 0.5 = 3 kg·m (times g).
3. The child is 1 m left of the pivot: m × 1 = 3, so **m = 3 kg**.
/EX

EX: A metre stick
1. A metre stick (uniform, mass negligible here) is pivoted at 50 cm. A 200 g mass is at 20 cm. What mass at 70 cm balances it?
2. Left distance: 30 cm. Right distance: 20 cm. 200 × 30 = m × 20, so **m = 300 g**.
/EX

TRAP: Always measure the lever arm **perpendicular** to the line of the force, not just the distance along the beam. And the torque depends on the chosen pivot, so pick one pivot for the whole problem (a smart choice is the point where an unknown force acts, which makes that force's torque zero). Do not confuse torque (N·m) with energy (J), even though the unit looks the same.

### Recap
- Critical points: f′ = 0 or undefined. Closed-interval method: compare critical values and endpoints. Tests: first derivative (sign change) and second derivative (cup or cap).
- Word-problem method: objective, constraint, one variable, derivative, check, answer in words. Best box, can, fence.
- Torque τ = r F sin θ = F d⊥. Lever: F₁d₁ = F₂d₂. Balance: net torque zero about any pivot.
