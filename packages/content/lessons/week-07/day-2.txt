### Minimum time: the lifeguard problem
A lifeguard on the beach sees a swimmer in trouble. The lifeguard runs faster than they swim, so the straight line is not the quickest path. Is it better to run a bit farther along the sand and swim less?

EX: Setting up
1. The lifeguard is at A. The point C on the shore nearest to the swimmer is 100 m away along the beach, and the swimmer is 50 m out from C. The lifeguard runs at 5 m/s and swims at 2 m/s.
2. The lifeguard runs along the beach to a point P that is x metres before C, then swims straight to the swimmer.
3. Run distance: 100 − x. Swim distance: √(x² + 2500). Total time T(x) = (100 − x) ÷ 5 + √(x² + 2500) ÷ 2.
/EX

EX: Solving
1. T′(x) = −1/5 + x ÷ (2 √(x² + 2500)) = 0, so x ÷ √(x² + 2500) = 2/5.
2. Square: x² ÷ (x² + 2500) = 4/25, so 25x² = 4x² + 10,000, 21x² = 10,000, and **x = 21.8 m**.
3. T = 78.2 ÷ 5 + √(476 + 2500) ÷ 2 = 15.6 + 27.3 = **42.9 s**.
4. Compare: swimming straight from A takes √12,500 ÷ 2 = 55.9 s. Running all the way to C and then swimming takes 20 + 25 = 45 s. The best mix is better than both.
/EX

**A hidden law.** At the best point, T′ = 0 says (sin of the swim angle) ÷ v_swim = 1 ÷ v_run. With the angle measured from the line perpendicular to the shore, this is **sin θ₁ ÷ v₁ = sin θ₂ ÷ v₂**, which is **Snell's law of refraction** for light going between two media. Light, like the lifeguard, takes the path of least time (Fermat's principle). You can derive it in the challenge below.

### More problems with shapes
EX: Largest rectangle in a semicircle
1. A rectangle sits inside a semicircle of radius R with its base on the diameter. Maximise its area.
2. Let the half-width be x. Height √(R² − x²). Area A = 2x √(R² − x²).
3. A′ = 2√(R² − x²) − 2x² ÷ √(R² − x²) = 0, so R² − x² = x² and **x = R/√2**.
4. Height = R/√2 as well, area = **R²**. For R = 5 the best area is 25.
/EX

EX: Cutting a wire
1. A 40 m wire is cut into two pieces. One bends into a square and the other into a circle. Find where to cut so that the total area is smallest, and where it is largest.
2. Let the square use x metres: side x/4, area x²/16. The circle uses 40 − x: radius (40 − x) ÷ 2π, area (40 − x)² ÷ (4π).
3. A′ = x/8 − (40 − x) ÷ (2π) = 0. Then x (1/8 + 1/(2π)) = 40 ÷ (2π), so x (0.2842) = 6.366 and **x = 22.4 m** (square), 17.6 m (circle). Minimum area: 31.4 + 24.6 = **56.0 m²**.
4. Compare the endpoints: all circle (x = 0) gives 1600 ÷ (4π) = 127.3 m², all square gives 100 m². The **maximum is at the endpoint**: use the whole wire for the circle, 127.3 m².
/EX

EX: Revenue and profit
1. A company sells x units at price p = 100 − 0.5x. Revenue R = x (100 − 0.5x) = 100x − 0.5x². R′ = 100 − x = 0 gives **x = 100**, price 50, revenue **5000**.
2. If the cost is C(x) = 1000 + 20x + 0.01x², the profit is maximised where **marginal revenue equals marginal cost**: R′ = C′, so 100 − x = 20 + 0.02x, giving **x = 78.4**. This is a general rule in economics: stop producing when the extra revenue from one more item equals its extra cost.
/EX

EX: The best speed for a trip
1. A lorry's cost per kilometre is C(v) = 400 ÷ v + 0.0005 v² (driver's time plus fuel, with v in km/h).
2. C′ = −400 ÷ v² + 0.001 v = 0, so v³ = 400,000 and **v = 73.7 km/h**.
3. C = 5.43 + 2.72 = **8.15 per km**. Slower costs more driver time, faster costs more fuel.
/EX

TRAP: Always check that the point you found is a minimum (or maximum), and compare with the endpoints, as the wire problem shows. When you square an equation (like in the lifeguard problem), check that the answer fits the original (here x > 0). And when the problem asks for the maximum **and** the minimum, they can be at different places.

So **sin θ₁ ÷ v₁ = sin θ₂ ÷ v₂** ∎. Since refractive index n = c ÷ v, this is n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: static equilibrium | 35 min

### The two conditions
A body at rest (or moving at constant velocity without rotating) is in **equilibrium**. Two things must hold:

EQ: Σ F = 0 (no net force)     Σ τ = 0 (no net torque, about any point)

For a problem in a plane, this gives three equations: ΣFₓ = 0, ΣF_y = 0 and Στ = 0. They can find up to three unknown forces.

**Strategy.**
1. Draw the free-body diagram of the body: all forces at their points of action (weight at the centre of mass).
2. Choose axes for the forces.
3. Choose a **pivot** where one or more unknown forces act, so their torques vanish.
4. Write the three equations and solve.

EX: Two cables
1. A 100 N sign hangs from two cables, one at 30° above the horizontal to the left and one at 60° above the horizontal to the right.
2. Horizontal: T₁ cos 30° = T₂ cos 60°, so T₂ = √3 T₁.
3. Vertical: T₁ sin 30° + T₂ sin 60° = 100, so 0.5 T₁ + 1.5 T₁ = 100.
4. **T₁ = 50 N, T₂ = 86.6 N**.
/EX

EX: A ladder against a smooth wall
1. A 5 m uniform ladder (20 kg) rests with its foot 3 m from a smooth wall, so it reaches 4 m up the wall. The wall has no friction. Find the forces.
2. Forces: weight W = 196 N at the middle (1.5 m from the wall horizontally). The wall's push N_w is horizontal. The floor gives a normal force N_f (up) and friction f (toward the wall).
3. Torque about the foot: N_w × 4 = W × 1.5, so N_w = 196 × 1.5 ÷ 4 = **73.5 N**.
4. Horizontal: f = N_w = **73.5 N**. Vertical: N_f = W = **196 N**.
5. The foot is about to slip when f = μ N_f. So the ladder is safe if μ ≥ 73.5 ÷ 196 = **0.375**.
/EX

EX: A beam held by a cable
1. A uniform 2 m beam (10 kg) is hinged to a wall and held horizontal by a cable making 30° with the beam, attached at the beam's outer end. A 30 kg sign hangs at the outer end. Find the tension and the hinge forces (g = 9.8).
2. Torque about the hinge: the vertical part of the tension is T sin 30°. T sin 30° × 2 = 10g × 1 + 30g × 2 = 70g, so T = 70g = **686 N**.
3. Horizontal hinge force: H = T cos 30° = **594 N** (toward the wall's side opposite the cable pull).
4. Vertical: T sin 30° + V = 40g, so V = 392 − 343 = **49 N** upward.
/EX

BN: স্থিতিশীল ভারসাম্যের দুই শর্ত: মোট বল শূন্য (ΣF = 0) আর মোট টর্ক শূন্য (Στ = 0, যেকোনো বিন্দুর সাপেক্ষে)। ধাপ: মুক্ত বস্তু চিত্র আঁকো, ওজন ভরকেন্দ্রে দাও, এমন একটা কেন্দ্র বেছে নাও যেখানে অজানা বল কাজ করে (তার টর্ক শূন্য), তিনটি সমীকরণ লিখে সমাধান করো। মই যত শুয়ে যায়, ঘর্ষণ তত বেশি লাগে।

TRAP: The weight of a uniform body acts at its **centre**, not at an end. Do not forget the friction and the normal force at the floor. Also, the torque equation must use the same pivot for every force, and the lever arm is the perpendicular distance, not the length along the beam.
