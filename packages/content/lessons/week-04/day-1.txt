@section math | 2 · Math: lines, distances and circles | 60 min

### Why coordinates?
Descartes' idea: fix an origin and two perpendicular axes. Then a point is a pair (x, y), a curve is the set of points satisfying an equation, and a geometry problem becomes an algebra problem.

### Distance and midpoint
For points A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂), form a right triangle with horizontal leg |x₂ − x₁| and vertical leg |y₂ − y₁|. Pythagoras gives:

EQ: distance AB = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)     midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)

EX: Distance and midpoint
1. A(1, 2) and B(7, 10). Distance = √(36 + 64) = √100 = **10**.
2. Midpoint = (4, 6).
/EX

### The equation of a line
The **slope** is the rise over the run: m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). It equals tan θ, where θ is the angle the line makes with the positive x-axis.

| Form | Equation | Use when you know |
| Slope-intercept | y = mx + c | The slope and the y-intercept |
| Point-slope | y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) | The slope and one point |
| Two-point | (y − y₁) ÷ (y₂ − y₁) = (x − x₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁) | Two points |
| General | ax + by + c = 0 | Needed for the distance formula |

EX: Line through two points
1. Through (1, 2) and (4, 8). Slope = 6 ÷ 3 = 2.
2. y − 2 = 2(x − 1), so **y = 2x**.
/EX

### Parallel and perpendicular
- Parallel lines have equal slopes: m₁ = m₂.
- Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is −1: m₁ m₂ = −1.

**Why −1?** Rotate a line with direction (1, m) by 90°. The direction becomes (−m, 1), whose slope is 1 ÷ (−m) = −1/m. So the new slope is the negative reciprocal.

BN: সমান্তরাল রেখার ঢাল সমান। লম্ব রেখার ঢালের গুণফল −১। কারণ: দিক (1, m) কে ৯০° ঘোরালে হয় (−m, 1), যার ঢাল −1/m। অর্থাৎ ঢাল হয় উল্টো ও উল্টো চিহ্নের।

EX: Perpendicular bisector
1. Find the perpendicular bisector of A(1, 2) and B(5, 6).
2. Midpoint (3, 4). Slope of AB = 4 ÷ 4 = 1, so the perpendicular slope is −1.
3. y − 4 = −(x − 3), so **y = −x + 7**.
4. Every point on it is equally far from A and B. Check with (3, 4): distance to A is √(4 + 4) and to B is √(4 + 4) ✓.
/EX

### Distance from a point to a line
EQ: distance from (x₀, y₀) to ax + by + c = 0 equals |a x₀ + b y₀ + c| ÷ √(a² + b²)

**Idea of the proof.** The shortest distance is along the perpendicular. The vector (a, b) is perpendicular to the line. Moving from the line to the point along this direction, the value of ax + by + c grows by (a² + b²) per unit parameter, which is exactly how the formula normalises by √(a² + b²).

EX: Distance
1. Distance from (3, 4) to 3x − 4y + 5 = 0: |9 − 16 + 5| ÷ √(9 + 16) = 2 ÷ 5 = **0.4**.
2. Distance between the parallel lines 3x + 4y = 10 and 3x + 4y = 20: pick (0, 2.5) on the first, then |0 + 10 − 20| ÷ 5 = **2**.
/EX

### Circles
A circle is all points at distance r from the centre (h, k). Using the distance formula:

EQ: (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²

Expanding gives the general form x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0. To read off the centre and radius, **complete the square**.

EX: General to standard form
1. x² + y² − 6x + 4y − 12 = 0.
2. (x² − 6x + 9) + (y² + 4y + 4) = 12 + 9 + 4, so (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25.
3. Centre **(3, −2)**, radius **5**.
/EX

EX: Circle through three points
1. Points (0, 0), (4, 0) and (0, 2). Use x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0.
2. (0, 0): F = 0. (4, 0): 16 + 4D = 0, so D = −4. (0, 2): 4 + 2E = 0, so E = −2.
3. x² + y² − 4x − 2y = 0, so (x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 5. Centre (2, 1), radius √5.
/EX

EX: Tangent to a circle
1. The tangent at (3, 4) on x² + y² = 25 is perpendicular to the radius.
2. Radius slope = 4/3, so the tangent slope = −3/4.
3. y − 4 = −(3/4)(x − 3), so **3x + 4y = 25**.
/EX

EX: Line meets circle
1. Where does y = x + 1 meet x² + y² = 25?
2. x² + (x + 1)² = 25 gives 2x² + 2x − 24 = 0, so x² + x − 12 = 0, so (x + 4)(x − 3) = 0.
3. Points **(3, 4)** and **(−4, −3)**.
4. The discriminant tells the story: D > 0 means two points, D = 0 means tangent, D < 0 means no intersection.
/EX

BN: বৃত্তের সমীকরণ (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²। সাধারণ রূপ থেকে কেন্দ্র ও ব্যাসার্ধ পেতে পূর্ণবর্গ করো। বিন্দুতে স্পর্শক ব্যাসার্ধের সঙ্গে লম্ব। রেখা ও বৃত্ত মেলালে দ্বিঘাত সমীকরণ পাই: D > 0 হলে দুই বিন্দু, D = 0 হলে স্পর্শক, D < 0 হলে ছেদ নেই।

TRAP: When the slope is undefined (a vertical line, x = constant) the formulas with m do not apply. Treat vertical lines separately. Also, the formula for the distance from a point to a line needs the line in **general form** with all terms on one side.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: friction in depth | 30 min

### What friction really does
Friction is a contact force along the surface that opposes sliding (or the tendency to slide). The two laws from Week 2:

- Static: f ≤ μ_s N (any value up to a maximum).
- Kinetic: f = μ_k N (a fixed value while sliding).

New today: N is **not** always mg. Whenever other forces push into or lift off the surface, N changes, and friction changes with it.

### Pulling at an angle
A crate of mass m is pulled by a force F at angle θ above the horizontal. Vertical balance: N + F sin θ = mg, so **N = mg − F sin θ**. Pulling upward at an angle lightens the contact, which reduces friction. Horizontal: F cos θ − μ N = m a.

EX: Pulling a crate
1. m = 20 kg, F = 100 N at 30°, μ_k = 0.3, g = 9.8.
2. N = 196 − 100 × 0.5 = 146 N, so friction = 0.3 × 146 = 43.8 N.
3. Horizontal pull = 100 cos 30° = 86.6 N.
4. Net = 86.6 − 43.8 = 42.8 N, so **a = 2.14 m/s²**.
/EX

### The best angle to pull
For constant speed, F cos θ = μ(mg − F sin θ), so F (cos θ + μ sin θ) = μ m g.

EQ: F = μ m g ÷ (cos θ + μ sin θ)

To make F as small as possible, make the denominator as large as possible. The maximum of cos θ + μ sin θ is √(1 + μ²), reached when **tan θ = μ** (this is the same R sin(x + α) idea from Thursday, now used in physics).

EX: The best angle
1. m = 20 kg, μ = 0.3: best angle θ = tan⁻¹(0.3) = **16.7°**.
2. F_min = 0.3 × 196 ÷ √(1.09) = 58.8 ÷ 1.044 = **56.3 N**.
3. Compare with pulling horizontally: F = μ m g = 58.8 N. The saving is small for low μ but larger for high μ.
/EX

BN: কোণ করে ওপরের দিকে টানলে অভিলম্ব বল কমে: N = mg − F sin θ, ফলে ঘর্ষণও কমে। ধ্রুব বেগে টানতে ন্যূনতম বল লাগে যখন tan θ = μ, আর তখন F_min = μmg/√(1 + μ²)। এটা ত্রিকোণমিতির R sin(x + α) ধারণার সরাসরি প্রয়োগ।

### Stacked blocks
EX: Will the top block slip?
1. A 2 kg block sits on an 8 kg block. The coefficient of static friction between them is 0.4. The table is frictionless. A horizontal force F pulls the **bottom** block.
2. The top block is accelerated only by friction, so its maximum acceleration is μ_s g = 0.4 × 9.8 = 3.92 m/s².
3. If both move together, F = (2 + 8) × a. At the limit F = 10 × 3.92 = **39.2 N**.
4. Beyond 39.2 N, the top block slips behind the bottom block.
/EX

EX: Braking distance
1. A car on a dry road with μ = 0.7 brakes hard from 25 m/s (wheels locked).
2. a = μ g = 6.86 m/s², and v² = 2 a d gives d = 625 ÷ 13.72 = **45.6 m**.
3. On wet road with μ = 0.3: a = 2.94 and d = 625 ÷ 5.88 = **106 m**. Double the speed and the distance becomes four times larger.
/EX

TRAP: Kinetic friction does not depend on the area of contact or on the sliding speed (in this model). It depends only on μ and N. And do not use N = mg without checking vertical forces.

### Practice
LVL: Level 1 | Direct
P: A 10 kg box is dragged on the floor by 50 N horizontal force. μ_k = 0.3 (g = 9.8). Find the acceleration. => f = 29.4 N, so net = 20.6 N and **a = 2.06 m/s²**.
P: A car brakes with μ = 0.8 from 20 m/s. Find the stopping distance (g = 9.8). => d = 400 ÷ (2 × 7.84) = **25.5 m**.
LVL: Level 2 | Angled and stacked
P: A 15 kg sledge is pulled at constant speed by a rope at 25° above the horizontal. μ_k = 0.2 (g = 9.8). Find the tension. => F = μ m g ÷ (cos 25° + 0.2 sin 25°) = 29.4 ÷ (0.9063 + 0.0845) = 29.4 ÷ 0.9908 = **29.7 N**.
P: A 5 kg block rests on a 15 kg block, μ_s = 0.3 between them, table frictionless. What is the maximum horizontal force on the bottom block so that they move together? => a_max = 0.3 × 9.8 = 2.94 m/s². F = 20 × 2.94 = **58.8 N**.
P: A 4 kg block is pushed against a vertical wall by a horizontal force F. μ_s = 0.5. What is the smallest F that holds the block still (g = 9.8)? => Friction must balance weight: μ F = m g, so F = 39.2 ÷ 0.5 = **78.4 N**.
LVL: Challenge | Push or pull?
P: A 20 kg crate with μ = 0.4 is moved at constant speed either by pulling upward at 20° or by pushing downward at 20° with the same size of force along a line at 20° to the horizontal. Which needs more force, and by how much? (g = 9.8) => Pull: F = 78.4 ÷ (cos 20° + 0.4 sin 20°) = 78.4 ÷ (0.9397 + 0.1368) = 78.4 ÷ 1.0765 = **72.8 N**. Push: N = mg + F sin θ, so F cos θ = μ (mg + F sin θ), giving F = 78.4 ÷ (cos 20° − 0.4 sin 20°) = 78.4 ÷ (0.9397 − 0.1368) = 78.4 ÷ 0.8029 = **97.6 N**. Pushing needs **24.8 N more**, because pushing downward adds to the normal force.
