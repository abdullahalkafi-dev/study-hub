@section math | 2 · Math: sequences and series | 60 min

### Sequences
A sequence is a function from the positive integers: a₁, a₂, a₃, … Two families appear everywhere.

| Type | Rule | nth term |
| Arithmetic | add a fixed **common difference** d | aₙ = a + (n − 1) d |
| Geometric | multiply by a fixed **common ratio** r | aₙ = a rⁿ⁻¹ |

EX: Find terms
1. 3, 7, 11, … is arithmetic with a = 3 and d = 4. The 20th term is 3 + 19 × 4 = **79**.
2. 2, 6, 18, … is geometric with a = 2 and r = 3. The 8th term is 2 × 3⁷ = **4374**.
/EX

### Arithmetic series: the Gauss trick
The story goes that a young Gauss summed 1 + 2 + … + 100 instantly. Write the sum forwards and backwards:

S = 1 + 2 + 3 + … + 99 + 100
S = 100 + 99 + 98 + … + 2 + 1

Add column by column: every pair makes 101, and there are 100 pairs, so 2S = 100 × 101 and S = **5050**.

In general, for n terms with first term a and last term l:

EQ: Sₙ = n/2 (a + l) = n/2 (2a + (n − 1) d)

EX: Arithmetic sums
1. 3, 7, 11, … 20 terms: S₂₀ = 10 × (6 + 76) = **820**.
2. A theatre has 15 rows. The first row has 20 seats and each row has 2 more than the one before. Last row: 20 + 14 × 2 = 48. Total = 15 ÷ 2 × (20 + 48) = **510 seats**.
3. Three numbers in arithmetic progression sum to 21 and have product 315. Write them as 7 − d, 7, 7 + d (the middle is 21 ÷ 3). Then 7 (49 − d²) = 315, so d² = 4. The numbers are **5, 7, 9**.
/EX

BN: সমান্তর ধারার যোগফল: প্রথম ও শেষ পদ যোগ করে অর্ধেক পদসংখ্যা দিয়ে গুণ, Sₙ = n/2 (a + l)। গাউসের কৌশল: ধারাটা সামনে-পেছনে লিখে যোগ করলে প্রতিটা জোড়া একই যোগফল দেয়।

### Geometric series
Let S = a + a r + a r² + … + a rⁿ⁻¹. Multiply by r: r S = a r + a r² + … + a rⁿ. Subtract: S − r S = a − a rⁿ. So:

EQ: Sₙ = a (1 − rⁿ) ÷ (1 − r)     (r ≠ 1)

EX: A geometric sum
1. 2 + 6 + 18 + … (8 terms): S₈ = 2 × (3⁸ − 1) ÷ (3 − 1) = 3⁸ − 1 = **6560**.
2. The third term of a geometric sequence is 12 and the sixth is 96. Then r³ = 96 ÷ 12 = 8, so r = 2 and a = 12 ÷ 4 = 3. The sum of the first 10 terms is 3 × (2¹⁰ − 1) = **3069**.
/EX

### Infinite geometric series
If |r| < 1, then rⁿ shrinks toward 0 as n grows. So the sum of all the terms settles to a finite value:

EQ: S∞ = a ÷ (1 − r)     valid only for |r| < 1

EX: Infinite sums
1. 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 + … has a = 8 and r = 1/2, so S∞ = 8 ÷ (1/2) = **16**.
2. Repeating decimal 0.3333… = 0.3 + 0.03 + 0.003 + … has a = 0.3 and r = 0.1, so S∞ = 0.3 ÷ 0.9 = **1/3**.
3. 0.727272… = 0.72 + 0.0072 + … has a = 0.72 and r = 0.01, so S∞ = 0.72 ÷ 0.99 = 72/99 = **8/11**.
4. 0.999… has a = 0.9 and r = 0.1, so S∞ = 0.9 ÷ 0.9 = **1**. So 0.999… equals 1 exactly. It is not "just below" 1.
/EX

EX: A bouncing ball
1. A ball is dropped from 10 m. After each bounce, it rises to 60% of its previous height.
2. Down distance: 10. Then up and down each time: 2 × (6 + 3.6 + 2.16 + …), a geometric series with a = 6 and r = 0.6, whose sum is 6 ÷ 0.4 = 15.
3. Total distance = 10 + 2 × 15 = **40 m**, even though there are infinitely many bounces.
/EX

EX: A savings plan
1. You save 100 each month, and the account earns 0.5% a month. Each deposit grows for a different number of months, so the final amount is a geometric series.
2. At the end of month 12, the first deposit has grown by 1.005¹¹ and the last by 1.005⁰ (if deposits are made at the end of each month), so the total is 100 × (1.005¹² − 1) ÷ 0.005 = 100 × 12.336 = **1233.56**.
3. Total deposits: 1200. The interest earned is **33.56**.
/EX

BN: গুণোত্তর ধারা: Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r)। অসীম ধারা যোগফল পায় যখন |r| < 1: S∞ = a/(1 − r)। উদাহরণ: 0.999… = 1, কারণ a = 0.9, r = 0.1। লাফানো বল অসীম বার ওঠানামা করলেও মোট দূরত্ব সসীম।

### Sigma notation
The symbol Σ means "add up". Σₖ₌₁ⁿ f(k) means f(1) + f(2) + … + f(n).

Useful sums:

EQ: Σk = n(n + 1)/2     Σk² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6     Σk³ = (n(n + 1)/2)²

Quick checks: for n = 3, Σk² = 1 + 4 + 9 = 14 and 3 × 4 × 7 ÷ 6 = 14 ✓. For n = 4, Σk³ = 1 + 8 + 27 + 64 = 100 and (4 × 5 ÷ 2)² = 100 ✓.

EX: Telescoping
1. Sum 1/(k(k + 1)) from k = 1 to n. Split: 1/(k(k + 1)) = 1/k − 1/(k + 1).
2. The sum is (1 − 1/2) + (1/2 − 1/3) + … + (1/n − 1/(n + 1)). Almost everything cancels, leaving **1 − 1/(n + 1)**.
3. As n grows, the total tends to **1**.
/EX

EX: Using the formulas
1. Σ (2k + 1) from k = 1 to 10 = 2 × 55 + 10 = **120**.
2. Σ k² from k = 1 to 10 = 10 × 11 × 21 ÷ 6 = **385**.
/EX

TRAP: Using S∞ = a/(1 − r) when |r| ≥ 1. A series such as 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + … does not settle; it grows forever. Another trap is counting terms: from k = 5 to k = 12 there are 12 − 5 + 1 = **8** terms, not 7.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: orbits and Kepler's laws | 35 min

### Circular orbits from two ideas
A satellite in a circular orbit is in free fall around the planet. Gravity is the only force, so it must supply the centripetal force:

G M m ÷ r² = m v² ÷ r

The satellite mass cancels:

EQ: v = √(G M ÷ r)     T = 2π r ÷ v = 2π √(r³ ÷ (G M))

Two things follow. A higher orbit means a **slower** speed (v falls as r grows), and **T² is proportional to r³**.

BN: উপগ্রহ বৃত্তাকার কক্ষপথে ঘোরে কারণ মহাকর্ষই কেন্দ্রমুখী বল: GMm/r² = mv²/r। এতে উপগ্রহের ভর কাটা যায়, ফলে v = √(GM/r)। উঁচু কক্ষপথে গতি কম। পর্যায়কাল T = 2π√(r³/GM), অর্থাৎ T² ∝ r³।

EX: The International Space Station
1. r = 6371 + 400 = 6771 km, GM = 3.986 × 10¹⁴.
2. v = √(3.986 × 10¹⁴ ÷ 6.771 × 10⁶) = √(5.887 × 10⁷) = **7673 m/s** (about 27,600 km/h).
3. T = 2π × 6.771 × 10⁶ ÷ 7673 = **5545 s = 92.4 minutes**. The station circles the Earth about 15.6 times per day.
/EX

EX: A geostationary satellite
1. It must stay above one point of the Earth, so its period equals one rotation of the Earth: T = 86,164 s.
2. r³ = G M T² ÷ 4π² = 3.986 × 10¹⁴ × 7.424 × 10⁹ ÷ 39.48 = 7.497 × 10²², so r = **4.217 × 10⁷ m**.
3. Height above the surface = 42,166 − 6371 = **about 35,800 km**. TV dishes point at these satellites and never need to move.
/EX

EX: Weighing the Sun
1. The Earth orbits at r = 1.496 × 10¹¹ m with T = 3.156 × 10⁷ s.
2. From T² = 4π² r³ ÷ (G M): M = 4π² r³ ÷ (G T²) = 39.48 × 3.348 × 10³³ ÷ (6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ × 9.96 × 10¹⁴) = **1.99 × 10³⁰ kg**.
3. You can weigh a star just by watching a planet go round. The same method weighs Jupiter from the period of its moons.
/EX

### Kepler's three laws
Johannes Kepler found these from decades of data on Mars. Newton later explained them.

1. **Orbits are ellipses** with the Sun at one focus (here is where Tuesday's conics get used).
2. **A line from the Sun to a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times.** A planet moves fastest near the Sun (perihelion) and slowest far away (aphelion). Reason: gravity points to the Sun, so it exerts no torque, so the angular momentum (r × v) stays constant.
3. **T² ∝ a³**, where a is the semi-major axis. In years and astronomical units (AU): T² = a³.

EX: Using the third law
1. Mars has a = 1.524 AU. T = 1.524^1.5 = **1.88 years**.
2. Halley's comet has a period of 76 years, so a = 76^(2/3) = **17.9 AU**.
3. Its closest approach is 0.586 AU, so the farthest point is 2a − 0.586 = 35.9 − 0.586 = **35.3 AU**, beyond Neptune.
/EX

EX: Speeds at the two ends of an orbit
1. Earth's distance from the Sun is 0.983 AU at perihelion and 1.017 AU at aphelion.
2. Equal areas means r × v is constant, so v_perihelion ÷ v_aphelion = 1.017 ÷ 0.983 = **1.035**. The Earth moves about 3.5% faster in January than in July.
/EX

### Escape velocity (preview)
How fast must you throw something upward so that it never comes back? Using energy ideas (which we derive properly in the Work and Energy week), the result is:

EQ: v_esc = √(2 G M ÷ R) = √(2 g R)

EX: Escape
1. Earth: v_esc = √(2 × 9.81 × 6.371 × 10⁶) = √(1.25 × 10⁸) = **11,180 m/s** (about 11.2 km/s).
2. Moon: √(2 × 1.63 × 1.737 × 10⁶) = **2380 m/s**.
3. The escape speed is √2 times the circular-orbit speed at the surface. A planet with a high escape speed keeps its atmosphere. The Moon's low value explains why it has none.
/EX

TRAP: A satellite does not "escape gravity" when orbiting. Gravity is what keeps it in orbit. Also, r in every formula is measured from the **centre** of the planet, so add the planet's radius to the altitude. And a faster satellite does not go to a higher orbit by speeding up in a stable circle: higher orbits have lower speed.
