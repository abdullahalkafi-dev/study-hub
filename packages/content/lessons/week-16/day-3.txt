# The gradient and the direction of steepest climb. Then: field lines and charge distributions
lead: Standing on a hillside, which way is steepest? The answer is a vector you can compute: the gradient. It points uphill, its length is the slope, and it is always perpendicular to the contour line through your feet. This one idea powers heat flow, fluid flow, machine learning (gradient descent) and, in a few days, the link between the electric field and the potential. In physics, you learn to draw electric fields as lines, and to find the field of a rod, a ring and a disk of charge by adding up point charges.

BN: পাহাড়ের গায়ে দাঁড়িয়ে কোন দিকটা সবচেয়ে খাড়া? উত্তরটা একটা ভেক্টর যা হিসাব করা যায়: গ্রেডিয়েন্ট। এটা উপরের দিকে নির্দেশ করে, এর দৈর্ঘ্য হলো ঢাল, আর এটা সবসময় সমোন্নতি রেখার সাথে লম্ব। এই একটা ধারণা তাপ প্রবাহ, তরলের প্রবাহ, মেশিন লার্নিং (gradient descent) এবং কয়েক দিন পর বৈদ্যুতিক ক্ষেত্র ও বিভবের সম্পর্কের ভিত্তি। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে ক্ষেত্রকে রেখা দিয়ে আঁকা শিখবে এবং দণ্ড, বলয় ও চাকতির চার্জের ক্ষেত্র বিন্দু চার্জ যোগ করে বের করবে।

@section math | 2 · Math: the gradient | 55 min

### Definition
The **gradient** of f(x, y) is the vector of partial derivatives

EQ: ∇f = (f_x, f_y)     (and ∇f = (f_x, f_y, f_z) in three dimensions)

The **directional derivative** of f in the direction of a **unit** vector u is the rate of change when you walk that way:

EQ: D_u f = ∇f · u = |∇f| cos θ     (θ = the angle between ∇f and u)

Three facts follow immediately from the cos θ:
1. **Steepest ascent** is along ∇f (θ = 0), with rate **|∇f|**.
2. **Steepest descent** is along −∇f, with rate −|∇f|.
3. **Zero change** (D_u f = 0) is along the directions perpendicular to ∇f, which are the **contour lines**: ∇f is **perpendicular to the level curves**.

BN: ∇f = (f_x, f_y); একক ভেক্টর u বরাবর দিকগত অবকল D_u f = ∇f · u = |∇f| cos θ। সবচেয়ে খাড়া ওঠা ∇f বরাবর (হার |∇f|), সবচেয়ে খাড়া নামা −∇f বরাবর, আর ∇f এর লম্ব দিকে পরিবর্তন শূন্য, মানে সমোন্নতি রেখা বরাবর। অর্থাৎ ∇f সমোন্নতি রেখার লম্ব।

EX: Gradient numbers
1. **f = x² + y² at (1, 2):** ∇f = (2x, 2y) = **(2, 4)**, |∇f| = √20 = **4.472**. The steepest direction is (1, 2) ÷ √5: straight away from the origin.
2. **Walking towards (3, 4):** the unit vector is u = (3, 4) ÷ 5. D_u f = (2 × 3 + 4 × 4) ÷ 5 = 22 ÷ 5 = **4.4**, a bit less than the maximum 4.472.
3. **No change:** perpendicular to ∇f = (2, 4) is (2, −1) ÷ √5: D_u f = (4 − 4) ÷ √5 = **0** (walking along the circle x² + y² = 5).
4. **The hill h = 100 − x² − 2y² at (3, 2):** ∇h = (−2x, −4y) = **(−6, −8)**, |∇h| = **10 m per unit**. Uphill is (−0.6, −0.8): towards the top of the hill at the origin. A raindrop runs **downhill** along (6, 8) ÷ 10 with a slope of 10 m per unit. Walking along the contour (8, −6) ÷ 10 keeps your height.
5. **At 30° from the x-axis**, u = (cos 30°, sin 30°) = (0.866, 0.5): D_u h = −6 × 0.866 − 8 × 0.5 = −5.196 − 4 = **−9.196**.

### The gradient is perpendicular to level curves
EX: Tangent lines and normals
1. **The circle x² + y² = 25 at (3, 4):** f = x² + y² has ∇f = (6, 8) there. The tangent line is the set of points with ∇f · (x − 3, y − 4) = 0: 6 (x − 3) + 8 (y − 4) = 0, i.e. **3x + 4y = 25**.
2. **The ellipse x² + 4y² = 8 at (2, 1):** ∇f = (2x, 8y) = (4, 8). A tangent direction is (−8, 4) (any vector perpendicular to the gradient): (4, 8) · (−8, 4) = −32 + 32 = **0** ✓. The tangent line: 4 (x − 2) + 8 (y − 1) = 0, i.e. **x + 2y = 4**.
3. **Heat flow:** the heat flows from hot to cold along −∇T. For a plate T = 100 − x² − 2y² at (3, 2), −∇T = (6, 8): heat runs **across the isotherms**, never along them, at a strength of **10 °C per unit**. (Fourier's law: heat current = −κ ∇T.)
/EX

BN: ∇f ∥ লম্ব সমোন্নতি রেখার। বৃত্ত x² + y² = 25 এর (3, 4) বিন্দুতে ∇f = (6, 8), স্পর্শক 3x + 4y = 25। তাপ প্রবাহ −∇T বরাবর, সমতাপ রেখা বরাবর কখনোই নয়।

### Gradient descent: walking downhill by calculus
To **minimise** f, take small steps against the gradient: **(x, y) → (x, y) − η ∇f**, where η is the step size (the learning rate). This is how neural networks are trained: f is the error and (x, y) are millions of weights.

EX: Three steps downhill
f = x² + 4y², starting at (4, 2), with η = 0.1. ∇f = (2x, 8y).
1. **Step 1:** ∇f = (8, 16). New point: (4 − 0.8, 2 − 1.6) = **(3.2, 0.4)**. f went from 32 to **10.88**.
2. **Step 2:** ∇f = (6.4, 3.2). New point: (3.2 − 0.64, 0.4 − 0.32) = **(2.56, 0.08)**. f = 6.5536 + 0.0256 = **6.579**.
3. **Step 3:** ∇f = (5.12, 0.64). New point: (2.56 − 0.512, 0.08 − 0.064) = **(2.048, 0.016)**. f = 4.194 + 0.001 = **4.195**.
4. **Observation:** y shrinks fast (the surface is steep in y) and x slowly: the gradient does not point straight at the minimum when the valley is long and narrow. With η too large (say 0.3), the y step overshoots (y → 2 − 0.3 × 16 = −2.8) and the method diverges. This is why learning rates matter.
/EX

TRAP: The gradient is a **vector** with two components, while the directional derivative is a **number**. The direction u must be a **unit vector**: divide (3, 4) by 5 before taking the dot product. And ∇f points in the direction of the **fastest increase** of f, so water runs along −∇h, not +∇h.

### Field lines
We draw the electric field with **field lines**: curves whose tangent at every point is the direction of E. The rules:

1. Lines start on **positive** charges and end on **negative** charges (or go to infinity).
2. The **density** of lines shows the **strength** of the field (more crowded = stronger).
3. Lines **never cross** (E has one direction at a point).
4. Lines meet a conductor's surface at **right angles** (shown on Day 5).

Pictures to remember: a positive point charge: lines radiating outward. Two opposite charges (a dipole): lines leaving + and curving to −. Two equal charges: lines repel and leave a **null point** midway. A uniform field (between two large plates): parallel equally spaced lines.

BN: ক্ষেত্ররেখা: এমন বক্ররেখা যার প্রতিটি বিন্দুতে স্পর্শক E এর দিক। নিয়ম: ধনাত্মক চার্জ থেকে শুরু, ঋণাত্মকে শেষ; রেখার ঘনত্ব = ক্ষেত্রের তীব্রতা; রেখা কখনো কাটাকাটি করে না; পরিবাহীর পৃষ্ঠে লম্বভাবে মেশে। দুই সমান ধনাত্মক চার্জের মাঝে শূন্য ক্ষেত্র বিন্দু (null point)।

### Adding up a continuous distribution
For charge spread over a line, a ring or a disk, cut it into tiny pieces dq, find the field of each (k dq ÷ r²), and add the **components** (symmetry cancels many of them). Charge densities: λ = charge per length, σ = charge per area.

EX: A charged rod
A rod of length L = 0.4 m carries Q = 10 nC uniformly (λ = Q ÷ L = 25 nC/m). At a point on its **perpendicular bisector**, a distance y = 0.1 m away:
1. **Symmetry:** the components along the rod cancel; only the component perpendicular to the rod survives.
2. **The integral** (adding all pieces) gives **E = k Q ÷ (y √(y² + (L/2)²))**.
3. **Numbers:** √(0.01 + 0.04) = 0.2236. E = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 10⁻⁸ ÷ (0.1 × 0.2236) = **4020 N/C**, perpendicular to the rod.
4. **The infinite line:** for L → ∞ with the same λ the formula becomes **E = 2 k λ ÷ y** = 2 × 8.99 × 10⁹ × 2.5 × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 0.1 = **4495 N/C**. The finite rod's value is 89% of this, because its ends are far enough away to matter.
/EX

EX: A ring and a disk
1. **Ring (Day 2):** on the axis E = k Q z ÷ (z² + a²)^(3/2). The field is zero at the centre (z = 0, symmetry) and has its **maximum 0.385 k Q ÷ a²** at z = a ÷ √2. For Q = 10 nC and a = 5 cm that maximum is 0.385 × 8.99 × 10⁹ × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 0.0025 = **13,840 N/C**, at z = **3.54 cm**.
2. **Disk:** radius R, surface density σ. On the axis at distance z: **E = (σ ÷ 2ε₀) [1 − z ÷ √(z² + R²)]**, with σ ÷ 2ε₀ = 5.65 × 10⁴ N/C for σ = 1 μC/m². At R = 10 cm, z = 5 cm: 56,497 × (1 − 0.05 ÷ 0.1118) = 56,497 × 0.5528 = **31,230 N/C**.
3. **The infinite sheet:** for R → ∞ (or z ≪ R) the bracket → 1 and **E = σ ÷ 2ε₀ = 56,500 N/C**, independent of z: a uniform field, the same on both sides, pointing away from a positive sheet. Two opposite sheets (a parallel-plate capacitor) give E = σ ÷ ε₀ between them and zero outside. We will derive this in two lines with Gauss's law tomorrow.
4. **Far away:** for z = 1 m the disk's formula gives 56,497 × (1 − 1 ÷ √1.01) = **280.4 N/C**, and a point charge with the disk's Q = σ π R² = 31.4 nC gives 282.4 N/C: from far away the disk looks like a point.
/EX

TRAP: Do not add the **magnitudes** of the pieces: add the **components**. On the perpendicular bisector of a rod only the component along y survives. And formulas like 2 k λ ÷ y are for an **infinite** line: a real rod is shorter and its field is smaller (89% in our example).
