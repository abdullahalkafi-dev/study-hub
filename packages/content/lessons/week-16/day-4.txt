# Maxima, minima and saddle points. Then: charges moving in electric fields and dipoles in fields
lead: Engineers constantly ask "what is the best?": the cheapest box, the best-fit line, the lowest-energy shape. For functions of two variables the answer comes from the same idea as one variable (the slope is zero), plus a new twist: a flat point can be a bottom, a top or a saddle, and the second-derivative test (which secretly uses last week's eigenvalues) tells which. In physics, you put charges and dipoles in an electric field and follow what happens: electrons steered in a screen, ink drops steered in a printer, and the water molecules in your microwave oven.

@section math | 2 · Math: maxima, minima and saddle points | 55 min

### Critical points
A function f(x, y) can have a local maximum or minimum only where the surface is flat: **∇f = 0**, that is **f_x = 0 and f_y = 0** at the same time. Such a point is a **critical point**. At a critical point the surface can be a bowl bottom (**minimum**), a hilltop (**maximum**) or a **saddle** (up in one direction, down in another, like a mountain pass).

### The second-derivative test
At a critical point compute the second partials and the **discriminant**

EQ: D = f_xx f_yy − (f_xy)²

- **D > 0 and f_xx > 0:** local **minimum**.
- **D > 0 and f_xx < 0:** local **maximum**.
- **D < 0:** **saddle** point.
- **D = 0:** the test says nothing (look at the function directly).

The reason: D is the **determinant of the Hessian matrix** H = [[f_xx, f_xy], [f_xy, f_yy]]. H is symmetric, so it has real eigenvalues (last week). **Both eigenvalues positive** means curving up in every direction: a minimum. Both negative: a maximum. **Opposite signs:** a saddle. (And D is the product of the eigenvalues.)

BN: সংকট বিন্দু: ∇f = 0 (f_x = 0 ও f_y = 0)। D = f_xx f_yy − f_xy² (Hessian ম্যাট্রিক্সের নির্ণায়ক)। D > 0 ও f_xx > 0 হলে ক্ষুদ্রতম, D > 0 ও f_xx < 0 হলে বৃহত্তম, D < 0 হলে জিনবিন্দু। কারণ: Hessian প্রতিসম, তার eigenvalue দুটো ধনাত্মক হলে সব দিকে উপরে বাঁকা (min), দুটো ঋণাত্মক হলে max, বিপরীত চিহ্ন হলে saddle।

EX: Classifying critical points
1. **f = x² + y² − 2x − 4y + 5.** f_x = 2x − 2 = 0 and f_y = 2y − 4 = 0: the point **(1, 2)**. f_xx = 2, f_yy = 2, f_xy = 0, D = 4 > 0 and f_xx > 0: a **minimum**, f = 1 + 4 − 2 − 8 + 5 = **0**.
2. **f = x³ − 3x + y².** f_x = 3x² − 3 = 0 gives x = ±1, f_y = 2y = 0 gives y = 0. Points (1, 0) and (−1, 0). f_xx = 6x, f_yy = 2, f_xy = 0. At (1, 0): D = 6 × 2 = 12 > 0, f_xx = 6 > 0: **minimum**, f = −2. At (−1, 0): D = −6 × 2 = −12 < 0: **saddle**, f = 2.
3. **f = x³ + y³ − 3xy.** f_x = 3x² − 3y = 0 and f_y = 3y² − 3x = 0. From the first y = x²; then x⁴ = x, so x = 0 or x = 1: points (0, 0) and (1, 1). f_xx = 6x, f_yy = 6y, f_xy = −3. At (0, 0): D = 0 − 9 = −9 < 0: **saddle**. At (1, 1): D = 36 − 9 = 27 > 0, f_xx = 6 > 0: **minimum**, f = 1 + 1 − 3 = **−1**. The Hessian there is [[6, −3], [−3, 6]] with eigenvalues 9 and 3, both positive ✓.
4. **f = x² − y²:** the origin has D = 2 × (−2) = −4 < 0: **saddle**. **f = −x² − y²:** D = 4 > 0, f_xx = −2 < 0: **maximum**.
5. **f = x² + xy + y² − 3x:** f_x = 2x + y − 3 = 0 and f_y = x + 2y = 0 give x = 2, y = −1. D = 2 × 2 − 1 = 3 > 0 and f_xx = 2 > 0: **minimum**, f = 4 − 2 + 1 − 6 = **−3**.

### Optimisation in the real world
EX: Best box and best line
1. **The cheapest open box.** An open-top box (square base x by y, height z) must hold 4 m³. Material: A = x y + 2 z (x + y). With z = 4 ÷ (x y): A = x y + 8 ÷ y + 8 ÷ x. A_x = y − 8 ÷ x² = 0 and A_y = x − 8 ÷ y² = 0 give x = y and x³ = 8: **x = y = 2 m, z = 1 m**, A = 4 + 4 + 4 = **12 m²**. Check: A_xx = 16 ÷ x³ = 2, A_yy = 2, A_xy = 1, D = 3 > 0 and A_xx > 0: a **minimum** ✓. (The best open box has a height of half its side.)
2. **The best-fit line (least squares).** Fit y = m x + c to the points (0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 4). Minimise S (m, c) = Σ (y − m x − c)². Setting S_m = 0 and S_c = 0 gives the **normal equations**: 5 m + 3 c = 10 and 3 m + 3 c = 7. Subtracting: 2 m = 3, **m = 1.5**, **c = 0.833**. The residuals are 0.167, −0.333, 0.167 and S = **0.167**. The Hessian of S is [[10, 6], [6, 6]] with D = 24 > 0: a minimum (eigenvalues 14.3 and 1.7).
3. **The soda can.** A can of volume 355 cm³ with the smallest area A = 2π r² + 2V ÷ r: A′ = 4π r − 2V ÷ r² = 0 gives r³ = V ÷ 2π = 56.5, **r = 3.84 cm**, **h = V ÷ π r² = 7.67 cm = 2r**, A = **277.5 cm²** (real cans are a little taller, for manufacturing reasons).

### Closed regions
On a closed bounded region the largest and smallest values occur either at a **critical point inside** or on the **boundary**. Check both and compare. For example, f = x y on the square 0 ≤ x, y ≤ 2: the only interior critical point is (0, 0) (on the boundary), the maximum **4** is at the corner (2, 2) and the minimum **0** is along the two axes.

TRAP: If D = 0 the test is **inconclusive**, it does not mean "saddle". For f = x⁴ + y⁴ the origin has D = 0 and is still a minimum. Also remember a critical point is only a **local** extremum; the global one may be on the boundary or elsewhere. And a saddle in two variables is not rare: it is where the minimum along one direction meets the maximum along another.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: charges and dipoles in electric fields | 35 min

### A charge in a uniform field
A charge q in a field E has F = q E and a = q E ÷ m, constant. It follows a **parabola**, exactly like a ball in gravity, but with the direction set by the sign of the charge.

EX: An electron between two plates
An electron enters horizontally at v₀ = 3 × 10⁷ m/s between plates of length 5 cm with a vertical field of 2000 N/C.
1. **Acceleration:** a = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2000 ÷ 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ = **3.52 × 10¹⁴ m/s²** (gravity is negligible: 10¹³ times smaller).
2. **Time between the plates:** t = 0.05 ÷ 3 × 10⁷ = **1.67 × 10⁻⁹ s**.
3. **Deflection:** y = ½ a t² = 0.5 × 3.52 × 10¹⁴ × (1.67 × 10⁻⁹)² = **4.9 × 10⁻⁴ m = 0.49 mm**.
4. **Exit direction:** v_y = a t = 5.86 × 10⁵ m/s and the angle is tan⁻¹ (5.86 × 10⁵ ÷ 3 × 10⁷) = **1.12°**. This is how old oscilloscope and TV tubes steer the beam: change E to move the spot.

### A dipole in a field
In a uniform field E a dipole p feels **no net force** (the +q and −q forces cancel) but a **torque** that tries to line it up with E:

EQ: τ = p E sin θ     U = − p E cos θ     (θ = the angle between p and E)

The energy is lowest (−pE) when p is parallel to E (θ = 0) and highest (+pE) when it is antiparallel (θ = 180°).

EX: Dipoles in action
1. **A pair:** q = 2 nC separated by d = 1 cm, so p = 2 × 10⁻¹¹ C m, in E = 5000 N/C. The maximum torque (at 90°) is p E = **10⁻⁷ N m**, and the energy swing between aligned and anti-aligned is 2 p E = **2 × 10⁻⁷ J**.
2. **A water molecule** (p = 6.2 × 10⁻³⁰ C m) in E = 10⁶ N/C: τ_max = **6.2 × 10⁻²⁴ N m**, and p E = 6.2 × 10⁻²⁴ J. Compare with the thermal energy at 300 K: k T = 1.381 × 10⁻²³ × 300 = 4.14 × 10⁻²¹ J. The ratio is p E ÷ k T = **0.0015**: thermal jiggling beats the alignment, and only about 0.1% of the molecules are lined up.
3. **The microwave oven:** the field E oscillates at 2.45 GHz. The water dipoles in food try to follow it, rotate back and forth, collide with their neighbours and turn the energy into heat. (Ice, whose molecules are locked in a lattice, heats poorly, which is why frozen food is heated unevenly.)
4. **A dipole in a non-uniform field** also feels a **net force** (it is pulled towards the strong field): this is why a charged comb picks up neutral bits of paper.

TRAP: In a **uniform** field, a dipole has torque but **no net force**; only in a non-uniform field is there a force too. The energy U = −p E cos θ is lowest when the dipole **lines up with the field**. And when you classify critical points, first make sure ∇f = 0 at the point, a nonzero D alone means nothing.

- Critical point: ∇f = 0. Hessian test: D = f_xx f_yy − f_xy²: D > 0 and f_xx > 0 minimum, D > 0 and f_xx < 0 maximum, D < 0 saddle, D = 0 inconclusive. D = λ₁ λ₂ of the Hessian. Least squares: normal equations give the best line, always a minimum. Optimum box has height half its side; optimum can has h = 2r.
- Charge in a uniform field: a = qE ÷ m, parabola. Dipole in a field: τ = pE sin θ, U = −pE cos θ, no net force in a uniform field. Water dipoles and the microwave oven.
