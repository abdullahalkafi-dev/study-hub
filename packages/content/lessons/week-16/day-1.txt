# Functions of two variables and partial derivatives. Then: electric charge and Coulomb's law
lead: So far every function had one input. The real world has many: the pressure of a gas depends on temperature and volume, the height of a hill depends on east and north, the temperature of a plate depends on x and y. To handle this you change one variable at a time and freeze the others: that is a partial derivative. In physics you start electricity with the simplest law of all, Coulomb's law: the force between two charges. It looks just like gravity, and it is about 10³⁹ times stronger.

BN: এ পর্যন্ত প্রতিটি ফাংশনে একটাই ইনপুট ছিল। বাস্তবে অনেক: গ্যাসের চাপ নির্ভর করে তাপমাত্রা ও আয়তনের উপর, পাহাড়ের উচ্চতা পূর্ব ও উত্তরের উপর, পাতের তাপমাত্রা x ও y এর উপর। এটা সামলাতে একবারে একটা চলক বদলাও, বাকিগুলো স্থির রাখো: একেই আংশিক অবকল বলে। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে বিদ্যুৎ শুরু সবচেয়ে সরল সূত্র দিয়ে: কুলম্বের সূত্র, দুটো চার্জের মধ্যে বল। দেখতে মাধ্যাকর্ষণের মতো, কিন্তু প্রায় 10³⁹ গুণ শক্তিশালী।

@section math | 2 · Math: functions of two variables | 55 min

### Seeing a function of two variables
A function f(x, y) gives one number for every point (x, y) of the plane. Its graph z = f(x, y) is a **surface** over the plane. Drawing surfaces is hard, so we use **level curves** (contour lines on a map): the curve f(x, y) = c for several values of c.

EX: Reading level curves
1. **f(x, y) = x² + y².** The level curve f = c is a circle of radius √c. The surface is a bowl (a paraboloid) with its lowest point at the origin.
2. **f(x, y) = x² + 4y².** For f = 4: x² ÷ 4 + y² ÷ 1 = 1, an **ellipse** with semi-axes 2 and 1. The bowl is steeper in the y direction, so the contours are squeezed along y.
3. **f(x, y) = x² − y².** For f = 1: a hyperbola. The surface is a **saddle**: it goes up along x and down along y (the same hyperbolas as the quadratic forms last week).
4. **A hill:** h(x, y) = 100 − x² − 2y² (metres; x and y in units of 10 m). At (0, 0) the height is **100 m**; at (3, 2): 100 − 9 − 8 = **83 m**. Contour h = 50: x² + 2y² = 50, an ellipse.
/EX

BN: z = f(x, y) একটা তল। চিত্র আঁকা কঠিন বলে সমোন্নতি রেখা (level curve) ব্যবহার করি: f(x, y) = c। x² + y² এর জন্য বৃত্ত, x² + 4y² এর জন্য উপবৃত্ত, x² − y² এর জন্য অধিবৃত্ত (জিন-আকৃতি)।

### Partial derivatives
To see how f changes with x alone, **freeze y** like a constant and differentiate with respect to x:

EQ: ∂f/∂x = lim (h → 0) [f(x + h, y) − f(x, y)] ÷ h     ∂f/∂y = lim (h → 0) [f(x, y + h) − f(x, y)] ÷ h

Geometrically, ∂f/∂x is the **slope of the surface** when you walk in the x direction (the slope of the slice y = constant). We also write f_x and f_y.

EX: Computing partial derivatives
1. **f = x² y.** f_x = 2xy (y is a constant) and f_y = x². At (2, 3): **f_x = 12** and **f_y = 4**. So near (2, 3), f = 12 grows by about 12 per unit of x and by about 4 per unit of y.
2. **f = x³ + 3xy² − 2y.** f_x = 3x² + 3y² and f_y = 6xy − 2.
3. **f = sin (xy).** f_x = y cos (xy), f_y = x cos (xy) (the chain rule, treating the other variable as constant).
4. **f = e^(x² + y²).** f_x = 2x e^(x² + y²) and f_y = 2y e^(x² + y²).
5. **Check by the definition:** for f = x² y at (2, 3) with h = 0.001: [f (2.001, 3) − f (2, 3)] ÷ 0.001 = (3 × 4.004001 − 12) ÷ 0.001 = 0.012003 ÷ 0.001 = **12.003**, very close to 12 ✓.
/EX

### Why the order does not matter, and equations from physics
For nice functions the **mixed partials are equal**: f_xy = f_yx (differentiate in either order).

EX: Mixed partials and a physical law
1. **f = x³ y² + sin (xy).** f_x = 3x² y² + y cos (xy). Then f_xy = 6x² y + cos (xy) − xy sin (xy). Going the other way: f_y = 2x³ y + x cos (xy), f_yx = 6x² y + cos (xy) − xy sin (xy). **They agree** ✓.
2. **The ideal gas:** P = n R T ÷ V. For n = 1 mol, T = 300 K, V = 0.025 m³: P = 8.314 × 300 ÷ 0.025 = **99,768 Pa**. ∂P/∂T = nR ÷ V = **332.6 Pa/K** (heat the gas at constant volume). ∂P/∂V = −nRT ÷ V² = **−3.99 × 10⁶ Pa/m³** (squeeze it at constant temperature). So one extra kelvin raises the pressure by 333 Pa, and one extra litre (0.001 m³) lowers it by about 3990 Pa.
3. **Laplace's equation:** u = x² − y² satisfies u_xx + u_yy = 2 + (−2) = **0**. Functions like this describe the steady temperature of a plate and (next weeks) the electric potential where there is no charge.
4. **A hill:** h = 100 − x² − 2y². At (3, 2): h_x = −2x = **−6** and h_y = −4y = **−8**. Walking east you descend 6 m per unit, walking north 8 m per unit.
/EX

BN: মিশ্র আংশিক অবকল সমান: f_xy = f_yx। আদর্শ গ্যাসে P = nRT/V: ∂P/∂T = nR/V (স্থির আয়তনে গরম করা), ∂P/∂V = −nRT/V² (স্থির তাপমাত্রায় চাপা)। u = x² − y² এর u_xx + u_yy = 0 (ল্যাপলাসের সমীকরণ)।

TRAP: When you differentiate with respect to x, the other variable is a **constant**, not zero: the derivative of x² y with respect to x is 2xy, not 2x. And in sin (xy) the chain rule still applies: the extra factor is y (or x), the derivative of the inside.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: electric charge and Coulomb's law | 35 min

### Charge
Matter contains two kinds of **electric charge**, called positive and negative. Like charges **repel**, unlike charges **attract**. Charge is measured in **coulombs (C)**. Three facts you must know:

- Charge is **conserved**: it is never created or destroyed, only moved.
- Charge is **quantised**: it comes in multiples of e = **1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C** (the electron has −e, the proton +e).
- Objects are normally neutral. Rubbing moves electrons from one surface to another, and the objects end up charged.

BN: দুই ধরনের চার্জ: ধনাত্মক ও ঋণাত্মক। সমজাতীয় বিকর্ষণ করে, বিপরীত আকর্ষণ করে। একক কুলম্ব (C)। চার্জ সংরক্ষিত (সৃষ্টি বা ধ্বংস হয় না), কোয়ান্টায়িত (e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C এর গুণিতক)। ঘষলে ইলেকট্রন এক পৃষ্ঠ থেকে অন্যটিতে যায়।

### Coulomb's law
For two point charges q₁ and q₂ a distance r apart, the force on each has magnitude

EQ: F = k |q₁ q₂| ÷ r²     k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m²/C² = 1 ÷ (4π ε₀)     (ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N m²))

The force acts **along the line** joining the charges: away from each other if the charges have the same sign, towards each other if opposite.

EX: First numbers
1. **Two 1 μC charges, 10 cm apart:** F = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (10⁻⁶)² ÷ (0.1)² = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 10⁻¹² ÷ 0.01 = **0.899 N**, about the weight of a 90 g object.
2. **Double the distance:** F falls to a quarter: **0.225 N**. Triple it: one ninth.
3. **A hydrogen atom:** the electron and proton are 5.29 × 10⁻¹¹ m apart. F = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹)² ÷ (5.29 × 10⁻¹¹)² = **8.24 × 10⁻⁸ N**. The gravitational force between them is only 3.63 × 10⁻⁴⁷ N. The ratio is **2.3 × 10³⁹**: gravity is utterly negligible inside atoms.
4. **How big is a coulomb?** Two 1 C charges 1 m apart would repel with 8.99 × 10⁹ N, the weight of nearly a million tonnes. A coulomb is a huge amount of charge; everyday static charges are micro- or nanocoulombs.
/EX

### Superposition: many charges
The force on a charge from several others is the **vector sum** of the individual forces. Coulomb forces do not interfere with each other.

EX: Charges on a line
Charges +2 μC at x = 0 and −3 μC at x = 0.2 m. A +1 μC test charge is placed:
1. **Between them, at x = 0.1 m:** the +2 μC repels it towards +x with 8.99 × 10⁹ × 2 × 10⁻¹² ÷ 0.01 = **1.798 N**. The −3 μC attracts it also towards +x with 8.99 × 10⁹ × 3 × 10⁻¹² ÷ 0.01 = **2.697 N**. Total **4.495 N towards +x** (the forces add because they point the same way).
2. **Beyond the negative charge, at x = 0.3 m:** from +2 μC (0.3 m away) a push of 8.99 × 10⁹ × 2 × 10⁻¹² ÷ 0.09 = **0.200 N** towards +x. From −3 μC (0.1 m away) a pull of **2.697 N** towards −x. Net: **0.200 − 2.697 = −2.497 N**, i.e. 2.50 N towards −x.
3. **Where is the net force zero?** It must be outside the pair, on the side of the **smaller** charge: at x = −d. The two forces balance when 2 ÷ d² = 3 ÷ (d + 0.2)², so (d + 0.2) ÷ d = √1.5 = 1.2247 and d = 0.2 ÷ 0.2247 = **0.890 m**. The force is zero at x = −0.890 m (the equilibrium point is unstable).
/EX

BN: কয়েকটি চার্জের বলের ভেক্টর যোগফলই মোট বল। রেখার উপরে দুটো চার্জের মাঝখানে বলগুলো একই দিকে, তাই যোগ হয়; বাইরে বিপরীত দিকে, তাই বিয়োগ। শূন্য বলের বিন্দু ছোট চার্জের দিকে বাইরে।

### Static shock: your own capacitor
Walking on a carpet can leave your body at about **10 kV** with a capacitance of about **100 pF** (10⁻¹⁰ F). A capacitor holds Q = C V and energy ½ C V².

EX: The door handle
1. **Charge:** Q = C V = 10⁻¹⁰ × 10⁴ = **10⁻⁶ C = 1 μC**.
2. **Energy:** ½ C V² = 0.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 10⁸ = **5 × 10⁻³ J = 5 mJ**: enough for a sharp sting, nowhere near dangerous.
3. **The spark gap:** air breaks down at about 3 × 10⁶ V/m, so 10 kV jumps a gap of 10⁴ ÷ (3 × 10⁶) = **3.3 mm**. You feel the shock when your finger is still a few millimetres from the handle, and a lower voltage gives a shorter spark.
4. **Number of electrons moved:** 10⁻⁶ ÷ 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **6.2 × 10¹² electrons**, a tiny fraction of the ~10²⁸ in your body.

TRAP: Coulomb's law gives a **force vector**: the formula F = k q₁ q₂ ÷ r² gives only the **size** if you drop the signs. Decide the direction from the signs (like repel, unlike attract) and add forces as **vectors**. And remember r is the distance between the **centres** in the denominator, squared: doubling r divides F by 4, not by 2.
