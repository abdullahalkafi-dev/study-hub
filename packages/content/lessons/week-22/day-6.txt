@section math | 2 · Part A: transforms, sampling and the FFT (14 marks, about 55 min) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 11
P: (1 mark) The transform of e^(−3t) for t > 0 is 1 ÷ (3 + iω). Find its size and phase at ω = 4. => |F| = 1 ÷ √(9 + 16) = **0.2** and the phase is **−atan (4 ÷ 3) = −53.1°**.
P: (1 mark) A rectangular pulse lasts 0.5 ms. Where is the first zero of its spectrum? => At f = 1 ÷ τ = **2 kHz**.
P: (1 mark) A signal is delayed by 5 ms. Find the phase change at 40 Hz (in degrees). => −2π × 40 × 0.005 = −1.257 rad = **−72°**; the size does not change.
P: (1 mark) Audio up to 4.5 kHz modulates a carrier of 810 kHz (AM). Find the band occupied and the bandwidth. => **805.5 to 814.5 kHz**, a bandwidth of **9 kHz**.
P: (2 marks) A system obeys y′ + 200 y = x (t). Find its transfer function and the gain and phase at ω = 150 rad/s. => H = **1 ÷ (200 + iω)** (1 mark). |H| = 1 ÷ √(200² + 150²) = 1 ÷ 250 = **0.004** and the phase is **−atan (0.75) = −36.9°** (1 mark).
P: (1 mark) For the pulse e^(−at), what fraction of the energy lies below ω = 10 a? (Use atan 10 ≈ 1.471.) => (2 ÷ π) × 1.471 = **0.937 (93.7%)**.
P: (1 mark) A rectangular pulse of height 1 and width 2 ms is convolved with itself. Find the shape, base and peak height of the result. => A **triangle** with base **4 ms** and peak **2 ms** (in units of height² × time).
P: (2 marks) A sound plus its echo, y = x (t) + 0.6 x (t − 0.5 ms). Find the largest and smallest gain, the spacing of the notches and the frequency of the first notch. => Gain between **1.6** and **0.4** (1 mark). The comb spacing is 1 ÷ T = **2 kHz** and the first notch (cos = −1) is at **1 kHz** (1 mark).
P: (1 mark) A 13 kHz tone is sampled at 20 kHz. What frequency appears, and what is the smallest sampling rate for a signal limited to 8 kHz? => The tone is above 10 kHz, so it appears at 20 − 13 = **7 kHz**; the sampling rate must be **above 16 kHz**.
P: (2 marks) A 2048-point DFT is taken at 48 kHz. Find the bin spacing and the record length. => Spacing 48000 ÷ 2048 = **23.4 Hz** (1 mark); length 2048 ÷ 48 kHz = **42.7 ms** (1 mark).
P: (1 mark) Find the speed-up of the FFT over the DFT for N = 4096. => N ÷ log₂ N = 4096 ÷ 12 = **341**.

CHECK: math

@section physics | 3 · Part B: induction, Maxwell and the grid (6 marks, about 35 min) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 12 to 17
P: (1 mark) A rod of length 0.6 m moves at 5 m/s across a 0.5 T field in a circuit of 3 Ω. Find the EMF, the current and the braking force. => ε = 0.5 × 0.6 × 5 = **1.5 V**; I = **0.5 A**; F = B I ℓ = 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.6 = **0.15 N** (power 0.75 W = I² R ✓).
P: (1 mark) A generator has 200 turns of area 0.02 m² in a 0.3 T field and turns at 3000 rpm. Find the peak EMF, the RMS value and the frequency (2 poles). => ω = 314.2 rad/s; ε_peak = 200 × 0.3 × 0.02 × 314.2 = **377 V**; RMS = **266.6 V**; f = **50 Hz**.
P: (1 mark) A transformer steps 11 kV up to 400 kV with 500 turns on the primary. Find the secondary turns and, for 50 MW, both currents. => N_s = 500 × 400 ÷ 11 = **18,182 turns**; I_s = 50 × 10⁶ ÷ 400,000 = **125 A**; I_p = 50 × 10⁶ ÷ 11,000 = **4545 A**.
P: (1 mark) The 400 kV line (single-phase equivalent) has R = 8 Ω. Find the loss and its percentage. => **125² × 8 = 125 kW**, which is **0.25%** of 50 MW.
P: (1 mark) A capacitor with circular plates of radius 4 cm is charged by 1.5 A. Find B inside at r = 2 cm and outside at r = 8 cm. => Inside B = μ₀ I r ÷ (2π R²) = 2 × 10⁻⁷ × 1.5 × 0.02 ÷ 0.0016 = **3.75 μT**. Outside B = μ₀ I ÷ (2π r) = 2 × 10⁻⁷ × 1.5 ÷ 0.08 = **3.75 μT** (a coincidence of these numbers, the formulas are different).
P: (1 mark) Sunlight of 800 W/m² falls on a surface. Find E₀ and B₀, and the wavelength of a 98 MHz FM radio wave. => E₀ = √(2 × 800 ÷ (8.854 × 10⁻¹² × 3 × 10⁸)) = **776 V/m**; B₀ = **2.59 μT**; λ = 3 × 10⁸ ÷ 98 × 10⁶ = **3.06 m**.

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | An integral from Parseval
P: The pulse f (t) = 1 for |t| < 1 has the transform F (ω) = 2 sin ω ÷ ω. Use Parseval to find the integral of (sin ω ÷ ω)² over all ω. => ∫ f² dt = 2. Parseval: 2 = (1 ÷ 2π) ∫ 4 sin² ω ÷ ω² dω, so ∫ sin² ω ÷ ω² dω = **π**. No amount of ordinary calculus will give this easily.

LVL: Challenge | Why a falling grid frequency is dangerous for a transformer
P: A transformer with 1150 primary turns and core area 10 cm² runs on 230 V RMS at 50 Hz. Find the peak core flux density, and the value if the frequency falls to 40 Hz at the same voltage. => V_peak = 325.3 V; Φ_peak = V_peak ÷ (2π f N) = 325.3 ÷ (2π × 50 × 1150) = 9.0 × 10⁻⁴ Wb, so B_peak = Φ ÷ A = **0.90 T**. At 40 Hz, B scales as 1 ÷ f: **1.125 T**. Silicon steel saturates at about 1.7 to 2 T, and designers keep the normal value well below it, because near saturation the magnetising current rises sharply and the transformer overheats. A grid operator disconnects loads long before this happens.
