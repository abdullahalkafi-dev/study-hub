# Spectrograms and compression. Then: electromagnetic waves and the power station
lead: Music changes with time, so a single Fourier transform of a whole song is useless: it says which notes occur but not when. The answer is the spectrogram, a picture made of many short windowed FFTs, and it is the first step of speech recognition, music identification and MP3 compression. In physics, Maxwell's equations produce a wave, and we learn its properties: speed, ratio of E to B, energy and spectrum. Then we follow the whole chain from the coal in a power station to the socket in your wall, using every idea of this week.

BN: গান সময়ের সাথে বদলায়, তাই পুরো গানের একটি ফুরিয়ে রূপান্তর শুধু বলে কোন সুর আছে, কখন আছে বলে না। সমাধান spectrogram: অনেক ছোট window এর FFT দিয়ে আঁকা ছবি। এটাই speech recognition, গান শনাক্তকরণ ও MP3 compression এর প্রথম ধাপ। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে ম্যাক্সওয়েলের সমীকরণ থেকে তরঙ্গ পাই, এর বৈশিষ্ট্য দেখি, তারপর কয়লা থেকে ঘরের সকেট পর্যন্ত পুরো শৃঙ্খল অনুসরণ করি।

@section math | 2 · Math: spectrograms and compression | 55 min

### The time-frequency picture
Cut the signal into short frames of length T, multiply each by a window (Hann), take the FFT, and place the magnitudes side by side: time along one axis, frequency along the other, brightness for the strength. This is the **short-time Fourier transform** and its picture is the spectrogram.

There is an unavoidable trade-off. A frame of length T has a bin spacing of about Δf = 1 ÷ T:

EQ: Δt × Δf ≈ 1

A short window locates events in time but blurs the pitch. A long window gives sharp pitch but blurs when it happened. (This is the same duration-bandwidth rule as Day 1, and it is also the Heisenberg uncertainty principle of quantum mechanics.)

BN: সংকেতকে ছোট ফ্রেমে (দৈর্ঘ্য T) কেটে প্রতিটিকে window দিয়ে গুণ করে FFT করি, তারপর পাশাপাশি সাজাই: একদিকে সময়, অন্যদিকে কম্পাঙ্ক, উজ্জ্বলতা শক্তি। অনিবার্য ভারসাম্য: Δt × Δf ≈ 1। ছোট window সময় ঠিক বলে কিন্তু সুর ঝাপসা, বড় window উল্টো।

EX: Reading a spectrogram
1. **Frame of 1024 samples at 44.1 kHz:** T = 23.2 ms and Δf = **43 Hz**. One second of sound gives about **43 frames**.
2. **A chirp** (a bird, or a sliding siren) sweeping from 1 kHz to 3 kHz in 1 s moves at 2000 Hz/s. During a frame of 23 ms it moves 46 Hz, about one bin. The best window length for a chirp of rate r is T = 1 ÷ √r = 1 ÷ √2000 = **22 ms**, which is why the standard 1024-sample frame suits speech and birdsong.
3. **A word spoken:** vowels last 100 ms and have pitch lines at 100–250 Hz; consonants like "s" are broadband noise above 4 kHz lasting 50 ms. A window of 20 to 30 ms captures both.
4. **Mains hum:** a faint horizontal line at 50 Hz (and its multiples 100, 150 Hz) in a recording means electrical interference from the mains. A notch filter removes it.
/EX

### Compression
Many signals have a spectrum whose energy is concentrated in a few coefficients. Keep these, throw the rest away, and the file shrinks while the signal barely changes. For the decaying pulse e^(−at) of Day 2, the energy below ω₀ is (2 ÷ π) atan (ω₀ ÷ a):
- 90% of the energy lies below **6.3 a**, 99% below **63.7 a**, 99.9% below **637 a**.

MP3 and AAC go further: a model of hearing (**masking**) says a weak tone near a loud one cannot be heard, so it is not stored, and the bits are given first to the frequency bands where the ear is most sensitive.

BN: অনেক সংকেতের শক্তি কয়েকটি সহগে কেন্দ্রীভূত; সেগুলো রাখি, বাকি ফেলে দিই, ফাইল ছোট হয় অথচ সংকেত প্রায় একই থাকে। e^(−at) এর জন্য ৯০% শক্তি 6.3a এর নিচে, ৯৯% 63.7a এর নিচে। MP3 কানের মাস্কিং মডেলও ব্যবহার করে: জোরালো সুরের পাশে দুর্বল সুর শোনা যায় না, তাই রাখা হয় না।

EX: How much can we save?
1. **A pure pulse e^(−at)** with a = 1000 rad/s: 99% of the energy is below ω = **63,700 rad/s** (10.1 kHz). If we sample at 44.1 kHz (Nyquist 22.05 kHz), we keep about **46%** of the coefficients: a ratio of about 2.2 : 1.
2. **CD audio:** 44,100 × 16 bits × 2 channels = **1411 kbit/s**. MP3 at 128 kbit/s is a ratio of **11 : 1**.
3. **Speech for a phone:** only 300 to 3400 Hz is kept, sampled at 8 kHz with 8 bits: **64 kbit/s**, a ratio of 22 : 1 against CD audio.
4. **A picture:** JPEG applies the same idea to 8 × 8 blocks of pixels (a two-dimensional cosine transform) and drops the weak high-frequency coefficients.
/EX

TRAP: A spectrogram cannot be sharp in both directions. A longer window is not "better": it blurs fast events. Compression that throws data away (lossy) cannot be undone, and each re-compression adds new errors. A high compression ratio does not mean high quality: it depends on how much of the discarded part the listener could hear.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: electromagnetic waves and the power station | 35 min

### The wave
Combining Faraday and Ampère-Maxwell in empty space gives a wave equation for E and B moving at c. For a plane wave moving along x,

EQ: E = E₀ sin (kx − ωt),   B = B₀ sin (kx − ωt),   E₀ = c B₀,   c = f λ

E and B are at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel, in phase, and carry energy at the rate **S = E B ÷ μ₀** (the Poynting vector). The average intensity is

EQ: I = ½ ε₀ c E₀²

BN: ফ্যারাডে ও অ্যাম্পিয়ার-ম্যাক্সওয়েল মিলিয়ে শূন্যে তরঙ্গ সমীকরণ। E ও B পরস্পর ও চলার দিকের সাথে লম্ব, একই দশায়, E₀ = cB₀, c = fλ। শক্তির প্রবাহ S = EB/μ₀ (Poynting), গড় তীব্রতা I = ½ε₀cE₀²।

EX: Waves around us
1. **Sunlight at the ground:** I ≈ 1000 W/m². E₀ = √(2 I ÷ (ε₀ c)) = **868 V/m** and B₀ = E₀ ÷ c = **2.9 μT**.
2. **A 1 W phone transmitter at 10 m** spread over a sphere: I = 1 ÷ (4π × 100) = **0.80 mW/m²**; E₀ = **0.77 V/m** and B₀ = **2.6 nT**. Tiny, yet an antenna can detect it.
3. **Wavelengths** λ = c ÷ f: mains 50 Hz → **6000 km**; FM radio 100 MHz → **3.0 m** (a quarter-wave antenna of 75 cm); Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz → **12.5 cm**; visible light 700 nm (red) → f = **4.3 × 10¹⁴ Hz**, 400 nm (violet) → **7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz**.
4. **The spectrum** is only one family: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays. They differ in frequency, nothing else.
/EX

### The power station
Now follow the energy:
1. **Fuel to heat.** Burning coal at 24 MJ/kg heats steam to 550 °C. A real plant turns about 38% of the heat into electricity (the Carnot limit between 823 K and 300 K would be 63.5%, from the thermal weeks). For **1000 MW** of electricity: heat 1000 ÷ 0.38 = **2630 MW**, so the coal is 2630 × 10⁶ ÷ 24 × 10⁶ = **110 kg/s ≈ 395 tonnes per hour**.
2. **Turbine to generator.** The steam turns the turbine at 3000 rpm, driving a 2-pole generator (Day 2): f = 50 Hz.
3. **Three phases.** Three coils 120° apart give voltages V sin ωt, V sin (ωt − 120°), V sin (ωt − 240°). Their sum is **zero** at every instant, so a balanced load needs no return wire and the total power is perfectly **constant**, not pulsing, which is why big motors run smoothly. The line-to-line voltage is √3 × phase voltage: 230 V × 1.732 = **398 V ≈ 400 V**. The three-phase power is P = √3 V_L I_L cos φ.
4. **Step-up** to 400 kV (Day 3). At 25 kV with power factor 0.9 the current for 1000 MW would be 1000 × 10⁶ ÷ (√3 × 25,000 × 0.9) = **25,660 A**; at 400 kV it is **1604 A** per phase.
5. **Home.** Step-down transformers: 400 kV → 33 kV → 11 kV → 400 V three-phase → 230 V single-phase to each house.

BN: শক্তির পথ: কয়লা (24 MJ/kg) → বাষ্প 550 °C → টারবাইন 3000 rpm → জেনারেটর 50 Hz। তিন ফেজ: তিনটি কয়েল 120° ব্যবধানে, যোগফল সর্বদা শূন্য, মোট ক্ষমতা ধ্রুব। লাইন ভোল্টেজ = √3 × ফেজ ভোল্টেজ = 400 V। ক্ষমতা P = √3 V_L I_L cos φ। 400 kV এ ট্রান্সমিশন, তারপর ধাপে ধাপে নামিয়ে 230 V।

EX: A balanced three-phase load
1. A factory machine draws 10 A in each phase of a 230 V per phase supply with power factor 0.8: P = 3 × 230 × 10 × 0.8 = **5.52 kW**. Check with the line formula: √3 × 398 × 10 × 0.8 = **5.52 kW** ✓.
2. At the power factor 0.8 the current needed for the same power is 25% larger than at 1.0 (Week 20, power-factor correction).
3. **The frequency tells the grid's health.** All generators are locked to the same 50 Hz: if demand exceeds supply, the turbines slow down and the frequency sags. A drop to 49.5 Hz is a signal to bring more power in, or to shed load.
/EX

TRAP: E and B in a wave are **in phase** (unlike current and voltage in an inductor). The intensity uses the amplitude E₀ with the factor ½ (for the average of sin²). Line voltage and phase voltage differ by √3 for a star connection, not 3. Do not confuse the generator's 2-pole speed with the number of phases: three phases mean three separate coils.
