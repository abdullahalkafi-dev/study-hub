# Line integrals and conservative fields. Then: potential from the field, and the field from the potential
lead: A line integral adds up a field along a curve: the work a force does on a moving object, the voltage drop along a path. For some fields the answer depends only on the end points, not on the road: these are conservative fields, and they have a potential. Gravity and the electric field of fixed charges are of this kind, which is why potential energy and voltage make sense at all. Friction and swirling flows are not. Today you learn to test for it, and in physics you use the link both ways: V from E by integrating, E from V by taking the gradient.

BN: রেখা যোগজ কোনো বক্ররেখা বরাবর ক্ষেত্র যোগ করে: চলন্ত বস্তুর উপর বলের কাজ, পথ বরাবর ভোল্টেজ পতন। কিছু ক্ষেত্রে উত্তর শুধু শুরু ও শেষ বিন্দুর উপর নির্ভর করে, পথের উপর নয়: এগুলো সংরক্ষণশীল ক্ষেত্র, আর এদের বিভব আছে। মাধ্যাকর্ষণ ও স্থির চার্জের বৈদ্যুতিক ক্ষেত্র এই ধরনের, তাই স্থিতিশক্তি ও ভোল্টেজের অর্থ আছে। ঘর্ষণ ও ঘূর্ণি প্রবাহ নয়। আজ এটা পরীক্ষা করা শিখবে, আর পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে সম্পর্কটা দুই দিকে ব্যবহার করবে: E থেকে V যোগজ করে, V থেকে E গ্রেডিয়েন্ট নিয়ে।

@section math | 2 · Math: line integrals | 55 min

### Definition
Describe a curve C by a position r (t) = (x (t), y (t)) for a ≤ t ≤ b. The **line integral of a vector field** F = (P, Q) along C is

EQ: ∫_C F · dr = ∫ₐᵇ F (r (t)) · r′ (t) dt = ∫ (P dx + Q dy)

It is the **work** done by the force F along the path. The integral changes sign if you go the other way. (The line integral of a scalar function, for example the mass of a wire with density λ, is ∫ λ ds with ds = |r′| dt, the arc length.)

BN: বক্ররেখা C: r(t) = (x(t), y(t)), a ≤ t ≤ b। রেখা যোগজ ∫ F · dr = ∫ F(r(t)) · r′(t) dt = ∫(P dx + Q dy), মানে F বল দিয়ে পথ বরাবর কাজ। উল্টো দিকে গেলে চিহ্ন বদলায়। তারের ভর ∫λ ds (ds = |r′| dt)।

EX: Work along paths
1. **F = (y, x) from (0, 0) to (1, 1) along the straight line** r = (t, t): F = (t, t), r′ = (1, 1): F · r′ = 2t. ∫₀¹ 2t dt = **1**.
2. **The same field along the parabola** y = x²: r = (t, t²), F = (t², t), r′ = (1, 2t): F · r′ = t² + 2t² = 3t². ∫₀¹ 3t² dt = **1**. The **same answer** along a different path.
3. **F = (−y, x) once round the unit circle** r = (cos t, sin t), 0 ≤ t ≤ 2π: F = (−sin t, cos t), r′ = (−sin t, cos t): F · r′ = 1. The integral is **2π**: a swirl always pushes along.
4. **The same field from (1, 0) to (−1, 0)** along the x-axis: r = (1 − 2t, 0), F = (0, 1 − 2t), r′ = (−2, 0): F · r′ = 0, so the integral is **0**; along the upper semicircle it is π. **Different answers** for different paths between the same end points.
5. **A wire with density λ = 1 + x kg/m along the x-axis from 0 to 2:** M = ∫₀² (1 + x) dx = **4 kg**.
/EX

### Conservative fields
A field is **conservative** if ∫ F · dr depends only on the end points. Equivalent statements:

1. F = ∇f for some function f (the **potential function**), and then **∫_C F · dr = f (B) − f (A)** (the fundamental theorem for line integrals).
2. The integral around **every closed loop** is zero.
3. In two dimensions, for nice regions: **∂P/∂y = ∂Q/∂x** (the cross-derivatives agree, as in Week 16: f_xy = f_yx).

BN: সংরক্ষণশীল ক্ষেত্র: ∫F·dr শুধু প্রান্ত বিন্দুর উপর নির্ভর করে। সমতুল্য: F = ∇f (বিভব ফাংশন), তখন ∫F·dr = f(B) − f(A); প্রতিটি বদ্ধ লুপে যোগজ শূন্য; দুই মাত্রায় ∂P/∂y = ∂Q/∂x।

EX: Testing and finding potentials
1. **F = (y, x):** ∂P/∂y = 1 = ∂Q/∂x ✓ conservative, with f = x y. (That is why both paths gave 1 = f (1, 1) − f (0, 0).)
2. **F = (−y, x):** ∂P/∂y = −1 but ∂Q/∂x = +1 ✗ **not** conservative. The "curl" ∂Q/∂x − ∂P/∂y = **2** measures the swirl.
3. **F = (2xy, x²):** ∂P/∂y = 2x = ∂Q/∂x ✓, f = x² y. From (0, 0) to (2, 1): f = 4 − 0 = **4**, along any path.
4. **Finding f for F = (2xy + 3, x² − 1).** Integrate P in x: f = x² y + 3x + g (y). Differentiate in y and match Q: x² + g′ (y) = x² − 1, so g = −y. **f = x² y + 3x − y**. From (0, 0) to (1, 2): f = 2 + 3 − 2 = **3**.
5. **Gravity near the Earth** F = (0, −m g) = ∇ (−m g y): the work from (0, 0) to (3, 4) is −m g × 4. For m = 2 kg: **−78.4 J**, whatever the path, and the potential energy is U = −f = m g y.
6. **Friction is not conservative:** its work depends on the length of the path: a longer path wastes more energy.
7. **A closed loop:** for F = (x², y²), ∂P/∂y = 0 = ∂Q/∂x, so the circulation around any closed curve is **0**, and f = (x³ + y³) ÷ 3 gives **3** from (0, 0) to (1, 2).
/EX

TRAP: For a conservative field you may choose the **easiest path** between the end points (for example straight lines). For a non-conservative field you must follow the **given** path. The test ∂P/∂y = ∂Q/∂x works on a region without holes: the field (−y, x) ÷ (x² + y²) passes the test but circulates 2π around the origin, because of the hole at the origin. And the line integral of F · dr is a **number** (work), not a vector.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: potential and the field | 35 min

### From field to potential
The electric force is conservative, so the potential difference between two points is the **line integral** of the field:

EQ: V (B) − V (A) = −∫_A^B E · dl

and, the other way round,

EQ: E = −∇V = (−∂V/∂x, −∂V/∂y, −∂V/∂z)

The field points **downhill** in potential, perpendicular to the **equipotential** surfaces (V = constant), with strength equal to the steepness. (This is exactly Week 16's gradient: V plays the part of the height of a hill, and −∇V is the direction a ball would roll.) The unit V/m = N/C.

BN: V(B) − V(A) = −∫E·dl এবং E = −∇V। ক্ষেত্র বিভবের ঢালের দিকে (নিচের দিকে), সমবিভব তলের সাথে লম্ব, মান = ঢালের তীব্রতা। V হলো পাহাড়ের উচ্চতা, −∇V হলো বল গড়ানোর দিক।

EX: Both directions
1. **Uniform field:** E = 5000 V/m across a gap of 2 cm: ΔV = E d = **100 V**. Between the plates V changes linearly, and equipotentials are planes parallel to the plates. A 12 V battery across a gap of 1 mm makes **12,000 V/m**.
2. **Point charge:** V = kQ ÷ r gives E = −dV/dr = kQ ÷ r² ✓. The equipotentials are spheres.
3. **Field from a given potential:** V = 5x² − 2xy (volts). E = −∇V = (−10x + 2y, 2x). At (1, 2): **E = (−6, 2) V/m**, |E| = **6.32 V/m**.
4. **Infinite line (λ = 25 nC/m):** E = 2kλ ÷ r gives V (r) = −2kλ ln r + constant. The difference between r = 0.1 m and r = 0.2 m is 2kλ ln 2 = 449.5 × 0.693 = **311.6 V**. (There is no zero at infinity for an infinite line, so we measure differences.)
5. **A ring on its axis:** V = kQ ÷ √(z² + a²). For Q = 10 nC, a = 5 cm, z = 12 cm: V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 0.13 = **691.5 V**. Then E = −dV/dz = kQ z ÷ (z² + a²)^(3/2) = **4910 N/C**, exactly the value found by adding up the field pieces in Week 16, but with one derivative instead of a vector integral.
6. **A disk on its axis:** V = (σ ÷ 2ε₀) [√(z² + R²) − z]. For σ = 1 μC/m², R = 0.1 m, z = 0.05 m: V = 56,497 × (0.1118 − 0.05) = **3492 V**, and −dV/dz = (σ ÷ 2ε₀)(1 − z ÷ √(z² + R²)) = **31,230 N/C** ✓.
/EX

### Conductors and equipotentials
EX: Real potentials
1. **A conductor is an equipotential.** A charged sphere (R = 0.1 m, Q = 10 nC) is at V = kQ ÷ R = **899 V** everywhere on and inside it. A uniformly charged **insulating** sphere has V (r ≤ R) = kQ (3R² − r²) ÷ 2R³ : at the centre 1.5 kQ ÷ R = **1349 V**, at r = 5 cm **1236 V**, at the surface **899 V**. (Derived by integrating E = kQ r ÷ R³ inwards from the surface.)
2. **Van de Graaff generator:** a sphere of radius 15 cm at 100 kV carries Q = V R ÷ k = 10⁵ × 0.15 ÷ 8.99 × 10⁹ = **1.67 μC**. The sparks begin at V = E R = 3 × 10⁶ × 0.15 = **450 kV**.
3. **Work along an equipotential is zero:** moving a charge along a surface of constant V costs no energy, because the field is perpendicular to the displacement (E · dl = 0).
4. **A cell membrane:** a potential difference of 70 mV across a thickness of 7 nm gives E = 0.07 ÷ 7 × 10⁻⁹ = **10⁷ V/m**, three times the breakdown field of air. Membranes survive because they are not air, and the field is what drives the ion channels in your nerves.
5. **A uniform field in two dimensions:** E = (100, 50) V/m. Going from (0, 0) to (3, 4): ΔV = −E · Δr = −(300 + 200) = **−500 V**.
6. **Lightning:** a cloud base 2 km above the ground in an average field of 5 × 10⁴ V/m has ΔV = E d = **10⁸ V** relative to the ground (tomorrow's energy estimate).
/EX

TRAP: E = −∇V has a **minus sign**: the field points from high to low potential. Potential difference is **path independent** only because the electrostatic field is conservative; changing magnetic fields (Week 22) break this. And remember V = 0 is a **choice**: only differences matter, so a conductor at 899 V and the Earth at 0 V can both be "grounded" depending on where you set zero.

### Recap
- Line integral ∫ F · dr = ∫ F (r (t)) · r′ (t) dt (work). Conservative: F = ∇f, ∫ = f (B) − f (A), zero on loops, ∂P/∂y = ∂Q/∂x (on regions without holes). The curl ∂Q/∂x − ∂P/∂y measures the swirl. Gravity conservative, friction not.
- V (B) − V (A) = −∫ E · dl, E = −∇V (downhill, ⟂ equipotentials). Plates: E = V ÷ d. Line: ΔV = 2kλ ln (r₂ ÷ r₁). Ring: kQ ÷ √(z² + a²). Conductor: one V. Sphere: kQ ÷ R. Lightning: Q ΔV.
