# Triple integrals, cylindrical and spherical coordinates. Then: electric potential and energy
lead: Add one more variable and you can add up over a solid: its volume, its mass, its centre of mass, its moment of inertia. Cylinders and balls are awkward in x, y, z but easy in cylindrical and spherical coordinates, which match their symmetry. In physics, you stop thinking about forces and think about energy: electric potential is the energy per unit charge, a single number at each point that holds the same information as the whole field. It explains batteries, voltage and how fast a particle moves after a voltage boost.

BN: আরেকটা চলক যোগ করলে কঠিন বস্তুর উপর যোগ করা যায়: আয়তন, ভর, ভরকেন্দ্র, জড়তা ভ্রামক। নলাকার ও গোলক x, y, z এ কঠিন, কিন্তু নলাকার ও গোলীয় স্থানাঙ্কে সহজ, কারণ এরা প্রতিসাম্যের সাথে মেলে। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে বলের বদলে শক্তি নিয়ে ভাববে: বিভব হলো প্রতি একক চার্জে শক্তি, প্রতি বিন্দুতে একটা সংখ্যা যা পুরো ক্ষেত্রের তথ্য ধরে রাখে। এটা ব্যাটারি, ভোল্টেজ এবং ভোল্টেজ পাওয়ার পর কণা কত দ্রুত চলে তা বোঝায়।

@section math | 2 · Math: triple integrals | 55 min

### Triple integrals
For a function f (x, y, z) over a solid region E, ∭_E f dV adds f × (tiny volume). With f = 1 it gives the **volume**; with a density ρ it gives the **mass** M = ∭ ρ dV. In Cartesian coordinates dV = dx dy dz, and the integral is three nested single integrals.

EX: A box
∭ x y z dV over the cube [0, 1]³ = (∫₀¹ x dx)(∫₀¹ y dy)(∫₀¹ z dz) = (1/2)³ = **1/8**. The volume of a box 2 × 3 × 4 is ∭ 1 dV = **24**.
/EX

### Cylindrical and spherical coordinates
Choose coordinates that match the shape:

| System | Coordinates | Volume element dV |
| Cartesian | (x, y, z) | dx dy dz |
| Cylindrical | (r, θ, z): x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ | **r dr dθ dz** |
| Spherical | (ρ, φ, θ): x = ρ sin φ cos θ, y = ρ sin φ sin θ, z = ρ cos φ | **ρ² sin φ dρ dφ dθ** |

(φ is the angle from the z-axis, 0 to π; θ is the angle around, 0 to 2π.)

BN: আকৃতির সাথে মেলে এমন স্থানাঙ্ক নাও। নলাকার: dV = r dr dθ dz। গোলীয়: dV = ρ² sin φ dρ dφ dθ (φ = z-অক্ষ থেকে কোণ, 0 থেকে π)। বাড়তি r ও ρ² sin φ কখনো ভুলো না।

EX: Volumes and masses
1. **A cylinder (radius R, height h):** ∫₀^{2π} ∫₀^R ∫₀^h r dz dr dθ = 2π × R²/2 × h = **π R² h**. For R = 2, h = 5: **62.83**.
2. **A ball of radius R:** ∫₀^{2π} ∫₀^π ∫₀^R ρ² sin φ dρ dφ dθ = (2π)(2)(R³/3) = **4π R³ ÷ 3**. For R = 2: **33.51**.
3. **A cone** (base radius R, height h): the radius at height z is R (1 − z ÷ h), so V = ∫₀^h π R² (1 − z ÷ h)² dz = π R² h ÷ 3. For R = 3 and h = 4: **37.70**, a third of the cylinder.
4. **A ball with density ρ = ρ₀ (1 − ρ ÷ R)** (denser in the centre): M = ∫₀^R 4π ρ² ρ₀ (1 − ρ ÷ R) dρ = 4π ρ₀ (R³/3 − R³/4) = **π ρ₀ R³ ÷ 3**. For R = 1 m and ρ₀ = 1000 kg/m³: **1047 kg**, a quarter of a uniform ball of density 1000 (4189 kg).
5. **The Earth's mean density:** V = 4π (6.371 × 10⁶)³ ÷ 3 = 1.083 × 10²¹ m³, M = 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, so mean ρ = **5511 kg/m³**. The surface rock is only 2700 kg/m³: the core must be much denser.
6. **A hemisphere's centre of mass** (R, z ≥ 0): ∭ z dV = ∫₀^{2π} ∫₀^{π/2} ∫₀^R ρ cos φ ρ² sin φ dρ dφ dθ = 2π × (1/2) × R⁴/4 = π R⁴/4. The volume is 2π R³/3. So **z̄ = 3R ÷ 8**. For R = 0.4 m: **0.15 m**.
/EX

### Moments of inertia from integrals
EX: Solid sphere and cylinder
1. **A solid cylinder (mass M, radius R) about its axis:** I = ∭ ρ_m r² dV = ρ_m ∫₀^{2π} ∫₀^R ∫₀^h r³ dz dr dθ = ρ_m (2π h R⁴ ÷ 4) = ½ (ρ_m π R² h) R² = **½ M R²**. For M = 3 kg and R = 0.1 m: **0.015 kg m²**.
2. **A solid sphere about a diameter:** the distance from the z-axis is ρ sin φ, so I = ρ_m ∭ ρ² sin²φ · ρ² sin φ dρ dφ dθ = ρ_m (R⁵ ÷ 5)(2π)(4/3) = 8π ρ_m R⁵ ÷ 15. With M = 4π ρ_m R³ ÷ 3 this is **I = (2/5) M R²** (using ∫₀^π sin³φ dφ = 4/3). For M = 5 kg and R = 0.2 m: **0.08 kg m²**.
3. These are exactly the numbers in Week 8's table: they were derived from integrals like these.
/EX

TRAP: Do not forget the **extra factors** in dV: r in cylindrical and ρ² sin φ in spherical coordinates. In spherical coordinates the angle φ runs from 0 to π (from the north pole to the south pole), not to 2π. And a variable ρ (distance from the origin) is not the density ρ: this is why we write the density as ρ_m or σ or λ when you use spherical coordinates.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: electric potential and energy | 35 min

### Potential energy of charges
Bring a charge q from far away (where U = 0) to a distance r from a charge Q. The work you do is the **electric potential energy**:

EQ: U = k Q q ÷ r     (positive for like charges, negative for opposite charges)

**Electric potential** is the potential energy **per unit charge**: V = U ÷ q. For a point charge Q, and for any number of charges (it is a **scalar**, so you add numbers, not vectors):

EQ: V = k Q ÷ r     V = Σ k Qᵢ ÷ rᵢ     (unit: volt, 1 V = 1 J/C)

The work done by the field when a charge q moves from A to B is **W = q (V_A − V_B)**.

BN: স্থিতিশক্তি U = kQq/r (অসীম থেকে আনার কাজ)। বিভব V = U/q = kQ/r, একক ভোল্ট (V = J/C)। বিভব স্কেলার, তাই সংখ্যা যোগ করো, ভেক্টর নয়। ক্ষেত্র বিন্দু A থেকে B তে q সরালে কাজ W = q(V_A − V_B)।

EX: Potential numbers
1. **A 10 nC charge:** at 0.1 m V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 0.1 = **899 V**; at 0.3 m **299.7 V**. At 0.5 m for 2 nC: **35.96 V**.
2. **Work to bring 1 nC in** to 0.1 m: U = q V = 10⁻⁹ × 899 = **8.99 × 10⁻⁷ J**.
3. **Moving from A (r = 0.1 m) to B (r = 0.3 m)** a 2 nC charge in the field of 10 nC: ΔV = 899 − 299.7 = 599.3 V and the field does W = q ΔV = 2 × 10⁻⁹ × 599.3 = **1.20 × 10⁻⁶ J** (positive: like charges repel it outward).
4. **Opposite charges:** +2 nC at x = 0 and −2 nC at x = 0.2 m. At (0.3, 0): V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 2 × 10⁻⁹ (1 ÷ 0.3 − 1 ÷ 0.1) = 17.98 × (−6.667) = **−119.9 V**. At the midpoint V = **0** (the two terms cancel) although E ≠ 0 there: zero potential does not mean zero field.
5. **Four equal charges** of 1 nC at the corners of a square of side 0.2 m: at the centre (0.1414 m from each) V = 4 × 8.99 × 10⁹ × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 0.1414 = **254 V**, while the field there is **0** (the four fields cancel). Potential adds as numbers, fields as vectors.
6. **Energy of a configuration:** three 1 μC charges at the corners of an equilateral triangle of side 0.3 m: U = 3 × k q² ÷ a = 3 × 8.99 × 10⁹ × 10⁻¹² ÷ 0.3 = **0.0899 J**. A charged sphere (Q = 1 μC, R = 0.1 m) has energy k Q² ÷ 2R = **0.045 J**.

### The electron-volt and energy conservation
A charge q moved through a potential difference ΔV gains kinetic energy **ΔKE = q ΔV**. The **electron-volt** is the energy of one electron through one volt: **1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J**.

EX: Speeds from voltages
1. **An electron through 500 V:** KE = 500 eV = 8.01 × 10⁻¹⁷ J, so v = √(2 × 8.01 × 10⁻¹⁷ ÷ 9.109 × 10⁻³¹) = **1.33 × 10⁷ m/s** (4.4% of the speed of light: classical physics is still fine).
2. **A proton through 10 kV:** v = √(2 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 10⁴ ÷ 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷) = **1.38 × 10⁶ m/s**. A proton is 1836 times heavier, so it is about 43 times slower than an electron at the same energy.
3. **The hydrogen atom:** U = −k e² ÷ r = −8.99 × 10⁹ × (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹)² ÷ 5.29 × 10⁻¹¹ = −4.36 × 10⁻¹⁸ J = **−27.2 eV**. The orbiting electron has KE = +13.6 eV (half of |U|), so the total is **−13.6 eV**: the energy to ionise hydrogen.
4. **Rutherford's experiment:** an α-particle (charge 2e) with 5 MeV heads straight at a gold nucleus (charge 79e). It stops at distance r where k (2e)(79e) ÷ r = 5 MeV: r = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 158 × (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹)² ÷ (5 × 10⁶ × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹) = **4.55 × 10⁻¹⁴ m = 45.5 fm**. That is much smaller than an atom (10⁻¹⁰ m) and showed that the positive charge sits in a tiny nucleus.
5. **Two 3 μC charges 0.2 m apart** hold U = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 9 × 10⁻¹² ÷ 0.2 = **0.405 J**. Released, two 0.1 kg masses fly apart each with KE = 0.2023 J, so v = √(2 × 0.2023 ÷ 0.1) = **2.01 m/s**.

TRAP: Potential is a **scalar**: add the numbers with their **signs** (V of a negative charge is negative). Zero potential does not mean zero field (the midpoint of a dipole), and zero field does not mean zero potential (the centre of the square). Be careful that U = k Q q ÷ r is the energy of the **pair**, and do not count it twice when you add up the energy of many charges: sum over pairs.
