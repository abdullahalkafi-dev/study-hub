# Double integrals. Then: electric flux and Gauss's law
lead: A single integral adds up a quantity along a line. A double integral adds up a quantity over a region of a plane: the mass of a plate whose density varies, the volume under a surface, the average temperature of a sheet. You compute it as two ordinary integrals, one after the other. In physics, you meet the most powerful tool of electrostatics: Gauss's law. It says the total electric flux through any closed surface depends only on the charge inside, and it turns many hard field problems into one line of algebra.

BN: একটা যোগজ রেখা বরাবর কোনো রাশি যোগ করে। দ্বিঘাত যোগজ সমতলের একটা অঞ্চলের উপর কোনো রাশি যোগ করে: পরিবর্তনশীল ঘনত্বের পাতের ভর, তলের নিচের আয়তন, পাতের গড় তাপমাত্রা। হিসাব হয় দুটো সাধারণ যোগজ পরপর করে। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে তড়িৎস্থিতিবিদ্যার সবচেয়ে শক্তিশালী হাতিয়ার: গাউসের সূত্র। এটা বলে যেকোনো বদ্ধ তলের ভেতর দিয়ে মোট বৈদ্যুতিক ফ্লাক্স শুধু ভেতরের চার্জের উপর নির্ভর করে, আর এতে অনেক কঠিন ক্ষেত্রের সমস্যা এক লাইনের বীজগণিতে নামে।

@section math | 2 · Math: double integrals | 55 min

### From Riemann sums to a double integral
Cut a rectangle R = [a, b] × [c, d] into small cells of area ΔA. In each cell take the value of f, multiply by ΔA, and add. As the cells shrink the sum tends to the **double integral**

EQ: ∬_R f (x, y) dA = lim Σ f (xᵢ, yⱼ) ΔA

If f ≥ 0 this is the **volume** under the surface z = f (x, y) above R. If f = 1 it is the **area** of R.

### Computing it: iterated integrals
**Fubini's theorem:** for a rectangle you may integrate in either order. Do the inner integral first, treating the other variable as a constant, then the outer one:

EQ: ∬_R f dA = ∫ₐᵇ [ ∫_c^d f (x, y) dy ] dx = ∫_c^d [ ∫ₐᵇ f (x, y) dx ] dy

BN: আয়তক্ষেত্রকে ছোট ঘরে ভাগ করে প্রতিটিতে f × ΔA যোগ করলে দ্বিঘাত যোগজ। f ≥ 0 হলে এটা তলের নিচের আয়তন; f = 1 হলে ক্ষেত্রফল। হিসাব: ভিতরের যোগজ আগে (অন্য চলক ধ্রুবক), তারপর বাইরের। আয়তক্ষেত্রে ক্রম বদলানো যায় (Fubini)।

EX: Four double integrals
1. **∬ x y dA over [0, 2] × [0, 3].** Inner: ∫₀³ x y dy = x × 9/2 = 4.5 x. Outer: ∫₀² 4.5 x dx = 4.5 × 2 = **9**. Since f = x · y separates, it is also (∫₀² x dx)(∫₀³ y dy) = 2 × 4.5 = 9 ✓.
2. **∬ (x² + y) dA over [0, 1] × [0, 2].** Inner: ∫₀² (x² + y) dy = 2x² + 2. Outer: ∫₀¹ (2x² + 2) dx = 2/3 + 2 = **8/3 = 2.667**.
3. **Volume under z = 4 − x − y over [0, 2] × [0, 2].** ∫₀² ∫₀² (4 − x − y) dy dx. Inner: 4 × 2 − 2x − 2 = 6 − 2x. Outer: ∫₀² (6 − 2x) dx = 12 − 4 = **8**. The **average height** is 8 ÷ area 4 = **2**.
4. **Average temperature.** A square plate −1 ≤ x, y ≤ 1 has T = 100 − x² − 2y². ∬ T dA = 100 × 4 − ∬ x² dA − 2 ∬ y² dA = 400 − 4/3 − 8/3 = **396**. Average = 396 ÷ 4 = **99 °C** (hotter at the centre, cooler at the edges).
/EX

### Uses
EX: What a double integral measures
1. **Mass of a plate** with surface density σ (x, y): M = ∬ σ dA. A plate [0, 2] × [0, 1] m with σ = 1 + x kg/m²: ∫₀¹ ∫₀² (1 + x) dx dy = ∫₀¹ (2 + 2) dy = **4 kg**.
2. **Centre of mass:** x̄ = (1 ÷ M) ∬ x σ dA. For the plate above: ∬ x (1 + x) dA = ∫₀² (x + x²) dx × 1 = 2 + 8/3 = 14/3, so x̄ = 14/3 ÷ 4 = **1.167 m** (right of centre 1 m, since the right side is heavier).
3. **Probability:** a probability density p (x, y) satisfies ∬ p dA = 1. (This is the setup of the normal distribution in Phase 4.)
4. **Swapping the order** is a trick: the same number, an easier path. For example ∫₀¹ ∫ₓ¹ e^(y²) dy dx cannot be done in this order, but over the same triangle (0 ≤ x ≤ y ≤ 1) it equals ∫₀¹ ∫₀^y e^(y²) dx dy = ∫₀¹ y e^(y²) dy = (e − 1) ÷ 2 = **0.859**.
/EX

TRAP: In an iterated integral the **limits of the inner integral may depend on the outer variable** (for non-rectangular regions, tomorrow), but the limits of the outer integral must be **constants**. When integrating in y, x is a constant: pull it out in front. And the order only changes freely if the region is described correctly in the new order.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: electric flux and Gauss's law | 35 min

### Electric flux
The **flux** of a field through a surface counts how much field "passes through" it. For a flat surface of area A in a uniform field E, with θ the angle between E and the **normal** to the surface:

EQ: Φ = E A cos θ = E · A     (unit: N m²/C)

For a curved surface in a varying field, split the surface into small patches and add: **Φ = ∬ E · dA**. This is the double integral of today applied to a surface.

EX: Flux numbers
1. **A rectangle 0.2 m × 0.3 m in E = 500 N/C at 60° to the normal:** Φ = 500 × 0.06 × cos 60° = **15 N m²/C**.
2. **Face-on (θ = 0):** Φ = 500 × 0.06 = **30**. **Edge-on (θ = 90°):** Φ = **0**.
3. **A sphere of radius r around a point charge Q.** E = kQ ÷ r² is the same at every point and points along the outward normal, so Φ = E × 4π r² = (kQ ÷ r²)(4π r²) = **4π k Q = Q ÷ ε₀**. The radius **cancels**: the flux does not depend on how big the sphere is.
/EX

### Gauss's law
The remarkable fact is that the result above holds for **any** closed surface, of any shape, and any arrangement of charges:

EQ: Φ_net = ∯ E · dA = Q_enclosed ÷ ε₀     (ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N m²))

Why: the field lines that leave a closed surface are the lines that start on the charges inside. Charges **outside** send in as many lines as go out: net flux zero. With the inverse-square law (1 ÷ r²) and the area of a sphere (r²), the numbers cancel perfectly: this is a mathematical consequence of the inverse-square form. Gauss's law is **Coulomb's law in a different form**, and it is one of the four Maxwell equations.

BN: তড়িৎ ফ্লাক্স Φ = E A cos θ (একক N m²/C)। গাউসের সূত্র: যেকোনো বদ্ধ তলের জন্য Φ_net = Q_ভিতরে/ε₀। বিন্দু চার্জকে ঘিরে গোলকে Φ = (kQ/r²)(4πr²) = 4πkQ = Q/ε₀, r কেটে যায়। তলের বাইরের চার্জ যতটা ঢোকায় ততটাই বের করে, নেট শূন্য। এটা কুলম্বের সূত্রেরই অন্য রূপ, ম্যাক্সওয়েলের চার সমীকরণের একটি।

EX: Gauss's law numbers
1. **A closed surface around 2 nC:** Φ = 2 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 8.85 × 10⁻¹² = **226 N m²/C**.
2. **A cube with the 2 nC at its centre.** By symmetry each of the 6 faces carries the same flux: 226 ÷ 6 = **37.7 N m²/C** per face. (Computing that by integrating E over one face would be a hard double integral.)
3. **Charge outside:** a 5 nC charge outside a closed box gives Φ_net = **0**. The lines go in on one side and out on the other.
4. **Find the charge from the flux:** if the net flux through a surface is −1130 N m²/C, then Q = ε₀ Φ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × (−1130) = **−1.0 × 10⁻⁸ C = −10 nC**.
5. **Two charges, +5 nC and −2 nC, inside one surface:** Φ = (5 − 2) × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 8.85 × 10⁻¹² = **339 N m²/C**. Only the **net** enclosed charge matters.
/EX

TRAP: Gauss's law uses the **net** charge inside the surface and the **net** flux through the **whole closed** surface. It does not say the field is zero when the flux is zero (charges outside, or a dipole, can leave the field nonzero with Φ = 0). And the area vector points **outward** from a closed surface, so flux leaving is positive.

LVL: Challenge | Why 1 ÷ r² matters
P: Suppose a force law were 1 ÷ r³. Would the flux through a sphere around a point charge depend on its radius? What does that say about Gauss's law? => E ∝ 1 ÷ r³, area ∝ r², so Φ ∝ **1 ÷ r**: it would shrink as the sphere grows. Gauss's law (flux independent of the surface) holds **only for the inverse-square law**. So Gauss's law and the exponent 2 in Coulomb's law stand or fall together (tested to better than 1 part in 10¹⁶).

### Recap
- ∬ f dA = iterated integral (inner first, other variable constant, Fubini for rectangles). Area, volume, mass M = ∬ σ dA, centre of mass x̄ = ∬ x σ dA ÷ M, average = ∬ f dA ÷ area. Swapping the order can make a hard integral easy.
- Flux Φ = ∬ E · dA = E A cos θ. Gauss: Φ_net = Q_enc ÷ ε₀ for any closed surface. Point charge: 4πkQ = Q ÷ ε₀. Cube: Φ ÷ 6 per face. Outside charge: no net flux. Needs the inverse square law.

BN: আজকের সারকথা: ∬ f dA = পুনরাবৃত্ত যোগজ (ভিতরেরটা আগে)। ভর M = ∬σ dA, ভরকেন্দ্র x̄ = ∬xσ dA/M, গড় = ∬f dA/ক্ষেত্রফল। ফ্লাক্স Φ = E·A = EA cos θ; গাউস: Φ_net = Q_ভিতরে/ε₀। বিন্দু চার্জে 4πkQ; ঘনকের প্রতি তলে Φ/6; বাইরের চার্জে নেট শূন্য; বিপরীত বর্গ নিয়মের উপর নির্ভর।
