@section math | 2 · Part A: Multiple integrals and vector calculus (14 marks, about 55 min) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 10
P: (1 mark) Evaluate ∬ (x + 2y) dA over [0, 2] × [0, 1]. => ∫₀¹ ∫₀² (x + 2y) dx dy = ∫₀¹ (2 + 4y) dy = 2 + 2 = **4**.
P: (1 mark) Find the area between y = x² and y = x by a double integral. => ∫₀¹ (x − x²) dx = 1/2 − 1/3 = **1/6**.
P: (1 mark) Evaluate ∬ y dA over the triangle 0 ≤ y ≤ x, 0 ≤ x ≤ 2. => ∫₀² x²/2 dx = (1/2)(8/3) = **4/3**.
P: (2 marks) Evaluate ∬ (x² + y²) dA over the disk of radius 2 using polar coordinates. => ∫₀^{2π} ∫₀² r² · r dr dθ (1 mark, including the extra r) = 2π × 16/4 = **8π = 25.1** (1 mark).
P: (2 marks) Find the volume under z = 9 − x² − y² above the plane z = 0. => The region is r ≤ 3. V = ∫₀^{2π} ∫₀³ (9 − r²) r dr dθ (1 mark) = 2π [9r²/2 − r⁴/4]₀³ = 2π (40.5 − 20.25) = **40.5π = 127.2** (1 mark).
P: (1 mark) Find the volume of a cone of base radius 3 and height 4. => V = π R² h ÷ 3 = π × 9 × 4 ÷ 3 = **12π = 37.7**.
P: (1 mark) Evaluate ∭ (x + y + z) dV over the cube [0, 1]³. => By symmetry each term gives 1/2: **3/2**.
P: (1 mark) Evaluate ∫ F · dr for F = (y, x) along the straight line from (0, 0) to (2, 3). => r = (2t, 3t): F · r′ = 3t × 2 + 2t × 3 = 12t, ∫₀¹ 12t dt = **6** (= x y at the end point, since F = ∇ (x y)).
P: (2 marks) Show that F = (2x + y, x + 3y²) is conservative and find ∫ F · dr from (0, 0) to (1, 2). => ∂P/∂y = 1 = ∂Q/∂x ✓ (1 mark). f = x² + x y + y³, so the integral is f (1, 2) − f (0, 0) = 1 + 2 + 8 = **11** (1 mark).
P: (2 marks) Find the divergence and the curl of F = (x² y, y² z, z² x) at (1, 1, 1). => div = 2xy + 2yz + 2zx = **6** (1 mark). curl = (∂R/∂y − ∂Q/∂z, ∂P/∂z − ∂R/∂x, ∂Q/∂x − ∂P/∂y) = (0 − y², 0 − z², 0 − x²) = **(−1, −1, −1)** (1 mark).

@section physics | 3 · Part B: Flux, Gauss's law and potential (6 marks, about 35 min) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 11 to 16
P: (1 mark) Find the flux through a 0.25 m² loop in a uniform field of 200 N/C when the normal makes 45° with the field. => Φ = 200 × 0.25 × cos 45° = **35.4 N m²/C**.
P: (1 mark) Find the net flux through a closed surface that encloses +3 nC, and the flux through each face of a cube with the charge at its centre. => 3 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 8.85 × 10⁻¹² = **339 N m²/C**; per face **56.5 N m²/C**.
P: (1 mark) A uniformly charged insulating sphere has Q = 6 nC and R = 3 cm. Find the field at r = 2 cm and r = 6 cm. => Inside: k Q r ÷ R³ = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 6 × 10⁻⁹ × 0.02 ÷ 2.7 × 10⁻⁵ = **3.996 × 10⁴ N/C**. Outside: k Q ÷ r² = 53.94 ÷ 0.0036 = **1.50 × 10⁴ N/C**.
P: (1 mark) Find the potential at 5 cm from a 2 nC charge and the potential energy of a 3 nC charge placed there. => V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 2 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 0.05 = **360 V**. U = q V = 3 × 10⁻⁹ × 360 = **1.08 × 10⁻⁶ J**.
P: (1 mark) An electron starts from rest and is accelerated through 300 V. Find its speed. => v = √(2 e V ÷ m) = √(2 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 300 ÷ 9.109 × 10⁻³¹) = **1.03 × 10⁷ m/s**.
P: (1 mark) The potential in a region is V = 4x² − 3x y. Find the electric field at (1, 2). => E = −∇V = (−8x + 3y, 3x) = **(−2, 3) V/m**.

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | The volume of a ball from a paraboloid trick
P: Use spherical coordinates to find ∭ (x² + y² + z²) dV over a ball of radius R, and use it to find the average value of r² in the ball. => ∫₀^{2π} ∫₀^π ∫₀^R ρ² · ρ² sin φ dρ dφ dθ = 2π × 2 × R⁵ ÷ 5 = **4π R⁵ ÷ 5**. Dividing by the volume 4π R³ ÷ 3: average r² = **3 R² ÷ 5**, so the root-mean-square distance from the centre is R √(3/5) = **0.775 R**. (Used in the potential of a uniformly charged sphere and in the moment of inertia I = (2/5) M R², which is (2/3) × the average of r² times M.)

LVL: Challenge | Two spheres, one rod
P: A conducting sphere of radius 10 cm (Q = 10 nC) and a pointed tip of effective radius 1 mm are joined by a wire and share one potential. Find that potential and the field at the tip's surface, and compare with the breakdown field 3 × 10⁶ V/m. => V = k Q ÷ R (the charge stays mostly on the large sphere) = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 0.1 = **899 V**. Tip field ≈ V ÷ r_tip = 899 ÷ 0.001 = **9 × 10⁵ V/m**: about 30% of breakdown. For a **10 kV** potential the tip field would be 10⁷ V/m, over three times the breakdown field, and corona would start.
