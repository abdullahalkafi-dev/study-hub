# Divergence, curl and the big theorems. Then: field energy and why lightning rods work
lead: The divergence measures how much a field spreads out from a point (a source or a sink). The curl measures how much it swirls around a point. Two theorems connect them to integrals you already know: the divergence theorem (the flux out of a closed surface equals the total divergence inside) and Stokes' theorem (the circulation around a loop equals the total curl through it). They are the mathematical form of Gauss's law and, next weeks, of Faraday's and Ampère's laws. In physics, you finally learn where the energy of the electric field sits (in the field itself) and use everything from this week to explain how a pointed rod protects a building from lightning.

BN: ডাইভারজেন্স মাপে একটা ক্ষেত্র কোনো বিন্দু থেকে কতটা ছড়ায় (উৎস বা শোষক)। কার্ল মাপে ক্ষেত্র বিন্দুর চারপাশে কতটা ঘোরে। দুটো উপপাদ্য এদের জানা যোগজের সাথে জোড়ে: ডাইভারজেন্স উপপাদ্য (বদ্ধ তল দিয়ে বেরোনো ফ্লাক্স = ভেতরের মোট ডাইভারজেন্স) আর স্টোকসের উপপাদ্য (লুপ ঘিরে সঞ্চালন = ভেতর দিয়ে মোট কার্ল)। এরা গাউসের সূত্রের এবং আগামী সপ্তাহগুলোতে ফ্যারাডে ও অ্যাম্পিয়ারের সূত্রের গাণিতিক রূপ। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে শেষ পর্যন্ত জানবে বৈদ্যুতিক ক্ষেত্রের শক্তি কোথায় থাকে (ক্ষেত্রেই), আর এই সপ্তাহের সবকিছু দিয়ে বুঝবে সূচালো দণ্ড কীভাবে বজ্র থেকে বাড়ি বাঁচায়।

@section math | 2 · Math: divergence, curl and two theorems | 55 min

### Divergence
For F = (P, Q, R) the **divergence** is a number at each point:

EQ: ∇ · F = ∂P/∂x + ∂Q/∂y + ∂R/∂z

Positive divergence: the field **spreads out** (a source); negative: it **converges** (a sink); zero: what flows in flows out. In fluid terms, the divergence of a velocity field is the **rate of expansion** per unit volume.

EX: Divergence
1. **F = (x, y, z):** ∇ · F = 1 + 1 + 1 = **3** (spreading from the origin equally everywhere).
2. **F = (2x, 3y, 4z):** **9**. **F = (x y, y z, z x):** y + z + x, which is **6** at (1, 2, 3).
3. **Expanding gas:** v = (0.1 x, 0.1 y, 0.1 z) m/s (with x, y, z in metres): ∇ · v = **0.3 s⁻¹**: any small volume grows by 30% each second.
4. **A point charge:** E = kQ (x, y, z) ÷ r³. ∂/∂x (x ÷ r³) = 1 ÷ r³ − 3x² ÷ r⁵ and the three terms add to 3 ÷ r³ − 3 r² ÷ r⁵ = **0** for r ≠ 0. The field has **no divergence away from the charge**: the field lines pass through empty space without starting or ending. All the "source" is concentrated at the charge itself.
/EX

### Curl
In two dimensions the **curl** is ∂Q/∂x − ∂P/∂y (as at the end of Day 4). In three dimensions it is a vector:

EQ: ∇ × F = (∂R/∂y − ∂Q/∂z, ∂P/∂z − ∂R/∂x, ∂Q/∂x − ∂P/∂y)

The curl measures the **swirl**: its direction is the axis of the swirl and its length is **twice the angular speed** of a tiny paddle wheel placed in the flow. **A conservative field has zero curl** (∇ × ∇f = 0).

BN: ডাইভারজেন্স ∇·F = P_x + Q_y + R_z: ধনাত্মক হলে ছড়ায় (উৎস), ঋণাত্মক হলে জড়ো হয় (শোষক)। কার্ল ∇×F ঘূর্ণি মাপে: দিক = ঘূর্ণি অক্ষ, মান = ছোট প্যাডেল চাকার কৌণিক বেগের দ্বিগুণ। সংরক্ষণশীল ক্ষেত্রের কার্ল শূন্য। বিন্দু চার্জের ক্ষেত্রের ডাইভারজেন্স চার্জ ছাড়া সব জায়গায় শূন্য।

EX: Curl
1. **F = (−y, x, 0):** curl = (0, 0, 1 − (−1)) = **(0, 0, 2)**. A rigid rotation v = (−ω y, ω x, 0) has curl **(0, 0, 2ω)**: twice the angular velocity ω.
2. **F = (y z, x z, x y) = ∇ (x y z):** curl = (x − x, y − y, z − z) = **(0, 0, 0)**, as it must be for a gradient.
3. **F = (y, x):** curl = 1 − 1 = **0** (the conservative field of Day 4). The unit-circle swirl (−y, x): curl **2**.
4. **The electric field of fixed charges** has **∇ × E = 0**: that is the differential form of "the electrostatic field is conservative", so V exists.
/EX

### The two theorems
EQ: ∯_S F · dA = ∭_E (∇ · F) dV     (Divergence theorem: closed surface S bounding the solid E)
EQ: ∮_C F · dr = ∬_S (∇ × F) · dA     (Stokes' theorem: loop C bounding the surface S)

The first says the **total flux out** equals the total **source strength inside**. The second says the **circulation round the edge** equals the total **swirl through the surface**.

EX: Checking the theorems
1. **Divergence theorem for F = (x, y, z) and a sphere of radius R:** the right side is 3 × (4π R³ ÷ 3) = **4π R³**. The left side: on the sphere F · n = R (F points outward with size R), area 4π R², flux **4π R³** ✓. For R = 2: **100.5**.
2. **The unit cube [0, 1]³ with F = (x, y, z):** ∭ 3 dV = **3**. Direct: the faces x = 1, y = 1, z = 1 each give F · n = 1 over area 1 (total 3); the faces x = 0, y = 0, z = 0 give F · n = −x = 0. Total **3** ✓.
3. **Stokes for F = (−y, x, 0) around a circle of radius R:** curl · n = 2, area π R², so ∮ = **2π R²** (found by direct integration on Day 4: R = 2 gives 8π). For R = 3: **56.5**.
4. **Gauss's law from the divergence theorem:** ∯ E · dA = ∭ (∇ · E) dV = Q ÷ ε₀ = ∭ (ρ ÷ ε₀) dV for any region, so **∇ · E = ρ ÷ ε₀** at every point: the **local form of Gauss's law** (the first Maxwell equation). Together with **∇ × E = 0** (electrostatics) this is all of electrostatics in two lines.
5. **Laplace's equation:** in empty space E = −∇V and ∇ · E = 0 give **∇² V = ∂²V/∂x² + ∂²V/∂y² + ∂²V/∂z² = 0** (the equation from Week 16, Day 1).
/EX

TRAP: Divergence and curl are **local** properties (at a point). A field with ∇ · F = 0 can still have a nonzero flux through a surface that does not close, and a field with ∇ × F = 0 can still circulate if the region has a hole. The divergence theorem needs a **closed** surface with the **outward** normal. Stokes' theorem needs the direction of the loop and the normal to follow the **right-hand rule**.

LVL: Challenge | The divergence of the inverse-square field
P: Show that ∇ · (r̂ ÷ r²) = 0 away from the origin, yet its flux through any sphere around the origin is 4π. Where is the divergence? => With r̂ ÷ r² = (x, y, z) ÷ r³ each term is r⁻³ − 3x² r⁻⁵ and the sum is zero for r ≠ 0. The flux through a sphere of radius r is (1 ÷ r²)(4π r²) = **4π**. By the divergence theorem the volume integral of the divergence is therefore 4π even though the divergence is zero everywhere except at the origin: it is concentrated there as a **delta function**, ∇ · (r̂ ÷ r²) = 4π δ³ (r). This is the mathematical form of a point charge.

### Where is the energy?
Assembling charges costs energy (Day 3). That energy is stored **in the field**, with density

EQ: u = ½ ε₀ E²     (J/m³)

The total energy is ∭ u dV over all space. Check with a charged sphere: ∫_R^∞ ½ ε₀ (kQ ÷ r²)² 4π r² dr = Q² ÷ (8π ε₀ R) = **k Q² ÷ 2R** ✓, exactly the energy found by building up the sphere shell by shell.

BN: ক্ষেত্রের শক্তির ঘনত্ব u = ½ε₀E² (J/m³)। মোট শক্তি = u এর আয়তন যোগজ। চার্জিত গোলকে ∫½ε₀E² dV = kQ²/2R, যা শেল-যোগ করে পাওয়া শক্তির সমান।

EX: Energy in fields
1. **Air at breakdown** (E = 3 × 10⁶ V/m): u = 0.5 × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 9 × 10¹² = **39.8 J/m³**.
2. **A capacitor** with plate area 0.01 m², gap 1 mm at 100 V: E = 10⁵ V/m, u = 0.5 × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 10¹⁰ = **0.0443 J/m³**, volume 0.01 × 0.001 = 10⁻⁵ m³, energy **4.43 × 10⁻⁷ J**. Compare ½ C V² with C = ε₀ A ÷ d = 8.85 × 10⁻¹¹ F: ½ × 8.85 × 10⁻¹¹ × 10⁴ = **4.43 × 10⁻⁷ J** ✓. (Capacitors are Week 18.)
3. **A thundercloud:** a region of 3 km × 3 km × 2 km (1.8 × 10¹⁰ m³) with an average field of 10⁵ V/m holds 0.0443 × 1.8 × 10¹⁰ = **8 × 10⁸ J** in its field: the same order as the **1.5 × 10⁹ J** of a lightning bolt (Day 4). The bolt discharges the energy stored in the field of the cloud.
/EX

### Lightning rods
Putting together Day 5 of last week (sharp points concentrate charge) and this week (V is the same on a conductor, E = −∇V, energy):

EX: Why a pointed rod works
1. **The tip sees an enormous field.** Near a conducting tip of radius R the field is roughly E ≈ V ÷ R (for a sphere it is exactly V ÷ R). A tip of radius 1 mm at a potential of 3 kV already reaches E = 3 × 10⁶ V/m, the breakdown of air: V = E R = 3 × 10⁶ × 10⁻³ = **3 kV**. A ball of radius 10 cm needs **300 kV**.
2. **Corona and the "upward streamer".** When the cloud's field reaches the tip, the air around the tip ionises before the field anywhere else is strong enough. A conducting path of ions rises from the tip to meet the downward leader of the lightning. The strike is therefore **caught by the rod** and carried to the ground through a thick conductor (a "down conductor") instead of through the building.
3. **A rough rule for the protected area:** a rod of height h protects roughly a cone beneath it, with a ground radius of about h (a 45° half-angle), for example a 15 m rod covers a circle of about 15 m radius. More careful designs use the "rolling sphere" method.
4. **The size of the current:** a typical stroke carries about 30 kA for 50 μs (Q ≈ 1.5 C) and a bigger one 200 kA. A copper conductor of cross-section 50 mm² (R = ρ L ÷ A = 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ × 10 ÷ 5 × 10⁻⁵ = 3.4 mΩ per 10 m) dissipates I² R t = (3 × 10⁴)² × 3.4 × 10⁻³ × 5 × 10⁻⁵ = **153 J**. The 10 m of copper has a mass of 4.5 kg, so it warms by only 153 ÷ (4.5 × 385) = **0.09 K**: the metal path is safe. A tree or a wall of brick, with resistance of kilo-ohms, is not: they explode as the water boils.
5. **Safe behaviour in a storm:** stay in a building or a metal-bodied car (Faraday cage). If you are in the open, crouch with your feet together, so that the voltage between your feet (the "step voltage") is small when the current spreads through the ground. The ground near the strike is not at one potential: V falls with distance from the strike point.
6. **A sharp tip or a round one for high voltage?** Equipment at high voltage (substations, HV test labs) uses **smooth rounded shapes** (corona rings) to keep E below breakdown and prevent unwanted discharge: the opposite of a lightning rod, with the same physics.
/EX

TRAP: A lightning rod does **not** "attract" lightning from far away or "discharge the cloud" by itself: it provides a **preferred, safe path** to ground for a strike that is going to happen anyway. The simple formula E ≈ V ÷ R holds for a sphere and gives only the right **order of magnitude** for a real tip. And the energy density ½ ε₀ E² is in the **field**, in the space between the charges, not "on the charges".
