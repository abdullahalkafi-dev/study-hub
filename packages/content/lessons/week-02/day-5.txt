@section math | 2 · Math: the log laws and solving equations | 60 min

### Before calculators: the problem logs solved
In 1614 John Napier published tables of logarithms. Astronomers and navigators had to multiply huge numbers by hand, like 3,481,972 × 6,208. A single slip ruined a whole calculation. The idea: replace multiplication, which is slow, with addition, which is fast. Look up the logs, add them, then look up the answer. It worked so well that slide rules based on logs were used by engineers until the 1970s, including the people who put a man on the Moon.

BN: ১৬১৪ সালে নেপিয়ার লগের টেবিল বানালেন। বিশাল দুটো সংখ্যা গুণ করা ছিল কষ্টের আর ভুলের ঝুঁকিপূর্ণ কাজ। ধারণাটা ছিল: গুণকে যোগে বদলাও, কারণ যোগ সহজ। প্রযুক্তিবিদরা স্লাইড রুল (লগ ভিত্তিক) ১৯৭০ এর দশক পর্যন্ত ব্যবহার করেছেন, চাঁদে মানুষ পাঠানোর হিসাবেও।

### Law 1: the product law
EQ: log_b(M · N) = log_b(M) + log_b(N)

**Proof.** Let m = log_b(M) and n = log_b(N). Written as exponents, M = bᵐ and N = bⁿ. Multiply: M · N = bᵐ · bⁿ = bᵐ⁺ⁿ (the exponent law from Week 1). Now read this as a log: log_b(M · N) = m + n. ∎

EX: Check with numbers
1. log₂(8 · 4) = log₂(32) = 5.
2. log₂(8) + log₂(4) = 3 + 2 = 5 ✓.
/EX

### Law 2: the quotient law
EQ: log_b(M ÷ N) = log_b(M) − log_b(N)

**Proof.** With M = bᵐ and N = bⁿ: M ÷ N = bᵐ ÷ bⁿ = bᵐ⁻ⁿ. So log_b(M ÷ N) = m − n. ∎

### Law 3: the power law
EQ: log_b(Mᵏ) = k · log_b(M)

**Proof.** With M = bᵐ: Mᵏ = (bᵐ)ᵏ = bᵐᵏ (power of a power). So log_b(Mᵏ) = mk = k · log_b(M). ∎

This law is the one that lets you **bring an unknown exponent down to the ground**, so you can solve for it. It is also the step we skipped when we proved the change of base rule on Thursday.

KEY: Product → sum. Quotient → difference. Power → multiplier. But there is **no** rule for log(M + N) or log(M − N).

BN: তিনটা নিয়ম: গুণ → যোগ, ভাগ → বিয়োগ, ঘাত → সামনে গুণক। প্রমাণ আসলে ঘাতের নিয়ম থেকে এসেছে (গুণে ঘাত যোগ, ভাগে ঘাত বিয়োগ, ঘাতের ঘাতে গুণ)। সবচেয়ে বেশি কাজে লাগে তৃতীয়টা, কারণ ঘাতে থাকা অজানাকে নিচে নামিয়ে আনে। কিন্তু log(M + N) এর কোনো সহজ নিয়ম নেই।

TRAP: log(M + N) is **not** log(M) + log(N). Test: log(10 + 10) = log(20) ≈ 1.301, but log(10) + log(10) = 2. Also log(M) ÷ log(N) is **not** log(M ÷ N).

EX: Expand log_b(x³ · √y ÷ z)
1. Product and quotient: log(x³) + log(√y) − log(z).
2. Power law, with √y = y^(1/2): **3 log x + ½ log y − log z**.
/EX

EX: Condense 2 log 3 + log 5 − log 15 into a single log
1. 2 log 3 = log 9.
2. log 9 + log 5 = log 45.
3. log 45 − log 15 = log(45 ÷ 15) = **log 3**.
/EX

EX: Use log 2 ≈ 0.301 and log 3 ≈ 0.477 to find log 6, log 8 and log 5
1. log 6 = log(2 · 3) = 0.301 + 0.477 = **0.778**.
2. log 8 = log(2³) = 3 × 0.301 = **0.903**.
3. log 5 = log(10 ÷ 2) = 1 − 0.301 = **0.699**.
/EX

### Solving log equations
Strategy: (1) combine the logs into one log, (2) rewrite as an exponent, (3) solve, (4) **check that every log in the original is defined**. A log needs a positive input.

EX: Solve log₂(x) + log₂(x − 2) = 3
1. Combine: log₂[x(x − 2)] = 3.
2. Exponent form: x(x − 2) = 2³ = 8, so x² − 2x − 8 = 0.
3. Factor: (x − 4)(x + 2) = 0, so x = 4 or x = −2.
4. Check: x = −2 makes log₂(−2) undefined. So **x = 4** only. Verify: log₂4 + log₂2 = 2 + 1 = 3 ✓.
/EX

EX: Solve log₃(x + 2) − log₃(x − 1) = 1
1. Combine: log₃[(x + 2) ÷ (x − 1)] = 1.
2. Exponent form: (x + 2) ÷ (x − 1) = 3.
3. x + 2 = 3x − 3, so 2x = 5 and **x = 5/2**.
4. Check: log₃(4.5) − log₃(1.5) = log₃(3) = 1 ✓.
/EX

BN: লগ সমীকরণ সমাধানের ধাপ: (১) লগগুলো এক লগে জোড়া লাগাও, (২) ঘাতের আকারে লেখো, (৩) সমাধান করো, (৪) **অবশ্যই যাচাই করো**: মূল সমীকরণের লগের ভেতরে ধনাত্মক সংখ্যা বসছে কি না। যে উত্তরে লগের ভেতর ঋণাত্মক হয়, সেটা বাদ। যেমন x = −2 বাদ গেল।

### Solving exponential equations with logs
Now the problem from Thursday is solved. If the unknown is in the exponent and the bases cannot be matched, **take a log of both sides** and use the power law.

EX: Solve 3ˣ = 20
1. Take log of both sides: log(3ˣ) = log(20).
2. Power law: x · log 3 = log 20.
3. x = log 20 ÷ log 3 = 1.3010 ÷ 0.4771 = **2.727**.
4. Check: 3² = 9 and 3³ = 27, and 20 is in between ✓.
/EX

EX: Solve 2^(3x − 1) = 50
1. Take ln of both sides: (3x − 1) ln 2 = ln 50.
2. 3x − 1 = ln 50 ÷ ln 2 = 3.9120 ÷ 0.6931 = 5.644.
3. 3x = 6.644, so **x = 2.215**.
/EX

EX: Solve e^(0.3t) = 5
1. Take ln of both sides. ln(e^(0.3t)) = 0.3t, because ln and e undo each other.
2. 0.3t = ln 5 = 1.6094.
3. **t = 5.36**.
/EX

### e and ln: the natural pair
The natural logarithm ln(x) = log_e(x) is the inverse of eˣ. So **ln(eˣ) = x** and **e^(ln x) = x**. It follows every rule above, with base e. Physicists, engineers and statisticians use ln almost everywhere, because e makes calculus clean.

### Applications
EX: Doubling time
1. At 8% growth per year, 1.08ᵗ = 2.
2. t = ln 2 ÷ ln 1.08 = 0.6931 ÷ 0.07696 = **9.0 years**.
3. For any rate r: doubling time ≈ 0.693 ÷ ln(1 + r), and for small r roughly **72 ÷ (r in percent)**. That is the "rule of 72".
/EX

EX: Carbon dating
1. Living things take in carbon-14, which decays with half-life 5730 years. A wooden tool has 30% of the original C-14. How old is it?
2. 0.3 = (1/2)^(t ÷ 5730). Take ln: ln 0.3 = (t ÷ 5730) · ln 0.5.
3. t = 5730 × (−1.204) ÷ (−0.693) = 5730 × 1.737 = **about 9950 years**.
/EX

### Straight lines from curves: log scales
If y = y₀ · aˣ, then log y = log y₀ + x · log a. This is a straight line in x and log y. So when data grows exponentially, plotting **log y** against x gives a **straight line**, and the slope gives log a. Scientists use this to check whether data is exponential and to find the growth rate. Graph paper with a log axis is called semi-log paper.

BN: যদি y = y₀ · aˣ হয়, তাহলে log y = log y₀ + x · log a, যা x আর log y এর মধ্যে একটা সরলরেখা। তাই ডেটা সূচকীয়ভাবে বাড়ছে কি না যাচাই করতে log y এর গ্রাফ আঁকা হয়: সরল রেখা এলে হ্যাঁ। ঢালটা দেয় log a।

@section physics | 3 · Physics: Newton's third law and friction | 35 min

### Forces come in pairs
Push on a wall. You feel the wall push back on your hand. Step off a small boat and the boat moves backward. A rocket pushes gas downward and the gas pushes the rocket upward. In every case, two objects interact, and each pushes on the other.

KEY: Newton's third law: if object A exerts a force on object B, then B exerts a force on A that is **equal in size, opposite in direction, and of the same type**. The two forces act on **different objects**.

BN: নিউটনের তৃতীয় সূত্র: A যদি B এর ওপর বল দেয়, তাহলে B ও A এর ওপর সমান মানের উল্টো দিকের বল দেয়। দুটো বল **দুটো ভিন্ন বস্তুর** ওপর কাজ করে। তাই এরা একে অপরকে বাতিল করে না, কারণ বাতিল হতে হলে একই বস্তুর ওপর থাকতে হতো।

**Why don't they cancel?** Because they act on different objects. To decide if something accelerates, you add up only the forces **on that object**.

EX: Walking
1. Your foot pushes the ground backward (friction on the ground).
2. By the third law, the ground pushes your foot **forward** with an equal force.
3. This forward force on you is what accelerates you. On ice, the ground can only provide a tiny push, so walking is hard.
/EX

EX: Two skaters
1. A 80 kg skater pushes a 60 kg skater with 120 N on smooth ice.
2. The first pushes the second with 120 N, so the second pushes the first back with **120 N** the other way.
3. Accelerations: 120 ÷ 80 = **1.5 m/s²** for the heavier skater and 120 ÷ 60 = **2.0 m/s²** for the lighter skater, in opposite directions. Equal forces, unequal accelerations, because the masses differ.
/EX

EX: Two blocks and a rope
1. A 4 kg block is pulled by 20 N on a frictionless table. It is attached by a rope to a 6 kg block behind it.
2. Whole system: 20 ÷ (4 + 6) = **2 m/s²**.
3. Tension: it is the force that accelerates the 6 kg block alone: T = 6 × 2 = **12 N**.
4. Check on the 4 kg block: 20 − 12 = 8 = 4 × 2 ✓.
/EX

TRAP: The weight of a book and the normal force from the table are **not** a third-law pair. They act on the same object. The pair of "Earth pulls the book down" is "the book pulls Earth up".

### Friction
Friction is the contact force along a surface that resists sliding.

- **Static friction** keeps an object from starting to slide. It adjusts to match the push, up to a maximum: f_s ≤ μ_s N.
- **Kinetic friction** acts on an object already sliding: **f_k = μ_k N**. The numbers μ_s and μ_k are the **coefficients of friction**, and μ_s is usually bigger than μ_k.

EX: Pushing a crate
1. A 20 kg crate sits on a floor with μ_s = 0.5 and μ_k = 0.3. Weight = 196 N, so the normal force N = 196 N.
2. To start moving, you must beat static friction: 0.5 × 196 = **98 N**.
3. Once it slides, kinetic friction is 0.3 × 196 = **58.8 N**.
4. You push with 100 N. The net force is 100 − 58.8 = 41.2 N, so a = 41.2 ÷ 20 = **2.06 m/s²**.
/EX

BN: স্থির ঘর্ষণ (static) বস্তুকে নড়তে দেয় না, এর সর্বোচ্চ মান μ_s × N। গতিশীল ঘর্ষণ (kinetic) f = μ_k × N, যা স্থির ঘর্ষণের সর্বোচ্চ মানের চেয়ে সাধারণত কম। তাই ঠেলে শুরু করা কঠিন, একবার চললে সহজ। N হলো অভিলম্ব বল, ওজন নয় (যদিও সমতলে সমান হতে পারে)।

LVL: Challenge | The horse and cart
P: "If the cart pulls back on the horse with the same force the horse pulls the cart, how can the cart ever move?" Answer clearly. => The two forces act on **different objects**. For the cart, only the forward pull from the horse (and friction from the ground) matter. For the horse, the ground pushes it forward more than the cart pulls it back. The horse accelerates because the **ground pushes it forward** (third-law pair with its feet pushing back), and that is bigger than the cart's backward pull.
