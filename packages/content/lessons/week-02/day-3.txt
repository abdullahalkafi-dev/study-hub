@section math | 2 · Math: exponential functions | 55 min

### Adding versus multiplying
A straight line grows by **adding** the same amount each step. A savings account that earns 1000 taka per year in simple interest is linear.

An exponential grows by **multiplying** by the same factor each step. If your money grows 8% every year, each year it is multiplied by 1.08.

EX: Two plans
1. Plan A: you save 100 taka every day (linear). Plan B: you start with 1 taka and double every day (exponential).
2. After 10 days: A has 1000. B has 2¹⁰ = 1024.
3. After 20 days: A has 2000. B has 2²⁰ = **1,048,576**.
4. After 30 days: A has 3000. B has 2³⁰ = **1,073,741,824**, over a billion.
/EX

KEY: An **exponential function** has the variable in the exponent: f(x) = aˣ with a > 0 and a ≠ 1. The number a is the **base**, and it is the multiplier per unit step.

BN: সরলরেখায় প্রতিধাপে সংখ্যা যোগ হয়। সূচকীয় ফাংশনে প্রতিধাপে একই সংখ্যা দিয়ে গুণ হয়। x² এ x হলো base আর ঘাত ধ্রুবক; 2ˣ এ ঘাতটাই চলক। ৩০ দিনে সরল পরিকল্পনায় ৩০০০ আর দ্বিগুণ-হওয়া পরিকল্পনায় ১০০ কোটির বেশি, এটাই সূচকীয় বৃদ্ধির শক্তি।

### The graph and its properties
GRAPH: y = 2ˣ and y = (1/2)ˣ | x | y | -3,0.125 -2,0.25 -1,0.5 0,1 1,2 2,4 3,8 ;; -3,8 -2,4 -1,2 0,1 1,0.5 2,0.25 3,0.125 | The teal curve is 2ˣ (growth). The orange curve is (1/2)ˣ (decay). They are mirror images in the y-axis. Both pass through (0, 1).

- **Passes through (0, 1)** because a⁰ = 1.
- **Always positive:** a power of a positive number is never zero or negative. The range is y > 0.
- **Horizontal asymptote y = 0:** it gets closer and closer to the x-axis but never touches it.
- **Domain:** all real numbers. We can put in any x, including 0.5 (that means √2 for base 2) or −3.
- If a > 1 it **grows**. If 0 < a < 1 it **decays**.

### Growth and decay in the real world
A quantity that changes by a fixed percentage each period follows:

EQ: P(t) = P₀ · (1 + r)ᵗ  (growth)     P(t) = P₀ · (1 − r)ᵗ  (decay)

where P₀ is the starting amount, r the rate per period as a decimal, and t the number of periods.

EX: A bank deposit
1. 1000 taka at 8% per year, compounded yearly. How much after 5 years?
2. A = 1000 × 1.08⁵ = 1000 × 1.4693 = **1469.33 taka**.
/EX

EX: A phone loses value
1. A phone worth 30,000 taka loses 20% of its value each year. What is it worth after 3 years?
2. V = 30000 × 0.8³ = 30000 × 0.512 = **15,360 taka**.
/EX

**Half-life.** For radioactive atoms or a medicine in your blood, we often use the time T it takes to halve. Then:

EQ: N(t) = N₀ · (1/2)^(t ÷ T)

EX: A medicine
1. A dose of 800 mg has a half-life of 6 hours. How much is left after 18 hours?
2. 18 ÷ 6 = 3 half-lives, so (1/2)³ = 1/8. 800 ÷ 8 = **100 mg**.
/EX

BN: ক্ষয়ের হিসাবে half-life মানে অর্ধেক হতে যত সময় লাগে। ১৮ ঘণ্টা মানে ৩ বার অর্ধেক: ৮০০ → ৪০০ → ২০০ → ১০০। সূত্র N₀ × (½)^(t/T)। বৃদ্ধির সূত্রে শতকরা হারকে দশমিকে লিখে (১ + r) দিয়ে গুণ করো।

### Compounding more often, and the number e
Suppose a bank pays 100% interest per year on 1 taka. If it compounds once, you get 1 × 2 = 2. If it compounds twice (50% each half-year): 1.5² = 2.25. What if it compounds more and more often?

| Times per year, n | Value of (1 + 1/n)ⁿ |
| 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 2.25 |
| 4 | 2.4414 |
| 12 | 2.6130 |
| 365 | 2.7146 |
| 1,000,000 | 2.71828 |

The values do not explode. They settle toward a number we call **e ≈ 2.71828**. It is an irrational number like π, and it is the natural base for continuous growth.

EQ: Continuous growth:  P(t) = P₀ · e^(rt)

Why is e "natural"? Among all exponential curves, y = eˣ is the one whose **slope at any point equals its own height**. In Week 8 you will see that this makes calculus beautifully simple. For now, treat e as a number you meet on the calculator key **eˣ** (and its inverse, **ln**, which is Thursday's topic).

EX: Continuous growth
1. A culture of 500 bacteria grows continuously at rate r = 0.3 per hour. How many after 4 hours?
2. N = 500 × e^(0.3 × 4) = 500 × e^1.2 = 500 × 3.3201 = **1660**.
/EX

### Solving simple exponential equations by matching bases
If both sides can be written with the same base, the exponents must be equal. (This works because an exponential is one-to-one.)

EX: Solve 3^(2x − 1) = 27
1. Write 27 as 3³.
2. Match exponents: 2x − 1 = 3, so **x = 2**.
/EX

EX: Solve 4ˣ = 8
1. Write both as powers of 2: (2²)ˣ = 2³, so 2^(2x) = 2³.
2. 2x = 3, so **x = 3/2**.
/EX

EX: Solve 9ˣ = 1/27
1. 9 = 3² and 1/27 = 3⁻³. So 3^(2x) = 3⁻³.
2. 2x = −3, so **x = −3/2**.
/EX

TRAP: Matching bases only works if both sides are **pure powers of the same base**. 2ˣ = 10 cannot be solved this way because 10 is not a nice power of 2. That is exactly the problem logarithms solve, and it is the story of tomorrow.

### Transformations again
Yesterday's rules apply: y = 2^(x − 3) is 2ˣ shifted right by 3. y = 2ˣ + 1 shifts up by 1, and its asymptote moves to y = 1.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: Newton's first law | 35 min

### The old idea and the new idea
For almost 2000 years people believed that a force is needed to **keep** something moving. Look around: a rolling ball stops, a cart stops, a pushed book stops. It seems obvious.

Galileo noticed something deeper. Roll a ball down one ramp and up another. On a rough ramp it rises a bit less each time. On a smoother ramp, it rises almost to the same height. On a perfectly smooth ramp that goes on forever, he reasoned it would never stop. The thing that stops everyday objects is **friction**, a force, not some natural tendency to rest.

### Newton's first law
KEY: An object stays at rest, or moves at **constant velocity** in a straight line, unless a **net force** acts on it.

Constant velocity means no acceleration. So the first law says: **zero net force means zero acceleration**, and zero acceleration means the net force is zero. An object at rest and an object moving steadily are in the same situation, called **equilibrium**.

BN: নিউটনের প্রথম সূত্র: নিট বল শূন্য হলে বস্তু স্থির থাকে বা সমবেগে সোজা পথে চলতে থাকে। চলতে থাকতে কোনো বল লাগে না। বলের দরকার বেগ বদলাতে। রাস্তায় বল থামে কারণ ঘর্ষণ বল তাকে থামায়, থেমে থাকাটা বস্তুর স্বাভাবিক ধর্ম নয়।

### Inertia
The tendency of an object to resist changes in its velocity is called **inertia**. **Mass** is the measure of inertia: more mass means more resistance to change.

You feel inertia all the time. When a bus brakes suddenly, you lurch forward. No force pushes you forward. Your body simply tries to continue at its old velocity while the bus slows. When a bus starts quickly, you press into your seat because your body resists speeding up.

### Equilibrium: balanced forces
In equilibrium, the forces cancel: the vector sum is zero.

EX: A box at constant velocity
1. You push a crate along a floor at constant speed with a 80 N horizontal force.
2. Constant velocity means zero net force, so friction must be **80 N backwards**.
3. If you push harder (100 N), the net force is 20 N forward and the crate **accelerates**. It does not "go faster at constant speed".
/EX

EX: A skydiver at terminal velocity
1. A 70 kg skydiver falls faster and faster. Air resistance grows with speed.
2. At some speed, the drag equals the weight: D = 686 N. Now the net force is zero.
3. By the first law, the skydiver falls at **constant velocity** (about 55 m/s for a spread-eagle posture). This is **terminal velocity**.
/EX

EX: Three forces in equilibrium
1. Forces of 6 N east and 8 N north act on an object. What third force keeps it in equilibrium?
2. The resultant of the first two is √(36 + 64) = 10 N at 53° north of east.
3. The third force must cancel it: **10 N pointing the opposite way**, at 53° south of west.
/EX

TRAP: "An object moving at constant speed has no forces acting on it." Wrong. It can have **many forces whose sum is zero**. The correct statement is that the **net** force is zero. A car cruising at 80 km/h still has engine force and air drag, balanced.
