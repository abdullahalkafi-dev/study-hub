# Integration by parts. Then: simple harmonic motion
lead: Integration by parts is the product rule run backwards. It handles integrals like x eˣ, x cos x and ln x that substitution cannot touch. In physics, you meet the most important motion in all of science: the oscillation. A mass on a spring, a pendulum, a vibrating atom, an alternating current: they all follow the same equation and the same sine wave.

BN: খণ্ডাংশে সমাকলন (integration by parts) হলো গুণনের অন্তরকলন নিয়ম উল্টো করে চালানো। এটা x eˣ, x cos x, ln x এর মতো সমাকলন সামলায়, যা প্রতিস্থাপনে হয় না। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে আজ বিজ্ঞানের সবচেয়ে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ গতির সঙ্গে পরিচয়: দোলন। স্প্রিংয়ে ঝোলানো ভর, দোলক, কম্পমান পরমাণু, পরিবর্তী প্রবাহ: সবারই সমীকরণ ও সাইন তরঙ্গ একই।

@section math | 2 · Math: integration by parts | 55 min

### The product rule backwards
The product rule says d/dx (u v) = u′ v + u v′. Integrate both sides and rearrange:

EQ: ∫ u dv = u v − ∫ v du     (where dv = v′ dx and du = u′ dx)

This turns a hard integral ∫ u dv into a hopefully easier one, ∫ v du. The skill is choosing u and dv.

**Choosing u: the LIATE rule.** Pick u as the first type from this list that appears: **L**ogarithm, **I**nverse trig, **A**lgebraic (powers of x), **T**rig, **E**xponential. The rest is dv. (You want u to get simpler when differentiated and dv to be easy to integrate.)

BN: ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du। u বাছাই: LIATE, মানে লগারিদম, উল্টো ত্রিকোণমিতি, বীজগাণিতিক (x এর ঘাত), ত্রিকোণমিতি, সূচকীয়। এই তালিকায় যে আগে আসে তাকে u নাও (অন্তরকলনে সরল হয়), বাকিটা dv (সহজে সমাকলনযোগ্য)।

PREDICT: For ∫ x eˣ dx, which factor would you pick as u: the x or the eˣ? Think about what happens when you differentiate and integrate each. => Pick u = x. Differentiating x gives 1 (simpler), while eˣ stays the same whether you differentiate or integrate it. If you chose u = eˣ, the x would grow to x²/2 and the integral would get worse.

EX: Basic cases
1. ∫ x eˣ dx: u = x, dv = eˣ dx, so du = dx and v = eˣ. Result = x eˣ − ∫ eˣ dx = **eˣ (x − 1) + C**. Check: derivative = eˣ (x − 1) + eˣ = x eˣ ✓.
2. ∫ x cos x dx: u = x, dv = cos x dx, v = sin x. Result = x sin x − ∫ sin x dx = **x sin x + cos x + C**.
3. ∫ ln x dx: write as ∫ ln x · 1 dx, with u = ln x, dv = dx, du = dx ÷ x, v = x. Result = x ln x − ∫ 1 dx = **x ln x − x + C**.
4. ∫ x ln x dx: u = ln x, dv = x dx, v = x²/2. Result = (x²/2) ln x − ∫ (x/2) dx = **(x²/2) ln x − x²/4 + C**.
5. ∫ arctan x dx: u = arctan x, dv = dx. Result = x arctan x − ∫ x ÷ (1 + x²) dx = **x arctan x − ½ ln(1 + x²) + C**.
/EX

EX: Twice in a row
1. ∫ x² eˣ dx: first u = x²: x² eˣ − ∫ 2x eˣ dx. Then the remaining integral is 2 ∫ x eˣ dx = 2 eˣ (x − 1). Result = x² eˣ − 2 eˣ (x − 1) = **eˣ (x² − 2x + 2) + C**.
2. ∫ x² cos x dx = x² sin x − 2 ∫ x sin x dx = x² sin x − 2 (−x cos x + sin x) = **x² sin x + 2x cos x − 2 sin x + C**.
/EX

EX: The cycle trick
1. I = ∫ eˣ sin x dx. Take u = sin x, dv = eˣ: I = eˣ sin x − ∫ eˣ cos x dx.
2. Again, u = cos x: ∫ eˣ cos x dx = eˣ cos x + ∫ eˣ sin x dx = eˣ cos x + I.
3. So I = eˣ sin x − eˣ cos x − I, giving 2I = eˣ (sin x − cos x). **I = ½ eˣ (sin x − cos x) + C.**
/EX

### Definite integrals by parts
The formula is ∫ₐᵇ u dv = [u v] from a to b − ∫ₐᵇ v du.

EX: Definite examples
1. ∫₀¹ x eˣ dx = [eˣ (x − 1)] = 0 − (−1) = **1**.
2. ∫₁ᵉ ln x dx = [x ln x − x] = (e − e) − (0 − 1) = **1**.
3. ∫₀^π x sin x dx = [−x cos x + sin x] = (π + 0) − 0 = **π**. (Fourier series, in Phase 3, are built from integrals like this.)
/EX

BN: u = x, dv = eˣ dx নিলে ∫xeˣ dx = eˣ(x − 1)। u = x, dv = cos x dx নিলে ∫x cos x dx = x sin x + cos x। ln x কে ln x · 1 লিখে u = ln x নাও: ∫ln x dx = x ln x − x। eˣ sin x এর বেলায় দুইবার by parts করলে আবার মূল সমাকলন ফিরে আসে, তখন সমীকরণ সমাধান করে I = ½eˣ(sin x − cos x)।

TRAP: Pick u and dv **before** integrating, and **differentiate u, integrate dv**, never both the same way. If the new integral looks worse (power goes up), swap your choice. Remember the minus sign in front of ∫ v du, and in definite integrals evaluate the [u v] term at both limits. Finally, always check by differentiating, since by-parts answers hide sign errors.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: simple harmonic motion | 35 min

### The spring equation
A spring pulls back with a force proportional to its stretch (Hooke's law): F = −k x. Put that in Newton's second law:

EQ: m x″ = −k x     so     x″ = −ω² x     with     ω = √(k ÷ m)

This is the **simple harmonic motion (SHM)** equation: the acceleration is proportional to the displacement and points the opposite way. The solution is a sine wave:

EQ: x(t) = A cos(ω t + φ)     v = −A ω sin(ω t + φ)     a = −A ω² cos(ω t + φ)

**Check:** x″ = −A ω² cos(ω t + φ) = −ω² x ✓. A is the **amplitude**, ω the angular frequency, φ the phase (set by the start). The period and frequency are:

EQ: T = 2π ÷ ω = 2π √(m ÷ k)     f = 1 ÷ T     ω = 2π f

Notice that the period does **not depend on the amplitude**. A stiffer spring (larger k) oscillates faster, a heavier mass slower.

BN: স্প্রিং বল F = −kx, তাই mx″ = −kx, অর্থাৎ x″ = −ω²x, ω = √(k/m)। এই হলো সরল ছন্দিত গতির (SHM) সমীকরণ: ত্বরণ সরণের সমানুপাতিক ও বিপরীতমুখী। সমাধান x = A cos(ωt + φ)। বেগ −Aω sin, ত্বরণ −Aω² cos। পর্যায়কাল T = 2π√(m/k) স্পন্দনের বিস্তারের ওপর নির্ভর করে না।

EX: A spring-mass system
1. m = 0.5 kg, k = 200 N/m, released from rest at x = A = 0.05 m.
2. ω = √(200 ÷ 0.5) = √400 = **20 rad/s**. T = 2π ÷ 20 = **0.314 s**. f = **3.18 Hz**.
3. Max speed v_max = A ω = 0.05 × 20 = **1.0 m/s** (at the centre). Max acceleration a_max = A ω² = 0.05 × 400 = **20 m/s²** (at the ends).
4. x(t) = 0.05 cos 20t. At t = 0.1 s, 20t = 2 rad: x = 0.05 cos 2 = **−0.0208 m**, and v = −1.0 sin 2 = **−0.909 m/s**.
/EX

EX: Finding A and φ from the start
1. ω = 10 rad/s. At t = 0 the mass is at x₀ = 0.04 m moving at v₀ = 0.6 m/s.
2. x₀ = A cos φ and v₀ = −A ω sin φ. So A² = x₀² + (v₀ ÷ ω)² = 0.0016 + 0.0036 = 0.0052, **A = 0.0721 m**.
3. tan φ = −v₀ ÷ (ω x₀) = −1.5, with cos φ > 0, so **φ = −0.983 rad**.
/EX

EX: Springs together and a hanging mass
1. Two 100 N/m springs side by side (parallel) act like k = 200 N/m. In a line (series) they act like 1 ÷ k = 1/100 + 1/100, k = 50 N/m. With a 1 kg mass: parallel T = 2π√(1/200) = **0.444 s**, series T = 2π√(1/50) = **0.889 s**.
2. A 0.4 kg mass hangs and stretches a spring 0.098 m at equilibrium. Then k = m g ÷ x = 3.92 ÷ 0.098 = 40 N/m. ω = √(40 ÷ 0.4) = **10 rad/s**, T = 0.628 s. Gravity only shifts the equilibrium. It does not change T.
/EX

TRAP: **ω is in rad/s, not Hz**: T = 2π ÷ ω, and f = ω ÷ 2π. Keep your calculator in **radians** for cos(ω t + φ). Do not mix up the maximum speed (A ω, at the centre) with the maximum acceleration (A ω², at the ends). And the period does not depend on A or on g (for a vertical spring).

LVL: Challenge | Is it really SHM?
P: A block floats in water with a flat bottom of area A_b and mass m. Pushed down by a small amount x, the extra buoyant force is ρ g A_b x upward. Show that it undergoes SHM and find T for m = 2 kg, A_b = 0.02 m², ρ = 1000 kg/m³. => The restoring force is F = −ρ g A_b x, which has the form −k x with k = ρ g A_b. So SHM with ω = √(ρ g A_b ÷ m). k = 1000 × 9.8 × 0.02 = 196 N/m. ω = √(196 ÷ 2) = 9.9 rad/s. T = 2π ÷ 9.9 = **0.635 s**.
