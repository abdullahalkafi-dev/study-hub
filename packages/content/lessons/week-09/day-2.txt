# Trigonometric integrals. Then: energy in simple harmonic motion
lead: Sines and cosines appear everywhere in physics and engineering, so you need to integrate them confidently, even when they are squared, cubed or multiplied together. A handful of identities do almost all the work. They also explain why the average of sin² is exactly one half, which is the reason AC voltage is quoted as an RMS value, and why a vibrating spring swaps energy evenly between motion and stretch.

BN: সাইন-কোসাইন পদার্থবিজ্ঞান ও প্রকৌশলের সর্বত্র, তাই বর্গ, ঘন বা গুণফল অবস্থাতেও এদের সমাকলন জানা দরকার। কয়েকটা অভেদই প্রায় সব কাজ করে। এরাই ব্যাখ্যা করে কেন sin² এর গড় ঠিক ½, আর কেন AC ভোল্টেজ RMS মানে বলা হয়, এবং কেন স্পন্দনরত স্প্রিং গতি ও টানের মধ্যে শক্তি সমানভাবে বদলায়।

@section math | 2 · Math: trigonometric integrals | 55 min

### The identities to know
| Identity | Use |
| sin² x + cos² x = 1 | Turn an even power into the other function |
| sin² x = (1 − cos 2x) ÷ 2 | Integrate even powers |
| cos² x = (1 + cos 2x) ÷ 2 | Integrate even powers |
| sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x | Combine products |
| sin A sin B = ½ [cos(A − B) − cos(A + B)] | Products of different frequencies |
| sin A cos B = ½ [sin(A + B) + sin(A − B)] | Products of different frequencies |
| cos A cos B = ½ [cos(A − B) + cos(A + B)] | Products of different frequencies |

BN: মনে রাখার অভেদ: sin² + cos² = 1; sin²x = (1 − cos 2x)/2; cos²x = (1 + cos 2x)/2; sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x। ভিন্ন কম্পাঙ্কের গুণফলকে যোগে বদলানো যায়: sin A sin B = ½[cos(A − B) − cos(A + B)]।

### Even powers: half-angle identities
EX: Squares
1. ∫ sin² x dx = ∫ (1 − cos 2x) ÷ 2 dx = **x/2 − sin 2x ÷ 4 + C**.
2. ∫ cos² x dx = **x/2 + sin 2x ÷ 4 + C**.
3. ∫₀^π sin² x dx = [x/2 − sin 2x ÷ 4] = **π/2**. So the average of sin² over a half period is (π/2) ÷ π = **1/2**.
4. ∫₀^(π/2) sin⁴ x dx: sin⁴ x = (1 − cos 2x)² ÷ 4 = (3 − 4 cos 2x + cos 4x) ÷ 8, so the integral = (3π/2) ÷ 8 = **3π/16 ≈ 0.589**. (The cos terms integrate to zero over this interval.)
5. ∫ sin² x cos² x dx = ∫ (sin 2x)² ÷ 4 dx = ∫ (1 − cos 4x) ÷ 8 dx = **x/8 − sin 4x ÷ 32 + C**.
/EX

### Odd powers: save one factor
If one power is odd, peel off one factor and use sin² = 1 − cos² (or the reverse) so that a substitution works.

EX: Cubes and mixes
1. ∫ sin³ x dx = ∫ (1 − cos² x) sin x dx. Let u = cos x, du = −sin x dx: −∫ (1 − u²) du = −u + u³/3 = **−cos x + cos³ x ÷ 3 + C**.
2. ∫ sin² x cos³ x dx = ∫ sin² x (1 − sin² x) cos x dx. Let u = sin x: ∫ (u² − u⁴) du = **sin³ x ÷ 3 − sin⁵ x ÷ 5 + C**.
3. ∫₀^(π/2) sin³ x cos² x dx: u = cos x from 1 to 0: ∫₀¹ (1 − u²) u² du = 1/3 − 1/5 = **2/15**.
4. ∫ sin x cos x dx = **sin² x ÷ 2 + C** (u = sin x).
/EX

### Other basics
EX: Tangent and secant
1. ∫ sec² x dx = tan x + C. Since tan² x = sec² x − 1: ∫ tan² x dx = **tan x − x + C**.
2. ∫₀^(π/4) tan² x dx = [tan x − x] = 1 − π/4 = **0.2146**.
3. ∫ sec x dx = ln abs(sec x + tan x) + C (standard result, to be memorised).
/EX

### Products of different frequencies: orthogonality
EX: Why sines of different frequencies "don't see each other"
1. ∫₀^(2π) sin(m x) sin(n x) dx with m, n whole numbers. Use sin A sin B = ½ [cos((m − n)x) − cos((m + n)x)].
2. If m ≠ n, both cosine terms integrate over a full number of periods and give **0**.
3. If m = n, the first cosine becomes cos 0 = 1, so we get ½ ∫ (1 − cos 2mx) dx = **π**.
4. Likewise ∫₀^(2π) sin(m x) cos(n x) dx = **0** for all whole m, n. This "orthogonality" is the key that makes Fourier series work in Phase 3: you can pick out each frequency separately by integrating.
/EX

BN: জোড় ঘাত: half-angle অভেদে ভেঙে নাও: ∫sin²x dx = x/2 − sin2x/4। বিজোড় ঘাত: একটা গুণক আলাদা করে বাকিটাকে অন্যটার ঘাতে লিখো, তারপর প্রতিস্থাপন (u = cos x ইত্যাদি)। ভিন্ন কম্পাঙ্কের sin(mx)sin(nx) এর 0 থেকে 2π সমাকলন m ≠ n হলে 0, m = n হলে π: এই লম্বতা (orthogonality) পরে ফুরিয়ার সিরিজের ভিত্তি।

TRAP: Never write ∫ sin² x dx = sin³ x ÷ 3. The power rule works only if the derivative of the inside is present. Use the half-angle identity. When using sin 2x forms, remember the **inside factor**: ∫ cos 2x dx = sin 2x ÷ 2, not sin 2x. And for definite integrals over a whole number of periods, sin and cos integrate to **zero**, but sin² and cos² do not (they average to 1/2).

@section physics | 3 · Physics: energy in SHM | 35 min

### Two kinds of energy, one total
At displacement x the spring stores PE = ½ k x² and the mass has KE = ½ m v². With no friction their total never changes:

EQ: E = ½ m v² + ½ k x² = ½ k A²     so     v = ω √(A² − x²)

At the ends (x = ±A) all the energy is potential. At the centre it is all kinetic: ½ m v_max² = ½ k A², so v_max = A ω ✓. The energy depends on A², so doubling the amplitude quadruples the energy.

With x = A cos ωt: PE = ½ k A² cos² ωt and KE = ½ k A² sin² ωt. Both oscillate at twice the frequency, and **each averages to E/2** over a cycle, since sin² and cos² average to 1/2.

BN: যেকোনো মুহূর্তে PE = ½kx², KE = ½mv², এবং মোট E = ½kA²। দুই প্রান্তে সব স্থিতিশক্তি, কেন্দ্রে সব গতিশক্তি। দুটোই দ্বিগুণ কম্পাঙ্কে ওঠানামা করে এবং প্রতিটির গড় E/2। বিস্তার দ্বিগুণ করলে শক্তি চার গুণ। v = ω√(A² − x²) সূত্রে যেকোনো x এ বেগ পাওয়া যায়।

EX: Using energy
1. m = 0.5 kg, k = 200 N/m, A = 0.05 m. E = ½ × 200 × 0.0025 = **0.25 J**.
2. At x = 0.03 m: PE = ½ × 200 × 0.0009 = 0.09 J, so KE = 0.25 − 0.09 = 0.16 J and v = √(2 × 0.16 ÷ 0.5) = **0.8 m/s** ✓ (matches ω√(A² − x²) = 20 × 0.04).
3. KE = PE when ½ k x² = ¼ k A², so **x = A ÷ √2 = 0.707 A**.
4. At x = A ÷ 2: PE = E/4, so KE = 3E/4.
/EX

### Energy methods for springs
EX: A block hits a spring
1. A 2 kg block slides at 3 m/s on a smooth table into a spring with k = 800 N/m. KE → PE: ½ × 2 × 9 = ½ × 800 x², so x² = 9 ÷ 400 and **x = 0.15 m**.
/EX

EX: A ball dropped onto a spring
1. A 1 kg ball is released 0.5 m above a spring (k = 1000 N/m). It compresses the spring by x before stopping. Energy: m g (0.5 + x) = ½ k x², so 500 x² − 9.8 x − 4.9 = 0.
2. x = (9.8 + √(96.04 + 9800)) ÷ 1000 = (9.8 + 99.48) ÷ 1000 = **0.109 m**.
/EX

EX: A spring gun
1. A spring (k = 500 N/m) is compressed 0.1 m and launches a 0.05 kg ball horizontally. E = ½ × 500 × 0.01 = 2.5 J. v = √(2 × 2.5 ÷ 0.05) = **10 m/s**.
/EX

### A hanging spring: gravity changes nothing
For a vertical spring, measure displacement y from the **equilibrium** position (where k d = m g). The total potential energy (elastic plus gravitational) is ½ k (y + d)² − m g y = ½ k y² + k d y + ½ k d² − m g y = **½ k y² + const**, because k d = m g. So the energy equation has exactly the same form, with y in place of x.

### SHM and circular motion
Look at a point moving around a circle of radius A at angular speed ω. Its shadow on the x-axis is x = A cos(ωt + φ), the SHM solution. So SHM is the "side view" of uniform circular motion, and ω, T and f mean the same in both.

TRAP: Do not use v = ω √(A² − x²) with the wrong amplitude (A is the maximum displacement, not the total spread). The energy is ½ k A², not ½ k (2A)². And a vertical spring's x is measured from the **equilibrium**, not from the unstretched length. If you use the unstretched length you must include gravity.

### Recap
- Trig integrals: even powers by half-angle identities (sin² = (1 − cos 2x) ÷ 2), odd powers by saving one factor, products of different frequencies by sum identities. Orthogonality: ∫₀^(2π) sin mx sin nx dx = 0 (m ≠ n) or π (m = n). The average of sin² is 1/2, so RMS = peak ÷ √2.
- SHM energy: E = ½ k A² = ½ m v² + ½ k x², v = ω √(A² − x²). KE and PE each average E/2. Vertical springs: use displacement from equilibrium.
