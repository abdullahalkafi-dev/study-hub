# Improper integrals. Then: damped oscillations and shock absorbers
lead: What is the area under a curve that goes on forever? Sometimes it is finite, which sounds impossible but is true, and sometimes it is infinite. Improper integrals settle which. They give you the energy needed to escape the Earth, the total charge that flows through a decaying current, and the foundations of probability and Laplace transforms. In physics, you add friction to the oscillator: damping, the reason a car does not bounce for ever.

BN: যে বক্ররেখা অনন্ত পর্যন্ত গেছে তার নিচের ক্ষেত্রফল কত? কখনো সসীম (শুনতে অসম্ভব, কিন্তু সত্যি), কখনো অসীম। অপ্রকৃত সমাকলন (improper integral) এটা ঠিক করে। এর সাহায্যে পাওয়া যায় পৃথিবী ছেড়ে পালানোর শক্তি, ক্ষয়িষ্ণু প্রবাহের মোট চার্জ, আর সম্ভাব্যতা ও ল্যাপলাস রূপান্তরের ভিত্তি। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে দোলকে ঘর্ষণ যোগ হবে: অবমন্দন (damping), যার কারণে গাড়ি চিরকাল লাফায় না।

@section math | 2 · Math: improper integrals | 55 min

### Two kinds of "improper"
An integral is **improper** if (1) a limit is infinity, or (2) the function blows up inside the interval. We handle both with a limit:

EQ: ∫ₐ^∞ f(x) dx = lim (t → ∞) ∫ₐᵗ f(x) dx

If the limit is a finite number, the integral **converges** to it. Otherwise it **diverges**.

BN: অপ্রকৃত সমাকলন দুই ধরনের: (১) সীমা অনন্ত, (২) ফাংশন ব্যবধানের ভেতরে অসীম হয়ে যায়। দুটোই লিমিট দিয়ে সামলাই: ∫ₐ^∞ f dx = lim(t → ∞) ∫ₐᵗ f dx। লিমিট সসীম হলে সমাকলন অভিসারী (converges), না হলে অপসারী (diverges)।

EX: Infinite upper limit
1. ∫₁^∞ 1 ÷ x² dx = lim [−1/x] from 1 to t = lim (1 − 1/t) = **1**. The infinite region has finite area 1.
2. ∫₁^∞ 1 ÷ x dx = lim [ln x] from 1 to t = lim ln t = **∞**. Diverges, but only barely: 1/x falls too slowly.
3. ∫₀^∞ e^(−x) dx = lim [−e^(−x)] from 0 to t = **1**.
4. ∫₀^∞ x e^(−x) dx: by parts, [−e^(−x)(x + 1)] from 0 to ∞ = 0 − (−1) = **1** (since t e^(−t) → 0).
5. ∫_(−∞)^∞ 1 ÷ (1 + x²) dx = [arctan x] = π/2 − (−π/2) = **π**.
/EX

EX: The function blows up
1. ∫₀¹ 1 ÷ √x dx = lim (s → 0⁺) [2√x] from s to 1 = **2**. Converges.
2. ∫₀¹ 1 ÷ x dx = lim (s → 0⁺) [ln x] from s to 1 = 0 − ln s = **∞**. Diverges.
3. ∫₀¹ ln x dx = [x ln x − x] from 0 to 1 = −1 − 0 = **−1** (since x ln x → 0 by L'Hôpital).
/EX

### The p-test
The family 1 ÷ xᵖ is the yardstick for all comparisons:

EQ: ∫₁^∞ 1 ÷ xᵖ dx = 1 ÷ (p − 1)  if p > 1,  and diverges if p ≤ 1

And ∫₀¹ 1 ÷ xᵖ dx converges (to 1 ÷ (1 − p)) if p < 1 and diverges if p ≥ 1. The boundary is p = 1 in both cases.

The second number shows the final total area, or 0 to mean that it diverges. | p:Power p:0.5:2.9:0.3:2 ;; b:Upper limit b:1:100:1:10 | (1-b^(1-p))/(p-1):Area from 1 to b:3 ;; (p>1)*(1/max(p-1,0.001)):Total area to infinity (0 means it diverges):3 | x^(-p) | 1,10 | 0,1

### Comparison and standard results
- If 0 ≤ f ≤ g and ∫ g converges, then ∫ f converges. If ∫ f diverges, then ∫ g diverges.
- ∫₀^∞ e^(−x²) dx = √π ÷ 2 (the Gaussian integral, central to statistics).
- ∫₀^∞ xⁿ e^(−x) dx = n! for whole n (the gamma function): n = 0 gives 1, n = 1 gives 1, n = 2 gives 2.

EX: Comparison in action
1. ∫₁^∞ 1 ÷ (x² + 1) dx: since 1 ÷ (x² + 1) ≤ 1 ÷ x² and ∫₁^∞ 1 ÷ x² converges, this converges (to π/4 ≈ 0.785).
2. ∫₂^∞ 1 ÷ (x² − 1) dx = [½ ln((x − 1) ÷ (x + 1))] = 0 − ½ ln(1/3) = **½ ln 3 ≈ 0.549**.
3. ∫₁^∞ (ln x) ÷ x² dx: by parts, [−(ln x) ÷ x − 1 ÷ x] from 1 to ∞ = 0 − (−1) = **1**.
/EX

EX: Gabriel's horn (a paradox)
1. Spin y = 1/x for x ≥ 1 about the x-axis. The volume is π ∫₁^∞ 1 ÷ x² dx = **π**, finite.
2. The surface area is at least 2π ∫₁^∞ 1 ÷ x dx = ∞, infinite. So the horn could be filled with a finite amount of paint, yet could never be painted on the outside with a finite amount. (A real horn has no thickness, so there is no contradiction.)
/EX

BN: p-পরীক্ষা: ∫₁^∞ 1/xᵖ dx অভিসারী যদি p > 1 (মান 1/(p−1)), অপসারী যদি p ≤ 1। সীমানা p = 1। ∫₀^∞ xⁿe^(−x) dx = n! (গামা ফাংশন), ∫₀^∞ e^(−x²) dx = √π/2। গ্যাব্রিয়েলের শিঙায় আয়তন সসীম (π) কিন্তু তলের ক্ষেত্রফল অসীম।

TRAP: Do not just plug ∞ into the antiderivative without a limit. And "the function goes to 0" is **not enough** for convergence: 1 ÷ x → 0 yet ∫ 1 ÷ x diverges. Also check for **hidden** problem points inside the interval: ∫₋₁¹ 1 ÷ x² dx is improper at 0, and it diverges (it is not −2).

@section physics | 3 · Physics: damped oscillations | 35 min

### Adding friction
A real oscillator loses energy. A shock absorber, a door closer and the air all produce a force that opposes the velocity: F = −c v (c is the damping coefficient, in N·s/m). Newton's second law becomes:

EQ: m x″ + c x′ + k x = 0

For light damping the solution is a sine wave inside a shrinking exponential:

EQ: x(t) = A e^(−γ t) cos(ω_d t)     with     γ = c ÷ (2m)     ω_d = √(ω₀² − γ²)     ω₀ = √(k ÷ m)

(Try x = e^(rt): then m r² + c r + k = 0, so r = −γ ± i ω_d. We will prove this properly in the differential equations weeks.)

The decay is set by γ: the amplitude drops by a factor e every 1 ÷ γ seconds, and halves every (ln 2) ÷ γ seconds.

**Three regimes**, set by the critical damping coefficient c_c = 2 √(k m):
- **Underdamped** (c < c_c): oscillates while fading.
- **Critically damped** (c = c_c): returns to rest fastest without overshooting.
- **Overdamped** (c > c_c): creeps back slowly.

Engineers describe it by the damping ratio ζ = c ÷ c_c.

BN: ঘর্ষণ থাকলে বল F = −cv, সমীকরণ mx″ + cx′ + kx = 0। হালকা অবমন্দনে সমাধান x = A e^(−γt) cos(ω_d t), γ = c/(2m), ω_d = √(ω₀² − γ²)। γ ঠিক করে ক্ষয়ের হার। তিন অবস্থা: c < c_c = 2√(km) হলে দুলতে দুলতে ক্ষয় (underdamped); c = c_c হলে সবচেয়ে দ্রুত স্থির, দোলে না (critical); c > c_c হলে ধীরে ফেরে (overdamped)। শক-অ্যাবজর্বার ডিজাইন করা হয় critical এর কাছাকাছি।

EX: A quarter of a car
1. m = 400 kg on a spring with k = 25,000 N/m. ω₀ = √62.5 = **7.91 rad/s**. The critical damping is c_c = 2 √(25,000 × 400) = **6325 N·s/m**.
2. A shock absorber with c = 3000 N·s/m: ζ = 3000 ÷ 6325 = **0.47**. γ = 3000 ÷ 800 = **3.75 per second**. ω_d = √(62.5 − 14.06) = **6.96 rad/s**, so T_d = 0.903 s.
3. The half-amplitude time is 0.693 ÷ 3.75 = **0.185 s**. After one full oscillation the amplitude is e^(−3.75 × 0.903) = **3.4%** of the start. The car bounces about once, then settles.
/EX

### Energy and the quality factor
The energy E = ½ m v² + ½ k x² now falls, and the rate is easy to find by differentiating:

EQ: dE/dt = m v a + k x v = v (m a + k x) = −c v²

It always decreases (never increases). For light damping the energy decays like E₀ e^(−2γt). The **quality factor** Q = ω₀ ÷ (2γ) tells you how many oscillations the system rings for: each cycle loses about 2π ÷ Q of its energy. A bell or a guitar string has Q in the hundreds, and a car suspension has Q of about 1.

EX: Energy decay
1. m = 1 kg, k = 400 N/m, c = 2 N·s/m, A = 0.1 m. γ = 1, E₀ = ½ × 400 × 0.01 = 2 J. After 1 s the energy is 2 e^(−2) = **0.27 J**.
2. Q = ω₀ ÷ (2γ) = 20 ÷ 2 = **10**. The exact loss per cycle is 1 − e^(−2γ T_d) = 1 − e^(−0.63) = **47%**. The shortcut 2π ÷ Q would give 63%, so it is only reliable for large Q.
3. A lightly damped oscillator with c = 0.2 (same m and k): γ = 0.1, Q = 100, and each cycle loses about 2π ÷ 100 = **6%** of its energy.
/EX

TRAP: Damping slows the oscillation too: ω_d is **smaller** than ω₀. Do not confuse γ = c ÷ (2m) with c ÷ m. The critical value is c_c = 2√(km), not √(km). And in the critically damped case the formula x = A e^(−γt) cos(ω_d t) no longer applies, since there is no oscillation at all.
