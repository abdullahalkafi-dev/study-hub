# Numerical integration and applications. Then: forced oscillations and resonance
lead: Most integrals that appear in real engineering have no neat formula, so computers approximate them. Today you learn the two methods behind that, the trapezoid rule and Simpson's rule, and see how accurate they are. You also use integrals for work, pumping and arc length. In physics, you push the oscillator with a periodic force and meet resonance, the reason bridges can sway, glasses can shatter and radios can tune in a station.

BN: বাস্তব প্রকৌশলের অধিকাংশ সমাকলনের সুন্দর সূত্র থাকে না, তাই কম্পিউটার আসন্ন মান বের করে। আজ শিখবে এর পেছনের দুই পদ্ধতি: ট্রাপিজয়েড নিয়ম ও সিম্পসনের নিয়ম, এবং এরা কতটা নিখুঁত। সঙ্গে কাজ, পাম্পিং ও বক্ররেখার দৈর্ঘ্যে সমাকলন। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে দোলকে পর্যায়ক্রমিক বল দেবে এবং অনুনাদ (resonance) দেখবে, যার কারণে সেতু দুলতে পারে, কাচ ভাঙতে পারে, আর রেডিও স্টেশন ধরতে পারে।

@section math | 2 · Math: numerical integration and applications | 55 min

### Why approximate
Some antiderivatives cannot be written with the standard functions (for example e^(−x²)), and real data come only as a table of values. Numerical integration replaces the curve by simple pieces we can integrate exactly.

BN: কিছু সমাকলনের প্রতিঅন্তরকলজ প্রচলিত ফাংশনে লেখা যায় না (যেমন e^(−x²)), আর বাস্তব উপাত্ত আসে সারণিতে। সংখ্যাভিত্তিক সমাকলন বক্ররেখাকে এমন সরল টুকরোয় বদলায় যা সহজে সমাকলন হয়।

### The trapezoid rule
Join the points by straight lines. With n slices of width h = (b − a) ÷ n:

EQ: Tₙ = (h ÷ 2) [f₀ + 2 f₁ + 2 f₂ + … + 2 fₙ₋₁ + fₙ]     (the ends count once, the middle values twice)

### Simpson's rule
Fit **parabolas** through each pair of slices. With n **even**:

EQ: Sₙ = (h ÷ 3) [f₀ + 4 f₁ + 2 f₂ + 4 f₃ + … + 4 fₙ₋₁ + fₙ]     (weights 1, 4, 2, 4, …, 2, 4, 1)

**Accuracy.** The trapezoid error shrinks like h² (double n, error ÷ 4). The Simpson error shrinks like **h⁴** (double n, error ÷ 16), and Simpson is exact for every polynomial up to degree 3.

EX: ∫₀¹ e^(−x²) dx with n = 4
1. h = 0.25. Values: f(0) = 1, f(0.25) = 0.93941, f(0.5) = 0.77880, f(0.75) = 0.56978, f(1) = 0.36788.
2. **Trapezoid:** 0.125 × [1 + 2 (0.93941 + 0.77880 + 0.56978) + 0.36788] = 0.125 × 5.94387 = **0.74298**.
3. **Simpson:** (0.25 ÷ 3) × [1 + 4 (0.93941 + 0.56978) + 2 (0.77880) + 0.36788] = 0.08333 × 8.96226 = **0.74686**.
4. The true value is 0.74682. Trapezoid is off by 0.0038, Simpson by only 0.00004.
/EX

EX: ∫₀^π sin x dx with n = 4
1. h = π/4. Values: 0, 0.7071, 1, 0.7071, 0.
2. Trapezoid: (π/8) × [0 + 2 (2.4142) + 0] = **1.896** (error 0.10).
3. Simpson: (π/12) × [0 + 4 (0.7071) + 2 (1) + 4 (0.7071) + 0] = 0.2618 × 7.657 = **2.0046** (error 0.005).
/EX

EX: Why Simpson is exact for parabolas
1. Take y = a x² + b x + c on [−h, h], using the points x = −h, 0, h. The true area is 2 a h³ ÷ 3 + 2 c h.
2. y(−h) + 4 y(0) + y(h) = (a h² − b h + c) + 4c + (a h² + b h + c) = 2 a h² + 6c. Times h ÷ 3: **2 a h³ ÷ 3 + 2 c h** ✓. The cubic term is odd and cancels, so Simpson is exact up to degree 3.
/EX

### Applications of the integral
**Work by a variable force:** W = ∫ F dx. **Pumping:** each thin layer is lifted a different distance, so W = ∫ (weight of layer) × (distance lifted). **Arc length:** a curve y = f(x) has length ∫ √(1 + (f′)²) dx.

EX: Pumping water
1. A cylindrical tank has radius 1 m and height 3 m and is full of water (ρ = 1000). A layer at height y (thickness dy) has weight ρ g π R² dy and must rise 3 − y.
2. W = ρ g π R² ∫₀³ (3 − y) dy = 1000 × 9.8 × π × (9/2) = **1.39 × 10⁵ J**.
/EX

EX: A hanging chain
1. A 5 m chain of mass 10 kg (λ = 2 kg/m) hangs over the edge. Pulling it up, the piece at distance y below the edge weighs λ g dy and rises y. W = ∫₀⁵ 2 × 9.8 × y dy = 19.6 × 25/2 = **245 J**. (Same as lifting the whole 98 N weight through half its length, 2.5 m ✓.)
/EX

EX: Length of a curve
1. y = x^(3/2) from x = 0 to 4. y′ = (3/2) x^(1/2), so 1 + y′² = 1 + 2.25 x.
2. L = ∫₀⁴ √(1 + 2.25x) dx. Substituting u = 1 + 2.25x (du = 2.25 dx): L = (1 ÷ 2.25)(2/3)[u^(3/2)] from 1 to 10 = 0.2963 × (31.62 − 1) = **9.07**. (The straight line from (0, 0) to (4, 8) has length 8.94, a little shorter ✓.)
/EX

BN: ট্রাপিজয়েড নিয়ম: Tₙ = (h/2)[f₀ + 2f₁ + … + 2fₙ₋₁ + fₙ] (ত্রুটি h² এর মতো কমে)। সিম্পসন (n জোড়): Sₙ = (h/3)[f₀ + 4f₁ + 2f₂ + … + 4fₙ₋₁ + fₙ] (ত্রুটি h⁴ এর মতো কমে; তিন ঘাত পর্যন্ত নিখুঁত)। প্রয়োগ: পরিবর্তনশীল বলের কাজ W = ∫F dx, পাম্পিংয়ে প্রতিটি স্তর আলাদা দূরত্বে ওঠে, বক্ররেখার দৈর্ঘ্য ∫√(1 + f′²) dx।

TRAP: Simpson needs an **even** number of slices, and the weights alternate 4, 2, 4, 2 (not all 4). Do not forget the factor **h ÷ 3** (Simpson) or **h ÷ 2** (trapezoid). A numerical answer without an error idea is half an answer: compare two values of n, and if they agree to the digits you need, stop.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: forced oscillations and resonance | 35 min

### Pushing an oscillator
Add a periodic driving force F₀ cos ωt to a damped oscillator:

EQ: m x″ + c x′ + k x = F₀ cos ωt

After the start-up wobble dies (the transient, which decays as e^(−γt)), the mass oscillates at the **driving** frequency ω with a constant amplitude:

EQ: A(ω) = F₀ ÷ √((k − m ω²)² + (c ω)²)

**Resonance:** when the drive frequency is close to the natural frequency ω₀ = √(k ÷ m), the first term in the square root nearly vanishes and the amplitude is large. At ω = ω₀ exactly:

EQ: A(ω₀) = F₀ ÷ (c ω₀) = Q × (F₀ ÷ k)     with     Q = ω₀ ÷ (2γ) = m ω₀ ÷ c

So the amplitude is **Q times the static deflection** F₀ ÷ k. A lightly damped system (large Q) responds enormously.

**Phase:** well below resonance the mass moves with the force. Exactly at resonance it lags by 90°. Well above resonance it moves against the force (180°).

BN: পর্যায়ক্রমিক বল F₀ cos ωt যোগ করলে স্থায়ী অবস্থায় ভর চালক কম্পাঙ্ক ω তেই দোলে, বিস্তার A = F₀/√((k − mω²)² + (cω)²)। ω ≈ ω₀ = √(k/m) হলে অনুনাদ (resonance): বিস্তার = F₀/(cω₀) = Q × (F₀/k)। অবমন্দন কম (Q বড়) হলে সাড়া বিশাল। ফেজ: অনুনাদের অনেক নিচে বলের সাথে, ঠিক অনুনাদে ৯০° পিছিয়ে, অনেক ওপরে বিপরীতে।

EX: A driven oscillator
1. m = 1 kg, k = 400 N/m, c = 2 N·s/m, F₀ = 10 N. ω₀ = 20 rad/s. The static deflection is F₀ ÷ k = **0.025 m**. Q = 20 ÷ 2 = 10.
2. At ω = 10: (k − m ω²) = 300, c ω = 20, A = 10 ÷ √(90,000 + 400) = **0.033 m**.
3. At ω = 20 (resonance): A = 10 ÷ (2 × 20) = **0.25 m**, ten times the static deflection.
4. At ω = 40: (k − m ω²) = −1200, c ω = 80, A = 10 ÷ √(1,440,000 + 6400) = **0.0083 m**. The fast pushing barely moves the mass.
/EX

### Resonance in the real world
- **Washboard roads.** The quarter-car (m = 400 kg, k = 25,000 N/m) has ω₀ = 7.91 rad/s, so f₀ = 7.91 ÷ 2π = 1.26 Hz. Bumps 2 m apart excite it when v = f λ = 1.26 × 2 = **2.5 m/s (9 km/h)**: the car shakes violently at that speed but is calmer both slower and faster.
- **Bridges.** In 1940 the Tacoma Narrows Bridge twisted itself apart in a steady wind. On opening day in 2000, London's Millennium Bridge swayed sideways because walkers fell into step with its sway. Soldiers break step when crossing bridges.
- **Tuning.** A radio circuit is a driven oscillator: the tuning knob changes ω₀ to match one station's frequency, and the high Q rejects the neighbours.
- **Glass.** A singing voice at a wine glass's natural frequency, with enough power, can shatter it.
- **Damping design.** A building in an earthquake zone needs enough damping (or a tuned mass damper) so that its large Q cannot build up.

TRAP: Resonance is not exactly at ω₀ when damping is large: the peak shifts slightly lower, to ω² = ω₀² − 2γ². For light damping the difference is tiny. Also do not forget the **phase**, and remember that the amplitude is for the steady state: right after switching on, the transient makes the motion messy for a few 1 ÷ γ seconds.

### Recap
- Trapezoid: Tₙ = (h ÷ 2)[f₀ + 2f₁ + … + fₙ], error ~ h². Simpson: Sₙ = (h ÷ 3)[f₀ + 4f₁ + 2f₂ + … + fₙ], n even, error ~ h⁴, exact up to cubics. Applications: work ∫ F dx, pumping, arc length ∫ √(1 + f′²) dx.
- Driven oscillator: m x″ + c x′ + k x = F₀ cos ωt. Steady amplitude A = F₀ ÷ √((k − mω²)² + (cω)²). At resonance A = Q F₀ ÷ k, with Q = mω₀ ÷ c. Peak at ω² = ω₀² − 2γ².

BN: আজকের সারকথা: ট্রাপিজয়েড Tₙ = (h/2)[f₀ + 2f₁ + … + fₙ] (ত্রুটি ~h²); সিম্পসন Sₙ = (h/3)[f₀ + 4f₁ + 2f₂ + … + fₙ] (ত্রুটি ~h⁴, তিন ঘাত পর্যন্ত নিখুঁত)। চালিত দোলক: A = F₀/√((k − mω²)² + (cω)²), অনুনাদে A = Q·F₀/k, Q = mω₀/c।
