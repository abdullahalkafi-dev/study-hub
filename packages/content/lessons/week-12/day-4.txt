### The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra
Every polynomial of degree n ≥ 1 with complex coefficients has **exactly n complex roots**, counted with multiplicity. So it factors completely:

EQ: aₙ zⁿ + … + a₀ = aₙ (z − r₁)(z − r₂) … (z − rₙ)

You never need numbers beyond the complex ones. Two consequences you will use:

1. For real coefficients, non-real roots come in **conjugate pairs**, so an odd-degree real polynomial always has at least one real root.
2. **Vieta's formulas:** for z² + b z + c: r₁ + r₂ = −b and r₁ r₂ = c. In general the sum of roots is −aₙ₋₁ ÷ aₙ and the product is (−1)ⁿ a₀ ÷ aₙ.

BN: বীজগণিতের মূল উপপাদ্য: n ঘাতের প্রতিটি বহুপদীর ঠিক n টা জটিল মূল আছে (পুনরাবৃত্তিসহ)। বাস্তব সহগে অবাস্তব মূল অনুবন্ধী জোড়ায় আসে, তাই বিজোড় ঘাতে অন্তত একটা বাস্তব মূল থাকবেই। ভিয়েতা: z² + bz + c এর মূলের যোগফল −b, গুণফল c।

EX: Factoring
1. z³ − 1 = (z − 1)(z² + z + 1). Roots: **1** and (−1 ± i√3) ÷ 2 (the cube roots of unity).
2. z⁴ − 16 = (z² − 4)(z² + 4): roots **±2** and **±2i**.
3. z³ − 3z² + 7z − 5 has roots 1 and 1 ± 2i: (z − 1)(z² − 2z + 5) = z³ − 2z² + 5z − z² + 2z − 5 = z³ − 3z² + 7z − 5 ✓. Vieta: sum = 1 + 2 = 3 ✓, product = 1 × 5 = 5 ✓.
4. For z² − 4z + 13 (roots 2 ± 3i): sum 4 ✓, product 4 + 9 = 13 ✓.
/EX

### The complex exponential
Define e^z for z = x + i y by

EQ: e^(x + i y) = e^x (cos y + i sin y)

Its modulus is e^x and its argument is y. It satisfies the usual rules: e^(z + w) = e^z e^w, and d/dz e^z = e^z. The big new fact: e^z is **periodic**, with period 2πi, since e^(z + 2πi) = e^z.

EX: Evaluating
1. e^(iπ) = −1 and e^(2πi) = 1.
2. e^(1 + iπ/2) = e × i = **2.718 i**.
3. e^(−0.5 + 3i): modulus e^(−0.5) = **0.6065**, argument **3 rad** (171.9°).
4. Solve e^z = −1: modulus e^x = 1 so x = 0, and y = π + 2πk. So **z = iπ(2k + 1)**: infinitely many solutions.
/EX

### Why this matters: damped oscillations
The function x(t) = Re e^((−γ + iω) t) = e^(−γ t) cos(ω t) is exactly a damped oscillation: the real part of the exponent gives decay, the imaginary part gives oscillation. In Weeks 15 and 16 this will solve differential equations.

EX: A damped wave
1. z = (−0.5 + 3 i) t. At t = 2: e^(−1) = 0.3679 and the angle is 6 rad.
2. x(2) = 0.3679 × cos 6 = 0.3679 × 0.9602 = **0.353**.
3. The envelope e^(−0.5 t) halves every ln 2 ÷ 0.5 = **1.386 s**.
/EX

### The complex logarithm
Since e^(ln r + iθ) = r e^(iθ), we define

EQ: ln z = ln r + i (θ + 2πk)

It is many-valued because θ is only defined up to 2π. The **principal value** takes −π < θ ≤ π.

EX: Logarithms of "impossible" numbers
1. ln(−1) = ln 1 + iπ = **iπ**. And ln(i) = **iπ/2**.
2. ln(−e) = 1 + iπ.
3. **i^i:** i^i = e^(i ln i) = e^(i × iπ/2) = e^(−π/2) = **0.2079**, a real number!
4. Check by reversing: ln(i^i) = i ln i = −π/2 ✓.
/EX

EX: Trig functions as exponentials
1. cos θ = (e^(iθ) + e^(−iθ)) ÷ 2 and sin θ = (e^(iθ) − e^(−iθ)) ÷ 2i.
2. With θ = i y: cos(i y) = (e^(−y) + e^(y)) ÷ 2 = cosh y. So cos(2i) = cosh 2 = **3.762**, which exceeds 1. Cosine is unbounded over the complex numbers.
/EX

BN: e^(x+iy) = e^x(cos y + i sin y): দৈর্ঘ্য e^x, কোণ y। e^z এর পর্যায়কাল 2πi। e^((−γ+iω)t) এর বাস্তব অংশ e^(−γt)cos ωt, অর্থাৎ ক্ষয়িষ্ণু দোলন। ln z = ln r + iθ (বহুমান)। ln(−1) = iπ, আর i^i = e^(−π/2) ≈ 0.2079, একটা বাস্তব সংখ্যা।

TRAP: ln z is **many-valued**, so rules such as ln(z²) = 2 ln z can fail by multiples of 2πi. Also, e^z has no real-looking "growth only" behaviour: its real part can oscillate wildly. And a polynomial of degree n has n roots **counted with multiplicity**: (z − 1)² has the root 1 twice.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: viscosity and turbulence | 35 min

### Viscosity: friction inside a fluid
Bernoulli ignored friction. Real fluids resist flow: layers moving at different speeds drag on each other. The **viscosity** η (Pa·s) measures this.

| Fluid | η (Pa·s) |
| Air | 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ |
| Water (20 °C) | 1.0 × 10⁻³ |
| Blood | 3.5 × 10⁻³ |
| Olive oil | 0.08 |
| Glycerine | 1.4 |
| Honey | about 10 |

BN: সান্দ্রতা η হলো তরলের ভেতরের ঘর্ষণ। বিভিন্ন বেগের স্তরগুলো একে অপরকে টানে। পানির η = 10⁻³, মধুর প্রায় 10, বাতাসের 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s।

### Poiseuille's law: flow in a pipe
For smooth (laminar) flow of a viscous fluid in a pipe of radius r and length L under a pressure difference ΔP:

EQ: Q = π r⁴ ΔP ÷ (8 η L)

The huge result is the **fourth power** of the radius: the speed profile is a parabola, with the fluid at the wall at rest, and flow is dominated by the centre.

EX: Flow in a tube
1. Water, r = 1 mm, L = 0.5 m, ΔP = 1000 Pa: Q = π × 10⁻¹² × 1000 ÷ (8 × 10⁻³ × 0.5) = π × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 4 × 10⁻³ = **7.85 × 10⁻⁷ m³/s = 0.785 mL/s**.
2. Doubling the radius multiplies Q by 2⁴ = **16**.
3. **A narrowed artery.** If plaque narrows an artery radius by 20% (r → 0.8 r), then r⁴ falls to 0.4096: the flow drops to **41%** at the same pressure. To keep the same flow, the heart must raise ΔP by 1 ÷ 0.4096 = **2.44 times**. This is why even modest narrowing is dangerous.
/EX

### Laminar and turbulent flow
At low speed the flow is smooth in layers (**laminar**). At high speed it becomes irregular with eddies (**turbulent**). The dimensionless **Reynolds number** decides which:

EQ: Re = ρ v D ÷ η

For a pipe, Re below about 2000 is laminar and above about 4000 is turbulent. Re compares inertia (ρ v²) to viscous forces (η v ÷ D).

EX: Reynolds numbers
1. Water in a 2 cm pipe at 1 m/s: Re = 1000 × 1 × 0.02 ÷ 10⁻³ = **20,000**, turbulent (tap water at full flow).
2. **Blood in the aorta:** ρ = 1060, v = 0.3 m/s, D = 0.02 m, η = 3.5 × 10⁻³: Re = 1060 × 0.3 × 0.02 ÷ 3.5 × 10⁻³ = **1817**, near the limit. Exercise or narrowing can make the flow turbulent, which causes the murmur a doctor hears with a stethoscope.
3. **Honey** poured from a spoon: ρ = 1400, v = 0.1, D = 0.01, η = 10: Re = **0.14**: smooth and slow.
/EX

### Drag and terminal speed
For slow flow around a sphere of radius r (Re ≪ 1), **Stokes' law**: F = 6π η r v. For fast motion (Re large), the drag is quadratic: F = ½ ρ C_d A v².

EX: Terminal speeds
1. **Stokes.** A steel ball (ρ = 7800, r = 2 mm) falling in glycerine (ρ = 1260, η = 1.4): at terminal speed, weight − buoyancy = drag, so v = 2 r² (ρ_s − ρ_f) g ÷ (9 η) = 2 × 4 × 10⁻⁶ × 6540 × 9.8 ÷ 12.6 = **0.0407 m/s = 4.07 cm/s**. Check: Re = 1260 × 0.0407 × 0.004 ÷ 1.4 = 0.15 ≪ 1 ✓.
2. **Drizzle:** r = 0.1 mm in air (η = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵): v = 2 × 10⁻⁸ × 998.8 × 9.8 ÷ (9 × 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) = **1.2 m/s**, a gentle fall.
3. **Skydiver** (quadratic drag): m = 80 kg, C_d = 1.0, A = 0.7 m²: v = √(2 m g ÷ (ρ C_d A)) = √(1568 ÷ 0.84) = **43.2 m/s** (about 155 km/h). With a parachute (A = 25 m², C_d = 1.5): v = √(1568 ÷ 45) = **5.9 m/s**.
/EX

TRAP: Poiseuille's law needs **laminar** flow: check that Re is below 2000 before you use r⁴. Stokes' law needs Re well below 1: it works for drizzle but not for bullets. And do not use viscosity values for water when the fluid is warm blood or cold oil: η changes strongly with temperature.
