# Complex numbers in engineering: impedance and phasors. Then: fluids in motion
lead: Complex numbers earn their keep in electrical engineering. A resistor, a coil and a capacitor all become "complex resistances" and an AC circuit turns into ordinary algebra. In physics, you move from fluids at rest to fluids in motion, with two powerful rules: the continuity equation (what goes in must come out) and Bernoulli's equation (energy conservation for a flowing fluid).

BN: জটিল সংখ্যা তড়িৎ প্রকৌশলে সবচেয়ে কাজে লাগে। রোধ, কুণ্ডলী আর ধারক সবই "জটিল রোধ" হয়ে যায় এবং AC বর্তনী সাধারণ বীজগণিতে নেমে আসে। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে স্থির তরল থেকে এবার চলমান তরলে যাবে, দুটো শক্তিশালী নিয়ম নিয়ে: ধারাবাহিকতা সমীকরণ (যা ঢোকে তা বেরোয়) ও বার্নুলির সমীকরণ (প্রবাহী তরলের শক্তির সংরক্ষণ)।

@section math | 2 · Math: phasors and impedance | 55 min

### AC signals as rotating arrows
An AC voltage v(t) = V₀ cos(ω t + φ) is the real part of a rotating complex number V₀ e^(i(ω t + φ)). Since every signal in a linear circuit has the same ω, we can drop the common factor e^(iωt) and keep just the **phasor**

EQ: V = V₀ e^(iφ)     (a complex number with the amplitude as its length and the phase as its angle)

Two things become easy:

1. **Adding sinusoids** is adding complex numbers.
2. **Differentiating** is multiplying by iω, since d/dt e^(iωt) = iω e^(iωt). Integrating is dividing by iω.

BN: AC ভোল্টেজ v = V₀cos(ωt + φ) হলো ঘূর্ণায়মান তীর V₀e^(i(ωt+φ)) এর বাস্তব অংশ। সব সংকেতের ω একই বলে সেটা বাদ দিয়ে শুধু ফেজর V = V₀e^(iφ) রাখা যায়। যোগ করা মানে জটিল সংখ্যা যোগ, অবকলন মানে iω দিয়ে গুণ, সমাকলন মানে iω দিয়ে ভাগ।

EX: Adding two sinusoids
1. Combine 3 cos ωt + 4 sin ωt. Since sin ωt = cos(ωt − 90°), the phasors are 3 and 4 e^(−i 90°) = −4i.
2. Sum: 3 − 4i. Modulus √(9 + 16) = 5, angle atan(−4 ÷ 3) = −53.13°.
3. So 3 cos ωt + 4 sin ωt = **5 cos(ωt − 53.13°)**. Test at t = 0: left 3, right 5 cos 53.13° = 5 × 0.6 = 3 ✓.
/EX

### Impedance: Ohm's law for AC
The relation V = Z I holds with a complex **impedance** Z. For the three basic components, using v = L di/dt and i = C dv/dt:

| Component | Impedance Z | Meaning |
| Resistor R | R | Voltage in phase with current |
| Inductor L | i ω L | Voltage leads the current by 90° |
| Capacitor C | 1 ÷ (i ω C) = −i ÷ (ω C) | Voltage lags the current by 90° |

The imaginary part of Z is the **reactance** X, so Z = R + i X, with X = ω L − 1 ÷ (ω C) for a series RLC circuit.

EQ: abs(Z) = √(R² + X²)     phase φ = atan(X ÷ R)     I₀ = V₀ ÷ abs(Z)

PREDICT: In a series circuit the inductor's reactance is +40 Ω and the capacitor's is −25 Ω. Do they add up or partly cancel? What is the total reactance? => They partly cancel: X = 40 − 25 = **+15 Ω**. The signs are opposite because the coil leads and the capacitor lags.

EX: A series RLC circuit
1. R = 30 Ω, L = 0.1 H, C = 100 μF = 10⁻⁴ F, ω = 400 rad/s. Then ωL = 40 Ω and 1 ÷ (ωC) = 1 ÷ (400 × 10⁻⁴) = 25 Ω.
2. Z = 30 + i (40 − 25) = **30 + 15 i Ω**. abs(Z) = √(900 + 225) = √1125 = **33.54 Ω**. Phase = atan(15 ÷ 30) = **26.57°** (voltage leads current).
3. With a 100 V amplitude supply: I₀ = 100 ÷ 33.54 = **2.98 A**, and the current lags the voltage by 26.57°.
4. **Resonance** at ω₀ = 1 ÷ √(L C) = 1 ÷ √(0.1 × 10⁻⁴) = 1 ÷ √(10⁻⁵) = **316.2 rad/s**. There X = 0, Z = R = 30 Ω, the current is largest (3.33 A).
/EX

### Parallel and series combinations
Impedances combine like resistors: series add, parallel use 1 ÷ Z = 1 ÷ Z₁ + 1 ÷ Z₂.

EX: Parallel
1. Z₁ = 3 + 4 i and Z₂ = 3 − 4 i. Series: Z = 6 Ω. Parallel: Z = Z₁ Z₂ ÷ (Z₁ + Z₂) = (9 + 16) ÷ 6 = 25 ÷ 6 = **4.167 Ω**.
2. A resistor R in parallel with a capacitor: 1 ÷ Z = 1 ÷ R + i ω C. This is the basis of a low-pass filter.
/EX

EX: An RC filter
1. R = 1 kΩ, C = 1 μF in series. The output across C is V_out ÷ V_in = (1 ÷ iωC) ÷ (R + 1 ÷ iωC) = 1 ÷ (1 + i ω R C).
2. Magnitude = 1 ÷ √(1 + (ω R C)²). The **cutoff** is where ω R C = 1: ω_c = 1 ÷ (R C) = **1000 rad/s** (159 Hz). There the magnitude is 1 ÷ √2 = **0.707**, and the phase is −45°.
3. At ω = 10,000 rad/s: ωRC = 10, magnitude = 1 ÷ √101 = **0.0995**: high frequencies are cut.
/EX

BN: প্রতিবন্ধকতা (impedance): রোধ R, কুণ্ডলী iωL, ধারক 1/(iωC)। Z = R + iX। |Z| = √(R² + X²), ফেজ = atan(X/R)। অনুনাদে X = 0, ω₀ = 1/√(LC)। RC ফিল্টারে কাটঅফ ω_c = 1/(RC), সেখানে মান 0.707 ও ফেজ −45°।

TRAP: Never add amplitudes of out-of-phase signals directly: 3 V and 4 V in quadrature combine to **5 V**, not 7 V. And do not forget the signs: capacitive reactance is **negative** in Z = R + i (ωL − 1 ÷ ωC).

@section physics | 3 · Physics: fluids in motion | 35 min

### Continuity: what goes in must come out
For an incompressible fluid, the volume flow rate is the same at every point along a pipe:

EQ: Q = A v = constant     so     A₁ v₁ = A₂ v₂

The fluid speeds up in a narrow section.

EX: Flow rate
1. A pipe of diameter 2 cm carries water at 3 m/s. A = π (0.01)² = 3.14 × 10⁻⁴ m². **Q = 9.42 × 10⁻⁴ m³/s = 0.942 L/s**.
2. **Garden hose:** pipe radius 1.0 cm narrows to a nozzle of radius 0.25 cm: the area ratio is 16. Water at 1 m/s in the hose leaves the nozzle at **16 m/s**.
3. **Blood:** the aorta (radius 1.0 cm) carries blood at 0.30 m/s: Q = π (0.01)² × 0.30 = 9.42 × 10⁻⁵ m³/s = **5.65 L/min**, matching the heart's output. The capillaries have a total area about 2000 times larger, so blood moves at 0.30 ÷ 2000 = **0.15 mm/s**, slow enough to exchange oxygen.
/EX

BN: ধারাবাহিকতা: সংনম্য নয় এমন তরলের জন্য Q = Av ধ্রুব, তাই A₁v₁ = A₂v₂। সরু জায়গায় তরল দ্রুত চলে। পাইপের ব্যাসার্ধ অর্ধেক হলে ক্ষেত্রফল চার ভাগের এক ভাগ, তাই বেগ চার গুণ।

### Bernoulli: energy for a flowing fluid
Apply the work-energy theorem to a small parcel of fluid. Along a streamline (steady, frictionless, incompressible flow):

EQ: P + ½ ρ v² + ρ g h = constant

Pressure energy, kinetic energy and gravitational energy per unit volume trade off. **Where the fluid is fast, the pressure is low.**

EX: Applications
1. **Venturi tube.** A horizontal pipe narrows from 20 cm² to 10 cm². At v₁ = 2 m/s, v₂ = 4 m/s. ΔP = ½ ρ (v₂² − v₁²) = 0.5 × 1000 × (16 − 4) = **6000 Pa**, lower in the narrow part. A manometer measures the flow speed this way.
2. **Airplane wing.** Air flows at 70 m/s over the top and 60 m/s below. ΔP = ½ × 1.2 × (4900 − 3600) = 0.6 × 1300 = **780 Pa**. On a wing area of 16 m² the lift is 780 × 16 = **12,480 N**, enough to support about 1270 kg.
3. **Torricelli's law.** A hole at depth h below the surface of an open tank. At the surface P = P₀, v ≈ 0, height h above the hole. At the hole P = P₀. So ρ g h = ½ ρ v² and **v = √(2 g h)**, the speed of a body falling through h. For h = 5 m: v = √98 = **9.90 m/s**.
/EX

### Draining a tank: a differential equation
The tank has cross-section A, the hole has area a, the water height is h. Continuity: A dh/dt = −a √(2 g h). This is separable: dh ÷ √h = −(a ÷ A) √(2 g) dt, so 2 (√h − √h₀) = −(a ÷ A) √(2 g) t. The tank empties (h = 0) at

EQ: T = (A ÷ a) × 2 √h₀ ÷ √(2 g)

EX: How long?
1. A = 1 m², a = 1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m², h₀ = 2 m. T = 10⁴ × 2 × 1.4142 ÷ 4.427 = **6389 s ≈ 1 h 46 min**.
2. From the solution, √h = √h₀ (1 − t ÷ T). Half the height means √h = 0.707 √h₀, so t = 0.293 T, about **29%** of the total time. So the **upper half drains faster** than the lower half.
/EX

TRAP: Bernoulli applies along a **streamline** with no friction, so it fails for thick oil or when turbulence is violent. Do not say "the pressure is higher where the fluid is slow" and then forget the height term: a fluid that rises loses pressure even at the same speed. And in Torricelli's law the exit speed depends on the **depth below the surface**, not the total height of the tank.
