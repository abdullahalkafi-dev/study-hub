@section math | 2 · Part A: Complex numbers (14 marks, about 55 min) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 13
P: (1 mark) Compute (5 + i)(2 − 3i). => 10 − 15i + 2i − 3i² = 10 − 13i + 3 = **13 − 13i**.
P: (1 mark) Write (7 + i) ÷ (1 − i) in the form a + bi. => Multiply top and bottom by 1 + i: (7 + 7i + i + i²) ÷ 2 = (6 + 8i) ÷ 2 = **3 + 4i**.
P: (1 mark) Solve z² − 6z + 13 = 0. => z = (6 ± √(36 − 52)) ÷ 2 = (6 ± 4i) ÷ 2 = **3 ± 2i**.
P: (1 mark) Write −√3 + i in polar form. => r = √(3 + 1) = 2. The point is in quadrant 2 with reference angle 30°, so θ = **150°**. Answer **2 e^(i 150°)**.
P: (1 mark) Compute (1 + i)¹². => r = √2, θ = 45°. r¹² = 64 and 12 × 45° = 540° ≡ 180°. Result **−64**.
P: (1 mark) Find all cube roots of −27. => r = 27, θ = 180°, so the cube roots have modulus 3 and angles 60°, 180°, 300°: **3/2 + (3√3/2) i, −3, 3/2 − (3√3/2) i**.
P: (1 mark) Describe the locus abs(z − 3i) = abs(z + 3i). => Points equally far from 3i and −3i: the perpendicular bisector, which is the **real axis** (Im z = 0).
P: (1 mark) Find e^(2 + iπ) in exact form. => e² (cos π + i sin π) = **−e²** (about −7.389).
P: (1 mark) Find the principal value of ln(−2). => ln r + iθ with r = 2, θ = π: **ln 2 + iπ** (about 0.693 + 3.142 i).
P: (1 mark) Solve (1 + i) z = 3 − i. => z = (3 − i)(1 − i) ÷ 2 = (3 − 3i − i + i²) ÷ 2 = (2 − 4i) ÷ 2 = **1 − 2i**.
P: (1 mark) Show that z = 2 is a root of z³ − 4z² + 9z − 10 and find the other two roots. => 8 − 16 + 18 − 10 = 0 ✓. Divide: z³ − 4z² + 9z − 10 = (z − 2)(z² − 2z + 5). The quadratic gives z = (2 ± √(4 − 20)) ÷ 2 = **1 ± 2i**.
P: (1 mark) Rotate the point 4 + 3i by 90° anticlockwise about the point 1 + i. => z − c = 3 + 2i. Times i: −2 + 3i. Add c: **−1 + 4i**.
P: (2 marks) A series circuit has R = 8 Ω, an inductive reactance of 12 Ω and a capacitive reactance of 6 Ω. Find Z in the form a + bi, its magnitude, the phase angle (give tan φ) and the current amplitude when the supply amplitude is 50 V. => Z = 8 + i (12 − 6) = **8 + 6i Ω** (1 mark). abs(Z) = √(64 + 36) = **10 Ω**, tan φ = 6 ÷ 8 = **0.75** (φ = 36.9°), I₀ = 50 ÷ 10 = **5 A** (1 mark).

@section physics | 3 · Part B: Fluids (6 marks, about 35 min) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 14 to 19
P: (1 mark) Find the gauge pressure and the absolute pressure in atmospheres at 25 m depth in seawater (ρ = 1025 kg/m³). => Gauge = 1025 × 9.8 × 25 = **251,125 Pa ≈ 251 kPa**. Absolute = 101,325 + 251,125 = 352,450 Pa = **3.48 atm**.
P: (1 mark) A hydraulic lift has pistons of radius 3 cm and 15 cm. What force on the small piston lifts a 1500 kg car, and how far must the small piston move to raise the car by 2 cm? => Area ratio = (15 ÷ 3)² = 25. Weight = 1500 × 9.8 = 14,700 N, so F = 14,700 ÷ 25 = **588 N**. Distance = 2 × 25 = **50 cm**.
P: (1 mark) A log has a volume of 0.4 m³ and a density of 650 kg/m³. What is the largest mass that can be placed on it before it is fully submerged in fresh water? => Maximum buoyancy = 1000 × 0.4 = 400 kg-equivalent. The log's own mass = 650 × 0.4 = 260 kg. Load = **140 kg**.
P: (1 mark) Water flows at 1.5 m/s through a horizontal pipe of cross-section 6 cm² that narrows to 2 cm². Find the speed in the narrow part and the pressure drop. => Speed = 1.5 × 6 ÷ 2 = **4.5 m/s**. ΔP = ½ × 1000 × (4.5² − 1.5²) = 500 × (20.25 − 2.25) = **9000 Pa**.
P: (1 mark) Find the exit speed of water from a small hole 3.2 m below the surface of an open tank. => v = √(2 × 9.8 × 3.2) = √62.72 = **7.92 m/s**.
P: (1 mark) The radius of a blood vessel narrows by 10% at the same pressure difference. By what percentage does the flow rate fall? => Q ∝ r⁴, so 0.9⁴ = 0.6561. The flow falls by **34.4%**.

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | A cosine sum from roots of unity
P: Using the fifth roots of unity, show that cos 72° + cos 144° = −1/2. => The fifth roots of unity sum to zero, so the real parts also sum to zero: 1 + cos 72° + cos 144° + cos 216° + cos 288° = 0. Since cos 216° = cos 144° and cos 288° = cos 72°, we get 1 + 2 (cos 72° + cos 144°) = 0, so **cos 72° + cos 144° = −1/2**. Check: 0.3090 − 0.8090 = −0.5 ✓.

LVL: Challenge | A siphon
P: A siphon carries water out of a tank. The exit is 0.8 m below the water surface, and the top of the bend is 0.5 m above the surface. Find the exit speed, the pressure at the top of the bend and the greatest height the bend could have before the water column would break (pressure falls to zero). Assume ideal flow. => Bernoulli between the surface and the exit: v = √(2 × 9.8 × 0.8) = **3.96 m/s**, and by continuity the speed in the pipe is the same everywhere. At the top of the bend: P = P₀ − ρ g h − ½ ρ v² = 101,325 − 4,900 − 7,840 = **88,585 Pa** (0.874 atm). The column breaks when P = 0: ρ g h = P₀ − ½ ρ v², so h = (101,325 − 7,840) ÷ 9800 = **9.54 m** (the ideal limit, about 10 m for water).
