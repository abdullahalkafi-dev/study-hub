# Polar form, Euler and De Moivre. Then: buoyancy and Archimedes
lead: Yesterday you wrote complex numbers as a + bi. Today you write them as a length and an angle. In this form multiplication becomes "multiply the lengths, add the angles", and powers and roots become trivial. In physics you meet the force that makes ships float and balloons rise: buoyancy.

BN: গতকাল জটিল সংখ্যা লিখেছিলে a + bi আকারে। আজ লিখবে দৈর্ঘ্য ও কোণ দিয়ে (polar form)। এই আকারে গুণ মানে "দৈর্ঘ্য গুণ, কোণ যোগ", আর ঘাত ও মূল বের করা খুব সহজ। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে শিখবে যে বল জাহাজ ভাসায় আর বেলুন ওড়ায়: প্লবতা (buoyancy)।

@section math | 2 · Math: polar form and Euler | 55 min

### Length and angle
A point (a, b) can be described by its distance r from the origin and the angle θ from the positive real axis (the **argument**, arg z):

EQ: a = r cos θ     b = r sin θ     r = √(a² + b²)     tan θ = b ÷ a

So z = r (cos θ + i sin θ). Euler's formula e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ compresses this to

EQ: z = r e^(iθ)

BN: বিন্দু (a, b) কে দূরত্ব r আর কোণ θ দিয়ে লেখা যায়: a = r cos θ, b = r sin θ। অয়লারের সূত্র e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ দিয়ে z = r e^(iθ)। কোণ মাপা হয় ধনাত্মক বাস্তব অক্ষ থেকে ঘড়ির উল্টো দিকে। সাবধান: tan θ = b/a থেকে কোণ বের করলে কোন চতুর্ভাগে বিন্দুটা আছে তা দেখে নিতে হবে।

EX: Converting both ways
1. z = 1 + i: r = √2, θ = 45°. So z = **√2 e^(i 45°)**.
2. z = −1 + i√3: r = √(1 + 3) = 2. The point is in quadrant 2, with reference angle 60°, so θ = **120°**. A calculator giving atan(−√3) = −60° would be wrong: add 180°.
3. z = 3 − 3i: r = 3√2 ≈ 4.243, θ = **−45°** (or 315°).
4. Back to rectangular: 4 e^(i 30°) = 4 (cos 30° + i sin 30°) = 4 (0.866 + 0.5 i) = **3.464 + 2 i** (that is 2√3 + 2i).
/EX

PREDICT: Take z₁ = 2 e^(i 20°) and z₂ = 3 e^(i 50°). What do you expect for the modulus and the angle of z₁ z₂? => Modulus **6** and angle **70°**. Moduli multiply, angles add, because e^(ia) e^(ib) = e^(i(a + b)).

### Multiplication and division
Using the exponent rule:

EQ: z₁ z₂ = r₁ r₂ e^(i(θ₁ + θ₂))     z₁ ÷ z₂ = (r₁ ÷ r₂) e^(i(θ₁ − θ₂))

The product **scales** by r₂ and **rotates** by θ₂. This is the geometry behind every rotation in computer graphics, robotics and signal processing.

EX: Multiplying in polar form
1. (1 + i)(1 + i√3): moduli √2 and 2, angles 45° and 60°. Product: modulus **2√2 ≈ 2.828**, angle **105°**. Check by direct multiplication: (1 − √3) + (1 + √3) i = −0.732 + 2.732 i. Its modulus is √(0.536 + 7.464) = √8 ✓, and it lies in quadrant 2 with angle 105° ✓.
2. (2 e^(i 70°)) ÷ (4 e^(i 30°)) = 0.5 e^(i 40°).
3. Conjugate: if z = r e^(iθ) then z̄ = r e^(−iθ), so z z̄ = r² ✓.
/EX

### De Moivre's theorem and roots
Repeating the multiplication n times gives

EQ: (r e^(iθ))ⁿ = rⁿ e^(i n θ)     that is     (cos θ + i sin θ)ⁿ = cos nθ + i sin nθ

EX: Powers
1. (1 + i)⁸: r = √2, θ = 45°. r⁸ = 16, nθ = 360°. Result **16** (matches yesterday).
2. (√3 + i)⁶: r = 2, θ = 30°. 2⁶ = 64, 6 × 30° = 180°. Result **−64**.
3. (1 − i)¹⁰: r = √2, θ = −45°. r¹⁰ = 32, angle −450° ≡ −90°. Result 32 e^(−i 90°) = **−32 i**.
/EX

To find an n-th root of z = r e^(iθ), note that adding 360° to θ describes the same point, so there are **n different roots**:

EQ: z^(1/n) = r^(1/n) e^(i (θ + 360° k) ÷ n),     k = 0, 1, …, n − 1

They sit at the corners of a regular n-sided polygon centred at the origin.

EX: Roots
1. **Cube roots of 8:** r = 8, θ = 0°. Roots 2 e^(i 0°) = 2, 2 e^(i 120°) = **−1 + i√3**, 2 e^(i 240°) = **−1 − i√3**. Check: (−1 + i√3)² = 1 − 2i√3 − 3 = −2 − 2i√3, times (−1 + i√3) gives 2 − 2i√3 + 2i√3 + 6 = 8 ✓.
2. **Fourth roots of −4:** r = 4, θ = 180°. Roots √2 e^(i 45°, 135°, 225°, 315°) = **±1 ± i**. Check: (1 + i)⁴ = (2i)² = −4 ✓.
3. **Roots of unity:** the sixth roots of 1 are at 0°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240°, 300°. Their sum is **0**, and so is the sum of the n-th roots of 1 for any n > 1.
/EX

EX: A trig identity for free
1. Expand (cos θ + i sin θ)³ = cos 3θ + i sin 3θ using the binomial theorem: the real part is cos³θ − 3 cos θ sin²θ.
2. Replace sin²θ with 1 − cos²θ: cos 3θ = cos³θ − 3 cos θ (1 − cos²θ) = **4 cos³θ − 3 cos θ**.
3. Test at θ = 0: 4 − 3 = 1 = cos 0 ✓. Test at θ = 60°: 4 × 0.125 − 3 × 0.5 = −1 = cos 180° ✓.
/EX

BN: ডি-মোভারের সূত্র: (re^(iθ))ⁿ = rⁿe^(inθ)। n-তম মূল n টা, বিন্দুগুলো একটা সুষম বহুভুজের কোণায় বসে: কোণ (θ + 360°k)/n। 8 এর ঘনমূল তিনটা: 2, −1 + i√3, −1 − i√3। এককের n-তম মূলগুলোর যোগফল সবসময় 0।

TRAP: Always check the quadrant before you accept the angle from tan⁻¹. For z = −1 − i the calculator gives 45°, but the real answer is **225°** (or −135°). And when taking roots, do not stop at k = 0: there are n of them.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: buoyancy and Archimedes | 35 min

### Why things float
Pressure increases with depth, so the water pushes **up** on the bottom of a submerged object harder than it pushes **down** on the top. The difference is the **buoyant force**.

**Archimedes' principle:** the buoyant force equals the weight of the fluid displaced.

EQ: B = ρ_fluid V_displaced g

Proof for a cuboid of height h and area A, submerged with its top at depth y: the force up on the bottom is ρ g (y + h) A, the force down on the top is ρ g y A. The net is ρ g h A = ρ g V ✓.

BN: আর্কিমিডিসের সূত্র: প্লবতা বল = অপসারিত তরলের ওজন, B = ρ_তরল V_অপসারিত g। প্রমাণ: ঘনকের নিচের তলায় চাপ বেশি, ওপরের তলায় কম, তাই নিট বল ওপরের দিকে ρ g h A = ρ g V।

EX: Weighing under water
1. A 1000 cm³ aluminium block (ρ = 2700) has weight 2.7 × 9.8 = **26.46 N**. Fully submerged in water: B = 1000 × 10⁻³ × 9.8 = **9.8 N**. A scale reads the **apparent weight** 26.46 − 9.8 = **16.66 N**.
2. **The crown test.** A crown has mass 0.5 kg, so weight 4.9 N. In water a scale reads 4.5 N, so B = 0.4 N and V = 0.4 ÷ 9800 = 4.08 × 10⁻⁵ m³. Density = 0.5 ÷ 4.08 × 10⁻⁵ = **12,250 kg/m³**. Pure gold is 19,300, so the crown is not pure gold. (This is the story of Archimedes.)
/EX

### Floating
An object floats when B equals its weight. A floating object displaces a volume of fluid of the same **weight** as the object:

EQ: ρ_object V_object = ρ_fluid V_submerged     so     V_submerged ÷ V_object = ρ_object ÷ ρ_fluid

EX: How much is above the surface?
1. A cube of side 0.1 m and density 600 kg/m³ in water: submerged fraction **0.60**, so **6 cm** under water. Its weight is 600 × 10⁻³ × 9.8 = 5.88 N, equal to B ✓.
2. **Iceberg.** Ice has ρ = 917. In seawater (1025): submerged fraction 917 ÷ 1025 = **0.895**, so **10.5%** is above the water. In fresh water the fraction above is only 8.3%.
3. **Ship.** A ship of mass 5 × 10⁶ kg displaces 5 × 10⁶ ÷ 1025 = **4878 m³** of seawater. In fresh water it displaces 5000 m³, so it sits slightly lower. That is why a ship has a Plimsoll line with different marks.
/EX

### Balloons and airships
Air is also a fluid. A balloon of volume V with a lighter gas inside has net lift (ρ_air − ρ_gas) V g, minus the weight of the envelope and the load.

EX: Lifting
1. **Hot-air balloon,** V = 2500 m³. Outside air 1.2 kg/m³, inside hot air 0.95: lift = (1.2 − 0.95) × 2500 = **625 kg** equivalent, so about 6125 N. The envelope, basket and passengers must weigh less than 625 kg.
2. **Helium** (0.17 kg/m³): each cubic metre lifts 1.2 − 0.17 = **1.03 kg**. A 14-litre party balloon lifts 0.0144 kg = 14 g, slightly more than its rubber, so it rises slowly.
3. **Submarine:** it takes in water to raise its average density to 1025 kg/m³ and sink, or blows it out with compressed air to rise.
/EX

### Stability: a floating cylinder oscillates
Push a floating cylinder of cross-section A down by x. The extra displaced volume is A x, so the buoyancy force increases by ρ g A x, which is a **restoring force** −k x with k = ρ g A. That is **SHM** with ω = √(ρ g A ÷ m).

EX: A floating log
1. A cylinder of mass 0.5 kg and cross-section A = 0.005 m² floats upright in water. ω² = 1000 × 9.8 × 0.005 ÷ 0.5 = **98**, so ω = **9.90 rad/s**.
2. T = 2π ÷ 9.90 = **0.635 s**.
3. A heavier cylinder (same A) oscillates more slowly: T ∝ √m.
/EX

TRAP: Archimedes' principle uses the volume of **fluid displaced**, which is the submerged volume for a floating object but the whole volume for a fully submerged one. And never say "heavy things sink": a ship is very heavy and floats, because its **average density** is below that of water.
