### Points, distances and rotations
Treat a point (x, y) as the number z = x + i y. Then:

1. The distance between z₁ and z₂ is **abs(z₁ − z₂)**.
2. Rotating z about the origin by an angle α (anticlockwise) is **multiplying by e^(iα)**.
3. Rotating z about a centre c by α: **z′ = c + e^(iα)(z − c)** (shift to the origin, rotate, shift back).
4. Scaling by k about c: z′ = c + k (z − c).

BN: বিন্দু (x, y) = z = x + iy। দূরত্ব = |z₁ − z₂|। মূলবিন্দুর চারদিকে α কোণে ঘোরানো = e^(iα) দিয়ে গুণ। কেন্দ্র c এর চারদিকে ঘোরাতে হলে: z′ = c + e^(iα)(z − c), অর্থাৎ আগে মূলবিন্দুতে সরাও, ঘোরাও, আবার ফেরাও।

EX: Rotations
1. Rotate 2 + i about the origin by 90°: multiply by i: i (2 + i) = −1 + 2 i, the point **(−1, 2)**.
2. Rotate 2 + i about c = 1 by 90°: z − c = 1 + i; times i gives −1 + i; add back c: **0 + i**, the point (0, 1). Check: the distance from c is √2 before and after ✓.
3. **Equilateral triangle:** on the segment from a = 0 to b = 2, the third vertex is a + e^(i 60°)(b − a) = 2 (0.5 + 0.866 i) = **1 + 1.732 i**. All three sides have length 2 ✓.
4. **Robot arm:** two links of 1 m and 0.5 m. The first at 30°, the second at 45° relative to it (so 75° absolute). The tip is at 1 e^(i 30°) + 0.5 e^(i 75°) = (0.866 + 0.5 i) + 0.5 (0.2588 + 0.9659 i) = **0.995 + 0.983 i**, which is 1.40 m from the base.
/EX

### Describing curves
EX: Loci
1. **abs(z − c) = r** is a circle of centre c and radius r. For example abs(z − 2 i) = 3 is the circle with centre (0, 2) and radius 3.
2. **abs(z − a) = abs(z − b)** is the perpendicular bisector of the segment from a to b. For abs(z − 1) = abs(z + 1): the points equally far from 1 and −1 form the line **Re z = 0** (the imaginary axis).
3. **abs(z − 1) + abs(z + 1) = 4:** the sum of the distances to two points is constant, an ellipse with a = 2, c = 1, b = √(4 − 1) = **√3**.
4. **arg(z) = π/4** is the half-line y = x with x > 0.
/EX

EX: Roots of unity make regular polygons
1. The five fifth roots of 1 are the vertices of a regular pentagon inscribed in the unit circle.
2. The distance from 1 to the k-th root is 2 sin(180° k ÷ 5). The four distances are 2 sin 36°, 2 sin 72°, 2 sin 108°, 2 sin 144°, so their product is 16 sin²36° sin²72° = 16 × 0.3455 × 0.9045 = **5.000**.
3. This is a special case of **Π (1 − ωᵏ) = n** (the product of the n − 1 chords from one vertex of a regular n-gon is n).
/EX

### Fractals: the Mandelbrot set
Pick a complex number c. Start with z₀ = 0 and repeat **z_(n+1) = z_n² + c**. If the sequence stays bounded, c belongs to the **Mandelbrot set**. A useful fact: if abs(z_n) ever exceeds **2**, the sequence escapes to infinity.

EX: Iterating by hand
1. **c = 1:** 0, 1, 2, 5, 26, … escapes. Not in the set.
2. **c = −1:** 0, −1, 0, −1, … cycles. In the set.
3. **c = i:** z₁ = i, z₂ = i² + i = −1 + i, z₃ = (−1 + i)² + i = −2i + i = −i, z₄ = (−i)² + i = −1 + i, then it repeats z₃ = −i. A cycle of two points: **in the set**.
4. **c = 0.5:** 0, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0625, 1.629, 3.15 > 2: escapes. In fact c = 0.25 is the rightmost real point of the set.
5. **c = −2:** 0, −2, 2, 2, 2, … stays bounded: in the set (the leftmost real point).
/EX

BN: ম্যান্ডেলব্রট: z₀ = 0, z_(n+1) = z_n² + c। ক্রমটা সীমিত থাকলে c সেটের ভেতরে। |z| কখনো 2 ছাড়ালে সেটা অসীমে চলে যায়। c = 1: 0, 1, 2, 5, 26 (বাইরে); c = −1: 0, −1, 0, −1 (ভেতরে); c = i একটা দুই বিন্দুর চক্র (ভেতরে)।

TRAP: Rotating about a centre is **not** just multiplying by e^(iα): first subtract the centre, rotate, then add it back. And a locus equation like abs(z − 1) = abs(z + 1) describes a **line**, not a circle: always square both sides and compare with a known shape.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: surface tension and capillarity | 35 min

### The skin of a liquid
Molecules inside a liquid are pulled equally in all directions. Molecules at the surface have neighbours only on one side, so they are pulled inward: the surface acts like a stretched skin that tries to be as small as possible. The **surface tension** γ is the force per unit length along the surface (N/m), or equivalently the energy per unit area (J/m²).

| Liquid | γ (N/m) |
| Water (20 °C) | 0.072 |
| Soap solution | 0.025 |
| Ethanol | 0.022 |
| Mercury | 0.485 |

BN: তরলের পৃষ্ঠের অণুগুলোকে ভেতরের দিকে টানা হয়, তাই পৃষ্ঠ একটা টানটান পর্দার মতো ছোট হতে চায়। পৃষ্ঠটান γ = প্রতি একক দৈর্ঘ্যে বল (N/m) = প্রতি একক ক্ষেত্রফলে শক্তি (J/m²)। পানির γ = 0.072 N/m।

EX: Forces and energies
1. A soap film on a U-shaped wire of width 5 cm, with a sliding bar: the film has **two** surfaces, so the force on the bar is F = 2 γ L = 2 × 0.025 × 0.05 = **2.5 × 10⁻³ N**.
2. **Water strider:** mass 10 mg, weight 9.8 × 10⁻⁵ N. Six legs with a total contact length of 6 cm can provide at most γ L = 0.072 × 0.06 = **4.3 × 10⁻³ N**, about 44 times its weight.
3. **Energy:** stretching the surface by an area ΔA costs γ ΔA.
/EX

### Pressure inside drops and bubbles
The surface tension squeezes a drop. Equating the force pulling a hemisphere's rim (2π R γ) with the pressure force (ΔP π R²) gives, for a drop with one surface:

EQ: ΔP = 2 γ ÷ R     and for a soap bubble (two surfaces)     ΔP = 4 γ ÷ R

Smaller drops and bubbles have **higher** pressure inside.

EX: Pressures
1. A water droplet with R = 1 mm: ΔP = 2 × 0.072 ÷ 0.001 = **144 Pa**.
2. A soap bubble with R = 1 cm, γ = 0.025: ΔP = 4 × 0.025 ÷ 0.01 = **10 Pa**. With R = 0.5 cm it is **20 Pa**.
3. If a small and a large bubble are connected by a tube, the small bubble has the higher pressure and **empties into the larger one**. The same effect makes large drops grow at the cost of small ones in fog.
4. **Lungs:** the alveoli would collapse if the water lining the surface had γ = 0.072. A natural soap-like substance (surfactant) lowers γ so that small alveoli do not empty into large ones.
/EX

### Capillary rise
Where a liquid meets a solid wall the surface bends at a **contact angle** θ. In a thin tube, the surface tension pulls the liquid up if θ < 90° (it "wets" the wall) or pushes it down if θ > 90°. Balance the weight of the column ρ g π r² h with the vertical pull 2π r γ cos θ:

EQ: h = 2 γ cos θ ÷ (ρ g r)

EX: Rise and depression
1. **Water in glass** (θ ≈ 0), r = 0.5 mm: h = 2 × 0.072 ÷ (1000 × 9.8 × 0.0005) = 0.144 ÷ 4.9 = **2.94 cm**. For r = 0.1 mm the rise is **14.7 cm**.
2. **Mercury in glass** (θ = 140°, cos θ = −0.766), r = 0.5 mm: h = 2 × 0.485 × (−0.766) ÷ (13,600 × 9.8 × 0.0005) = −0.7430 ÷ 66.64 = **−1.12 cm**: the mercury is **depressed**.
3. **Tall trees.** In a xylem tube of radius 10 μm, capillarity alone gives h = 0.144 ÷ (1000 × 9.8 × 10⁻⁵) = **1.47 m**, far less than the 100 m of a redwood. Trees also use evaporation at the leaves (transpiration), which creates a negative pressure that pulls the water column up.
4. **Towels and paper** absorb water because their tiny pores make the capillary rise large.
/EX
