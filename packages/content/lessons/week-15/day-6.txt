@section math | 2 · Part A: Eigenvalues and symmetric matrices (14 marks, about 55 min) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 13
P: (1 mark) Find the eigenvalues of A = [[4, 1], [2, 3]]. => trace 7, det 10: λ² − 7λ + 10 = 0, **λ = 5 and 2**.
P: (1 mark) Find an eigenvector of A = [[4, 1], [2, 3]] for λ = 5, and check it. => A − 5I = [[−1, 1], [2, −2]], so −v₁ + v₂ = 0 and **v = (1, 1)**. Check: A v = (5, 5) = 5 v ✓.
P: (1 mark) Write down the eigenvalues of [[3, 5], [0, −2]] without calculating. => Triangular: the diagonal entries, **3 and −2**.
P: (1 mark) A has eigenvalues 5 and 2. Give the eigenvalues of A², A⁻¹ and A + 3I. => A²: **25 and 4**. A⁻¹: **1/5 and 1/2**. A + 3I: **8 and 5**.
P: (2 marks) Diagonalise A = [[4, 1], [2, 3]] (the eigenvector for λ = 2 is (1, −2)) and use it to find A³. => P = [[1, 1], [1, −2]], D = diag (5, 2), P⁻¹ = (1/3) [[2, 1], [1, −1]] (1 mark). A³ = P diag (125, 8) P⁻¹ = (1/3) [[125 + 8, ...]] giving **[[86, 39], [78, 47]]** (1 mark). Check by multiplying: A² = [[18, 7], [14, 11]], A³ = A² A = [[72 + 14, 18 + 21], [56 + 22, 14 + 33]] = [[86, 39], [78, 47]] ✓.
P: (1 mark) A population has juveniles and adults with the matrix [[0, 2], [0.5, 0.5]]. Find the long-run growth factor. => λ² − 0.5 λ − 1 = 0, λ = (0.5 + √4.25) ÷ 2 = (0.5 + 2.062) ÷ 2 = **1.281**: the population grows by **28% per step**.
P: (1 mark) Two equal masses of 1 kg, each joined to a wall by a spring of 100 N/m and to each other by a spring of 20 N/m. Find the two normal-mode frequencies. => ω₁ = √(100 ÷ 1) = **10 rad/s** (in phase). ω₂ = √((100 + 2 × 20) ÷ 1) = √140 = **11.83 rad/s** (opposite).
P: (1 mark) For the symmetric matrix [[3, 1], [1, 3]] find unit eigenvectors and the matrix Q with A = Q D Qᵀ. => λ = 4 with (1, 1) ÷ √2 and λ = 2 with (1, −1) ÷ √2. **Q = (1 ÷ √2) [[1, 1], [1, −1]]**, D = diag (4, 2).
P: (1 mark) Identify the curve 2x² + 2xy + 2y² = 1 and find its semi-axes. => A = [[2, 1], [1, 2]], λ = 3 and 1: 3u² + v² = 1, an **ellipse** with semi-axes **1 ÷ √3 = 0.577** and **1**.
P: (1 mark) Is [[2, 3], [3, 5]] positive definite? And [[1, 2], [2, 1]]? => First: a = 2 > 0 and det = 10 − 9 = 1 > 0: **yes**. Second: det = 1 − 4 = −3 < 0: **no**.
P: (1 mark) The data (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6) have s_xx = 1, s_yy = 4, s_xy = 2. Find λ₁, λ₂, the first principal component and the share explained. => trace 5, det 4 − 4 = 0, **λ = 5 and 0**. PC1 = **(1, 2) ÷ √5**, explaining **100%**: the points lie exactly on a line.
P: (1 mark) A plate has σ_x = 30, σ_y = −10 and τ = 40 MPa. Find the principal stresses and the maximum shear. => trace 20, det −300 − 1600 = −1900, λ² − 20λ − 1900 = 0, λ = 10 ± √(100 + 1900) = 10 ± 44.72: **54.7 and −34.7 MPa**. Maximum shear = (54.72 + 34.72) ÷ 2 = **44.7 MPa**.
P: (1 mark) Find the principal moments of 1 kg at (1, 2) and 1 kg at (−1, −2). => Σ m x² = 2, Σ m y² = 8, Σ m xy = 4: I = [[8, −4], [−4, 2]], trace 10, det 16 − 16 = 0: **λ = 10 and 0**. The moment is 0 about the axis along the line of the masses and 10 kg m² about the perpendicular axis (check: each mass is √5 away, 2 × 5 = 10 ✓).

@section physics | 3 · Part B: Entropy and engines (6 marks, about 35 min) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 14 to 19
P: (1 mark) Four coins are tossed. Find the probability of exactly one head and the entropy of a macrostate with W = 10²². => 4 ÷ 16 = **25%**. S = k ln W = 1.381 × 10⁻²³ × 50.66 = **7.0 × 10⁻²² J/K**.
P: (1 mark) Find the entropy change when 2 kg of water is heated from 20 °C to 50 °C. => ΔS = m c ln (T₂ ÷ T₁) = 2 × 4186 × ln (323.15 ÷ 293.15) = 8372 × 0.0975 = **816 J/K**.
P: (1 mark) 800 J of heat flows from a reservoir at 400 K to one at 300 K. Find the total entropy change. => −800 ÷ 400 + 800 ÷ 300 = −2 + 2.667 = **+0.667 J/K**.
P: (1 mark) Find the ideal Otto efficiency for a compression ratio of 7 (γ = 1.4). => 1 − 7^(−0.4) = 1 − 0.459 = **54.1%**.
P: (1 mark) One mole of ideal gas expands freely into four times its volume. Find ΔS. => R ln 4 = 8.314 × 1.386 = **11.5 J/K** (equal to N k ln 4).
P: (1 mark) A real engine takes 1000 J at 600 K, rejects heat at 300 K and produces 300 J. Find the entropy produced and the lost work. => Q_C = 700 J. ΔS = 700 ÷ 300 − 1000 ÷ 600 = 2.333 − 1.667 = **0.667 J/K**. Lost work = 300 × 0.667 = **200 J** (check: Carnot gives 500 J, and 500 − 300 = 200 ✓).

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | The Fibonacci eigenvalue
P: Show that the golden ratio φ = (1 + √5) ÷ 2 is an eigenvalue of the Fibonacci matrix [[1, 1], [1, 0]], find its eigenvector, and use it to explain why F(n+1) ÷ F(n) → φ. => λ² − λ − 1 = 0 gives λ = φ = 1.618 and −0.618. For φ: (A − φI) v = 0 gives v = (φ, 1). Applying A n times multiplies the φ-component by φⁿ and the other by (−0.618)ⁿ → 0, so the vector (F(n+1), F(n)) turns towards (φ, 1) and the ratio tends to **φ = 1.618**.

LVL: Challenge | Entropy of a mixed gas
P: A box is divided in two halves, one with 1 mol of helium and one with 1 mol of neon, at the same T and P. The partition is removed. Find ΔS, and say what happens if both halves hold the same gas. => Each gas doubles its volume: ΔS = 2 × R ln 2 = 2 × 5.76 = **11.5 J/K**. For the **same** gas on both sides, nothing observable changes (the molecules are identical, so W does not change), so **ΔS = 0**. This is Gibbs's paradox: entropy counts only distinguishable arrangements.
