# Diagonalisation and powers of matrices. Then: the Carnot cycle
lead: Once you know the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a matrix, you can turn it into a diagonal one, and raising a diagonal matrix to the hundredth power is trivial. That is how engineers predict the long-term behaviour of systems. In physics, you meet the most efficient engine that nature allows, the Carnot engine, and the formula that limits every power plant and car: e = 1 − T_C ÷ T_H.

BN: কোনো ম্যাট্রিক্সের eigenvalue ও eigenvector জানলে তাকে কর্ণ ম্যাট্রিক্সে (diagonal) রূপান্তর করা যায়, আর কর্ণ ম্যাট্রিক্সের একশো ঘাত বের করা খুব সহজ। এভাবেই প্রকৌশলীরা ব্যবস্থার দীর্ঘমেয়াদি আচরণ আন্দাজ করেন। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে প্রকৃতির অনুমোদিত সবচেয়ে দক্ষ ইঞ্জিন, কার্নো ইঞ্জিন, এবং প্রতিটি বিদ্যুৎকেন্দ্র ও গাড়ির সীমা e = 1 − T_C/T_H শিখবে।

### A = P D P⁻¹
Suppose A has n independent eigenvectors v₁ … vₙ with eigenvalues λ₁ … λₙ. Put the eigenvectors as the columns of P and the eigenvalues on the diagonal of D. Then A P = P D, so

EQ: A = P D P⁻¹     and therefore     Aⁿ = P Dⁿ P⁻¹     (Dⁿ just raises each diagonal entry to the n-th power)

The idea: in the eigenvector basis, A acts as a plain stretch. P⁻¹ converts to that basis, D stretches, and P converts back.

BN: A এর স্বাধীন eigenvector গুলো P এর স্তম্ভ আর eigenvalue গুলো D এর কর্ণ হলে A = PDP⁻¹, তাই Aⁿ = PDⁿP⁻¹। eigenvector এর ভিত্তিতে A শুধু টানে। P⁻¹ সেই ভিত্তিতে নেয়, D টানে, P ফিরিয়ে আনে। **n টা ভিন্ন eigenvalue** থাকলে সবসময় n টা স্বাধীন eigenvector পাওয়া যায়।

EX: Diagonalising A = [[2, 1], [1, 2]]
1. Eigenvalues 3 and 1 with eigenvectors (1, 1) and (1, −1). So P = [[1, 1], [1, −1]] and D = [[3, 0], [0, 1]].
2. det P = −2, so P⁻¹ = (1/2) [[1, 1], [1, −1]].
3. Check: P D = [[3, 1], [3, −1]], and P D P⁻¹ = (1/2) [[3 + 1, 3 − 1], [3 − 1, 3 + 1]] = **[[2, 1], [1, 2]]** ✓.
4. **Powers:** Aⁿ = P Dⁿ P⁻¹ = (1/2) [[3ⁿ + 1, 3ⁿ − 1], [3ⁿ − 1, 3ⁿ + 1]]. For n = 2: (1/2) [[10, 8], [8, 10]] = [[5, 4], [4, 5]] ✓ (the direct product). For n = 10: 3¹⁰ = 59,049, so A¹⁰ = [[29,525, 29,524], [29,524, 29,525]].
/EX

EX: Diagonalising A = [[4, 1], [2, 3]]
1. Eigenvalues 5 and 2, with eigenvectors (1, 1) and (1, −2). P = [[1, 1], [1, −2]], det P = −3, P⁻¹ = (1/3) [[2, 1], [1, −1]].
2. Aⁿ = P diag (5ⁿ, 2ⁿ) P⁻¹ has the entries: first row ((2 × 5ⁿ + 2ⁿ) ÷ 3, (5ⁿ − 2ⁿ) ÷ 3) and second row ((2 × 5ⁿ − 2 × 2ⁿ) ÷ 3, (5ⁿ + 2 × 2ⁿ) ÷ 3).
3. Check n = 1: (10 + 2) ÷ 3 = 4 ✓, (5 − 2) ÷ 3 = 1 ✓, (10 − 4) ÷ 3 = 2 ✓, (5 + 4) ÷ 3 = 3 ✓. Check n = 2: [[18, 7], [14, 11]], and the direct product [[16 + 2, 4 + 3], [8 + 6, 2 + 9]] ✓.
4. **n = 5:** 5⁵ = 3125 and 2⁵ = 32. A⁵ = [[2094, 1031], [2062, 1063]]. Checks: trace = 3157 = 3125 + 32 ✓. det = 2094 × 1063 − 1031 × 2062 = 2,225,922 − 2,125,922 = 100,000 = 10⁵ ✓ (det A = 10).
/EX

### More about diagonalising
EX: Which matrices work?
1. **Distinct eigenvalues:** [[1, 2, 0], [0, 3, 1], [0, 0, 2]] is triangular with eigenvalues 1, 3, 2: all different, so it is diagonalisable. Eigenvectors: for 1: (1, 0, 0). For 3: (1, 1, 0). For 2: (−2, −1, 1). Check the last: A (−2, −1, 1) = (−2 − 2, −3 + 1, 2) = (−4, −2, 2) = 2 (−2, −1, 1) ✓.
2. **Not always:** the shear [[1, 1], [0, 1]] has only one eigen-direction, so it is **not** diagonalisable.
3. **Square root of a matrix:** take the square roots of the eigenvalues. For A = [[2, 1], [1, 2]]: √A = P diag (√3, 1) P⁻¹ = (1/2) [[√3 + 1, √3 − 1], [√3 − 1, √3 + 1]] = [[1.366, 0.366], [0.366, 1.366]]. Check: 1.366² + 0.366² = 1.866 + 0.134 = 2.000 and 2 × 1.366 × 0.366 = 1.000 ✓.
4. **Reverse engineering:** find the matrix with eigenvalues 2 and 3 and eigenvectors (1, 0) and (1, 1). P = [[1, 1], [0, 1]], P⁻¹ = [[1, −1], [0, 1]], D = diag (2, 3). A = P D P⁻¹ = [[2, 3], [0, 3]] [[1, −1], [0, 1]] = **[[2, 1], [0, 3]]**. Check: A (1, 1) = (3, 3) ✓.
/EX

EX: Two rooms mixing
1. Each minute 10% of the air in each of two connected rooms moves to the other. With x = (amount in room 1, amount in room 2), x_{n+1} = A x_n where A = [[0.9, 0.1], [0.1, 0.9]].
2. Eigenvalues: **1** with eigenvector (1, 1) and **0.8** with eigenvector (1, −1). So Aⁿ = (1/2) [[1 + 0.8ⁿ, 1 − 0.8ⁿ], [1 − 0.8ⁿ, 1 + 0.8ⁿ]].

4. The difference between the rooms is 0.8ⁿ. It falls below 1% when 0.8ⁿ < 0.01, n > ln 0.01 ÷ ln 0.8 = 20.6: after **21 minutes**.
/EX

TRAP: Put the eigenvectors in P **in the same order** as the eigenvalues in D. P⁻¹ goes on the **right**: A = P D P⁻¹, not P⁻¹ D P. And a matrix with a repeated eigenvalue is not always diagonalisable (the shear): check that you really have n independent eigenvectors.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: the Carnot cycle | 35 min

### The best possible engine
In 1824 Sadi Carnot asked: what is the highest efficiency an engine can have between a hot reservoir at T_H and a cold one at T_C? His **Carnot cycle** is **reversible** (no friction, heat exchanged only when temperatures are equal), made of four steps:

1. **Isothermal expansion** at T_H: the gas absorbs Q_H = n R T_H ln (V₂ ÷ V₁).
2. **Adiabatic expansion:** the gas cools from T_H to T_C.
3. **Isothermal compression** at T_C: the gas rejects Q_C = n R T_C ln (V₃ ÷ V₄).
4. **Adiabatic compression:** the gas warms back from T_C to T_H.

From the adiabats, T_H V₂^(γ−1) = T_C V₃^(γ−1) and T_H V₁^(γ−1) = T_C V₄^(γ−1). Dividing: V₂ ÷ V₁ = V₃ ÷ V₄, so the two logarithms are equal and Q_C ÷ Q_H = T_C ÷ T_H.

EQ: e_Carnot = 1 − T_C ÷ T_H     (temperatures in kelvin)

**Carnot's theorem:** no engine between the same two reservoirs can beat a reversible engine, and all reversible engines have the same efficiency.

BN: কার্নো চক্র পরিবর্তনীয় (reversible), চারটি ধাপ: T_H তে সমোষ্ণ প্রসারণ, রুদ্ধতাপ প্রসারণ (ঠান্ডা হয়), T_C তে সমোষ্ণ সংকোচন, রুদ্ধতাপ সংকোচন। দক্ষতা e = 1 − T_C/T_H (কেলভিনে)। কার্নোর উপপাদ্য: একই দুই উৎসের মধ্যে কোনো ইঞ্জিন কার্নো ইঞ্জিনের চেয়ে দক্ষ হতে পারে না। 100% দক্ষতার জন্য T_C = 0 K লাগবে, যা অসম্ভব।

EX: A Carnot cycle in numbers
1. 1 mol of a monatomic gas (γ = 5/3), T_H = 500 K, T_C = 300 K. Start at V₁ = 10 L and expand isothermally to V₂ = 20 L.
2. **Q_H** = 8.314 × 500 × ln 2 = **2881 J**.
3. The adiabatic expansion to 300 K: V₃ = V₂ (T_H ÷ T_C)^(1/(γ−1)) = 20 × (5/3)^1.5 = 20 × 2.152 = **43.0 L**. The adiabatic compression back: V₄ = 10 × 2.152 = **21.5 L**. Check V₃ ÷ V₄ = 2 = V₂ ÷ V₁ ✓.
4. **Q_C** = 8.314 × 300 × ln 2 = **1729 J**.
5. **W** = 2881 − 1729 = **1153 J** and e = 1153 ÷ 2881 = **40.0%** = 1 − 300 ÷ 500 ✓.
/EX

### What Carnot tells us about real machines
EX: Limits
1. **Steam plant:** T_H = 823 K (550 °C), T_C = 303 K (30 °C): e_Carnot = 1 − 303 ÷ 823 = **63.2%**. Real plants reach about 40%, roughly two thirds of the limit.
2. **Which helps more?** From T_H = 600 K, T_C = 300 K (e = 50%): raising T_H by 100 K gives 1 − 300 ÷ 700 = **57.1%**. Lowering T_C by 100 K gives 1 − 200 ÷ 600 = **66.7%**. Cooling matters more, but it is much harder to achieve.
3. **Ocean thermal energy:** warm surface water (300 K) and deep water (277 K) allow only 1 − 277 ÷ 300 = **7.7%**, and real systems manage about 3%. That is why this idea needs huge water flows.
4. **An impossible claim:** an inventor says that his engine takes 1000 J from a reservoir at 500 K and rejects heat to one at 300 K while doing 600 J of work. The efficiency would be 60%, above the Carnot limit of **40%**: **impossible**.
5. **Refrigerators:** the best possible COP is T_C ÷ (T_H − T_C). For a fridge at 275 K in a 295 K kitchen: **13.75**. A real fridge has a COP of about 3, which is 22% of the limit. A heat pump heating at 293 K from 273 K air: COP_max = T_H ÷ (T_H − T_C) = **14.65** (real units: 3 to 4).
/EX

TRAP: The temperatures in the Carnot formula are **absolute (kelvin)**: using °C gives nonsense. The Carnot limit is for **reversible** engines, and real ones are always below it. And note that the Carnot efficiency depends on **both** temperatures: a power plant with a perfect design still cannot exceed 1 − T_C ÷ T_H.
