# Areas between curves, average value and volumes. Then: rolling and rotational energy
lead: With the fundamental theorem you can now measure far more than a simple area under one curve. You can find the area between two curves, the average value of a changing quantity (which is how engineers get RMS voltage) and even the volume of a solid like a cone or a sphere. In physics, you finish the week with rolling: a ball that rolls has both kinds of kinetic energy, and that decides who wins a race down a ramp.

BN: মৌলিক উপপাদ্য হাতে থাকলে এখন আরও অনেক কিছু মাপা যায়: দুই বক্ররেখার মাঝের ক্ষেত্রফল, পরিবর্তনশীল রাশির গড় মান (প্রকৌশলীরা এভাবে RMS ভোল্টেজ পান), এমনকি শঙ্কু বা গোলকের আয়তন। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে সপ্তাহ শেষ হবে গড়ানো দিয়ে: যে বল গড়ায় তার দুই ধরনের গতিশক্তিই থাকে, আর সেটাই ঠিক করে ঢালু বেয়ে দৌড়ে কে জিতবে।

### Area between two curves
If f(x) ≥ g(x) on [a, b], the area between them is the integral of the **top minus the bottom**:

EQ: Area = ∫ₐᵇ [f(x) − g(x)] dx

Steps: (1) sketch; (2) find where the curves meet (set f = g) for the limits; (3) decide which is on top; (4) integrate. If they cross in between, split at the crossing point.

EX: Between a parabola and a line
1. y = x and y = x² meet at x = 0 and x = 1, and x ≥ x² there. Area = ∫₀¹ (x − x²) dx = [x²/2 − x³/3] = 1/2 − 1/3 = **1/6**.
2. y = 4 − x² and y = x + 2: set equal, x² + x − 2 = 0, so x = −2 and x = 1. At x = 0: 4 on the parabola and 2 on the line, so the parabola is on top. Area = ∫₋₂¹ (2 − x − x²) dx = [2x − x²/2 − x³/3] = (2 − 0.5 − 0.333) − (−4 − 2 + 2.667) = 1.167 + 3.333 = **4.5**.
3. y = cos x and y = sin x on [0, π/4]: cos is on top. Area = [sin x + cos x] = (√2/2 + √2/2) − (0 + 1) = **√2 − 1 ≈ 0.414**.
/EX

The area is ∫₀^(1/a) (x − a x²) dx = 1 ÷ (6a²). | a:Parabola steepness a:0.5:3:0.1:1 | 1/a:Right meeting point x = 1/a:3 ;; 1/(6*a^2):Area between the curves:4 | x ;; a*x^2 | 0,2 | 0,2

BN: দুই বক্ররেখার মাঝের ক্ষেত্রফল = ∫(উপরের − নিচেরটা) dx। প্রথমে ছেদবিন্দু বের করে সীমা ঠিক করো (f = g), কোনটা উপরে দেখো, সমাকলন করো; মাঝখানে ছেদ করলে ভেঙে নাও। y = x আর y = x² এর মাঝের ক্ষেত্রফল 1/6।

### Average value
The average of n numbers is their sum ÷ n. For a continuous function the "sum" becomes an integral:

EQ: f_avg = (1 ÷ (b − a)) ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx

Geometric meaning: the rectangle of width (b − a) and height f_avg has the **same area** as the region under the curve. The mean value theorem for integrals says f actually equals f_avg at some point c in [a, b].

EX: Averages
1. f(x) = x² on [0, 3]: average = (1/3)(9) = **3**. It equals f at c = √3 ≈ 1.73.
2. sin x on [0, π]: (1/π)(2) = **2/π ≈ 0.637**.
3. A car's velocity v(t) = 3t² for 0 ≤ t ≤ 4: distance = 64 m, average velocity = 64 ÷ 4 = **16 m/s**.
4. sin² x on [0, π]: use sin² x = (1 − cos 2x) ÷ 2. ∫₀^π = [x/2 − sin 2x ÷ 4] = π/2, so the average is **1/2**.
/EX

**RMS (root mean square).** For an alternating voltage V = V₀ sin ωt, the mean of V² is V₀² × (1/2). The effective, or RMS, value is √(mean of V²) = **V₀ ÷ √2 = 0.707 V₀**. This is why the 170 V peak of household mains is quoted as 120 V.

BN: গড় মান = (1/(b−a))∫f dx; জ্যামিতিক অর্থ: একই ক্ষেত্রফলের আয়তক্ষেত্রের উচ্চতা। sin²x এর গড় 1/2, তাই AC ভোল্টেজের RMS = V₀/√2। ১৭০ ভোল্ট শীর্ষমান মানে কার্যকর মান ১২০ ভোল্ট।

### Volumes of revolution (disc method)
Spin the region under y = f(x) about the x-axis. A thin slice is a disc of radius f(x) and thickness dx, with volume π f² dx. So:

EQ: V = π ∫ₐᵇ [f(x)]² dx

EX: Cone and sphere
1. **Cone:** f(x) = (r ÷ h) x for 0 ≤ x ≤ h. V = π (r²/h²) ∫₀ʰ x² dx = π (r²/h²)(h³/3) = **π r² h ÷ 3** ✓.
2. **Sphere:** f(x) = √(R² − x²) for −R ≤ x ≤ R. V = π ∫ (R² − x²) dx = π [R² x − x³/3] from −R to R = π (2R³ − 2R³/3) = **4π R³ ÷ 3** ✓.
3. y = √x on [0, 4]: V = π ∫₀⁴ x dx = π × 8 = **25.1**.
/EX

TRAP: In "top minus bottom", check which curve is on top **on the whole interval** (test one point), not just near one end. If the curves cross, split the integral, or the areas will cancel. Also, V = π ∫ f² dx has the **square inside**: (∫ f dx)² is wrong. And average value divides by (b − a), not by the integral's number of terms.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: rolling and rotational energy | 35 min

### A rolling object has two kinds of energy
A wheel rolling without slipping moves forward at speed v and also spins at ω = v ÷ R. Its kinetic energy is the sum:

EQ: KE = ½ M v² + ½ I ω²     with     ω = v ÷ R,     I = k M R²     so     KE = ½ M v² (1 + k)

Here k is the shape factor: **hoop 1, solid disc ½, solid sphere 2/5, hollow sphere 2/3**. The ratio of rotational to translational KE is just k.

EX: A bowling ball
1. M = 7 kg, R = 0.11 m, v = 8 m/s. I = (2/5)(7)(0.0121) = 0.0339 kg·m². ω = 72.7 rad/s.
2. KE(trans) = ½ × 7 × 64 = **224 J**. KE(rot) = ½ × 0.0339 × 5289 = **89.6 J** (= 0.4 × 224 ✓). Total = **313.6 J**.
/EX

### Rolling down a ramp
Static friction holds the contact point still (no slipping) and does **no work**, so energy is conserved: M g h = ½ M v² (1 + k). Therefore:

EQ: v = √(2 g h ÷ (1 + k))     and     a = g sin θ ÷ (1 + k)

**The mass and the radius cancel.** Only the shape (k) matters, so a small marble and a big ball of the same shape tie.

EX: A race from h = 1.2 m
1. Sliding block (no friction, k = 0): v = √23.52 = **4.85 m/s**.
2. Solid sphere (k = 0.4): v = √(23.52 ÷ 1.4) = **4.10 m/s**.
3. Solid disc (k = 0.5): v = √(23.52 ÷ 1.5) = **3.96 m/s**.
4. Hoop (k = 1): v = √(23.52 ÷ 2) = **3.43 m/s**.
5. Order of finishing: sphere, disc, hoop. The more of the mass is far from the axis (large k), the more energy goes to spinning instead of forward motion.
/EX

EX: On a 30° ramp
1. Accelerations: sphere 9.8 × 0.5 ÷ 1.4 = **3.5 m/s²**, disc **3.27 m/s²**, hoop **2.45 m/s²**.
2. Minimum friction coefficient (solid sphere): friction supplies the torque, f R = I α = k M a, so f = 0.4 M × 3.5 = 1.4 M. The normal force is M g cos 30° = 8.49 M, so **μ ≥ 0.165**. On very slippery ground it slides instead of rolling.
/EX

TRAP: Static friction on a rolling object does **no work**, so do not subtract friction work from the energy equation, and do not use f = μ N unless the object is slipping. Also, rolling without slipping links v = ω R: use it to eliminate ω. And be careful that I must be about the centre of mass in KE = ½ M v² + ½ I ω².

LVL: Level 3 | Real
P: A cyclist freewheels down a 10 m high hill from rest. The bike and rider have mass 80 kg, and the two wheels (hoops, 0.7 kg each, radius 0.33 m) hold a small part of it. Ignoring drag, find the speed at the bottom, and by how much the wheels' rotation lowers the speed compared with a sliding block. => Each hoop rolls with KE(rot) = ½ M v² (k = 1), so the two wheels together carry ½ × 1.4 × v² of rotational energy. Energy: 80 × 9.8 × 10 = ½ × 80 v² + ½ × 1.4 v² = 40.7 v², so v² = 7840 ÷ 40.7 = 192.6 and **v = 13.9 m/s**. A sliding block gives √196 = 14.0 m/s, so the wheels' rotation costs only about **0.1 m/s** (they are a tiny share of the mass).
LVL: Challenge | Identify the mystery object
P: An unknown uniform round object rolls down a 30° ramp with a measured acceleration of 3.5 m/s². Find its shape factor k and say what it is. Then show that a hoop can never beat a frictionless sliding block on the same ramp. => 3.5 = 9.8 × 0.5 ÷ (1 + k), so 1 + k = 1.4 and **k = 0.4: a solid sphere**. For the second part: a hoop has a = g sin θ ÷ 2, while the block has a = g sin θ. So the block always wins by a factor of 2 in acceleration, and in general any k > 0 loses to k = 0.

### Recap
- Area between curves: ∫ (top − bottom) dx, limits from f = g, split at crossings. Average value: f_avg = (1 ÷ (b − a)) ∫ f dx. RMS of a sine = V₀ ÷ √2. Volume of revolution: π ∫ f² dx (cone π r² h ÷ 3, sphere 4π R³ ÷ 3).
- Rolling: v = ω R, KE = ½ M v² (1 + k). Down a ramp: v = √(2 g h ÷ (1 + k)), a = g sin θ ÷ (1 + k). Order: sphere, disc, hoop. Static friction does no work.
