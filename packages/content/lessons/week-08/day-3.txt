# The fundamental theorem of calculus. Then: rotational dynamics
lead: This is the most important theorem in the course. It says that the two ideas you have met, "area under a curve" and "undo the derivative", are the same thing. That one fact turns a limit of infinitely many sums into one line of arithmetic. In physics, you put Newton's second law into rotational form, τ = I α, and solve machines with spinning pulleys.

BN: এটা পুরো কোর্সের সবচেয়ে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ উপপাদ্য। এটি বলে, তোমার জানা দুটি ধারণা, "বক্ররেখার নিচের ক্ষেত্রফল" আর "অন্তরকলনকে উল্টানো", আসলে একই জিনিস। এই একটি তথ্য অসংখ্য যোগফলের লিমিটকে এক লাইনের হিসাবে নামিয়ে আনে। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে নিউটনের দ্বিতীয় সূত্রের ঘূর্ণন রূপ τ = Iα ব্যবহার করে ঘূর্ণায়মান পুলিসহ যন্ত্র সমাধান করবে।

@section math | 2 · Math: the fundamental theorem | 55 min

### Why area and antiderivative are the same
Think of a car. If v(t) is its speed, then the **area under the speed graph** from t = a to t = b is the distance travelled. But there is another way to find the distance: if s(t) is the odometer reading, the distance is s(b) − s(a). And s is an antiderivative of v, since s′ = v. So:

EQ: ∫ₐᵇ v(t) dt = s(b) − s(a)     where s′ = v

That is the theorem, in odometer language. No slicing is needed once you know an antiderivative.

BN: v(t) গতি হলে গতি-লেখচিত্রের নিচের ক্ষেত্রফল = অতিক্রান্ত দূরত্ব। আবার ওডোমিটারে s(t) হলে দূরত্ব = s(b) − s(a), এবং s′ = v, অর্থাৎ s হলো v এর প্রতিঅন্তরকলজ। তাই ক্ষেত্রফল বের করতে আর ফালি কাটতে হয় না, শুধু প্রতিঅন্তরকলজ লাগে।

### The two parts
**Part 1 (the area function).** If f is continuous and A(x) = ∫ₐˣ f(t) dt, then A′(x) = f(x). In words: the rate at which the accumulated area grows equals the height of the curve at that point. (Adding a strip of width dx and height f adds f dx of area.)

**Part 2 (evaluation).** If F′ = f, then

EQ: ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a) = [F(x)] from a to b

Any antiderivative works, because the constant C cancels in F(b) − F(a).

EX: Evaluating definite integrals
1. ∫₀³ x² dx = [x³ ÷ 3] = 27 ÷ 3 − 0 = **9** (matches the sum method of Day 2).
2. ∫₁² (3x² − 2x) dx = [x³ − x²] = (8 − 4) − (1 − 1) = **4**.
3. ∫₀^π sin x dx = [−cos x] = −cos π + cos 0 = 1 + 1 = **2**. (The area of one hump of the sine wave.)
4. ∫₁ᵉ (1 ÷ x) dx = [ln x] = ln e − ln 1 = **1**.
5. ∫₀¹ eˣ dx = [eˣ] = e − 1 = **1.718**.
6. ∫₁⁴ √x dx = [(2/3) x^(3/2)] = (2/3)(8 − 1) = **14/3**.
/EX

PREDICT: ∫₀³ (x² − 2x) dx: the graph dips below the axis between 0 and 2. Do you expect the integral to equal the **total area** between the curve and the axis? What would you have to do to get the total area? => No. The integral gives the signed area, so the part below the axis counts negative. For the total area, split at the zeros (x = 0 and x = 2) and add the absolute values of each piece.

### Signed area versus total area
EX: Integral or area?
1. ∫₀³ (x² − 2x) dx = [x³ ÷ 3 − x²] = 9 − 9 = **0**. The signed areas cancel.
2. The zeros are x = 0 and x = 2. ∫₀² = 8/3 − 4 = −4/3. ∫₂³ = 0 − (−4/3) = +4/3.
3. Total area = 4/3 + 4/3 = **8/3**.
/EX

EX: Displacement versus distance
1. v(t) = t² − 4t (m/s) for 0 ≤ t ≤ 5. The velocity is negative for 0 < t < 4 (the object moves backward), and positive after that.
2. **Displacement** = ∫₀⁵ v dt = [t³ ÷ 3 − 2t²] = 125/3 − 50 = **−8.33 m**.
3. **Distance travelled**: ∫₀⁴ = 64/3 − 32 = −10.67 and ∫₄⁵ = (−8.33) − (−10.67) = +2.33. Distance = 10.67 + 2.33 = **13.0 m**.
/EX

### Derivatives of integrals (Part 1 in use)
EX: Chain rule meets the theorem
1. d/dx ∫₀ˣ t² dt = **x²** (Part 1, immediately).
2. d/dx ∫₀^(x²) sin t dt = sin(x²) · 2x (Part 1 plus the chain rule).
3. If f(x) = 3 + ∫₀ˣ t dt, then f(x) = 3 + x² ÷ 2 and f′(x) = x ✓.
/EX

BN: ভাগ ১: A(x) = ∫ₐˣ f dt হলে A′(x) = f(x), অর্থাৎ জমা হওয়া ক্ষেত্রফলের বৃদ্ধির হার = বক্ররেখার উচ্চতা। ভাগ ২: F′ = f হলে ∫ₐᵇ f dx = F(b) − F(a), C কেটে যায়। প্রাপ্ত মান signed area; মোট ক্ষেত্রফল চাইলে শূন্যে ভেঙে পরম মান যোগ করো। সরণ ও অতিক্রান্ত দূরত্ব এভাবেই আলাদা হয়।

TRAP: When the antiderivative is evaluated, do **F(b) − F(a)** with every term, and watch the signs when a ≤ 0. Also, you never need + C in a definite integral. Finally, the theorem needs f to be continuous on [a, b]: ∫₋₁¹ (1 ÷ x²) dx is **not** [−1 ÷ x] = −2 because 1 ÷ x² blows up at 0, so the integral diverges.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: τ = I α and spinning pulleys | 35 min

### Newton's second law for rotation
Sum of torques about a fixed axis equals the moment of inertia times angular acceleration:

EQ: Στ = I α     with     τ = F r sin θ (perpendicular force × distance)

For a string wrapped around a wheel of radius R, a point on the rim moves like the string: a = α R. That single link couples the straight-line equations to the spinning ones.

**Method:** (1) draw each object; (2) for each straight-moving object, write F = m a; (3) for each spinning object, write Στ = I α; (4) link the two with a = α R; (5) solve.

BN: ঘূর্ণনে নিউটনের দ্বিতীয় সূত্র: Στ = Iα, যেখানে τ = (লম্ব বল) × দূরত্ব। দড়ি চাকার পরিধিতে জড়ানো থাকলে a = αR, এটাই সরল ও ঘূর্ণন সমীকরণ জোড়া দেয়। পদ্ধতি: প্রতিটা ভর আঁকো, সরল গতিতে F = ma, ঘূর্ণনে Στ = Iα, a = αR দিয়ে জোড়া, তারপর সমাধান।

EX: A string on a disc
1. A disc (M = 2 kg, R = 0.3 m) can spin about its centre. A string wrapped around it is pulled with tension T = 6 N.
2. τ = T R = 6 × 0.3 = 1.8 N·m. I = ½ × 2 × 0.09 = 0.09 kg·m².
3. α = τ ÷ I = 1.8 ÷ 0.09 = **20 rad/s²**.
/EX

EX: A hanging mass turns a wheel
1. A 2 kg mass hangs from a string wrapped around a solid disc (M = 6 kg, R = 0.25 m) on a fixed axle.
2. Mass: m g − T = m a. Disc: T R = I α = ½ M R² (a ÷ R), so T = ½ M a.
3. Add: m g = (m + M ÷ 2) a. So a = 19.6 ÷ (2 + 3) = **3.92 m/s²**. T = ½ × 6 × 3.92 = **11.76 N**. α = a ÷ R = **15.68 rad/s²**.
4. Check: τ = 11.76 × 0.25 = 2.94. I α = 0.1875 × 15.68 = 2.94 ✓. (A massless pulley would give a = g. The wheel's inertia slows the fall.)
/EX

EX: An Atwood machine with a heavy pulley
1. Masses m₁ = 2 kg and m₂ = 5 kg hang over a solid-disc pulley (M = 4 kg, R = 0.2 m). I = ½ M R², so I ÷ R² = M ÷ 2 = 2 kg.
2. m₂: m₂ g − T₂ = m₂ a. m₁: T₁ − m₁ g = m₁ a. Pulley: (T₂ − T₁) R = I α = (I ÷ R) a, so T₂ − T₁ = (M ÷ 2) a.
3. Add all three: (m₂ − m₁) g = (m₁ + m₂ + M ÷ 2) a. So **a = 3 × 9.8 ÷ (2 + 5 + 2) = 3.27 m/s²**.
4. T₁ = 2 (9.8 + 3.27) = **26.1 N** and T₂ = 5 (9.8 − 3.27) = **32.7 N**. The two tensions differ, and the difference (6.5 N) is what spins the pulley.
/EX

### Rotational work and power
Work done by a torque is the integral of τ over the angle turned, like W = ∫ F dx:

EQ: W = ∫ τ dθ     Power P = τ ω     and     W = ΔKE = ½ I ω₂² − ½ I ω₁²

EX: Work and power
1. A constant torque of 8 N·m turns a flywheel (I = 2 kg·m²) through 5 revolutions = 10π = 31.4 rad. W = 8 × 31.4 = **251 J**. From rest, ½ I ω² = 251, so ω = √251 = **15.8 rad/s**.
2. A variable torque τ = 2θ (N·m) acts from θ = 0 to 4 rad: W = ∫₀⁴ 2θ dθ = [θ²] = **16 J**.
3. A motor delivers 50 N·m at 3000 rpm (ω = 314 rad/s): P = 50 × 314 = **15.7 kW**.
/EX

TRAP: Do not use the same τ for different objects without checking: when a string passes over a pulley with mass, the tensions on the two sides are **different** (T₁ ≠ T₂), because their difference is what turns the pulley. Also, in Στ = I α the torques and I must be about the **same axis**, and a = α R only holds when the string does not slip.
