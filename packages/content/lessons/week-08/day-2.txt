@section math | 2 · Math: area and the definite integral | 55 min

### Distance as area
A car drives at a steady 20 m/s for 10 s. The distance is 20 × 10 = 200 m, which is the **area of the rectangle** under the speed-time graph. If the speed changes, the same idea works: cut the time into thin slices, treat the speed as constant on each slice, and add the little rectangles. The more slices, the better the estimate.

BN: গতি ধ্রুব হলে দূরত্ব = গতি × সময় = গতি-সময় লেখচিত্রের নিচের আয়তক্ষেত্রের ক্ষেত্রফল। গতি বদলালে সময়কে সরু ফালিতে ভাগ করো, প্রতি ফালিতে গতি ধ্রুব ধরো, আয়তক্ষেত্রগুলো যোগ করো। যত বেশি ফালি, তত নিখুঁত।

### Riemann sums
To find the area under y = f(x) from x = a to x = b with n rectangles:

EQ: Δx = (b − a) ÷ n     Area ≈ f(x₁)Δx + f(x₂)Δx + … + f(xₙ)Δx = Σ f(xᵢ) Δx

You choose where to measure the height: the **left** edge, the **right** edge, or the **midpoint** of each slice.

EX: Four rectangles for f(x) = x² on [0, 2]
1. Δx = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5. The edges are 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2.
2. **Left sum:** heights f(0), f(0.5), f(1), f(1.5) = 0, 0.25, 1, 2.25. Sum = 3.5, times 0.5 = **1.75** (too low).
3. **Right sum:** heights f(0.5), f(1), f(1.5), f(2) = 0.25, 1, 2.25, 4. Sum = 7.5, times 0.5 = **3.75** (too high).
4. The true area is 8/3 = 2.667, between the two. The average of left and right (the trapezoid estimate) is 2.75.
/EX

PREDICT: For an **increasing** function like x², will the left-rectangle sum underestimate or overestimate the true area? What happens as the number of rectangles grows? => The left sum underestimates (each rectangle's height is the lowest value on its slice) and the right sum overestimates. As n grows both sums close in on the same number, the exact area.

LAB: Rectangles closing in on the area | Area under y = x² from 0 to b. Increase the number of rectangles n and watch the left and right sums squeeze the exact value b³ ÷ 3 from below and above. | n:Number of rectangles n:1:100:1:4 ;; b:Right end b:1:3:0.5:2 | b^3*(n-1)*(2*n-1)/(6*n^2):Left sum:4 ;; b^3*(n+1)*(2*n+1)/(6*n^2):Right sum:4 ;; b^3/3:Exact area b³/3:4 ;; b^3*(n+1)*(2*n+1)/(6*n^2)-b^3/3:Error of right sum:4 | x^2 | 0,3 | 0,9

### The definite integral
Let n grow without limit. The sums settle on one number, called the **definite integral**:

EQ: ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = lim (n → ∞) Σ f(xᵢ) Δx

The ∫ is a stretched S, "sum". The dx is the thickness of a slice, as small as you like. Where the graph is below the axis the rectangles count **negative**, so the integral is the **signed area**: area above the axis minus area below it.

EX: Areas from geometry
1. ∫₀³ 2x dx is the triangle with base 3 and height 6: area = ½ × 3 × 6 = **9**.
2. ∫₀² √(4 − x²) dx is a quarter of a circle of radius 2: π × 4 ÷ 4 = **π**.
3. ∫₋₁¹ x dx: the triangle left of 0 is negative, the right one positive. Total = **0**.
4. ∫₀^(2π) sin x dx = **0**: the positive hump cancels the negative one.
/EX

**Properties** (all follow from the picture):
- ∫ₐᵇ f dx + ∫ᵦᶜ f dx = ∫ₐᶜ f dx (areas add).
- ∫ₐᵃ f dx = 0 (no width), and ∫ᵦₐ f dx = −∫ₐᵇ f dx (going backwards).
- ∫ₐᵇ (c f + g) dx = c ∫ₐᵇ f dx + ∫ₐᵇ g dx (linearity).
- If f ≥ g then ∫ f ≥ ∫ g.

### An exact area from the limit
We can compute an exact area with sums. Use Σ i = n(n + 1) ÷ 2 and Σ i² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1) ÷ 6.

EX: ∫₀ᵇ x² dx from first principles
1. Δx = b ÷ n and the right edges are xᵢ = i b ÷ n.
2. Sum = Σ (i b ÷ n)² (b ÷ n) = (b³ ÷ n³) Σ i² = (b³ ÷ n³) · n(n + 1)(2n + 1) ÷ 6 = b³ (1 + 1/n)(2 + 1/n) ÷ 6.
3. As n → ∞, (1 + 1/n)(2 + 1/n) → 2. So **∫₀ᵇ x² dx = b³ ÷ 3**. (With b = 2 this gives 8/3, matching the lab.)
/EX

EX: Two more by the same method
1. ∫₀ᵇ x dx = (b² ÷ n²) · n(n + 1) ÷ 2 → **b² ÷ 2** (the area of a triangle, ✓).
2. ∫₀ᵇ x³ dx: use Σ i³ = [n(n + 1) ÷ 2]². Then the sum is (b⁴ ÷ n⁴) · n²(n + 1)² ÷ 4 = b⁴ (1 + 1/n)² ÷ 4 → **b⁴ ÷ 4**.
3. Pattern: ∫₀ᵇ xⁿ dx = bⁿ⁺¹ ÷ (n + 1). The same exponent rule as the antiderivative, and tomorrow's theorem explains why.
/EX

BN: ক্ষেত্রফল = সীমা (limit) নিয়ে আয়তক্ষেত্রের যোগফল: ∫ₐᵇ f dx = lim Σ f(xᵢ)Δx। অক্ষের নিচের অংশ ঋণাত্মক, তাই এটি signed area। প্রথম নীতি থেকে ∫₀ᵇ x² dx = b³/3, ∫₀ᵇ x dx = b²/2, ∫₀ᵇ x³ dx = b⁴/4 বের হলো। কাল দেখব এই ঘাত সূত্র আর প্রতিঅন্তরকলজ একই জিনিস।

### Estimating from data
In real life you often have a table instead of a formula. Then use the **trapezoid rule**: for each slice, average the two end values and multiply by the width.

EX: A speedometer log
1. A car's speed (m/s) at 0, 10, 20, 30 and 40 s is 0, 8, 14, 18, 20.
2. Distance ≈ 10 × [(0 + 8) ÷ 2 + (8 + 14) ÷ 2 + (14 + 18) ÷ 2 + (18 + 20) ÷ 2] = 10 × (4 + 11 + 16 + 19) = **500 m**.
/EX

EX: A trip with three phases
1. A train accelerates uniformly from 0 to 20 m/s in 10 s, cruises at 20 m/s for 20 s, then brakes uniformly to rest in 5 s.
2. Area = ½ × 10 × 20 + 20 × 20 + ½ × 5 × 20 = 100 + 400 + 50 = **550 m**.
/EX

TRAP: A **definite** integral (with limits a and b) is a **number**. An **indefinite** integral (no limits) is a **family of functions** with + C. Also, "area" and "integral" differ when the graph dips below the axis: the integral subtracts that part. To get the true total area, split at the crossing points and add absolute values.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: moment of inertia by integration | 35 min

### From sums to integrals
For a continuous object, I = Σ m r² becomes an integral: cut the object into tiny pieces of mass dm, each at distance r from the axis, and add:

EQ: I = ∫ r² dm

You need to write dm in terms of the variable you integrate over (length x or radius r).

EX: A thin rod about its centre
1. The rod has mass M and length L, so the mass per unit length is M ÷ L. A slice of length dx at position x (measured from the centre) has dm = (M ÷ L) dx.
2. I = ∫ x² (M ÷ L) dx from −L/2 to L/2 = (M ÷ L) × 2 × (L/2)³ ÷ 3 = (M ÷ L)(L³ ÷ 12) = **M L² ÷ 12**.
/EX

EX: The same rod about one end
1. Measure x from the end: I = (M ÷ L) ∫₀ᴸ x² dx = (M ÷ L)(L³ ÷ 3) = **M L² ÷ 3**. Four times the centre value, because most of the mass is now far from the axis.
/EX

EX: A solid disc about its centre
1. Cut the disc into rings of radius r and thickness dr. The ring has area 2π r dr. The mass per unit area is σ = M ÷ (π R²), so dm = σ 2π r dr.
2. I = ∫₀ᴿ r² σ 2π r dr = 2π σ ∫₀ᴿ r³ dr = 2π σ R⁴ ÷ 4 = 2π (M ÷ π R²) R⁴ ÷ 4 = **½ M R²**. ✓ (This is where yesterday's table came from.)
/EX

BN: অবিচ্ছিন্ন বস্তুর জন্য I = Σmr² হয়ে যায় I = ∫ r² dm। দণ্ড (ভর M, দৈর্ঘ্য L): প্রতি একক দৈর্ঘ্যে ভর M/L, তাই dm = (M/L)dx এবং কেন্দ্রের সাপেক্ষে I = ML²/12, প্রান্তের সাপেক্ষে ML²/3। ডিস্ককে রিং এ কেটে (dm = σ·2πr dr) পাই I = ½MR², যা গতকালের সারণির উৎস।

### The parallel axis theorem
If you know I about the centre of mass, I about any **parallel** axis a distance d away is:

EQ: I = I_cm + M d²

Check with the rod: about the end, d = L/2, so I = M L² ÷ 12 + M L² ÷ 4 = M L² ÷ 3 ✓. The centre-of-mass axis always gives the smallest I (yesterday's challenge showed this for two masses).

EX: Using it
1. Disc of mass 4 kg and radius 0.5 m about an axis through its rim (parallel to the central axis): I = ½ × 4 × 0.25 + 4 × 0.25 = 0.5 + 1 = **1.5 kg·m²** (= 3/2 M R²).
2. A ring of mass 3 kg and radius 0.1 m about a point on its rim: I = M R² + M R² = **2 M R² = 0.06 kg·m²**.
/EX

LAB: Moving the axis away from the centre | A uniform rod of mass M and length L spins about an axis a distance d from its centre. The graph shows I = M L² ÷ 12 + M d², which is a parabola with its lowest point at d = 0. | M:Mass M (kg):1:5:0.5:2 ;; L:Length L (m):0.5:3:0.25:1.5 ;; d:Axis offset d (m):0:1.5:0.05:0.5 | M*L^2/12:I about the centre (kg·m²):3 ;; M*L^2/12+M*d^2:I at the chosen axis (kg·m²):3 ;; M*L^2/3:I about the end (kg·m²):3 | M*L^2/12+M*x^2 | 0,1.5 | 0,12

TRAP: The parallel axis theorem only works if one of the two axes goes through the **centre of mass**. To go from an axis at one end to another off-centre axis, first return to the centre of mass (subtract M d₁²), then add M d₂². And remember d is the perpendicular distance between the axes, squared.
