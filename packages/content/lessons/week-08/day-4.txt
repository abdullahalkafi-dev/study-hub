# Integration by substitution. Then: angular momentum and the ice skater
lead: Differentiation has the chain rule for functions inside functions. Integration has its mirror image, substitution, which untangles an integral by renaming the "inside" as a new variable. In physics, you meet the second big conservation law of rotation: angular momentum. It explains why a skater spins faster when pulling their arms in, why a diver tucks, and why planets speed up near the Sun.

BN: অন্তরকলনে ফাংশনের ভেতরে ফাংশন সামলাতে চেইন রুল আছে। সমাকলনে তার প্রতিবিম্ব হলো প্রতিস্থাপন (substitution): ভেতরের অংশকে নতুন চলক নাম দিয়ে জট খোলা। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে আসছে ঘূর্ণনের দ্বিতীয় বড় সংরক্ষণ সূত্র: কৌণিক ভরবেগ। এটাই ব্যাখ্যা করে স্কেটার হাত গুটালে কেন দ্রুত ঘোরে, ডুবুরি কেন শরীর গুটায়, আর গ্রহ সূর্যের কাছে কেন দ্রুত চলে।

@section math | 2 · Math: substitution | 55 min

### The idea
The chain rule says d/dx [F(g(x))] = F′(g(x)) · g′(x). Read it backwards:

EQ: ∫ f(g(x)) g′(x) dx = ∫ f(u) du     where u = g(x), du = g′(x) dx

**Recipe:** (1) choose u as the "inside" whose derivative also appears as a factor; (2) write du; (3) replace everything by u; (4) integrate; (5) put x back (or change the limits).

BN: চেইন রুল উল্টো করে পড়ো: ∫ f(g(x)) g′(x) dx = ∫ f(u) du, যেখানে u = g(x)। ধাপ: ভেতরের অংশ u বেছে নাও যার অন্তরকলজ গুণক হিসেবে আছে; du লেখো; সবকিছু u তে বদলাও; সমাকলন করো; আবার x বসাও।

PREDICT: In ∫ x e^(x²) dx, which part is the "inside function", and what is its derivative? Is that derivative present in the integral? => The inside is x², with derivative 2x. The integral has x, which is half of 2x, so we substitute u = x² and compensate with a factor of 1/2.

EX: Standard substitutions
1. ∫ 2x (x² + 1)³ dx: let u = x² + 1, du = 2x dx. The integral becomes ∫ u³ du = u⁴ ÷ 4 = **(x² + 1)⁴ ÷ 4 + C**.
2. ∫ x e^(x²) dx: u = x², du = 2x dx, so x dx = du ÷ 2. We get ½ ∫ eᵘ du = **½ e^(x²) + C**.
3. ∫ cos(3x + 1) dx: u = 3x + 1, du = 3 dx. We get (1/3) ∫ cos u du = **sin(3x + 1) ÷ 3 + C**.
4. ∫ sin x cos² x dx: u = cos x, du = −sin x dx. We get −∫ u² du = **−cos³ x ÷ 3 + C**.
5. ∫ 2x ÷ (x² + 1) dx: u = x² + 1 gives ∫ du ÷ u = **ln(x² + 1) + C**.
6. ∫ tan x dx = ∫ sin x ÷ cos x dx: u = cos x gives −∫ du ÷ u = **−ln abs(cos x) + C**.
7. ∫ (ln x) ÷ x dx: u = ln x, du = dx ÷ x, so ∫ u du = **(ln x)² ÷ 2 + C**.
/EX

EX: When x is left over
1. ∫ x √(x + 1) dx: let u = x + 1, so x = u − 1 and dx = du. We get ∫ (u − 1) u^(1/2) du = ∫ (u^(3/2) − u^(1/2)) du = (2/5) u^(5/2) − (2/3) u^(3/2) + C, with u = x + 1.
2. Check by differentiating: d/du of (2/5) u^(5/2) − (2/3) u^(3/2) is u^(3/2) − u^(1/2) = u^(1/2) (u − 1) = √(x + 1) · x ✓.
/EX

### Definite integrals: change the limits
When you substitute in a definite integral, convert the limits to u and never go back to x.

EX: Changing limits
1. ∫₀¹ 2x e^(x²) dx: u = x², so x = 0 gives u = 0 and x = 1 gives u = 1. ∫₀¹ eᵘ du = **e − 1 = 1.718**.
2. ∫₀² x ÷ √(x² + 5) dx: u = x² + 5, du = 2x dx. Limits: u = 5 to 9. ½ ∫₅⁹ u^(−1/2) du = [√u] = 3 − √5 = **0.764**.
3. ∫₀^(π/2) sin³ x cos x dx: u = sin x, limits 0 to 1. ∫₀¹ u³ du = **1/4**.
4. ∫₁ᵉ (ln x) ÷ x dx: u = ln x from 0 to 1. ∫₀¹ u du = **1/2**.
/EX

LAB: Substitution and area | The curve f(x) = 2x e^(x²) is the derivative of e^(x²). Substitution (u = x²) says ∫₀ᵇ 2x e^(x²) dx = e^(b²) − 1. Move b: the orange curve e^(x²) − 1 is the accumulated blue area. | b:Upper limit b:0.2:1.5:0.1:1 | exp(b^2)-1:Exact area e^(b²) − 1:4 ;; 2*b*exp(b^2):Height f(b):3 | 2*x*exp(x^2) ;; exp(x^2)-1 | 0,1.5 | 0,30

TRAP: Choose u so that **du is already in the integral** (up to a constant). If a leftover x remains after replacing, rewrite it with u (like x = u − 1) or choose a different u. Never move a function of x through the integral sign: you may only pull out constants. And in definite integrals, either change the limits or return to x before using the original limits, never mix them.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: angular momentum | 35 min

### Definition
Linear momentum is p = m v. The rotational version is **angular momentum**:

EQ: L = I ω     (for a point mass in a circle:  L = m v r)

Newton's second law in momentum form carries over: **the net torque is the rate of change of angular momentum**, τ = dL/dt. So if the net external torque is zero, L stays constant:

EQ: τ_net = 0   ⟹   I₁ ω₁ = I₂ ω₂

Units: kg·m²/s. Angular momentum has a direction (along the axis, by the right-hand rule), so spinning one way and the other way are opposite signs.

BN: কৌণিক ভরবেগ L = Iω (বিন্দু ভরের জন্য L = mvr)। টর্ক হলো L এর পরিবর্তনের হার: τ = dL/dt। বাইরের টর্ক শূন্য হলে L স্থির: I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂। অর্থাৎ I কমলে ω বাড়ে, আর L এর দিক আছে (ডানহাতি নিয়ম)।

EX: The ice skater
1. A skater spins at ω₁ = 2 rad/s with arms out, I₁ = 4 kg·m². She pulls her arms in so that I₂ = 1.2 kg·m².
2. L = I₁ ω₁ = 8 kg·m²/s stays constant (ice is nearly frictionless, so no external torque). ω₂ = 8 ÷ 1.2 = **6.67 rad/s**, more than three times faster (about 1.06 rev/s).
3. Kinetic energy: before ½ × 4 × 4 = **8 J**, after ½ × 1.2 × 44.4 = **26.7 J**. The extra 18.7 J does not appear from nowhere: it is the **work her muscles do** pulling her arms in against the "centrifugal" tendency to fly outward. Angular momentum is conserved, kinetic energy is not.
/EX

EX: A diver and a planet
1. A diver leaves the board with I = 15 kg·m² and ω = 2 rad/s, then tucks to I = 5 kg·m². ω₂ = 15 × 2 ÷ 5 = **6 rad/s**. Two full rotations (4π = 12.57 rad) take 12.57 ÷ 6 = **2.1 s**.
2. **Kepler's second law.** The Sun's pull is directed at the Sun, so it gives no torque about the Sun: L = m v r is conserved. At the Earth's farthest point (r = 1.521 × 10¹¹ m) the speed is 29.29 km/s. At the closest point (r = 1.471 × 10¹¹ m): v = 29.29 × 1.521 ÷ 1.471 = **30.29 km/s**. Closer means faster.
/EX

### Collisions that change the spin
Angular momentum is conserved whenever the colliding bodies exert only internal torques, so you can use it for "sticky" rotational collisions, just as you used momentum before.

EX: Clay hits a disc
1. A 0.2 kg clay lump flies at 5 m/s tangentially and sticks to the rim of a stationary disc (M = 2 kg, R = 0.5 m, I = ½ M R² = 0.25 kg·m²).
2. L before = m v R = 0.2 × 5 × 0.5 = 0.5. I after = 0.25 + 0.2 × 0.25 = 0.30.
3. ω = 0.5 ÷ 0.30 = **1.67 rad/s**.
/EX

EX: The merry-go-round
1. A merry-go-round (disc, M = 200 kg, R = 2 m, I = 400 kg·m²) turns at 1.5 rad/s. A 40 kg child running tangentially at 4 m/s in the same direction jumps on at the rim.
2. L before = 400 × 1.5 + 40 × 4 × 2 = 600 + 320 = 920. I after = 400 + 40 × 4 = 560.
3. ω = 920 ÷ 560 = **1.64 rad/s**.
/EX

TRAP: Angular momentum is conserved only if there is **no external torque** about the chosen axis. A skater's blades on the ice give almost none; a bicycle on the road does. Also, never conserve kinetic energy in a sticking collision, and do not forget the incoming particle's L = m v r about the **same axis** as the disc's (use the perpendicular distance r).

### Recap
- Substitution: ∫ f(g(x)) g′(x) dx = ∫ f(u) du. Choose u as the inside, write du, replace everything, integrate, convert back or change the limits. Leftover x: express it in u.
