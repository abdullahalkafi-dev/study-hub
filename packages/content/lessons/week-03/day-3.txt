@section math | 2 · Math: identities and equations | 60 min

### The identities you must know
From the unit circle (and Pythagoras), every point (cos θ, sin θ) is on a circle of radius 1, so:

EQ: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Divide every term by cos²θ and by sin²θ to get two more:

EQ: tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ     and     1 + cot²θ = csc²θ

Here the three **reciprocal functions** are:

EQ: sec θ = 1 ÷ cos θ     csc θ = 1 ÷ sin θ     cot θ = 1 ÷ tan θ = cos θ ÷ sin θ

And tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ.

BN: মূল আইডেন্টিটি sin²θ + cos²θ = 1। এটাকে cos²θ দিয়ে ভাগ করলে tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ, আর sin²θ দিয়ে ভাগ করলে 1 + cot²θ = csc²θ। এছাড়া তিনটা উল্টো ফাংশন: sec = 1/cos, csc = 1/sin, cot = 1/tan। একটা মনে রাখলে বাকিগুলো নিজে বের করতে পারো।

EX: Deriving tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ
1. Start with sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.
2. Divide every term by cos²θ: sin²θ ÷ cos²θ + 1 = 1 ÷ cos²θ.
3. The first term is tan²θ and the right side is sec²θ. So **tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ**.
/EX

### Proving identities
To prove an identity, work on **one side** (usually the more complicated) until it becomes the other. Never "move things across the equals sign", because that assumes what you are trying to prove.

**Strategies that work:**
1. Rewrite everything in terms of sin and cos.
2. Combine fractions with a common denominator.
3. Look for sin² + cos² = 1 in disguise.
4. Factor, or use a difference of squares.
5. Multiply top and bottom by a **conjugate** (like 1 − sin x).

EX: Prove (1 − cos²x) ÷ sin x = sin x
1. Left side: since 1 − cos²x = sin²x, we get sin²x ÷ sin x.
2. Cancel one sin x: **sin x** = right side ∎.
/EX

EX: Prove tan x + cot x = sec x csc x
1. Left side in sin and cos: sin x ÷ cos x + cos x ÷ sin x.
2. Common denominator sin x cos x: (sin²x + cos²x) ÷ (sin x cos x).
3. The top is 1: 1 ÷ (sin x cos x) = (1 ÷ cos x)(1 ÷ sin x) = **sec x csc x** ∎.
/EX

EX: Prove (1 + sin x) ÷ cos x = cos x ÷ (1 − sin x)
1. Multiply the left side's top and bottom by the conjugate (1 − sin x).
2. Top: (1 + sin x)(1 − sin x) = 1 − sin²x = cos²x. Bottom: cos x (1 − sin x).
3. So the left side is cos²x ÷ [cos x (1 − sin x)] = **cos x ÷ (1 − sin x)** ∎.
/EX

EX: Prove sin⁴x − cos⁴x = sin²x − cos²x
1. Factor as a difference of squares: (sin²x − cos²x)(sin²x + cos²x).
2. The second bracket is 1. So the left side is **sin²x − cos²x** ∎.
/EX

BN: আইডেন্টিটি প্রমাণ করতে এক পাশ নিয়ে কাজ করো (জটিল পাশটা), অন্য পাশের আকারে নিয়ে এসো। "এপাশ ওপাশ করে" সমান চিহ্ন পার করা চলবে না, কারণ তাতে যেটা প্রমাণ করতে চাও সেটাই ধরে নেওয়া হয়। কৌশল: সব sin আর cos এ লেখো, হর সমান করো, sin² + cos² = 1 খোঁজো, ফ্যাক্টর করো, বা conjugate দিয়ে গুণ করো।

### Solving trigonometric equations
A trig equation like sin x = 1/2 has **many** solutions because the waves repeat. First find the solutions in one period (usually 0 to 2π), then add the repeats.

**General solutions** (n is any whole number):

| Equation | General solution |
| sin x = a | x = α + 2πn or x = (π − α) + 2πn, where α = sin⁻¹(a) |
| cos x = a | x = ±α + 2πn, where α = cos⁻¹(a) |
| tan x = a | x = α + πn, where α = tan⁻¹(a) |

EX: Solve tan x = −1 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2π
1. tan is negative in quadrants II and IV. The reference angle is π/4.
2. **x = 3π/4 or x = 7π/4**.
3. General solution: x = 3π/4 + πn.
/EX

EX: Solve sin 2x = √3/2 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2π
1. Let u = 2x. Then u ranges from 0 to 4π (two full turns).
2. sin u = √3/2 gives u = π/3, 2π/3, then add 2π: 7π/3, 8π/3.
3. Divide by 2: **x = π/6, π/3, 7π/6, 4π/3**. Doubling the angle gave twice as many solutions.
/EX

### Solving with identities: quadratics in disguise
When an equation has both sin² and cos, or sin and cos², use sin² = 1 − cos² to get one function, then factor.

EX: Solve 2 sin²x + cos x = 1 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2π
1. Replace sin²x = 1 − cos²x: 2(1 − cos²x) + cos x = 1.
2. 2 − 2cos²x + cos x − 1 = 0, so 2cos²x − cos x − 1 = 0.
3. Factor (like a quadratic in c = cos x): (2c + 1)(c − 1) = 0.
4. cos x = −1/2 gives x = 2π/3 or 4π/3. cos x = 1 gives x = 0 or 2π.
5. **x = 0, 2π/3, 4π/3, 2π**.
/EX

EX: Solve 2 sin²x − sin x − 1 = 0 for 0 ≤ x < 2π
1. Factor: (2 sin x + 1)(sin x − 1) = 0.
2. sin x = −1/2 gives x = 7π/6 or 11π/6. sin x = 1 gives x = π/2.
3. **x = π/2, 7π/6, 11π/6**.
/EX

TRAP: Never divide both sides by sin x or cos x. You lose the solutions where that function is zero. Factor instead. For example, with sin x cos x = sin x: move everything left: sin x (cos x − 1) = 0, giving sin x = 0 or cos x = 1.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: projectile motion, horizontal launch | 30 min

### Two independent motions
Throw a ball sideways off a cliff. It moves forward and falls at the same time. The big idea:

KEY: **Horizontal and vertical motions are independent.** Horizontally there is no force (ignoring air), so the horizontal velocity stays **constant**. Vertically, gravity gives a constant downward acceleration g = 9.8 m/s². Time is the same for both, and links them.

A classic test: drop one ball and fire another horizontally from the same height at the same moment. They hit the ground **at the same time**. The sideways speed does not change how fast gravity pulls down.

BN: প্রক্ষেপ গতির মূল ধারণা: অনুভূমিক আর উল্লম্ব গতি পরস্পর স্বাধীন। অনুভূমিক দিকে কোনো বল নেই (বাতাস বাদ দিলে), তাই বেগ ধ্রুবক। উল্লম্ব দিকে g = 9.8 ত্বরণ। দুই দিকের সংযোগকারী হলো সময় t। একটা বল ফেলে দাও, আরেকটা পাশ থেকে ছোঁড়ো, দুটো একসাথে মাটিতে পড়বে।

### The equations
For a launch horizontally with speed u from height h (take down as positive):

| Direction | Equations |
| Horizontal | x = u t  (constant velocity, so vₓ = u) |
| Vertical | y = ½ g t²  and  v_y = g t (starts from rest vertically) |

Time to fall: t = √(2h ÷ g). Then the range (horizontal distance) is R = u t. Eliminating t gives the **path**: y = g x² ÷ (2u²), a **parabola**.

EX: A stone off a cliff
1. A stone is thrown horizontally at 15 m/s from the top of a 45 m cliff.
2. Time to fall: t = √(2 × 45 ÷ 9.8) = √9.18 = **3.03 s**.
3. Range: x = 15 × 3.03 = **45.5 m** from the base.
4. Velocity at impact: horizontal 15 m/s, vertical 9.8 × 3.03 = 29.7 m/s downward.
5. Speed = √(15² + 29.7²) = √1107 = **33.3 m/s**. Angle below the horizontal: tan⁻¹(29.7 ÷ 15) = **63.2°**.
/EX

FUNC: Path of the stone | x (m) | height (m) | 45-4.9*(x/15)**2 | 0,45 | 0,50 | - | The height above the ground is 45 − 4.9 (x ÷ 15)², a parabola. The stone reaches the ground at x = 45.5 m. Notice the path steepens as it falls.

EX: Dropping supplies
1. A plane flies level at 500 m with a speed of 60 m/s. A package is released. How far before the target should it be released?
2. t = √(2 × 500 ÷ 9.8) = **10.1 s**.
3. Horizontal distance = 60 × 10.1 = **606 m**. The package keeps the plane's forward speed, so it falls **below** the plane, not behind it (ignoring air).
/EX

EX: A rolling ball leaves a table
1. A ball leaves a 1.2 m high table at 2 m/s. Where does it land?
2. t = √(2.4 ÷ 9.8) = 0.495 s.
3. x = 2 × 0.495 = **0.99 m** from the table edge.
/EX

BN: ছোঁড়া বস্তুর পথ একটা অধিবৃত্ত (parabola)। উচ্চতা h থেকে অনুভূমিকভাবে u বেগে ছুঁড়লে পড়তে সময় লাগে t = √(2h/g), আর দূরত্ব = u × t। প্লেন থেকে ত্রাণ ফেললে সেটা প্লেনের সামনে এগিয়ে যেতে যেতে নিচে পড়ে, তাই লক্ষ্যের আগেই ছাড়তে হয়। সময় একই, তাই সময়ই দুই দিকের সেতু।

### Recap
- sin² + cos² = 1; tan² + 1 = sec²; 1 + cot² = csc². sec = 1/cos, csc = 1/sin, cot = 1/tan.
