### The sine rule
In a triangle with sides a, b, c opposite angles A, B, C:

EQ: a ÷ sin A = b ÷ sin B = c ÷ sin C

**Proof.** Drop a perpendicular of height h from vertex B to side b. In the left right-triangle, h = c sin A. In the right one, h = a sin C. So c sin A = a sin C, giving a ÷ sin A = c ÷ sin C. The same argument from another vertex links b. ∎

**Use it** when you know a side and its opposite angle, plus one more piece (an angle or another side).

EX: Finding a side
1. Triangle with A = 40°, B = 65° and a = 12. Find b and c.
2. C = 180° − 40° − 65° = 75°.
3. b = a sin B ÷ sin A = 12 × sin 65° ÷ sin 40° = 12 × 0.9063 ÷ 0.6428 = **16.9**.
4. c = 12 × sin 75° ÷ sin 40° = 12 × 0.9659 ÷ 0.6428 = **18.0**.
/EX

EX: The ambiguous case
1. Triangle with a = 8, b = 10 and A = 30°. Find B.
2. sin B = b sin A ÷ a = 10 × 0.5 ÷ 8 = 0.625.
3. B = 38.7° **or** B = 180° − 38.7° = 141.3°. Both are possible since sin of an angle and sin of its supplement are equal. Check that the angles fit: 30° + 141.3° = 171.3° < 180°, so a second triangle exists.
4. So there are **two valid triangles**. Always ask whether the second angle fits. This is called the ambiguous case.
/EX

### The cosine rule
EQ: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

**Proof.** Place C at the origin and B at (a, 0). Then A is at (b cos C, b sin C). The squared distance from A to B is c² = (a − b cos C)² + (b sin C)² = a² − 2ab cos C + b² cos²C + b² sin²C = **a² + b² − 2ab cos C**. ∎

When C = 90°, cos C = 0 and the cosine rule becomes Pythagoras. So it is a generalisation.

**Use it** when you know two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides.

EX: Two sides and the included angle
1. Sides 7 and 8 with 60° between them. Find the third side.
2. c² = 49 + 64 − 2 × 7 × 8 × cos 60° = 113 − 56 = 57.
3. c = **7.55**.
/EX

EX: Three sides, find an angle
1. A triangle has sides 5, 6 and 7. Find the largest angle (opposite 7).
2. cos C = (a² + b² − c²) ÷ (2ab) = (25 + 36 − 49) ÷ 60 = 12 ÷ 60 = 0.2.
3. C = cos⁻¹(0.2) = **78.5°**.
/EX

### Area of a triangle
EQ: Area = ½ a b sin C

This follows from the same height: area = ½ × base × height, with height = b sin C.

EX: Area
1. The triangle with sides 7 and 8 and an included angle of 60°: Area = ½ × 7 × 8 × sin 60° = 28 × 0.866 = **24.2**.
/EX

BN: দুটো নিয়ম: সাইন রুল a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, যখন একটা বাহু আর তার বিপরীত কোণ জানা আছে। কোসাইন রুল c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C, যখন দুই বাহু আর তাদের মধ্যবর্তী কোণ জানা, বা তিন বাহুই জানা। কোণ ৯০° হলে cos = 0 আর কোসাইন রুল হয়ে যায় পীথাগোরাস। ক্ষেত্রফল = ½ab sin C।

### Choosing the right rule
| You know | Use |
| A side and its opposite angle, plus one more angle or side | Sine rule |
| Two sides and the angle between them | Cosine rule |
| All three sides | Cosine rule (to find an angle) |
| Two angles and any side | Sum of angles = 180°, then sine rule |

EX: Two ships
1. Two ships leave a port at the same time. One sails 15 km, the other 20 km, with an angle of 80° between their paths. How far apart are they?
2. Two sides and the included angle, so use the cosine rule: d² = 225 + 400 − 2 × 15 × 20 × cos 80° = 625 − 600 × 0.1736 = 625 − 104.2 = 520.8.
3. d = **22.8 km**.
/EX

EX: Height of a tower from two measurements
1. From a point A on level ground the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 30°. After walking 50 m towards it, the angle becomes 45°. Find the height.
2. Let the tower be h and the distance from the second point be d. Then tan 45° = h ÷ d gives d = h. Also tan 30° = h ÷ (d + 50) = h ÷ (h + 50) = 1/√3.
3. So h√3 = h + 50, giving h (√3 − 1) = 50 and h = 50 ÷ 0.732 = **68.3 m**.
/EX

### Inverse trigonometric functions
sin x is not one-to-one over all real numbers, since many angles share the same sine. To define an inverse, we restrict the domain, just as we restricted x² to x ≥ 0 to get the square root.

| Function | Meaning | Output range |
| sin⁻¹ x (arcsin) | The angle whose sine is x | from −π/2 to π/2 |
| cos⁻¹ x (arccos) | The angle whose cosine is x | from 0 to π |
| tan⁻¹ x (arctan) | The angle whose tangent is x | between −π/2 and π/2 |

For sin⁻¹ and cos⁻¹, the input must be between −1 and 1.

FUNC: Inverse sine and inverse cosine | x | angle (radians) | asin(x) ;; acos(x) | -1,1 | -2,3.5 | - | Teal: sin⁻¹x, rising from −π/2 to π/2. Orange: cos⁻¹x, falling from π to 0. They are reflections of the restricted sine and cosine curves in the line y = x.

EX: Values
1. sin⁻¹(1/2) = **π/6**.
2. sin⁻¹(−1/2) = **−π/6** (the output range includes negative angles).
3. cos⁻¹(−1/2) = **2π/3** (in the second quadrant, as the range 0 to π demands).
4. tan⁻¹(−1) = **−π/4**.
/EX

EX: A trap
1. What is sin⁻¹(sin(5π/6))? The inner value is sin(5π/6) = 1/2.
2. The outer function returns the angle in its own range: sin⁻¹(1/2) = π/6.
3. So the answer is **π/6**, not 5π/6. The inverse functions only undo the originals inside their restricted range.
/EX

EX: Composite: sin(cos⁻¹(3/5))
1. Let θ = cos⁻¹(3/5). Then cos θ = 3/5 and θ is in quadrant I.
2. Draw the right triangle: adjacent 3, hypotenuse 5, so opposite = 4.
3. sin θ = **4/5**.
/EX

BN: sin x একটা ইনভার্স ফাংশন পেতে তার ডোমেইন সীমিত করতে হয়, যেমন x² কে x ≥ 0 এ সীমিত করে বর্গমূল পাই। sin⁻¹ এর আউটপুট −π/2 থেকে π/2 এর মধ্যে, cos⁻¹ এর 0 থেকে π, tan⁻¹ এর −π/2 থেকে π/2 এর মধ্যে। তাই sin⁻¹(sin(5π/6)) = π/6, 5π/6 নয়। ক্যালকুলেটর তোমাকে সবসময় এই সীমার মধ্যের কোণই দেবে।

### Splitting weight on a slope
A block sits on a ramp that makes an angle θ with the horizontal. Weight mg still points straight down, but the ramp's surface is tilted. So it is convenient to split the weight into two parts relative to the **slope**: one **along** the slope and one **perpendicular** to it.

**Why the angle is the same.** The angle between the weight and the perpendicular to the ramp equals the ramp's angle θ (tilting the ramp by θ tilts its perpendicular by θ). So:

EQ: component along the slope = mg sin θ     component perpendicular to the slope = mg cos θ

Check with extremes: for θ = 0 (flat), the along-slope part is 0 and the perpendicular part is mg ✓. For θ = 90° (a vertical wall), the whole weight is along the slope ✓.

BN: ঢালু তলে বস্তুর ওজন mg সোজা নিচে, কিন্তু আমরা তা দুই ভাগে ভাঙি: ঢাল বরাবর mg sin θ (যেটা বস্তুকে নিচে গড়াতে টানে) আর ঢালের লম্বভাবে mg cos θ (যেটাকে অভিলম্ব বল N সামলায়)। যাচাই: θ = 0 হলে ঢাল বরাবর অংশ শূন্য, θ = 90° হলে পুরো ওজনই ঢাল বরাবর।

### The method
1. Draw the FBD: weight (down), normal force N (perpendicular to the surface, away from it), friction (along the slope, opposite to sliding or the tendency).
2. Choose axes: x along the slope, y perpendicular to it.
3. Perpendicular: no acceleration, so **N = mg cos θ**.
4. Along the slope: net force = m a.

EX: A smooth slope
1. A block slides down a frictionless 30° slope. Find its acceleration.
2. Only mg sin θ acts along the slope: m a = m g sin 30°.
3. **a = g sin 30° = 4.9 m/s²**. The mass cancels, so every block slides with the same acceleration on a smooth slope.
4. If the slope is 30 m long and the block starts from rest: v² = 2 × 4.9 × 30 = 294, so **v = 17.1 m/s** at the bottom.
/EX

EX: A rough slope
1. A 5 kg block slides down a 25° slope with μ_k = 0.2.
2. N = mg cos 25° = 5 × 9.8 × 0.9063 = 44.4 N.
3. Friction (up the slope) f = 0.2 × 44.4 = 8.88 N.
4. Gravity along the slope: mg sin 25° = 49 × 0.4226 = 20.7 N (down the slope).
5. Net = 20.7 − 8.88 = 11.8 N, so **a = 11.8 ÷ 5 = 2.37 m/s²**. In one line: a = g(sin θ − μ cos θ) = 9.8 × (0.4226 − 0.1813) = 2.37 m/s².
/EX

EX: When does a block start to slide?
1. On a slope, friction must balance the along-slope part for the block to stay at rest: f = mg sin θ with f ≤ μ_s N = μ_s mg cos θ.
2. So it stays put when tan θ ≤ μ_s. At the steepest angle where it still holds, **tan θ = μ_s**, called the **angle of repose**.
3. For μ_s = 0.5: θ = tan⁻¹(0.5) = **26.6°**. This is also how you measure μ_s: tilt a ramp until the block starts to slide.
/EX

EX: Pushing up a slope at constant speed
1. A 10 kg crate is pushed up a 20° ramp at constant speed with a force parallel to the ramp. μ_k = 0.3.
2. Constant speed means net force zero along the slope: F = mg sin θ + μ mg cos θ.
3. F = 98 × (0.342 + 0.3 × 0.9397) = 98 × 0.6239 = **61.1 N**.
/EX

TRAP: The normal force is **not** mg on a slope. It is mg cos θ. Also the angle in "mg sin θ" is the angle of the **slope**, so double-check which angle you are given.

### Recap
- Sine rule: a ÷ sin A = b ÷ sin B = c ÷ sin C (watch the ambiguous case). Cosine rule: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. Area = ½ab sin C.
- Inverse trig: sin⁻¹ and tan⁻¹ give −π/2 to π/2, cos⁻¹ gives 0 to π.
- Slope: along = mg sin θ, perpendicular = mg cos θ, N = mg cos θ. Smooth slope a = g sin θ. Rough: a = g(sin θ − μ cos θ). Angle of repose tan θ = μ_s.

BN: আজকের সারকথা: সাইন রুল আর কোসাইন রুল দিয়ে যেকোনো ত্রিভুজ সমাধান, ক্ষেত্রফল ½ab sin C, ইনভার্স ত্রিকোণমিতিক ফাংশনের সীমা, আর ঢালু তলে ওজনের দুই উপাদান: mg sin θ (ঢাল বরাবর) আর mg cos θ (লম্ব)। স্থির থাকার শর্ত tan θ ≤ μ_s।
