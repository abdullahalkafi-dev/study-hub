@section math | 2 · Math: the unit circle and trig graphs | 60 min

### From triangle to circle
Draw a circle of radius 1 centred at the origin (the **unit circle**). Start at the point (1, 0) and rotate **counterclockwise** by an angle θ. You arrive at a point (x, y).

Look at the right triangle formed by that point, the origin and the x-axis. Its hypotenuse is the radius, which is 1. So:

- adjacent = x = cos θ ÷ 1, so **x = cos θ**
- opposite = y = sin θ ÷ 1, so **y = sin θ**

This gives us the definition that works for **any** angle, even 200°, −50° or 1000°:

EQ: the point on the unit circle at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ)     and     tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ

When the point has a negative x or y, cos or sin become negative. That is how trig functions extend beyond 90°. A **negative angle** means rotating clockwise, and an angle over 360° just goes round again.

KEY: sin θ = the **height** of the point on the unit circle. cos θ = its **horizontal** position. As you walk around the circle, sin and cos trace out the waves you are about to graph.

BN: একক বৃত্তে (ব্যাসার্ধ ১) কোণ θ তে যে বিন্দু পাওয়া যায়, তার স্থানাঙ্ক (cos θ, sin θ)। অর্থাৎ cos হলো বিন্দুর অনুভূমিক অবস্থান আর sin হলো উচ্চতা। এই সংজ্ঞা যেকোনো কোণের জন্য কাজ করে, এমনকি ঋণাত্মক বা ৩৬০° এর বেশি কোণের জন্যও। ঘড়ির উল্টো দিকে ঘোরা ধনাত্মক কোণ।

### Signs in the four quadrants
| Quadrant | Angle range | sin | cos | tan |
| I | 0° to 90° | + | + | + |
| II | 90° to 180° | + | − | − |
| III | 180° to 270° | − | − | + |
| IV | 270° to 360° | − | + | − |

Memory aid: **All Students Take Calculus**: **A**ll are positive in I, **S**in in II, **T**an in III, **C**os in IV.

### Reference angles: no new values to learn
The **reference angle** is the acute angle between the terminal arm and the x-axis. The value of sin, cos or tan at any angle equals the value at its reference angle, up to a sign given by the quadrant.

EX: sin 150°
1. 150° is in quadrant II. The reference angle is 180° − 150° = 30°.
2. sin is positive in II. So sin 150° = sin 30° = **1/2**.
/EX

EX: cos 210°
1. 210° is in quadrant III. Reference angle 210° − 180° = 30°.
2. cos is negative in III. So cos 210° = −cos 30° = **−√3/2**.
/EX

EX: tan(5π/3)
1. 5π/3 = 300°, in quadrant IV. Reference angle 60°.
2. tan is negative in IV. tan 300° = −tan 60° = **−√3**.
/EX

EX: Exact values at the axes
1. At 0 the point is (1, 0): cos 0 = 1, sin 0 = 0.
2. At π/2 it is (0, 1): cos = 0, sin = 1, and tan is **undefined** (division by zero).
3. At π it is (−1, 0). At 3π/2 it is (0, −1). Back to (1, 0) at 2π.
/EX

BN: কোনো কোণের মান বের করতে প্রথমে রেফারেন্স কোণ (x-অক্ষের সাথে তীক্ষ্ণ কোণ) বের করো, তারপর চতুর্ভাগ দেখে চিহ্ন ঠিক করো। তাই নতুন মান মুখস্থ করতে হয় না। 90° এ tan অসংজ্ঞায়িত কারণ cos = 0 আর ০ দিয়ে ভাগ চলে না।

### The graph of sine
Walk around the unit circle at steady speed and record the **height** (sin θ). You get a smooth wave that repeats every 2π.

FUNC: y = sin x and y = cos x | x (radians) | y | sin(x) ;; cos(x) | -2*pi,2*pi | -1.5,1.5 | pi | Teal: y = sin x. Orange: y = cos x. Both repeat every 2π and stay between −1 and 1. The cosine curve is the sine curve shifted left by π/2.

**Properties of sin x:**
- Period 2π (the pattern repeats every 2π). Range from −1 to 1. Amplitude 1.
- Passes through (0, 0). It is an **odd** function: sin(−x) = −sin x (symmetric about the origin).

**Properties of cos x:**
- Period 2π. Range from −1 to 1. Starts at its maximum, cos 0 = 1.
- It is an **even** function: cos(−x) = cos x (symmetric about the y-axis).
- cos x = sin(x + π/2), a shifted copy of sine.

### The graph of tangent
Since tan x = sin x ÷ cos x, tangent explodes wherever cos x = 0, that is at x = π/2, 3π/2, and so on. These are **vertical asymptotes**.

FUNC: y = tan x | x (radians) | y | tan(x) | -pi,pi | -4,4 | pi | The tangent curve has period π and vertical asymptotes at odd multiples of π/2. It rises from −∞ to +∞ between each pair of asymptotes.

BN: sin আর cos এর গ্রাফ ঢেউয়ের মতো, প্রতি 2π তে পুনরাবৃত্তি, মান −১ থেকে ১ এর মধ্যে। cos গ্রাফ হলো sin গ্রাফকে π/2 বামে সরানো। tan = sin ÷ cos, তাই যেখানে cos = 0 সেখানে tan অসীমে চলে যায়, সেখানে উল্লম্ব অ্যাসিম্পটোট। tan এর পর্যায়কাল (period) π।

### Transforming the waves
Yesterday's transformation rules (shift, stretch, flip) apply to waves. The general form:

EQ: y = A · sin(B(x − C)) + D

| Letter | What it does | How to read it |
| A | Amplitude (height of the wave from its middle) | The size of A, ignoring its sign |
| B | Squeezes the wave horizontally | Period = 2π ÷ B |
| C | Phase shift (slides it right) | Shift C to the right |
| D | Vertical shift (the midline) | Wave oscillates about y = D |

EX: y = 3 sin(2x) + 1
1. Amplitude = 3. Period = 2π ÷ 2 = **π**. Midline y = 1.
2. The maximum is 1 + 3 = 4 and the minimum is 1 − 3 = −2.
/EX

FUNC: y = 3 sin(2x) + 1 | x (radians) | y | 3*sin(2*x)+1 ;; sin(x) | -pi,2*pi | -3,5 | pi | The tall teal wave is y = 3 sin 2x + 1: amplitude 3, period π, midline at y = 1. The orange wave is plain y = sin x for comparison.

EX: Mains electricity
1. In Bangladesh the supply is 220 V at 50 Hz. The voltage peak is 220 × √2 = 311 V.
2. The voltage is V(t) = 311 sin(100π t), where t is in seconds.
3. Here B = 100π, so the period is 2π ÷ 100π = **0.02 s**, which gives 50 cycles per second ✓.
/EX

BN: A হলো বিস্তার (amplitude), B ঢেউকে চেপে দেয় (পর্যায়কাল = 2π ÷ B), C ডানে সরায়, D পুরো ঢেউকে ওপর-নিচে সরায়। যেমন বাংলাদেশের বিদ্যুৎ ২২০ V, ৫০ Hz, তার সর্বোচ্চ ভোল্টেজ ৩১১ V, আর সূত্র V = 311 sin(100πt)। পর্যায়কাল ০.০২ সেকেন্ড মানে প্রতি সেকেন্ডে ৫০ চক্র।

### Velocity depends on who is watching
A passenger sitting in a train is at rest relative to the train but moves at 100 km/h relative to the ground. Velocity is always measured **relative to something**. When two things move, we use:

EQ: v(A relative to B) = v(A relative to ground) − v(B relative to ground)

Equivalent: v(A relative to ground) = v(A relative to B) + v(B relative to ground). This is just vector addition, which you did yesterday.

EX: Cars on a road
1. Car A drives east at 90 km/h and car B ahead of it also drives east at 60 km/h.
2. A relative to B: 90 − 60 = **30 km/h east**. That is how fast A closes the gap.
3. If B were coming towards A at 60 km/h (west), A relative to B = 90 − (−60) = **150 km/h**.
/EX

BN: বেগ সবসময় কারো সাপেক্ষে মাপা হয়। A এর B এর সাপেক্ষে বেগ = A এর মাটির সাপেক্ষে বেগ − B এর মাটির সাপেক্ষে বেগ। একই দিকে চললে বেগ বিয়োগ হয়, উল্টো দিকে এলে যোগ হয় (দিক-চিহ্ন সহ)। তাই দুটো গাড়ি মুখোমুখি এলে সংঘর্ষের গতি ভয়ানক বেশি হয়।

### A boat crossing a river
A boat moves at 4 m/s relative to the water. The river flows at 3 m/s. The boat's actual velocity over the ground is the **sum** of these two vectors.

EX: Aiming straight across
1. The boat points straight across (perpendicular to the banks). The two velocity vectors are at right angles: 4 m/s across and 3 m/s downstream.
2. Ground speed = √(16 + 9) = **5 m/s**, at an angle tan⁻¹(3 ÷ 4) = **36.9° downstream** from the line across.
3. If the river is 200 m wide, the crossing time depends only on the across component: t = 200 ÷ 4 = **50 s**.
4. In 50 s the current carries the boat 3 × 50 = **150 m downstream**.
/EX

EX: Reaching the point directly opposite
1. To land directly opposite, the boat must aim upstream by an angle φ so that its upstream component cancels the current: 4 sin φ = 3.
2. sin φ = 3/4, so φ = **48.6° upstream** from the perpendicular.
3. The crossing speed is now 4 cos φ = 4 × 0.661 = **2.65 m/s**, so t = 200 ÷ 2.65 = **75.6 s**. It takes longer.
/EX

KEY: Perpendicular components do not affect each other. The across speed alone determines the crossing time, and the downstream speed alone determines the drift.

EX: An aeroplane in wind
1. A plane flies at an airspeed of 200 km/h, heading north. A wind blows from the west at 50 km/h, which pushes the plane east.
2. Ground velocity: 200 north and 50 east. Speed = √(200² + 50²) = **206 km/h**.
3. Direction: tan⁻¹(50 ÷ 200) = **14° east of north**. To fly due north on the ground, the pilot must aim into the wind: 200 sin φ = 50, so φ = 14.5° west of north.
/EX

EX: Rain on a runner
1. Rain falls vertically at 10 m/s. You run at 4 m/s along a road.
2. The rain relative to you has components: 10 m/s downward and 4 m/s backwards (opposite your motion). Speed = √(100 + 16) = **10.8 m/s**.
3. The rain seems to come from ahead, at tan⁻¹(4 ÷ 10) = **21.8° from the vertical**. You should tilt your umbrella forward by that angle.
/EX

BN: নদীতে নৌকা: সোজা ওপারে মুখ করলে নৌকা স্রোতে ভেসে যায়। পার হওয়ার সময় শুধু ওপারমুখী বেগ নির্ধারণ করে (200 ÷ 4 = 50 s), আর ভেসে যাওয়ার দূরত্ব = স্রোত × সময়। ঠিক বিপরীত বিন্দুতে পৌঁছাতে চাইলে স্রোতের উল্টো দিকে কিছুটা কোণ করে চালাতে হয়। লম্ব উপাদান একে অপরকে প্রভাবিত করে না, এই কথাটা প্রক্ষেপ গতিতেও (projectile) আসবে।

### Recap
- Unit circle: (cos θ, sin θ). tan θ = sin ÷ cos. Signs by quadrant (All Students Take Calculus).
- Reference angle gives the value; the quadrant gives the sign.
- sin and cos: period 2π, amplitude 1. tan: period π, asymptotes where cos = 0.
- y = A sin(B(x − C)) + D: amplitude |A|, period 2π ÷ B, shift C, midline D.
- Relative velocity: subtract velocities. Perpendicular components are independent.

BN: আজকের সারকথা: একক বৃত্তে (cos θ, sin θ), রেফারেন্স কোণ আর চতুর্ভাগ থেকে চিহ্ন, sin/cos এর পর্যায়কাল 2π, tan এর π, আর A sin(B(x − C)) + D এর অর্থ। সাপেক্ষ বেগ মানে ভেক্টর বিয়োগ, আর লম্ব উপাদানগুলো স্বাধীন।
