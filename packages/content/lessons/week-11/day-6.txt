@section math | 2 · Part A: mathematics (28 marks, about 80 minutes) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 11
P: (2 marks) Find lim (x → 0) (sin 3x − 3x) ÷ x³. => sin 3x = 3x − 27x³/6 + …, so sin 3x − 3x = −(9/2) x³ + …, and the limit is **−9/2**. (L'Hôpital: three rounds give −27 cos 3x ÷ 6 → −9/2.)
P: (2 marks) Differentiate f(x) = x^x for x > 0 and find f′(2). => ln f = x ln x, so f′ ÷ f = ln x + 1. **f′ = x^x (ln x + 1)**. f′(2) = 4 (ln 2 + 1) = **6.77**.
P: (3 marks) Water pours at 3 m³ per minute into an inverted cone of radius 2 m and height 4 m. How fast is the depth rising when the depth is 2 m? => At depth h the water radius is r = h ÷ 2, so V = (1/3) π r² h = π h³ ÷ 12. dV/dt = (π h² ÷ 4) dh/dt. At h = 2: 3 = π dh/dt, so **dh/dt = 3 ÷ π ≈ 0.955 m/min**.
P: (3 marks) Find the minimum value of f(x) = x² + 16 ÷ x for x > 0, and show that it is a minimum. => f′ = 2x − 16 ÷ x² = 0 gives x³ = 8, **x = 2**. f(2) = 4 + 8 = **12**. f″ = 2 + 32 ÷ x³ = 6 > 0 at x = 2, so it is a **minimum**.
P: (2 marks) Use Newton's method on f(x) = x² − 3 with x₀ = 2 to find x₂ to three decimal places. => x₁ = 2 − (4 − 3) ÷ 4 = 1.75. x₂ = 1.75 − (3.0625 − 3) ÷ 3.5 = 1.75 − 0.01786 = **1.732**.
P: (3 marks) Evaluate ∫₀¹ x² eˣ dx. => By parts twice: ∫ x² eˣ dx = eˣ (x² − 2x + 2). From 0 to 1: e (1) − (1)(2) = **e − 2 ≈ 0.718**.
P: (3 marks) Evaluate ∫₀^(π/2) sin³ x cos² x dx. => sin³ x cos² x = (1 − cos² x) cos² x sin x. With u = cos x (from 1 to 0, du = −sin x dx): ∫₀¹ (u² − u⁴) du = 1/3 − 1/5 = **2/15**.
P: (3 marks) Evaluate ∫₂⁴ 1 ÷ (x² − 1) dx. => 1 ÷ (x² − 1) = ½ [1 ÷ (x − 1) − 1 ÷ (x + 1)]. Integral = ½ [ln((x − 1) ÷ (x + 1))] from 2 to 4 = ½ [ln(3/5) − ln(1/3)] = ½ ln(9/5) = **0.294**.
P: (2 marks) Evaluate ∫₀^∞ x e^(−3x) dx. => By parts: [−x e^(−3x) ÷ 3 − e^(−3x) ÷ 9] from 0 to ∞ = 0 − (−1/9) = **1/9**.
P: (3 marks) Find the radius and the interval of convergence of Σ (2x)ⁿ ÷ √n. => The ratio tends to 2 abs(x), so **R = 1/2**. At x = 1/2: Σ 1 ÷ √n diverges (p = 1/2). At x = −1/2: Σ (−1)ⁿ ÷ √n converges (alternating). Interval **[−1/2, 1/2)**.
P: (2 marks) Find the Maclaurin series of eˣ cos x up to the term in x³. => (1 + x + x²/2 + x³/6)(1 − x²/2) = 1 + x + (x²/2 − x²/2) + (x³/6 − x³/2) = **1 + x − x³/3**.

@section physics | 3 · Part B: physics (12 marks, about 40 minutes) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 12 to 17
P: (2 marks) A 0.5 kg block slides at 3 m/s on a smooth table into a spring of stiffness 200 N/m. Find the maximum compression. => ½ m v² = ½ k x², so x = v √(m ÷ k) = 3 × √(0.0025) = **0.15 m**.
P: (2 marks) A solid disc rolls without slipping up a 30° incline, starting at 4 m/s at the bottom. How far up the incline does it go? => KE = ½ M v² (1.5) = M g h, so h = 1.5 × 16 ÷ 19.6 = 1.224 m. Distance along the slope = h ÷ sin 30° = **2.45 m**.
P: (2 marks) A disc (M = 3 kg, R = 0.4 m) is at rest on a frictionless axle. A 0.3 kg lump of clay moving at 6 m/s tangentially hits its rim and sticks. Find the final angular speed. => I_disc = ½ × 3 × 0.16 = 0.24. L = 0.3 × 6 × 0.4 = 0.72. I_after = 0.24 + 0.3 × 0.16 = 0.288. ω = 0.72 ÷ 0.288 = **2.5 rad/s**.
P: (2 marks) A damped oscillator has m = 0.5 kg, k = 50 N/m and c = 1 N·s/m. Find γ, ω_d, the quality factor Q, and the time for the amplitude to halve. => γ = c ÷ 2m = **1 per second**. ω₀ = 10 so ω_d = √99 = **9.95 rad/s**. Q = ω₀ ÷ (2γ) = **5**. Half-amplitude time = ln 2 ÷ γ = **0.693 s**.
P: (2 marks) A string (μ = 0.005 kg/m, T = 45 N, L = 0.75 m) is fixed at both ends. Find the wave speed, f₁ and f₂. => v = √(45 ÷ 0.005) = **94.9 m/s**. f₁ = 94.9 ÷ 1.5 = **63.2 Hz**. f₂ = **126.5 Hz**.
P: (2 marks) A 10 W source radiates sound uniformly. Find the level at 20 m, and the distance at which the level is 70 dB. => I = 10 ÷ (4π × 400) = 1.99 × 10⁻³ W/m². β = 10 log(1.99 × 10⁹) = **93.0 dB**. To fall by 23 dB, the distance grows by 10^(23/20) = 14.1: r = 20 × 14.1 = **283 m**.

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | Calculus and physics together
P: A hanging chain of length L and mass M lies coiled on a table, and its end is lifted at a constant speed v. Show that the force needed at the end is F = M g x ÷ L + (M ÷ L) v² when a length x is off the table (use F = dp/dt for the moving part). Find F when x = L ÷ 2, M = 2 kg, L = 4 m, v = 3 m/s. => The moving piece of length x has momentum p = (M x ÷ L) v. Newton: F − M g x ÷ L = dp/dt = (M ÷ L) v² (since dx/dt = v). So **F = M g x ÷ L + M v² ÷ L**. For the numbers: F = 2 × 9.8 × 0.5 + 2 × 9 ÷ 4 = 9.8 + 4.5 = **14.3 N**.
LVL: Challenge | Series and oscillators
P: Use the Taylor series to show that for small damping the damped frequency satisfies ω_d ≈ ω₀ (1 − γ² ÷ (2 ω₀²)), and find the percentage shift for γ = 1 and ω₀ = 10. => ω_d = ω₀ √(1 − γ² ÷ ω₀²) ≈ ω₀ (1 − γ² ÷ (2 ω₀²)) from (1 + x)^(1/2) ≈ 1 + x/2. The shift is γ² ÷ (2 ω₀²) = 1 ÷ 200 = **0.5%** (exact: √99 ÷ 10 = 0.99499).

### What comes next: Phase 3
Phase 3 (Weeks 12 to 23) takes you from single-variable calculus to the mathematics of engineering systems and the physics of matter and electricity. It begins next week with **complex numbers** (which you glimpsed in Euler's formula) and the physics of **fluids**. Over the phase you will meet matrices and linear systems, eigenvalues, multivariable calculus, multiple integrals, differential equations, Laplace transforms and Fourier series, paired with thermal physics, gases, entropy, electrostatics, circuits, magnetism, induction and Maxwell's equations.
