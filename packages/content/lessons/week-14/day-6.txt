@section math | 2 · Part A: Linear systems (14 marks, about 55 min) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 13
P: (1 mark) Solve x + 2y = 7 and 3x − y = 7. => From the second y = 3x − 7. Then x + 6x − 14 = 7, **x = 3**, **y = 2**. Check: 3 + 4 = 7 ✓.
P: (2 marks) Solve x + y + z = 6, 2x − y + z = 3, x + 2y − 2z = −1 by Gaussian elimination. => R₂ − 2R₁: (0, −3, −1 | −9). R₃ − R₁: (0, 1, −3 | −7). Add R₂ ÷ 3 to R₃: (0, 0, −10/3 | −10) (1 mark), so **z = 3**. Then y − 3z = −7 gives **y = 2**, and x = 6 − 2 − 3 = **1** (1 mark). Check eq. 3: 1 + 4 − 6 = −1 ✓.
P: (1 mark) How many solutions does x + y + z = 2, 2x + 2y + 2z = 5 have? => R₂ − 2R₁: (0, 0, 0 | 1), meaning 0 = 1. **None**.
P: (1 mark) Find the general solution of x + y + z = 3 and x − y = 1. => x = 1 + y. Then 1 + 2y + z = 3, so z = 2 − 2y. With y = t: **(1 + t, t, 2 − 2t)**, a line. Check t = 0: (1, 0, 2): 1 + 0 + 2 = 3 ✓, 1 − 0 = 1 ✓.
P: (1 mark) Use Cramer's rule on 2x + 3y = 8 and x − y = −1. => det = −2 − 3 = −5. x = (8 × (−1) − 3 × (−1)) ÷ (−5) = (−8 + 3) ÷ (−5) = **1**. y = (2 × (−1) − 8 × 1) ÷ (−5) = (−10) ÷ (−5) = **2**.
P: (1 mark) For which value of a does x + y = 1, x + a y = 1 not have a unique solution? Describe the solutions for that a. => det = a − 1 = 0, so **a = 1**. Both equations are x + y = 1: infinitely many solutions **(1 − t, t)**.
P: (1 mark) Do two Jacobi iterations from (0, 0) on 5x − y = 4 and −x + 4y = 3. The exact solution is (1, 1). => x = (4 + y) ÷ 5 and y = (3 + x) ÷ 4. Iteration 1: **(0.8, 0.75)**. Iteration 2: x = (4 + 0.75) ÷ 5 = 0.95, y = (3 + 0.8) ÷ 4 = 0.95: **(0.95, 0.95)**.
P: (1 mark) Is 2x + 5y = 1, 4x + y = 3 diagonally dominant? If not, how do you fix it? => Row 1: 2 < 5, so **no**. Swap the equations: 4x + y = 3, 2x + 5y = 1. Now 4 > 1 and 5 > 2: **dominant**.
P: (1 mark) Balance Al + HCl → AlCl₃ + H₂. => a Al + b HCl → c AlCl₃ + d H₂. Al: a = c. Cl: b = 3c. H: b = 2d. With c = 2: a = 2, b = 6, d = 3. **2 Al + 6 HCl → 2 AlCl₃ + 3 H₂**.
P: (1 mark) A circuit has mesh equations 6 i₁ − 2 i₂ = 10 and −2 i₁ + 4 i₂ = 0. Find i₁, i₂ and the current in the shared resistor. => i₁ = 2 i₂, so 12 i₂ − 2 i₂ = 10, **i₂ = 1 A**, **i₁ = 2 A**. The shared resistor carries i₁ − i₂ = **1 A**.
P: (1 mark) Find the rank of the matrix with rows (1, 2, 3), (2, 4, 6), (1, 1, 1) and the number of free variables in A x = 0. => R₂ − 2R₁ = (0, 0, 0). R₃ − R₁ = (0, −1, −2). **Rank 2**, so 3 − 2 = **1 free variable**.
P: (1 mark) Solve x + y = 2, x + 1.001 y = 2.001, then change the right side 2.001 to 2.002. What does it show? => First: 0.001 y = 0.001, so **(1, 1)**. Second: 0.001 y = 0.002, so y = 2, x = 0: **(0, 2)**. A change of 0.001 in the data moved the answer by 1: the system is **ill-conditioned** (det = 0.001).
P: (1 mark) Find the parabola y = a x² + b x + c through (0, 3), (1, 2), (2, 3). => c = 3. a + b = −1. 4a + 2b = 0, so 2a + b = 0. Then a = 1, b = −2: **y = x² − 2x + 3**. Check x = 2: 4 − 4 + 3 = 3 ✓.

@section physics | 3 · Part B: Gases and thermodynamics (6 marks, about 35 min) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 14 to 19
P: (1 mark) A gas of 4.0 L at 100 kPa and 300 K is changed to 150 kPa and 360 K. Find the new volume. => V₂ = 4.0 × (100 ÷ 150) × (360 ÷ 300) = **3.2 L**.
P: (1 mark) A cylinder of 12 L holds air at 15 MPa and 293 K. Find the number of moles, and the volume the gas would fill at 1 atm and the same temperature. => n = 15 × 10⁶ × 0.012 ÷ (8.314 × 293) = **73.9 mol**. V = P₁V₁ ÷ P₂ = 15 × 10⁶ × 12 ÷ 101,325 = **1776 L**.
P: (1 mark) A breathing mix has 30% oxygen. Find the maximum depth for an oxygen partial pressure of 1.4 atm. => P = 1.4 ÷ 0.30 = 4.67 atm absolute. Depth = (4.67 − 1) × 10 = **36.7 m**.
P: (1 mark) Find the rms speed of carbon dioxide (M = 0.044 kg/mol) at 350 K. => √(3 × 8.314 × 350 ÷ 0.044) = √198,402 = **445 m/s**.
P: (1 mark) 2 mol of an ideal gas at 400 K expands isothermally to twice its volume. Find the work done by the gas, the heat absorbed, and ΔU. => W = 2 × 8.314 × 400 × ln 2 = 6651 × 0.693 = **4610 J**. Q = **4610 J**. ΔU = **0**.
P: (1 mark) Air (γ = 1.4) at 300 K is compressed adiabatically to one eighth of its volume. Find the final temperature. => T₂ = 300 × 8^0.4 = 300 × 2.297 = **689 K** (416 °C).

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | A circle through three points
P: Find the circle x² + y² + D x + E y + F = 0 through (0, 0), (4, 0) and (0, 2). Give its centre and radius, and explain the geometry. => (0, 0): F = 0. (4, 0): 16 + 4D = 0, D = −4. (0, 2): 4 + 2E = 0, E = −2. The circle is x² + y² − 4x − 2y = 0, i.e. (x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 5: **centre (2, 1), radius √5**. The points (4, 0) and (0, 2) are the ends of a diameter (their midpoint is (2, 1) and their distance is √20 = 2√5), and the origin sees them at a right angle, as Thales' theorem says.

LVL: Challenge | Isothermal against adiabatic work
P: One mole of a monatomic ideal gas (γ = 5/3) at 300 K doubles its volume, once isothermally and once adiabatically. Compare the work done and explain. => Isothermal: W = R T ln 2 = 8.314 × 300 × 0.693 = **1729 J**. Adiabatic: T₂ = 300 × 2^(−2/3) = 189.0 K. W = C_V (T₁ − T₂) = 12.47 × 111.0 = **1384 J** (check: (P₁V₁ − P₂V₂) ÷ (γ − 1) = (2494 − 1571) ÷ (2/3) = 1384 J ✓). The isothermal gas does **more work** because it absorbs heat (1729 J) from its surroundings as it expands, while the adiabatic gas must pay for its work from its own internal energy and cools.
