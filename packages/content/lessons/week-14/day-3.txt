# Linear systems at work: circuits, chemistry and traffic. Then: mixtures of gases
lead: Today the algebra goes to work. Kirchhoff's laws turn an electric circuit into a linear system, balancing a chemical equation is a hidden homogeneous system, and counting cars at junctions gives a system with a free parameter. In physics, the ideal gas law meets mixtures: air is a blend of gases, and each gas contributes its own partial pressure, which decides how deep you can dive and why Everest is so hard to climb.

BN: আজ বীজগণিত কাজে নামবে। কির্শফের সূত্র বৈদ্যুতিক বর্তনীকে রৈখিক সমীকরণ জোটে বদলে দেয়, রাসায়নিক সমীকরণ সমতা করা আসলে একটা লুকানো সমসত্ব জোট, আর মোড়ে গাড়ি গোনা থেকে একটা মুক্ত পরামিতিসহ জোট পাওয়া যায়। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে আদর্শ গ্যাস সূত্র মিশ্রণের সাথে মিলবে: বাতাস গ্যাসের মিশ্রণ, প্রতিটি গ্যাস নিজের আংশিক চাপ দেয়, যা ঠিক করে কত গভীরে ডুব দেওয়া যায় আর এভারেস্ট কেন এত কঠিন।

### 1 · Electric circuits (Kirchhoff)
Kirchhoff's voltage law says the voltages around a loop add to zero. In **mesh analysis**, give each loop a current (i₁, i₂, …) and write one equation per loop. A resistor shared by two loops carries i₁ − i₂.

EX: A two-loop circuit
1. Loop 1 has a 12 V battery and R₁ = 2 Ω, loop 2 has R₂ = 3 Ω, and both share R₃ = 4 Ω.
2. Loop 1: 12 = 2 i₁ + 4 (i₁ − i₂), so **6 i₁ − 4 i₂ = 12**. Loop 2: 0 = 3 i₂ + 4 (i₂ − i₁), so **−4 i₁ + 7 i₂ = 0**.
3. From the second equation i₂ = 4 i₁ ÷ 7. Then 6 i₁ − 16 i₁ ÷ 7 = 12, so (26 ÷ 7) i₁ = 12 and **i₁ = 42/13 = 3.231 A**, **i₂ = 24/13 = 1.846 A**.
4. The shared resistor carries i₁ − i₂ = **1.385 A**.
5. **Energy check:** the battery supplies 12 × 3.231 = 38.77 W. The resistors dissipate 2 × 3.231² + 3 × 1.846² + 4 × 1.385² = 20.88 + 10.22 + 7.67 = **38.77 W** ✓.
/EX

### 2 · Balancing chemical equations
Atoms are conserved, so for each element the number on the left equals the number on the right. That gives a **homogeneous** linear system, with a free variable that fixes the overall scale.

EX: Balancing
1. **Propane burning:** a C₃H₈ + b O₂ → c CO₂ + d H₂O. Carbon: 3a = c. Hydrogen: 8a = 2d. Oxygen: 2b = 2c + d. Three equations, four unknowns, so one free variable. With a = t: c = 3t, d = 4t, 2b = 6t + 4t, b = 5t. The smallest whole numbers (t = 1): **C₃H₈ + 5 O₂ → 3 CO₂ + 4 H₂O**.
2. **Aluminium oxide:** a Al + b O₂ → c Al₂O₃. Aluminium: a = 2c. Oxygen: 2b = 3c. Take c = 2: a = 4, b = 3. **4 Al + 3 O₂ → 2 Al₂O₃**.
3. **Photosynthesis:** a CO₂ + b H₂O → c C₆H₁₂O₆ + d O₂. Carbon: a = 6c. Hydrogen: 2b = 12c. Oxygen: 2a + b = 6c + 2d. With c = 1: a = 6, b = 6, 18 = 6 + 2d, d = 6. **6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂**.
/EX

### 3 · Traffic flow
At each junction, the cars arriving equal the cars leaving. Cars cannot vanish, so the system is linear.

EX: A ring road
1. Four junctions A, B, C, D on a one-way ring, with flows x₁ (A to B), x₂ (B to C), x₃ (C to D), x₄ (D to A) in cars per hour. External cars entering and leaving (per hour): A: 300 in, 100 out. B: 150 in, 250 out. C: 400 in, 200 out. D: 50 in, 350 out. (Totals in and out are both 900.)
2. A: x₄ + 300 = x₁ + 100, so x₁ − x₄ = 200. B: x₁ + 150 = x₂ + 250, so x₂ = x₁ − 100. C: x₂ + 400 = x₃ + 200, so x₃ = x₂ + 200 = x₁ + 100. D: x₃ + 50 = x₄ + 350, so x₄ = x₃ − 300 = x₁ − 200 (consistent with A).
3. Rank 3, one free variable t = x₁. Solutions: **(t, t − 100, t + 100, t − 200)**.
4. Flows cannot be negative, so t ≥ 200. If a counter shows x₄ = 100, then t = 300 and the flows are **(300, 200, 400, 100)**.
/EX

### 4 · Fitting a curve through points
A quadratic y = a x² + b x + c through three points gives three linear equations in a, b, c.

EX: Interpolation
1. Points (0, 1), (1, 0), (2, 3). From x = 0: c = 1. From x = 1: a + b + 1 = 0, so a + b = −1. From x = 2: 4a + 2b + 1 = 3, so 2a + b = 1.
2. Subtract: a = 2, then b = −3. The parabola is **y = 2x² − 3x + 1**. Check at x = 2: 8 − 6 + 1 = 3 ✓.
3. In general, **n + 1 points determine a polynomial of degree n**, as the matrix with rows (xᵢⁿ, …, xᵢ, 1) is invertible when the x-values differ.
/EX

TRAP: In mesh analysis, **keep the current directions consistent**: a negative answer just means the true current runs the opposite way. In chemistry, check the result by counting atoms of **every** element on both sides. In traffic problems, a mathematical solution with a **negative** flow is not physically possible.

### Dalton's law of partial pressures
In a mixture of ideal gases, each gas fills the whole volume and behaves as if the others were absent. So

EQ: P_total = P₁ + P₂ + …     P_i = x_i P_total     (x_i = mole fraction = n_i ÷ n_total)

| Air component | Fraction by moles |
| Nitrogen | 78.08% |
| Oxygen | 20.95% |
| Argon | 0.93% |
| Carbon dioxide | 0.04% |

BN: ডাল্টনের সূত্র: মিশ্রণের মোট চাপ = প্রতিটি গ্যাসের আংশিক চাপের যোগফল, P_i = x_i P। বাতাসে অক্সিজেন 20.95%। আমাদের শরীরের জন্য অক্সিজেনের আংশিক চাপই গুরুত্বপূর্ণ, শতকরা হার নয়।

EX: Oxygen at altitude and depth
1. **Sea level:** P_O₂ = 0.2095 × 101.3 = **21.2 kPa**.
2. **3000 m** (P = 71 kPa): P_O₂ = 0.2095 × 71 = **14.9 kPa**, 70% of the sea-level value.
3. **Everest summit** (P = 34 kPa): P_O₂ = 0.2095 × 34 = **7.1 kPa**. It is like breathing 7% oxygen at sea level, which is why climbers use bottled oxygen.
4. **Scuba diving:** at 40 m the absolute pressure is about 5 atm, so P_O₂ = 0.2095 × 5 = **1.05 atm** and P_N₂ = 0.78 × 5 = **3.9 atm**. Oxygen becomes toxic above about 1.4 atm. For air, the limit is P = 1.4 ÷ 0.2095 = 6.68 atm, a depth of (6.68 − 1) × 10 = **57 m**. With "nitrox 32" (32% O₂), P = 1.4 ÷ 0.32 = 4.375 atm: a maximum depth of **34 m**.
/EX

### Humidity
Water vapour is a gas in the air too. The maximum partial pressure of water vapour at a given temperature is the **saturation vapour pressure**. The **relative humidity** is RH = P_vapour ÷ P_sat.

| Temperature (°C) | P_sat (kPa) |
| 10 | 1.23 |
| 15 | 1.71 |
| 20 | 2.34 |
| 25 | 3.17 |
| 30 | 4.25 |
| 40 | 7.38 |

EX: Dew point and humid air
1. **Dew point:** at 30 °C with 60% humidity, P_v = 0.6 × 4.25 = 2.55 kPa. The air is saturated when P_sat = 2.55 kPa, i.e. at about **21.4 °C**. Cooling below that, dew forms.
2. **Humid air is lighter:** at 30 °C and 100% humidity, P_v = 4.25 kPa. The mixture has a density of (97.07 kPa × 0.029 + 4.25 kPa × 0.018) × 1000 ÷ (8.314 × 303.15) = 2891.7 ÷ 2520.4 = **1.147 kg/m³**, compared with 1.166 for dry air: about 1.6% lighter, since water (M = 18) replaces nitrogen and oxygen (M = 29).
3. **Gas collected over water:** 100 mL of hydrogen at 25 °C with a total pressure of 101.3 kPa includes water vapour of 3.17 kPa, so P_H₂ = **98.1 kPa**, and n = 98,100 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ (8.314 × 298.15) = **3.96 × 10⁻³ mol**.
/EX

### Mixing and diffusing
EX: Opening a valve
1. A 2 L flask with nitrogen at 3 atm is joined to a 3 L flask with helium at 1 atm (same temperature). Final volume 5 L: P_N₂ = 3 × 2 ÷ 5 = **1.2 atm**, P_He = 1 × 3 ÷ 5 = **0.6 atm**, total **1.8 atm**.
2. **Graham's law:** the rate of effusion (leaking through a tiny hole) is proportional to 1 ÷ √M. Helium escapes **√(28 ÷ 4) = 2.65 times** faster than nitrogen, which is why a helium balloon deflates faster than an air balloon.
/EX

TRAP: Partial pressure is **mole fraction times the total pressure**, and the total pressure in a gas collected over water includes the vapour. At depth use **absolute** pressure (1 + depth ÷ 10 atm). And relative humidity is a **ratio of pressures at the same temperature**, not a fraction of water in the air by mass.
