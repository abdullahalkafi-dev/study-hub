# Systems with no solution or infinitely many. Then: the ideal gas law
lead: Not every system has exactly one answer. Some are contradictory, some leave a free choice. The rank of the matrix tells you which case you are in before you solve anything. In physics, you combine yesterday's three gas laws into one equation, PV = nRT, and learn to count atoms in moles.

BN: সব সমীকরণ জোটের ঠিক একটা উত্তর থাকে না। কোনোটা পরস্পরবিরোধী, কোনোটায় একটা মুক্ত পছন্দ থাকে। সমাধানের আগেই ম্যাট্রিক্সের rank বলে দেয় কোন ক্ষেত্র। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে গতকালের তিনটা গ্যাস সূত্র মিলিয়ে একটা সমীকরণ PV = nRT বানাবে এবং মোলে পরমাণু গোনা শিখবে।

@section math | 2 · Math: the three cases | 55 min

### Rank decides everything
Reduce [A | b] to echelon form. Compare the rank of A with the rank of [A | b], and with the number of unknowns n:

| Case | What happens in the echelon form | Solutions |
| rank A < rank [A | b] | A row (0, 0, 0 | c) with c ≠ 0, meaning 0 = c | **None** (inconsistent) |
| rank A = rank [A | b] = n | A pivot in every column | **Exactly one** |
| rank A = rank [A | b] < n | Some columns have no pivot | **Infinitely many**, with n − rank free variables |

BN: [A | b] কে সোপান আকারে আনো। rank A < rank [A | b] হলে 0 = c (c ≠ 0) সারি আসে, তাই কোনো সমাধান নেই। rank A = rank [A | b] = n হলে ঠিক একটা সমাধান। rank A = rank [A | b] < n হলে অসীম সমাধান, মুক্ত চলকের সংখ্যা = n − rank।

EX: No solution
1. x + y = 2 and x + y = 5. R₂ − R₁ gives (0, 0 | 3), meaning 0 = 3. **Inconsistent**. The two lines are parallel.
2. x + 2y + z = 4, 2x + 5y + 3z = 9, x + 3y + 2z = 6. R₂ − 2R₁: (0, 1, 1 | 1). R₃ − R₁: (0, 1, 1 | 2). R₃ − R₂: (0, 0, 0 | 1). **No solution**, although no two equations are contradictory by themselves: the three planes form a "triangular prism".
/EX

EX: Infinitely many solutions
1. x + y = 3 and 2x + 2y = 6. The second is twice the first: rank 1 with 2 unknowns, so there is **one free variable**. Put y = t: x = 3 − t. The solutions are **(3 − t, t)**: every point of a line.
2. x + 2y + z = 4, 2x + 5y + 3z = 9, x + 3y + 2z = 5. R₂ − 2R₁: (0, 1, 1 | 1). R₃ − R₁: (0, 1, 1 | 1). R₃ − R₂ = (0, 0, 0 | 0). Rank 2 with 3 unknowns, so **one free variable**: let z = t. Then y = 1 − t and x = 4 − 2y − z = 4 − 2 + 2t − t = 2 + t.
3. The solution set is **(2 + t, 1 − t, t)**: a **line** in space. Check t = 0: (2, 1, 0) gives 2 + 2 + 0 = 4 ✓, 4 + 5 + 0 = 9 ✓, 2 + 3 + 0 = 5 ✓. Check t = 1: (3, 0, 1) gives 3 + 0 + 1 = 4 ✓, 6 + 0 + 3 = 9 ✓, 3 + 0 + 2 = 5 ✓.
/EX

### Homogeneous systems
A system A x = 0 (all right-hand sides zero) always has the **trivial** solution x = 0. It has non-trivial solutions exactly when rank A < n, i.e. **det A = 0** (for a square matrix).

EX: Homogeneous
1. x + 2y = 0 and 2x + 4y = 0: rank 1. Put y = t: x = −2t. Solutions **(−2t, t)**.
2. x + 2y + z = 0, 2x + 5y + 3z = 0, x + y = 0. R₂ − 2R₁: (0, 1, 1 | 0). R₃ − R₁: (0, −1, −1 | 0). R₃ + R₂ = 0. Rank 2, free z = t, y = −t, x = −2y − z = 2t − t = t. Solutions **(t, −t, t)**. Check the third: t − t = 0 ✓, and the second: 2t − 5t + 3t = 0 ✓.
3. For which a does x + a y = 0, a x + 4y = 0 have non-trivial solutions? det = 4 − a² = 0, so **a = 2 or −2**.
/EX

### Planes in space
Each equation in three unknowns is a plane. Three planes can meet in a **point** (rank 3), in a **line** (rank 2, consistent), in a whole **plane** (rank 1, consistent), or **not at all** (inconsistent: parallel planes or a triangular prism).

EX: Triangular prism
1. x + y = 1, y + z = 1, x + 2y + z = 3. No two planes are parallel. But adding the first two gives x + 2y + z = 2, contradicting the third (= 3). **No solution**.
2. In matrix terms the normals (1, 1, 0), (0, 1, 1), (1, 2, 1) are dependent, since (1, 2, 1) = (1, 1, 0) + (0, 1, 1), while the right-hand sides do not follow the same rule (1 + 1 ≠ 3).
/EX

TRAP: "Infinitely many solutions" does **not** mean every number works: it means the solutions form a line or a plane. And a row (0, 0, 0 | 0) is harmless, while a row (0, 0, 0 | c) with c ≠ 0 means **no solution**. Also: a system with **fewer equations than unknowns** can never have exactly one solution.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: the ideal gas law | 35 min

### One law for all
Combine Boyle, Charles and Gay-Lussac, with the fact that the quantity of gas matters. Using the **mole** (1 mol = 6.022 × 10²³ particles, Avogadro's number N_A):

EQ: P V = n R T     R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)     (or  P V = N k T  with  k = 1.381 × 10⁻²³ J/K)

R = N_A k. This law describes any **ideal gas**: a good model when the gas is dilute (low pressure) and not too cold.

BN: আদর্শ গ্যাস সূত্র: PV = nRT, R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)। এক মোলে 6.022 × 10²³ কণা। বিকল্প রূপ PV = NkT, k = 1.381 × 10⁻²³ J/K। P পাস্কালে, V ঘনমিটারে, T কেলভিনে। এটা ঘন নয় এমন, খুব ঠান্ডা নয় এমন গ্যাসে ভালো কাজ করে।

EX: Using PV = nRT
1. **Molar volume at 0 °C and 1 atm:** V = R T ÷ P = 8.314 × 273.15 ÷ 101,325 = 0.02241 m³ = **22.41 L** per mole. At 20 °C it is **24.06 L**.
2. **Pressure:** 2 mol in 10 L at 300 K: P = 2 × 8.314 × 300 ÷ 0.010 = 498,840 Pa ≈ **499 kPa** (4.9 atm).
3. **A scuba tank** of 12 L at 200 bar (2.0 × 10⁷ Pa) and 20 °C: n = 2 × 10⁷ × 0.012 ÷ (8.314 × 293.15) = **98.5 mol**. At 1 atm this gas would occupy 98.5 × 24.06 = **2370 L**.
4. **Molecules in 1 cm³ of air:** N = P V ÷ (k T) = 101,325 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ (1.381 × 10⁻²³ × 293.15) = **2.5 × 10¹⁹**. The typical spacing is (10⁻⁶ ÷ 2.5 × 10¹⁹)^(1/3) = **3.4 nm**, about ten times the size of a molecule: air is mostly empty space.
/EX

### Density and molar mass
Since n = m ÷ M (mass over molar mass), P V = (m ÷ M) R T gives the density:

EQ: ρ = m ÷ V = P M ÷ (R T)

| Gas | Molar mass M (g/mol) |
| Hydrogen H₂ | 2.0 |
| Helium He | 4.0 |
| Nitrogen N₂ | 28 |
| Air (mixture) | 29 |
| Oxygen O₂ | 32 |
| Carbon dioxide CO₂ | 44 |

EX: Densities
1. **Air** at 20 °C and 1 atm: ρ = 101,325 × 0.029 ÷ (8.314 × 293.15) = **1.206 kg/m³**.
2. **Helium** at the same conditions: ρ = 101,325 × 0.004 ÷ 2437.2 = **0.166 kg/m³**. A 1 m³ helium balloon lifts (1.206 − 0.166) = 1.04 kg.
3. **CO₂:** ρ = 1.829 kg/m³, 1.5 times air, so CO₂ settles at the bottom of cellars and wells.
4. **A room** of 5 × 4 × 2.5 = 50 m³ at 20 °C contains 50 × 1.206 = **60.3 kg of air**. If the room warms to 30 °C with the window open, the density falls to 1.166 kg/m³, so the mass becomes 58.3 kg: **2.0 kg leaves**.
5. **At the top of Everest:** P = 34 kPa, T = 250 K: ρ = 34,000 × 0.029 ÷ (8.314 × 250) = **0.474 kg/m³**, only 39% of the sea-level density.
/EX

TRAP: Use SI units in PV = nRT: pressure in **pascals**, volume in **cubic metres** (1 L = 10⁻³ m³), temperature in **kelvin**, and then R = 8.314. If P is in kPa and V in litres, the product P V is in joules already. And the law is for **absolute** pressure.

NOTES:

### Recap
- Compare rank A and rank [A | b]: rank A < rank [A | b] means no solution; rank = n means one solution; rank < n means infinitely many, with n − rank free variables. Homogeneous systems have non-trivial solutions when det = 0. Three planes can meet in a point, a line, a plane, or not at all.
- Ideal gas law P V = n R T = N k T. R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), N_A = 6.022 × 10²³. Molar volume 22.4 L (0 °C) or 24.06 L (20 °C). Density ρ = P M ÷ (R T).

BN: আজকের সারকথা: rank A < rank [A | b] হলে সমাধান নেই; rank = n হলে একটা; rank < n হলে অসীম (n − rank টা মুক্ত চলক)। সমসত্ব জোটে det = 0 হলে অশূন্য সমাধান। আদর্শ গ্যাস: PV = nRT = NkT, মোলার আয়তন 22.4 L (0 °C), ঘনত্ব ρ = PM/(RT)।
