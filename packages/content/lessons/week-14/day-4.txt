# Cramer's rule and ill-conditioned systems. Then: the kinetic theory of gases
lead: Cramer's rule solves a system with determinants alone and shows exactly where trouble comes from: a small determinant. Some systems are so sensitive that a tiny change in the data throws the answer far away, and a good engineer must recognise them. In physics, you explain pressure and temperature from the motion of molecules: the kinetic theory, which gives the speed of air molecules (about 500 m/s) and why the Earth keeps its nitrogen but lost its hydrogen.

BN: ক্র্যামারের নিয়ম শুধু নির্ণায়ক দিয়ে সমীকরণ জোট সমাধান করে এবং ঠিক দেখায় সমস্যা কোথা থেকে আসে: ছোট নির্ণায়ক। কিছু জোট এত স্পর্শকাতর যে তথ্যে সামান্য বদল হলে উত্তর অনেক দূরে চলে যায়, আর ভালো প্রকৌশলীকে সেটা চিনতে হবে। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে অণুর গতি থেকে চাপ ও তাপমাত্রা ব্যাখ্যা করবে: গতীয় তত্ত্ব, যা বাতাসের অণুর গতি (প্রায় 500 m/s) দেয় এবং বলে কেন পৃথিবী নাইট্রোজেন ধরে রেখেছে কিন্তু হাইড্রোজেন হারিয়েছে।

@section math | 2 · Math: Cramer's rule and conditioning | 55 min

### Cramer's rule
For a square system A x = b with det A ≠ 0, each unknown is a ratio of determinants:

EQ: x_j = det (A_j) ÷ det (A)     where A_j is A with column j replaced by b

For two unknowns, with A = [[a, b], [c, d]] and right side (e, f): x = (e d − b f) ÷ (a d − b c) and y = (a f − e c) ÷ (a d − b c).

BN: ক্র্যামারের নিয়ম: x_j = det(A_j) / det(A), যেখানে A_j হলো A এর j-তম স্তম্ভের জায়গায় b বসানো ম্যাট্রিক্স। det A = 0 হলে এটা চলে না। ছোট জোটে সুন্দর, কিন্তু বড় জোটে খুব ব্যয়বহুল, তাই কম্পিউটারে গাউসীয় অপসারণই ব্যবহার হয়।

EX: Cramer's rule at work
1. **2 × 2:** 2x + y = 7 and x + 3y = 11. det A = 6 − 1 = 5. x = (7 × 3 − 1 × 11) ÷ 5 = 10 ÷ 5 = **2**. y = (2 × 11 − 7 × 1) ÷ 5 = 15 ÷ 5 = **3**.
2. **3 × 3:** x + y + z = 6, 2x − y + z = 6, x + 2y − z = 1. det A = 1 (1 − 2) − 1 (−2 − 1) + 1 (4 + 1) = −1 + 3 + 5 = **7**.
3. Replace column 1 by b = (6, 6, 1): rows (6, 1, 1), (6, −1, 1), (1, 2, −1). det = 6 (1 − 2) − 1 (−6 − 1) + 1 (12 + 1) = −6 + 7 + 13 = 14. So **x = 14 ÷ 7 = 2**.
4. Column 2: rows (1, 6, 1), (2, 6, 1), (1, 1, −1). det = 1 (−6 − 1) − 6 (−2 − 1) + 1 (2 − 6) = −7 + 18 − 4 = 7, so **y = 1**. Column 3: rows (1, 1, 6), (2, −1, 6), (1, 2, 1): det = 1 (−1 − 12) − 1 (2 − 6) + 6 (4 + 1) = −13 + 4 + 30 = 21, so **z = 3**.
5. The solution is **(2, 1, 3)**, matching Monday's elimination.
/EX

### When a tiny error ruins everything: conditioning
If det A is **small compared with the entries**, the lines (planes) are nearly parallel, and the crossing point moves a lot when the data change a little. Such a system is **ill-conditioned**.

EX: Nearly parallel lines
1. x + y = 2 and x + 1.0001 y = 2.0001. Subtract: 0.0001 y = 0.0001, so y = 1 and **x = 1**. Here det = 1.0001 − 1 = 0.0001.
2. Change the second right-hand side by 0.0001 (a relative change of 0.005%) to 2.0002: 0.0001 y = 0.0002, so **y = 2** and **x = 0**. The answer moved from (1, 1) to (0, 2): the error was amplified **10,000 times**.
3. The wrong answer (0, 2) nearly satisfies the equations: the second gives 0 + 2.0002 = 2.0002, a **residual** of only 0.0001. A small residual does **not** guarantee a small error in x.
4. **Condition number** κ = ‖A‖ ‖A⁻¹‖ (here using the largest row sum). A = [[1, 1], [1, 1.0001]] has ‖A‖ = 2.0001. A⁻¹ = 10⁴ [[1.0001, −1], [−1, 1]] has ‖A⁻¹‖ = 20,001. So **κ = 2.0001 × 20,001 ≈ 40,004**. The relative error in the solution can be up to κ times the relative error in the data, so about **log₁₀ 40,004 = 4.6 digits are lost**.
/EX

BN: ছোট det মানে রেখাগুলো প্রায় সমান্তরাল, তাই তথ্যে সামান্য ভুলে ছেদবিন্দু অনেক সরে যায়: এটাই ill-conditioned। কন্ডিশন সংখ্যা κ = ‖A‖‖A⁻¹‖: তথ্যের আপেক্ষিক ভুল κ গুণ বাড়তে পারে, log₁₀ κ সংখ্যক অঙ্ক হারায়। ছোট residual মানে ছোট ভুল নয়।

TRAP: Cramer's rule needs **det A ≠ 0**. Do not use it for large systems: computing n + 1 determinants of size n by expansion costs about n! operations, while elimination costs n³ ÷ 3. And do not trust an answer just because the residual is small: always ask about the **condition number**.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: kinetic theory | 35 min

### Pressure from molecular collisions
Picture N molecules of mass m in a box of volume V. A molecule bouncing off a wall changes its momentum by 2 m v_x. Averaging over all molecules and directions (⟨v_x²⟩ = ⟨v²⟩ ÷ 3):

EQ: P = (1 ÷ 3)(N ÷ V) m ⟨v²⟩

Compare with P V = N k T. The average translational kinetic energy per molecule is therefore

EQ: ½ m ⟨v²⟩ = (3 ÷ 2) k T     v_rms = √(3 k T ÷ m) = √(3 R T ÷ M)

**Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of molecules.** (At 300 K every ideal gas molecule has the same average kinetic energy, 6.2 × 10⁻²¹ J = 0.039 eV.)

BN: গতীয় তত্ত্ব: দেয়ালে অণুর ধাক্কা থেকে চাপ আসে: P = (1/3)(N/V) m⟨v²⟩। PV = NkT এর সাথে মিলিয়ে গড় গতিশক্তি ½m⟨v²⟩ = (3/2)kT। তাপমাত্রা অণুর গড় গতিশক্তির পরিমাপ। v_rms = √(3RT/M): হালকা অণু দ্রুত, ভারী অণু ধীর।

EX: Molecular speeds at 300 K
1. **Nitrogen** (M = 0.028 kg/mol): v_rms = √(3 × 8.314 × 300 ÷ 0.028) = **517 m/s**.
2. **Oxygen** (0.032): **484 m/s**. **Helium** (0.004): **1368 m/s**. **Hydrogen** (0.002): **1934 m/s**.
3. Air molecules move faster than a rifle bullet, but they collide so often that they do not get far: the **mean free path** λ = k T ÷ (√2 π d² P) with d = 3.7 × 10⁻¹⁰ m gives λ = **66 nm**, so each molecule has about 500 ÷ 6.6 × 10⁻⁸ = **7.6 × 10⁹ collisions per second**.
4. **Checking the formula with air:** N ÷ V = 2.5 × 10²⁵ m⁻³, m = 29 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ = 4.81 × 10⁻²⁶ kg, v_rms² = 2.52 × 10⁵ m²/s². P = (1 ÷ 3) × 2.5 × 10²⁵ × 4.81 × 10⁻²⁶ × 2.52 × 10⁵ = **1.01 × 10⁵ Pa** ✓.
/EX

### Equipartition and internal energy
Each independent way a molecule can store energy (each **degree of freedom**) holds an average of ½ k T. A monatomic gas (He, Ar) has 3 (translation only). A diatomic gas (N₂, O₂) at room temperature has 5 (3 translations and 2 rotations).

EQ: U = (f ÷ 2) n R T     C_V = (f ÷ 2) R     (3/2 R = 12.47 J/(mol·K), 5/2 R = 20.79 J/(mol·K))

EX: Energy in gases
1. **One mole of helium** at 300 K: U = 1.5 × 8.314 × 300 = **3741 J**. One mole of nitrogen: U = 2.5 × 8.314 × 300 = **6236 J**.
2. **Heating at constant volume:** 2 mol of helium from 300 K to 400 K needs Q = 2 × 12.47 × 100 = **2494 J**. For nitrogen: 2 × 20.79 × 100 = **4157 J**: more places to put the energy, so more energy is needed per kelvin.
3. 3 mol of argon heated by 50 K at constant volume: 3 × 12.47 × 50 = **1871 J**.
/EX

### The speed distribution and the escape of gases
Molecules do not all have the same speed. The **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** has a most probable speed v_p = √(2 R T ÷ M), a mean speed √(8 R T ÷ (π M)) and an rms speed √(3 R T ÷ M), in the ratio **1 : 1.128 : 1.225**. For nitrogen at 300 K these are 422, 476 and 517 m/s.

EX: Why Earth has no hydrogen
1. The escape speed from the Earth is **11.2 km/s**. A gas is kept for billions of years only if v_rms is below about **one sixth** of the escape speed (so the fast tail of the distribution is negligible).
2. In the upper atmosphere (T about 1000 K): hydrogen H₂ has v_rms = √(3 × 8.314 × 1000 ÷ 0.002) = **3531 m/s**, which is 32% of 11.2 km/s: it escapes. Helium: **2497 m/s** (22%): it escapes slowly. Oxygen: **883 m/s** (8%): it stays.
3. The **Moon** has an escape speed of only 2.4 km/s. At 400 K, nitrogen has v_rms = **597 m/s**, which is 25%: the Moon cannot hold an atmosphere.

LVL: Challenge | Derive P = (1/3)(N/V) m⟨v²⟩
P: Derive the pressure from a molecule's bounces in a cubic box of side L, and check that it agrees with P V = N k T. => A molecule with velocity component v_x hits a wall perpendicular to x and bounces back, with momentum change 2 m v_x. Comparing with P V = N k T gives **½ m ⟨v²⟩ = (3/2) k T** ✓.
