# Solving linear systems by Gaussian elimination. Then: pressure, volume and temperature of gases
lead: A linear system is a set of equations in which every unknown appears only to the first power. They show up everywhere: circuits, traffic, chemistry, economics, machine learning. Gaussian elimination is the method that solves them all, from two unknowns to two million. In physics you begin the study of gases with three simple laws that connect pressure, volume and temperature.

BN: রৈখিক সমীকরণ জোট হলো এমন সমীকরণের সেট যেখানে প্রতিটি অজানা শুধু প্রথম ঘাতে আছে। সার্কিট, ট্রাফিক, রসায়ন, অর্থনীতি, মেশিন লার্নিং, সবখানে এটা আসে। গাউসীয় অপসারণ (Gaussian elimination) এমন পদ্ধতি যা দুই অজানা থেকে বিশ লাখ অজানা পর্যন্ত সব সমাধান করে। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে গ্যাসের অধ্যয়ন শুরু হবে চাপ, আয়তন ও তাপমাত্রা জুড়ে দেওয়া তিনটা সহজ সূত্র দিয়ে।

@section math | 2 · Math: Gaussian elimination | 55 min

### From equations to an augmented matrix
A system such as

x + y + z = 6, 2x − y + z = 3, x + 2y − z = 2

can be written A x = b. We store it as the **augmented matrix** [A | b]: each row is one equation, each column one unknown, and the last column is the right-hand side.

BN: সমীকরণ জোট Ax = b আকারে লেখা যায়। আমরা এটাকে augmented matrix [A | b] এ রাখি: প্রতিটি সারি একটি সমীকরণ, প্রতিটি স্তম্ভ একটি অজানা, শেষ স্তম্ভ ডান পাশ। তিনটি সারি-প্রক্রিয়া (সারি অদলবদল, সারিকে অশূন্য সংখ্যা দিয়ে গুণ, এক সারির গুণিতক অন্য সারিতে যোগ) সমাধান বদলায় না।

### The method
1. **Forward elimination:** use row operations to create zeros below each pivot (the leading entry), moving left to right.
2. **Back substitution:** solve from the last equation upward.

EX: A 3 × 3 system
1. Rows: (1, 1, 1 | 6), (2, −1, 1 | 3), (1, 2, −1 | 2).
2. R₂ − 2R₁: (0, −3, −1 | −9). R₃ − R₁: (0, 1, −2 | −4).
3. Swap R₂ and R₃ (to get a convenient pivot): (0, 1, −2 | −4), (0, −3, −1 | −9).
4. R₃ + 3R₂: (0, 0, −7 | −21). So **z = 3**.
5. Back substitution: y − 2z = −4 gives y = −4 + 6 = **2**. Then x + y + z = 6 gives x = 6 − 2 − 3 = **1**.
6. Check in all equations: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 ✓. 2 − 2 + 3 = 3 ✓. 1 + 4 − 3 = 2 ✓. The solution is **(1, 2, 3)**.
/EX

EX: Three more
1. **2 × 2:** 2x + 3y = 12 and x − y = 1. From the second x = 1 + y. Then 2 + 2y + 3y = 12, so y = 2 and x = **3**. Check: 6 + 6 = 12 ✓.
2. **Another 3 × 3:** x + y + z = 6, 2x − y + z = 6, x + 2y − z = 1. R₂ − 2R₁: (0, −3, −1 | −6). R₃ − R₁: (0, 1, −2 | −5). Swap, then R₃ + 3R₂: (0, 0, −7 | −21): z = 3, y = −5 + 2 × 3 = 1, x = 6 − 1 − 3 = **2**. The solution is **(2, 1, 3)**.
3. **A mixture:** x kg of beans at €8/kg and y kg at €12/kg make 20 kg of blend at €10/kg. Then x + y = 20 and 8x + 12y = 200. Substituting x = 20 − y: 160 + 4y = 200, so y = 10 and x = **10**. Equal parts.
/EX

PREDICT: A system has two unknowns and two equations. Geometrically each equation is a line. In how many different ways can two lines meet in the plane? => Three: they cross at **one point** (one solution), they are **parallel** (no solution), or they are the **same line** (infinitely many). Tomorrow you will see all three in the algebra.

If the slopes are equal, the lines are parallel and the crossing is "n/a". | m1:Slope of line 1:-3:3:0.5:1 ;; c1:Intercept of line 1:-4:4:0.5:-1 ;; m2:Slope of line 2:-3:3:0.5:-1 ;; c2:Intercept of line 2:-4:4:0.5:3 | (c2-c1)/(m1-m2):x of the crossing:3 ;; m1*(c2-c1)/(m1-m2)+c1:y of the crossing:3 | m1*x+c1 ;; m2*x+c2 | -5,5 | -10,10

### Why pivoting matters
A computer works with rounded numbers. A tiny pivot gives a huge multiplier, which magnifies rounding errors. **Partial pivoting** means swapping rows so that the pivot has the largest absolute value in its column.

EX: A bad pivot
1. System: 0.0001 x + y = 1 and x + y = 2. The true solution is x = 1.0001, y = 0.9999.
2. **Without pivoting** with 3 significant digits: the multiplier is 1 ÷ 0.0001 = 10⁴. R₂ − 10⁴ R₁: the y-coefficient is 1 − 10⁴ = −9999, rounded to −1.00 × 10⁴; the right side is 2 − 10⁴ = −9998, rounded to −1.00 × 10⁴. So y = 1.00. Then 0.0001 x = 1 − 1.00 = 0 gives **x = 0**, which is badly wrong (the true x is 1.0001).
3. **With pivoting:** swap the rows so the pivot is 1: x + y = 2 and 0.0001 x + y = 1. The multiplier is 0.0001: (1 − 0.0001) y = 1 − 0.0002, i.e. 0.9999 y = 0.9998, which rounds to y = 1.00. Then x = 2 − 1.00 = **1.00** ✓.
/EX

BN: ফরোয়ার্ড অপসারণ (পিভটের নিচে শূন্য বানাও), তারপর পিছন থেকে প্রতিস্থাপন। কম্পিউটারে ছোট পিভট বড় গুণক দেয় এবং গোলাকার ভুল বাড়িয়ে দেয়, তাই partial pivoting: প্রতি স্তম্ভে সবচেয়ে বড় পরম মানের সংখ্যাকে পিভট বানাও।

TRAP: Do **every** row operation on the whole row, including the right-hand side. Do not forget the minus signs when subtracting. Always **check the answer** in the original equations. And never divide by a zero pivot: swap rows instead.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: the three gas laws | 35 min

### Pressure and what a gas is
A **gas** has no fixed shape or volume: its molecules fly freely and collide with the walls, each collision giving a push. Pressure is the average force per unit area: P = F ÷ A. Units: 1 atm = 101,325 Pa = 760 mmHg = 1.013 bar = 14.7 psi.

In gas laws, always use **absolute pressure** (not gauge pressure) and **absolute temperature** (kelvin).

BN: গ্যাসের অণুগুলো মুক্তভাবে ছুটে বেড়ায় এবং দেয়ালে ধাক্কা দেয়। চাপ = গড় বল / ক্ষেত্রফল। গ্যাসের সূত্রে সবসময় পরম চাপ (গেজ নয়) এবং পরম তাপমাত্রা (কেলভিন) ব্যবহার করো। 1 atm = 101,325 Pa = 760 mmHg।

### Boyle's law: constant temperature
For a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature, **pressure and volume are inversely proportional**:

EQ: P₁ V₁ = P₂ V₂

EX: Boyle
1. A syringe holds 60 mL of air at 1 atm. Push the plunger (slowly, to keep the temperature constant) to 20 mL: P₂ = 1 × 60 ÷ 20 = **3 atm** absolute (2 atm above atmospheric).
2. **Diver's lungs:** 6 L at the surface (1 atm). At 30 m the absolute pressure is 4 atm, so the air would occupy 1.5 L. If a diver holds their breath while rising from 30 m, the air would try to expand to 6 L, which is why divers must **never hold their breath on ascent**.
3. **Bubble rising from 20 m:** the absolute pressure there is (101,325 + 1000 × 9.8 × 20) ÷ 101,325 = 2.93 atm, so the volume grows by 2.93 times, and the radius by 2.93^(1/3) = 1.43: a bubble of 2 mm becomes **2.86 mm** at the surface.
/EX

The numbers give the new pressure and the product P V. | V:New volume V (L):0.5:6:0.25:1 | 200/V:Pressure (kPa):1 ;; 200/V*V:Product P × V (kPa·L):1 | 200/x | 0.5,6 | 0,420

### Charles's law: constant pressure
For a fixed amount of gas at constant pressure, **volume is proportional to absolute temperature**:

EQ: V₁ ÷ T₁ = V₂ ÷ T₂     (T in kelvin)

Lines of V against T in °C, for any gas, all point to V = 0 at **−273.15 °C**. That is how absolute zero was discovered.

EX: Charles
1. A balloon of 2.0 L at 20 °C (293.15 K) goes into a freezer at −18 °C (255.15 K): V₂ = 2.0 × 255.15 ÷ 293.15 = **1.74 L**.
2. A hot-air balloon: air heated from 15 °C to 100 °C expands by the factor 373.15 ÷ 288.15 = **1.295**, so about 23% of the air (1 − 1 ÷ 1.295) leaves through the opening, which makes the balloon lighter than the cool air around it.
/EX

LAB: Charles's law and absolute zero | A gas of 1 L at 20 °C is heated or cooled at constant pressure. The graph shows the volume against temperature in °C: a straight line that reaches zero at −273 °C. Choose the temperature to see the volume. | T:Temperature (°C):-250:150:10:20 | (T+273.15)/293.15:Volume (L):3 ;; T+273.15:Absolute temperature (K):1 | (x+273.15)/293.15 | -300,150 | 0,1.6

### Gay-Lussac's law and the combined law
At constant volume, **pressure is proportional to absolute temperature**: P₁ ÷ T₁ = P₂ ÷ T₂. Putting the three together:

EQ: P₁ V₁ ÷ T₁ = P₂ V₂ ÷ T₂

EX: Using the laws
1. **A car tyre** with an absolute pressure of 320 kPa at 20 °C heats to 50 °C on a motorway: P₂ = 320 × 323.15 ÷ 293.15 = **352.7 kPa** (10% higher).
2. **A weather balloon:** at the ground P = 101 kPa, T = 288 K and V = 2.0 m³. At altitude P = 25 kPa and T = 221 K: V₂ = 2.0 × (101 ÷ 25) × (221 ÷ 288) = **6.2 m³**. Balloons are filled only partly so that they can expand.
3. **A sealed can in a fire:** at constant volume, T from 300 K to 900 K triples the pressure, so the can bursts.
/EX

TRAP: Temperatures in the gas laws must be in **kelvin**. If you put 20 °C into Charles's law, the result is nonsense. Also use **absolute** pressure: a tyre "at 220 kPa" on a gauge is at 321 kPa absolute. And each law holds only when the other quantities stay constant.

NOTES:

### Recap
- Gaussian elimination: write [A | b], make zeros below each pivot, back-substitute. Row operations never change the solution. Use partial pivoting to avoid large multipliers. Check the answer.
- Gas laws (absolute P and T): Boyle P V = const (T fixed), Charles V ÷ T = const (P fixed), Gay-Lussac P ÷ T = const (V fixed). Combined: P V ÷ T = const. Absolute zero is −273.15 °C.

BN: আজকের সারকথা: গাউসীয় অপসারণ: [A | b] তে পিভটের নিচে শূন্য বানাও, পিছন থেকে প্রতিস্থাপন করো; partial pivoting ব্যবহার করো; উত্তর মিলিয়ে দেখো। গ্যাস সূত্র (পরম P ও T): বয়েল PV = ধ্রুব, চার্লস V/T = ধ্রুব, গে-লুসাক P/T = ধ্রুব; সম্মিলিত PV/T = ধ্রুব।
