# Iterative methods for big systems. Then: work, heat and the first law of thermodynamics
lead: Gaussian elimination is perfect for small systems, but physics and engineering produce systems with millions of unknowns, such as the temperature at every point of a plate. For those you guess, improve, and repeat: iterative methods. In physics, you meet the law that governs every engine and refrigerator: the first law of thermodynamics, which says that energy is conserved when heat, work and internal energy trade places.

BN: গাউসীয় অপসারণ ছোট জোটের জন্য নিখুঁত, কিন্তু পদার্থবিজ্ঞান ও প্রকৌশলে লাখ লাখ অজানার জোট আসে, যেমন প্লেটের প্রতিটি বিন্দুর তাপমাত্রা। সেখানে অনুমান করো, উন্নত করো, আবার করো: পুনরাবৃত্তি পদ্ধতি। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে প্রতিটি ইঞ্জিন ও ফ্রিজ যে সূত্র মেনে চলে তা শিখবে: তাপগতিবিদ্যার প্রথম সূত্র, যা বলে তাপ, কাজ ও অভ্যন্তরীণ শক্তি অদলবদল হলেও শক্তি সংরক্ষিত থাকে।

@section math | 2 · Math: iterative methods | 55 min

### Guess, improve, repeat
Solve each equation for its own unknown, put a guess on the right, and compute a new guess. Two ways:

- **Jacobi:** use only the old values in every update.
- **Gauss–Seidel:** use the newest values as soon as they are available.

EX: Jacobi on a 2 × 2 system
1. System: 4x − y = 7 and −x + 3y = 5. Rewrite: x = (7 + y) ÷ 4 and y = (5 + x) ÷ 3. The exact solution: from the first y = 4x − 7, then −x + 12x − 21 = 5, so x = 26/11 = **2.3636** and y = 27/11 = **2.4545**.
2. Start at (0, 0). Iteration 1: x = 7 ÷ 4 = 1.75, y = 5 ÷ 3 = 1.667. Iteration 2: x = (7 + 1.667) ÷ 4 = 2.167, y = (5 + 1.75) ÷ 3 = 2.25. Iteration 3: x = 2.3125, y = 2.389. Iteration 4: x = 2.347, y = 2.4375. Iteration 5: x = 2.359, y = 2.449.
3. The error shrinks by a constant factor each pair of steps (here 1/12).
/EX

EX: Gauss–Seidel on the same system
1. Start at (0, 0). Iteration 1: x = 7 ÷ 4 = 1.75, then (using the new x) y = (5 + 1.75) ÷ 3 = 2.25.
2. Iteration 2: x = (7 + 2.25) ÷ 4 = 2.3125, y = (5 + 2.3125) ÷ 3 = 2.4375. Iteration 3: x = 2.3594, y = 2.4531. Iteration 4: x = 2.3633, y = 2.4544.
3. After 4 iterations the error is about 0.0003, while Jacobi needed 8 iterations for the same accuracy: **Gauss–Seidel converges about twice as fast**. Its error shrinks by 1/12 **per iteration**.
/EX

### When does it converge?
A sufficient condition: the matrix is **strictly diagonally dominant**, meaning |a_ii| > Σ_(j≠i) |a_ij| in every row. The smaller the off-diagonal part relative to the diagonal, the faster the convergence.

EX: Dominance matters
1. 4x − y = 7, −x + 3y = 5: row 1: 4 > 1, row 2: 3 > 1. Dominant, converges.
2. **Swap the equations:** −x + 3y = 5 first, then 4x − y = 7. Now solving "row i for unknown i" gives x = 3y − 5 and y = 4x − 7. From (0, 0): (−5, −7), then (−26, −27), then (−86, −111): it **diverges**. The same system, a bad ordering.
3. **Number of iterations:** with Gauss–Seidel's factor 1/12, to reach an error of 10⁻⁴ we need (1/12)ⁿ < 10⁻⁴, so n > 4 ÷ log₁₀ 12 = **3.7**, i.e. 4 iterations. For Jacobi (factor 1/√12 per step): n > 4 ÷ log₁₀ √12 = **7.4**, i.e. 8 iterations.
/EX

### Why bother? Heat on a plate
In the steady state, the temperature at each interior point of a plate is the **average of its four neighbours** (that is Laplace's equation, discretised). Each node gives one linear equation, so a plate with 1000 × 1000 nodes means a million unknowns. Elimination would need about 10¹⁸ operations. But each iteration costs only about 5 million operations, and a few hundred iterations are enough.

EX: A small plate
1. A square with four interior nodes A (top left), B (top right), C (bottom left), D (bottom right). The top edge is held at 100 °C and the other edges at 0 °C. A = (100 + B + C) ÷ 4, B = (100 + A + D) ÷ 4, C = (A + D) ÷ 4, D = (B + C) ÷ 4.
2. By symmetry A = B and C = D: 3A − C = 100 and 3C = A. So C = A ÷ 3 and (8/3) A = 100: **A = B = 37.5 °C** and **C = D = 12.5 °C**.
3. Gauss–Seidel from all zeros: sweep 1 gives A = 25, B = 31.25, C = 6.25, D = 9.375. Sweep 2 gives A = 34.375, B = 35.94, C = 10.94, D = 11.72. The values are heading to 37.5 and 12.5.
4. **A rod:** nodes T₀ = 100, T₁, T₂, T₃, T₄ = 0 with each interior value the average of its neighbours gives the straight line **T₁ = 75, T₂ = 50, T₃ = 25**.
/EX

BN: Jacobi: শুধু পুরোনো মান ব্যবহার করো। Gauss–Seidel: নতুন মান সঙ্গে সঙ্গে ব্যবহার করো, প্রায় দ্বিগুণ দ্রুত। প্রতি সারিতে |a_ii| > Σ|a_ij| (কর্ণ প্রাধান্য) হলে অভিসৃত হয়, সমীকরণের ক্রম খারাপ হলে অপসারী হতে পারে। প্লেটের তাপমাত্রা: প্রতিটি ভেতরের নোড = চার প্রতিবেশীর গড়। লাখ লাখ অজানায় পুনরাবৃত্তিই একমাত্র উপায়।

TRAP: An iterative method can **diverge** if the matrix is not dominant, and a poor ordering of the equations can ruin a good problem. Always put the large coefficients on the diagonal. And stop the iteration by checking that **successive values agree to the accuracy you need**, not after a fixed number of steps.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: work and the first law | 35 min

### Work done by a gas
When a gas expands against a piston, it pushes the piston and does work. For a tiny volume change dV at pressure P, the work done **by** the gas is dW = P dV, so

EQ: W = ∫ P dV     (the area under the curve on a P–V diagram)

The **first law of thermodynamics** is conservation of energy:

EQ: ΔU = Q − W     (Q = heat added to the gas, W = work done by the gas)

BN: গ্যাস প্রসারিত হলে পিস্টনের ওপর কাজ করে: W = ∫P dV, যা P–V গ্রাফের নিচের ক্ষেত্রফল। তাপগতিবিদ্যার প্রথম সূত্র শক্তির সংরক্ষণ: ΔU = Q − W, যেখানে Q = গ্যাসে যোগ করা তাপ আর W = গ্যাসের করা কাজ। ধনাত্মক W মানে গ্যাস শক্তি হারায়।

### Four processes of an ideal gas
| Process | What is fixed | Work W | Heat Q | ΔU |
| Isochoric | V | 0 | n C_V ΔT | n C_V ΔT |
| Isobaric | P | P ΔV = n R ΔT | n C_P ΔT | n C_V ΔT |
| Isothermal | T | n R T ln (V₂ ÷ V₁) | = W | 0 |
| Adiabatic | Q = 0 | (P₁V₁ − P₂V₂) ÷ (γ − 1) | 0 | −W |

Here C_P = C_V + R and γ = C_P ÷ C_V (5/3 for monatomic gases, 7/5 = 1.4 for diatomic gases like air). In an adiabatic process P V^γ = constant and T V^(γ−1) = constant.

EX: One example for each process
1. **Isothermal:** 1 mol at 300 K expands from 10 L to 20 L. W = 8.314 × 300 × ln 2 = **1729 J**. Q = **1729 J** and ΔU = 0 (the gas takes in heat and turns all of it into work).
2. **Isobaric:** 2 mol of air heated from 300 K to 400 K at constant pressure. W = n R ΔT = 2 × 8.314 × 100 = **1663 J**. ΔU = 2 × 20.79 × 100 = 4157 J. Q = ΔU + W = **5820 J** (= n C_P ΔT with C_P = 29.1).
3. **Isochoric:** 1 mol of helium from 300 K to 600 K: W = 0, so Q = ΔU = 12.47 × 300 = **3741 J**.
4. **Adiabatic compression of air** in a diesel engine: V₁ = 1 L at 101.3 kPa and 293 K is compressed to V₂ = 0.1 L (ratio 10). P₂ = 101.3 × 10^1.4 = **2545 kPa** (25 atm). T₂ = 293 × 10^0.4 = 293 × 2.512 = **736 K = 463 °C**. Diesel fuel ignites at about 400 °C, so the compression alone lights the fuel, with no spark plug.
5. **Work in the diesel example:** W = (P₁V₁ − P₂V₂) ÷ (γ − 1) = (101.3 − 254.5) ÷ 0.4 = **−383 J** (383 J of work is done on the gas). Check: n = 101,300 × 0.001 ÷ (8.314 × 293) = 0.0416 mol, ΔU = 0.0416 × 20.79 × 443 = **383 J** ✓.
6. **Free expansion** (a gas rushing into a vacuum): W = 0 and Q = 0, so ΔU = 0 and the temperature of an ideal gas does not change.
/EX

### A cycle: how an engine works
Run the gas around a closed loop on the P–V diagram. The net work is the area enclosed.

EX: A rectangular cycle
1. A → B: expansion at constant P = 200 kPa from 1 L to 3 L. B → C: cooling at constant V = 3 L until P = 100 kPa. C → D: compression at P = 100 kPa from 3 L to 1 L. D → A: heating at constant V = 1 L back to 200 kPa.
2. Work: A → B: 200 × 2 = +400 J. C → D: 100 × (−2) = −200 J. Net work **W = 200 J** = the area of the rectangle (2 L × 100 kPa).
3. Heat in (monatomic gas): A → B: Q = (5/2) P ΔV = 2.5 × 200 × 2 = 1000 J. D → A: Q = ΔU = (3/2) V ΔP = 1.5 × 1 × 100 = 150 J. Total heat in = **1150 J**.
4. **Efficiency** = W ÷ Q_in = 200 ÷ 1150 = **17.4%**. The rest is exhausted as heat. Next week you will see why no engine can do better than the Carnot limit.
/EX

TRAP: Watch the sign convention: **W is the work done BY the gas**, so compression gives a negative W, and ΔU = Q − W. In the adiabatic formulas use **γ**, not the ratio C_V ÷ C_P. And remember that isothermal does not mean "no heat": the gas absorbs heat to do the work.

NOTES:

### Recap
- Iterative methods: Jacobi (old values), Gauss–Seidel (new values, about twice as fast). Convergence is guaranteed for strictly diagonally dominant matrices. Steady-state plate: each node is the average of its neighbours. Rod of nodes: a straight line.
- First law ΔU = Q − W, W = ∫ P dV. Isochoric W = 0. Isobaric W = P ΔV, Q = n C_P ΔT. Isothermal ΔU = 0, W = n R T ln (V₂ ÷ V₁). Adiabatic Q = 0, P V^γ = const, T V^(γ−1) = const. Net work in a cycle = enclosed area, efficiency = W ÷ Q_in.

BN: আজকের সারকথা: Jacobi (পুরোনো মান), Gauss–Seidel (নতুন মান, দ্বিগুণ দ্রুত); কর্ণ প্রাধান্য থাকলে অভিসৃত হয়। প্লেটে প্রতিটি নোড = প্রতিবেশীর গড়। প্রথম সূত্র ΔU = Q − W; আইসোকোরিক W = 0; আইসোবারিক W = PΔV; আইসোথার্মাল ΔU = 0; অ্যাডিয়াবেটিক Q = 0, PV^γ = ধ্রুব; চক্রে নিট কাজ = আবদ্ধ ক্ষেত্রফল।
