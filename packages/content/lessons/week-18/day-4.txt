# Applications and Euler's method. Then: dielectrics
lead: Not every differential equation has a neat formula, and in practice engineers solve most of them on a computer, step by step. The simplest recipe, Euler's method, just follows the slope for a small step and repeats. It is the ancestor of every simulation you will meet. You also use today's tools on a detective problem: when did a body die? In physics, you put an insulating material, a dielectric, between the plates of a capacitor and see why it increases the capacitance, why it weakens the field, and why water is such a good solvent.

@section math | 2 · Math: Euler's method and applications | 55 min

### Euler's method
Given y′ = f (x, y) and y (x₀) = y₀, follow the tangent for a small step h, then recompute the slope:

EQ: x_{n+1} = x_n + h     y_{n+1} = y_n + h f (x_n, y_n)

It is a first-order method: halving h roughly halves the error.

EX: Euler in action
1. **y′ = y, y (0) = 1** (exact: eˣ, e = 2.71828). With step h = 0.1 the factor per step is 1.1, so y (1) ≈ 1.1¹⁰ = **2.594** (4.6% too low). With h = 0.01: 1.01¹⁰⁰ = **2.705** (0.5% low). With h = 0.5: 1.5² = **2.25**. Smaller steps converge to e: (1 + 1/n)ⁿ → e.
2. **y′ = x − y, y (0) = 1** (exact: y = x − 1 + 2 e^(−x), y (1) = 2e⁻¹ = **0.7358**). With h = 0.25: y₁ = 1 + 0.25 × (−1) = 0.75, y₂ = 0.75 + 0.25 × (0.25 − 0.75) = 0.625, y₃ = 0.625 + 0.25 × (0.5 − 0.625) = 0.59375, y₄ = 0.59375 + 0.25 × (0.75 − 0.59375) = **0.6328**: 14% below the exact value. With h = 0.1 the result is **0.6974** (5.2% below).
3. **Logistic growth** P′ = 0.5 P (1 − P ÷ 1000), P₀ = 100 and h = 1 year: P₁ = 145.0, P₂ = 207.0, P₃ = 289.1, P₄ = 391.8, P₅ = **511.0**, compared with the exact **575** (11% low) because the growth rate is increasing and Euler always lags.
4. **A better recipe:** the improved Euler method (Heun) averages the slope at the beginning and at the end of the step. For y′ = y with h = 0.1 the factor is 1 + h + h²/2 = 1.105, so y (1) = 1.105¹⁰ = **2.7141**, error only **0.15%**. (Runge-Kutta 4, in Week 20, is far more accurate still.)
5. **Stability warning.** For y′ = −10 y with h = 0.3, Euler gives y_{n+1} = y_n (1 − 3) = −2 y_n: the numbers alternate and **grow** (1, −2, 4, −8, …) although the true solution decays. For decay equations the step must satisfy h < 2 ÷ |k| (here h < 0.2).
/EX

### Applications
EX: Detective work and engineering
1. **When did the death occur?** A body is found at noon at 28 °C in a room at 20 °C, and an hour later it is 25 °C. Normal body temperature is 37 °C. Newton's law: T − 20 = 17 e^(−kt) with t counted from death. The two readings give (28 − 20) ÷ (25 − 20) = e^(k × 1 h) = 1.6, so **k = ln 1.6 = 0.470 per hour**. Death occurred when T − 20 = 17: the time from death to noon is t = ln (17 ÷ 8) ÷ 0.470 = 0.754 ÷ 0.470 = **1.60 h = 96 minutes**. So the death was at about **10:24**. (Real cooling is more complicated, so this is only an estimate.)
2. **Quenching a metal part** from 600 °C to 100 °C in a 20 °C oil bath: T − 20 falls from 580 to 80 and k = 0.1 per second gives t = ln (580 ÷ 80) ÷ 0.1 = **19.8 s**.
3. **Terminal speed** (Day 2): with m = 70 kg and b = 10 kg/s the speed after 7 s is 43.4 m/s (63% of the terminal 68.6 m/s). The time constant is the time for 63% of the change, in all linear first-order systems.
4. **Time constants** of everyday systems: a cup of coffee 20–30 minutes; a house cooling about 10 hours; an RC circuit with R = 1 kΩ and C = 100 μF: 0.1 s; a drug with a half-life of 5 h: τ = t_½ ÷ ln 2 = 7.2 h.
/EX

TRAP: Euler's method is **approximate**: the error depends on the step size, and a step that is too big can give nonsense (instability). Always test by halving the step: if the answer changes a lot, the step was too big. In the cooling problem the formula T − T_a means you must subtract the room temperature in **both** readings before taking the ratio, and the "time of death" assumes a constant room temperature and a normal starting body temperature.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: dielectrics | 35 min

### What a dielectric does
A **dielectric** is an insulator. In an electric field its molecules **polarise**: the charges shift slightly, so that the face next to the positive plate gets a thin layer of negative **bound charge** and the other face a positive one. This bound charge produces a field that **opposes** the original field, so the net field inside is **reduced** by a factor κ (the **dielectric constant**, κ ≥ 1):

EQ: E = E₀ ÷ κ     C = κ C₀     σ_bound = σ (1 − 1 ÷ κ)

Typical κ: air 1.0006, paper 3.7, glass 5 to 10, water 80, barium titanate ceramic about 1000 or more.

BN: ডাইইলেকট্রিক = অপরিবাহী। ক্ষেত্রে এর অণুগুলো মেরুকৃত হয়, ফলে পৃষ্ঠে বাঁধা চার্জ (bound charge) দেখা দেয় যা মূল ক্ষেত্রের বিরোধিতা করে। নেট ক্ষেত্র E = E₀/κ, ধারকত্ব C = κC₀, বাঁধা চার্জ σ_b = σ(1 − 1/κ)। κ: বাতাস 1.0006, কাগজ 3.7, কাচ 5–10, পানি 80, সিরামিক 1000+।

EX: Filling a capacitor
Plates with A = 0.01 m², d = 1 mm (C₀ = 88.5 pF) with a dielectric κ = 5:
1. **New capacitance:** C = 5 × 88.5 = **442.5 pF**.
2. **Battery connected (V = 100 V stays fixed).** The charge rises to Q = C V = 44.25 nC (5 times more), E stays 10⁵ V/m (V ÷ d), and the energy rises to ½ C V² = **2.21 μJ** (5 times more). The battery supplies the extra charge.
3. **Battery disconnected (Q = 8.85 nC stays fixed).** C rises 5 times, so V falls to 100 ÷ 5 = **20 V**, E falls to **2 × 10⁴ V/m** and the energy falls to Q² ÷ 2C = **8.85 × 10⁻⁸ J** from 4.43 × 10⁻⁷ J. The difference, **3.5 × 10⁻⁷ J**, is the work done by the field **pulling the dielectric slab into the capacitor**: a slab is sucked in.
4. **Bound charge:** σ = Q ÷ A = 8.85 × 10⁻⁷ C/m². σ_bound = σ (1 − 1/5) = **7.08 × 10⁻⁷ C/m²**, leaving a net σ − σ_bound = 1.77 × 10⁻⁷ C/m² and E = 1.77 × 10⁻⁷ ÷ 8.85 × 10⁻¹² = **2 × 10⁴ V/m** ✓.
5. **Paper dielectric with a strength of about 16 MV/m**, d = 0.1 mm: the maximum voltage is 16 × 10⁶ × 10⁻⁴ = **1600 V**, and with A = 0.01 m² the capacitance is 3.7 × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.01 ÷ 10⁻⁴ = **3.27 nF**. Dielectrics both raise C **and** allow thinner gaps without sparks (air breaks down at 3 MV/m).
6. **A ceramic capacitor** (κ = 1200, A = 1 cm², d = 10 μm): C = 1200 × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 10⁻⁵ = **106 nF**: a chip the size of a rice grain with a stack of many such layers holds microfarads (the capacitors in every phone).
7. **Why water dissolves salt:** the force between Na⁺ and Cl⁻ at 0.28 nm is k e² ÷ r² = **2.9 × 10⁻⁹ N** in vacuum. In water (κ = 80) the force becomes **3.7 × 10⁻¹¹ N**, 80 times weaker: thermal motion can then pull the ions apart.
/EX

TRAP: Always say whether the battery is **connected** (V fixed) or **disconnected** (Q fixed): the answers are opposite. With the battery on, inserting the dielectric raises Q and U; with it off, V, E and U all fall by κ. A dielectric **reduces** the field (it does not cancel it) because the bound charge is smaller than the free charge by a factor 1 − 1/κ. And a conductor can be thought of as a dielectric with κ → ∞ (E = 0 inside).
