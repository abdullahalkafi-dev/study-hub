# What a differential equation is: growth, decay and cooling. Then: capacitance
lead: Physics, biology and engineering rarely hand you a formula. They hand you a rule for how a quantity changes: a population grows in proportion to its size, a hot drink cools faster the hotter it is, a radioactive sample decays in proportion to what is left. That rule is a differential equation, and solving it gives the formula. In physics, you meet the capacitor, a device that stores charge and energy in the electric field between two conductors. It is in every phone, camera flash, computer and power supply.

BN: পদার্থবিজ্ঞান, জীববিজ্ঞান ও প্রকৌশল সরাসরি সূত্র দেয় না। দেয় একটা নিয়ম যে রাশিটা কীভাবে বদলায়: জনসংখ্যা তার আকারের সমানুপাতে বাড়ে, গরম পানীয় যত গরম তত দ্রুত ঠান্ডা হয়, তেজস্ক্রিয় নমুনা যা বাকি আছে তার সমানুপাতে ক্ষয় হয়। এই নিয়মই অবকল সমীকরণ, আর এটা সমাধান করলে সূত্র পাওয়া যায়। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে ধারকের সাথে পরিচয়: দুটো পরিবাহীর মাঝের বৈদ্যুতিক ক্ষেত্রে চার্জ ও শক্তি জমানোর যন্ত্র। এটা প্রতিটি ফোন, ক্যামেরা ফ্ল্যাশ, কম্পিউটার ও পাওয়ার সাপ্লাইয়ে আছে।

@section math | 2 · Math: differential equations as rules of change | 55 min

### What a differential equation is
A **differential equation** (DE) is an equation that contains an unknown function and its derivatives. A **solution** is a function that makes it true. The **order** is the highest derivative that appears. Solving the DE y′ = f (x) is just integrating; most DEs are harder because y itself appears on the right.

EX: Checking solutions
1. **y′ = 2y.** Try y = 3 e^(2x): y′ = 6 e^(2x) = 2 × 3 e^(2x) = 2y ✓. In fact **y = C e^(2x)** works for every constant C: a **family** of solutions.
2. **y′ = −y + 1.** Try y = 1 + C e^(−x): y′ = −C e^(−x) and −y + 1 = −C e^(−x) ✓.
3. **y″ = −y** (order 2): y = cos x gives y″ = −cos x ✓, and y = sin x works too. (The next weeks.)
4. **Initial-value problem (IVP):** a DE plus the value at one point, for example y′ = 2y with y (0) = 5. From y = C e^(2x), C = 5: **y = 5 e^(2x)**, so y (1) = 5 e² = **36.95**.
/EX

BN: অবকল সমীকরণ = অজানা ফাংশন ও তার অবকল সমেত সমীকরণ। সমাধান = যে ফাংশন একে সত্য করে। ক্রম = সর্বোচ্চ অবকলের ক্রম। y′ = 2y এর সমাধান y = Ce^(2x) (অসংখ্য, C ধ্রুবক)। শুরুর শর্ত (y(0) = 5) দিলে C ঠিক হয়: y = 5e^(2x)।

### The most important equation: y′ = k y
A quantity whose rate of change is proportional to itself:

EQ: dy/dt = k y     ⟹     y (t) = y₀ e^(kt)

If k > 0 it is **exponential growth**, if k < 0 **exponential decay**. Doubling time: t_d = ln 2 ÷ k. Half-life: t_½ = ln 2 ÷ |k|.

EX: Growth and decay
1. **Bacteria:** 1000 cells double every 3 hours: k = ln 2 ÷ 3 = **0.2310 per hour**. After 10 h: N = 1000 × 2^(10/3) = **10,079** (a 10× increase in 10 hours).
2. **Carbon-14:** half-life 5730 years, so k = −ln 2 ÷ 5730 = **−1.21 × 10⁻⁴ per year**. After 2000 years the fraction left is 2^(−2000/5730) = **0.785** (78.5%).
3. **Age of a sample** that has 60% of its original C-14: t = ln (1 ÷ 0.6) ÷ 1.21 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.5108 ÷ 1.21 × 10⁻⁴ = **4223 years**.
4. **Continuous interest** at 5% per year: doubling time ln 2 ÷ 0.05 = **13.9 years**; 1000 grows to 1000 e^(0.5) = **1649** in 10 years.
5. **Caffeine** with a half-life of 5 h: from 200 mg, after 12 h you keep 200 × 2^(−2.4) = **37.9 mg** (19%).
/EX

### Newton's law of cooling
An object at temperature T in surroundings at T_a loses heat in proportion to the difference:

EQ: dT/dt = −k (T − T_a)     ⟹     T (t) = T_a + (T₀ − T_a) e^(−kt)

The difference T − T_a itself decays exponentially.

EX: A cup of coffee
1. A coffee at 90 °C in a 20 °C room cools to 60 °C in 10 minutes. Then 40 = 70 e^(−10k) and **k = ln (70 ÷ 40) ÷ 10 = 0.0560 per minute**.
2. **After 20 minutes:** T = 20 + 70 e^(−0.0560 × 20) = 20 + 70 × 0.3265 = **42.9 °C** (the difference fell from 70 to 22.9).
3. **Time to reach 30 °C:** 10 = 70 e^(−kt), t = ln 7 ÷ 0.0560 = **34.8 minutes**.
4. **Check with a different k = 0.05 per minute:** T (15) = 20 + 70 e^(−0.75) = **53.1 °C**.
/EX

TRAP: A differential equation has a **family** of solutions: the constant is fixed only by an initial condition. For cooling, the rate depends on the **difference** T − T_a, not on T: do not write dT/dt = −kT. Be careful with **units**: k has units of 1 ÷ time, so "per hour" and "per minute" differ by 60. And exponential growth is not "fast": it is a constant **percentage** growth (growth by the same factor in equal times).

@section physics | 3 · Physics: capacitance | 35 min

### What a capacitor is
A **capacitor** is two conductors (plates) close to each other. If you put +Q on one and −Q on the other, there is a potential difference V between them, proportional to Q. The ratio is the **capacitance**:

EQ: C = Q ÷ V     (unit: farad, 1 F = 1 C/V; practical units μF = 10⁻⁶ F, nF = 10⁻⁹ F, pF = 10⁻¹² F)

For **parallel plates** of area A and gap d (Week 16 and 17 gave E = σ ÷ ε₀ = Q ÷ (ε₀ A) and V = E d):

EQ: C = ε₀ A ÷ d     (ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m)

C depends only on the **geometry** (and, tomorrow, the material between the plates), not on the charge or the voltage.

BN: ধারক = কাছাকাছি দুটো পরিবাহী পাত। +Q ও −Q দিলে V হয়, C = Q/V (একক ফ্যারাড)। সমান্তরাল পাতের জন্য E = Q/(ε₀A), V = Ed থেকে C = ε₀A/d। C শুধু জ্যামিতির উপর নির্ভর করে, Q বা V এর উপর নয়।

EX: Capacitance numbers
1. **A = 0.01 m², d = 1 mm:** C = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.01 ÷ 10⁻³ = **88.5 pF**. At 100 V the charge is Q = C V = **8.85 nC**.
2. **How big for 1 farad?** With d = 1 mm: A = C d ÷ ε₀ = 1 × 10⁻³ ÷ 8.85 × 10⁻¹² = **1.13 × 10⁸ m²**, about 113 km². A farad is enormous. Real "supercapacitors" reach 1 F in a small package with porous carbon, where the area is about 2000 m² per gram and the gap is about 1 nm: C ≈ ε₀ A ÷ d = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 2000 ÷ 10⁻⁹ = **17.7 F per gram**.
3. **An isolated sphere** of radius R: C = 4π ε₀ R. For R = 10 cm: **11.1 pF**. The **Earth**: C = 4π ε₀ R = **7.1 × 10⁻⁴ F** (710 μF).
4. **A coaxial cable** (inner radius a = 1 mm, outer b = 5 mm): C per length = 2π ε₀ ÷ ln (b ÷ a) = 5.56 × 10⁻¹¹ ÷ 1.609 = **34.5 pF/m**, so a 10 m cable has 345 pF.
5. **Concentric spheres** with a = 5 cm and b = 6 cm: C = 4π ε₀ a b ÷ (b − a) = 1.112 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 0.003 ÷ 0.01 = **33.4 pF**.
6. **The electric field inside:** 12 V across a 0.1 mm gap gives E = 12 ÷ 10⁻⁴ = **1.2 × 10⁵ V/m**. A 470 μF capacitor at 12 V holds Q = 470 × 10⁻⁶ × 12 = **5.64 mC**.

TRAP: A capacitor stores charge **separation**, not net charge: the total charge on it is zero (+Q on one plate and −Q on the other), and the Q in C = Q ÷ V is the charge on **one plate**. The formula C = ε₀ A ÷ d holds for plates much **wider than the gap**. And C does not change when you change V: doubling V doubles Q.

### Recap
- Differential equation: unknown function plus derivatives; solutions form a family fixed by initial conditions. y′ = k y gives y = y₀ e^(kt): doubling time ln 2 ÷ k, half-life ln 2 ÷ |k|. Cooling: T = T_a + (T₀ − T_a) e^(−kt). Examples: bacteria, C-14, interest, caffeine, coffee.
- Capacitance C = Q ÷ V (farad). Parallel plates C = ε₀ A ÷ d. Sphere 4π ε₀ R. Coax 2π ε₀ ÷ ln (b ÷ a) per metre. Concentric spheres 4π ε₀ a b ÷ (b − a). C depends on geometry only.
