@section math | 2 · Math: sequences and series | 55 min

### Sequences
A **sequence** is a list a₁, a₂, a₃, … defined by a rule, such as aₙ = 1 ÷ n. We ask where it ends up as n grows: its **limit**.

| Sequence | Limit | Reason |
| 1 ÷ n | 0 | Gets smaller without bound |
| n ÷ (n + 1) | 1 | Divide top and bottom by n |
| (1 + 1/n)ⁿ | e = 2.718… | Defines e (Week 5) |
| rⁿ with abs(r) < 1 | 0 | Repeated shrinking |
| rⁿ with r > 1 | ∞ | Repeated growing |
| (−1)ⁿ | none | Jumps between −1 and 1 |

BN: অনুক্রম হলো সংখ্যার তালিকা যা একটা নিয়মে চলে, যেমন aₙ = 1/n। প্রশ্ন: n বাড়লে এটা কোথায় পৌঁছায়? সেটাই সীমা। 1/n → 0, n/(n+1) → 1, (1 + 1/n)ⁿ → e, আর |r| < 1 হলে rⁿ → 0।

### Series and partial sums
A **series** is the sum of a sequence: a₁ + a₂ + a₃ + … The **partial sum** Sₙ adds the first n terms. If Sₙ approaches a finite number S, the series **converges** to S. Otherwise it **diverges**.

EQ: Σ aₙ = lim (N → ∞) S_N     where S_N = a₁ + a₂ + … + a_N

PREDICT: Take the sum 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + … with each term half of the one before. Will it grow past every limit, or settle at a number? Guess the number before reading on. => It settles at **2**. Each step fills half of the remaining gap to 2: after 1 term the gap is 1, after 2 terms 1/2, after 3 terms 1/4, and so on. The gap shrinks to zero.

### The geometric series
Each term is r times the one before: a + a r + a r² + …

EQ: S_N = a (1 − r^N) ÷ (1 − r)     and for abs(r) < 1:     Σ a rⁿ = a ÷ (1 − r)

(Why: S_N − r S_N = a − a r^N, since all the middle terms cancel. If abs(r) < 1 then r^N → 0.) If abs(r) ≥ 1 the series diverges.

EX: Geometric series
1. 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + … : a = 1, r = 1/2: **2**.
2. 2 + 2/3 + 2/9 + … : a = 2, r = 1/3: 2 ÷ (2/3) = **3**.
3. 3 − 1 + 1/3 − 1/9 + … : a = 3, r = −1/3: 3 ÷ (4/3) = **9/4**.
4. 0.272727… = 27/100 + 27/10,000 + …: a = 0.27, r = 0.01: 0.27 ÷ 0.99 = 27/99 = **3/11**.
5. 0.999… = 0.9 ÷ (1 − 0.1) = **1**. Exactly 1, not "almost".
6. The sum of the first 10 terms of 1 + 3 + 9 + …: (3¹⁰ − 1) ÷ 2 = **29,524**. (A divergent series still has partial sums.)
/EX

The closer r is to 1, the longer it takes to reach the limit. | a:First term a:1:5:0.5:1 ;; r:Ratio r:0.1:0.9:0.1:0.5 ;; N:Number of terms N:1:30:1:5 | a*(1-r^N)/(1-r):Partial sum S_N:4 ;; a/(1-r):Limit a ÷ (1 − r):4 ;; a/(1-r)-a*(1-r^N)/(1-r):Remaining gap:4 | a*(1-r^x)/(1-r) ;; a/(1-r) | 0,30 | 0,50

BN: গুণোত্তর ধারা a + ar + ar² + …: আংশিক যোগফল S_N = a(1 − r^N)/(1 − r)। |r| < 1 হলে সীমা a/(1 − r), নইলে অপসারী। 0.999… = 0.9/(1 − 0.1) = 1, ঠিক ১, প্রায় নয়। 0.2727… = 27/99 = 3/11।

### More series
EX: Telescoping
1. Σ from n = 1 of 1 ÷ (n (n + 1)) = Σ (1/n − 1/(n + 1)) (partial fractions, Day 3 last week).
2. S_N = (1 − 1/2) + (1/2 − 1/3) + … + (1/N − 1/(N + 1)) = 1 − 1/(N + 1) → **1**.
/EX

EX: The harmonic series diverges
1. 1 + 1/2 + (1/3 + 1/4) + (1/5 + … + 1/8) + … : each bracket is at least 1/2 (for example 1/3 + 1/4 > 1/4 + 1/4). So the sum exceeds 1 + 1/2 + 1/2 + 1/2 + … and **diverges**.
2. But it diverges very slowly: S₁₀ = 2.93, S₁₀₀₀ = 7.49, S₁₀⁶ = 14.4. In general Sₙ ≈ ln n + 0.577.
3. So **terms going to zero is not enough** for convergence.
/EX

**The divergence test.** If aₙ does **not** tend to 0, then Σ aₙ diverges. (The converse is false: the harmonic series.)

For comparison, Σ 1 ÷ n² **converges** (to π²/6 ≈ 1.645, proved by Euler).

TRAP: Do not use the geometric formula when abs(r) ≥ 1. And do not say "the terms go to 0, so it converges": that is exactly the harmonic series trap. The formula a ÷ (1 − r) uses the **first term** a of the series as written, so check whether your sum starts at n = 0 or n = 1.

@section physics | 3 · Physics: what a wave is | 35 min

### A disturbance that travels
A **wave** transfers energy and information through a medium **without** moving the medium along. A crowd doing "the wave" in a stadium is the classic picture: each person only stands up and sits down, but the pattern travels around the stadium.

- **Transverse waves:** the medium moves **perpendicular** to the direction of travel (a rope, a guitar string, light).
- **Longitudinal waves:** the medium moves **along** the direction of travel in compressions and rarefactions (sound, a slinky pushed along its length).

A pulse of any shape that keeps its shape and moves to the right at speed v has the form y(x, t) = f(x − v t). Replace x − vt by x + vt for a wave moving left.

BN: তরঙ্গ মাধ্যমকে সরিয়ে না নিয়ে শক্তি ও তথ্য পাঠায়। স্টেডিয়ামে "ঢেউ" এর মতো: প্রত্যেকে শুধু দাঁড়ায় ও বসে, কিন্তু ধরনটা ছড়িয়ে পড়ে। অনুপ্রস্থ তরঙ্গে (দড়ি, আলো) মাধ্যম লম্ব দিকে নড়ে; অনুদৈর্ঘ্য তরঙ্গে (শব্দ) চলার দিকেই নড়ে। ডানে যাওয়া তরঙ্গ y = f(x − vt) আকারের।

### The sinusoidal wave
The most important wave is a sine wave:

EQ: y(x, t) = A sin(k x − ω t + φ)     with     k = 2π ÷ λ     ω = 2π f     v = ω ÷ k = f λ

- **A** amplitude (maximum displacement)
- **λ** wavelength (distance between crests), **k** wave number (rad/m)
- **f** frequency (Hz), **T** = 1 ÷ f period, **ω** angular frequency (rad/s)
- **v** wave speed: one wavelength passes in one period, so **v = λ ÷ T = f λ**

At any one point, the medium oscillates up and down in SHM with angular frequency ω. At one instant, the shape is a sine in x. **The wave speed v is not the speed of the particles.** The particle velocity is ∂y/∂t, and its maximum is A ω.

EX: Reading a wave
1. y = 0.02 sin(12.57 x − 25.13 t) in metres and seconds. Comparing: A = **0.02 m**, k = 12.57 rad/m so λ = 2π ÷ 12.57 = **0.5 m**, ω = 25.13 rad/s so f = **4 Hz**, and v = ω ÷ k = **2 m/s** (also fλ = 4 × 0.5 ✓).
2. At t = 0 and x = 0.2: y = 0.02 sin(2.513) = **0.0118 m**.
3. Maximum particle speed = A ω = 0.02 × 25.13 = **0.50 m/s**, far from the wave speed 2 m/s.
/EX

EX: Everyday waves
1. **Sound** in air at 20 °C travels at v = 343 m/s. A concert A of 440 Hz has λ = 343 ÷ 440 = **0.78 m**.
2. **Radio** waves travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s. For an FM station at 98 MHz: λ = 3 × 10⁸ ÷ 9.8 × 10⁷ = **3.06 m**.
3. **Lightning:** thunder arrives 3 s after the flash. The distance is 343 × 3 = **1029 m**, about 1 km (the light arrives almost instantly).
4. **Earthquakes** send P waves (longitudinal, 6 km/s) and S waves (transverse, 3.5 km/s). The S wave arrives 30 s after the P wave. The extra delay per km is 1 ÷ 3.5 − 1 ÷ 6 = 0.119 s, so the distance is 30 ÷ 0.119 = **252 km**. With three stations you can locate the epicentre.
/EX

### Why y = f(x − vt) is a wave: a first partial derivative
When a function depends on two variables (x and t), a **partial derivative** differentiates with respect to one while holding the other fixed. For y = f(x − vt), use the chain rule:

EQ: ∂y/∂t = −v f′(x − vt)     ∂²y/∂t² = v² f″(x − vt)     ∂y/∂x = f′     ∂²y/∂x² = f″     so     ∂²y/∂t² = v² ∂²y/∂x²

That last equation is the **wave equation**. Any shape travelling at speed v obeys it. (Week 10 Day 2 derives it for a real string.)

BN: y = A sin(kx − ωt + φ), k = 2π/λ, ω = 2πf, তরঙ্গ-দ্রুতি v = ω/k = fλ। মনে রাখো: তরঙ্গের দ্রুতি v কণার দ্রুতি নয়; কণার সর্বোচ্চ দ্রুতি Aω। শব্দ 343 m/s, রেডিও 3 × 10⁸ m/s। আংশিক অন্তরকলন: দুই চলকের ফাংশনে একটাকে স্থির রেখে অন্যটার অন্তরকলন; তাতে y = f(x − vt) তরঙ্গ সমীকরণ ∂²y/∂t² = v² ∂²y/∂x² মেনে চলে।

TRAP: **v = f λ is the wave speed; Aω is the particle speed. They are different things.** Another slip: for a given medium, v is fixed, so if you raise the frequency the wavelength shrinks (λ = v ÷ f), not the speed. And use radians in the sine: k x − ω t is in radians.

P: A pulse is y = 1 ÷ (1 + (x − 3t)²). In which direction and how fast does it move, and where is its peak at t = 2? => It has the form f(x − vt) with v = 3, so it moves **right at 3 m/s**. The peak is where x − 3t = 0, so at t = 2, **x = 6 m**.
P: Show that y = sin(kx − ωt) satisfies the wave equation with v = ω ÷ k. => ∂²y/∂t² = −ω² sin(kx − ωt) and ∂²y/∂x² = −k² sin(kx − ωt). So ∂²y/∂t² = (ω² ÷ k²) ∂²y/∂x², which has v² = ω² ÷ k² ✓.
LVL: Level 3 | Real
P: A radar pulse hits a target and returns after 0.2 ms. How far away is the target? => The pulse travels there and back: d = c t ÷ 2 = 3 × 10⁸ × 2 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 2 = **30,000 m = 30 km**.
P: Thunder arrives 5.5 s after lightning. How far is the storm, and is it a danger? => d = 343 × 5.5 = **1.9 km**. (The rule of thumb is about 3 s per km.) Storms within about 10 km can still strike.
LVL: Challenge | The chain rule makes waves
P: Show that any function y = f(x − vt) satisfies ∂²y/∂t² = v² ∂²y/∂x², and then show that y = f(x − vt) + g(x + vt), a wave in each direction, also satisfies it. What does this mean for two pulses crossing on a string? => For f(x − vt): ∂y/∂t = −v f′ and ∂²y/∂t² = v² f″, while ∂²y/∂x² = f″, so the equation holds. For g(x + vt): ∂²y/∂t² = v² g″ and ∂²y/∂x² = g″, also true. The equation is **linear**, so the sum of two solutions is a solution: the two pulses pass through each other without changing. At the crossing their displacements add (superposition), and afterwards each continues unchanged.

### Recap
- Sequence limit; series = limit of partial sums. Geometric: Σ a rⁿ = a ÷ (1 − r) for abs(r) < 1. Telescoping series collapse. The harmonic series diverges (slowly); Σ 1 ÷ n² converges. If terms do not tend to 0, the series diverges.
