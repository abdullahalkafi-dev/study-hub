### Limits in one line
Replace each function by its series, simplify, and read off the answer. Use "…" for the terms that vanish faster.

EX: Limits by series
1. lim (x → 0) (1 − cos x) ÷ x²: 1 − cos x = x²/2 − x⁴/24 + …, so the ratio = 1/2 − x²/24 + … → **1/2**.
2. lim (x → 0) (x − sin x) ÷ x³: x − sin x = x³/6 − x⁵/120 + …, so the ratio → **1/6**.
3. lim (x → 0) (cos x − 1 + x²/2) ÷ x⁴: the numerator is x⁴/24 − …, so the limit is **1/24**. (L'Hôpital would need four rounds.)
4. lim (x → 0) (ln(1 + x) − x) ÷ x²: ln(1 + x) − x = −x²/2 + x³/3 − …, so the limit is **−1/2**.
5. lim (x → 0) (e^(x²) − 1) ÷ (1 − cos x): numerator x² + x⁴/2 + …, denominator x²/2 − …, so the ratio → x² ÷ (x²/2) = **2**.
/EX

### Multiplying and composing series
Series can be added, multiplied and substituted into each other, as long as you keep track of the order you need.

EX: Combining
1. eˣ sin x up to x³: (1 + x + x²/2 + …)(x − x³/6 + …) = x + x² + (x³/2 − x³/6) + … = **x + x² + x³/3 + …**.
2. cos(x²) = 1 − x⁴/2 + x⁸/24 − …: substitute x² for x in the cosine series.
3. 1 ÷ (1 + x²) = 1 − x² + x⁴ − …: substitute −x² into the geometric series.
/EX

EX: Exact sums from series
1. Put x = 1/2 in −ln(1 − x) = Σ xⁿ ÷ n: Σ 1 ÷ (n 2ⁿ) = **ln 2 ≈ 0.693**.
2. Put x = 1 in eˣ: Σ from n = 0 of 1 ÷ n! = **e**.
3. The Leibniz series: 1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − 1/7 + … = **π/4** (arctan 1).
/EX

### Solving the oscillator equation with a series
Try y = Σ cₙ xⁿ in y″ = −y. Then y″ = Σ n (n − 1) cₙ xⁿ⁻², and matching powers of x gives the rule (n + 2)(n + 1) cₙ₊₂ = −cₙ.

EX: Where sine and cosine come from
1. Start with y(0) = 1 and y′(0) = 0, so c₀ = 1 and c₁ = 0. The rule gives c₂ = −c₀ ÷ 2 = −1/2, c₃ = 0, c₄ = −c₂ ÷ 12 = 1/24, c₅ = 0, c₆ = −1/720 …
2. y = 1 − x²/2 + x⁴/24 − … = **cos x**. Starting with y(0) = 0 and y′(0) = 1 gives **sin x**.
3. So the motion of a mass on a spring, x″ = −ω² x, has cosine and sine as its solutions because of this series calculation, not by magic.
/EX

BN: লিমিট ধারা দিয়ে: প্রতিটি ফাংশনের জায়গায় তার ধারা বসাও। (1 − cos x)/x² = 1/2 − x²/24 + … → 1/2; (x − sin x)/x³ → 1/6; (cos x − 1 + x²/2)/x⁴ → 1/24। ধারা গুণ ও প্রতিস্থাপন করা যায়: eˣ sin x = x + x² + x³/3 + …। y″ = −y কে y = Σcₙxⁿ ধরে সমাধান করলে cos x ও sin x পাওয়া যায়: SHM এর সমাধান সূত্র থেকে।

### Approximations that physics uses every day
EX: Gravity near the surface
1. Raising a mass m through height h costs ΔU = G M m (1 ÷ R − 1 ÷ (R + h)) = (G M m ÷ R) × (h ÷ R) × (1 ÷ (1 + h ÷ R)).
2. Expand 1 ÷ (1 + h ÷ R) ≈ 1 − h ÷ R. The leading term is ΔU ≈ (G M ÷ R²) m h = **m g h**, with g = G M ÷ R² = 9.8.
3. For h = 100 m, h ÷ R = 1.6 × 10⁻⁵, so m g h is accurate to **0.002%**. For h = R ÷ 10 (637 km), the exact ΔU = (G M m ÷ R)(0.0909) while m g h = (G M m ÷ R)(0.1): off by **10%**.
/EX

EX: Slow Doppler and relativity
1. 1 ÷ (1 − β) ≈ 1 + β + β² + …. For a source at β = v ÷ c = 0.1 the exact factor is 1.1111, and the first-order result 1 + β = 1.1 is **1% low**.
2. The kinetic energy (γ − 1) m c² = ½ m v² + (3/8) m v⁴ ÷ c² + … (Day 4): classical physics is the first term of relativistic physics.
/EX

TRAP: A limit by series needs enough terms: keep terms **up to the order of the denominator** (x⁴ below means expanding up to x⁴). Do not drop a term that is the same order as what you keep. And approximations are only valid **near the expansion point**: sin x ≈ x fails at x = 2.

### Intensity and the decibel
A sound wave carries power. The **intensity** is power per area, I (W/m²). A source of power P radiating equally in all directions has, at distance r (the area of a sphere is 4π r²):

EQ: I = P ÷ (4π r²)     (the inverse-square law)

Human hearing covers an enormous range, from I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m² (threshold) to about 1 W/m² (pain), so we use a logarithmic scale, the **decibel level**:

EQ: β = 10 log₁₀(I ÷ I₀)     (decibels, dB)

Each **factor of 10 in intensity adds 10 dB**. Doubling the intensity adds 3 dB. Doubling the distance cuts the intensity to 1/4, which is **−6 dB**.

| Sound | dB | Intensity (W/m²) |
| Threshold of hearing | 0 | 10⁻¹² |
| Whisper | 20 | 10⁻¹⁰ |
| Conversation | 60 | 10⁻⁶ |
| Busy traffic | 80 | 10⁻⁴ |
| Rock concert | 110 | 0.1 |
| Pain | 120 | 1 |

BN: তীব্রতা I = P/(4πr²) (ব্যস্ত-বর্গ নিয়ম)। শ্রবণ সীমা I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m² থেকে প্রায় 1 W/m² পর্যন্ত বিশাল পরিসর, তাই ডেসিবেল: β = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀)। তীব্রতা ১০ গুণ হলে +10 dB, দ্বিগুণ হলে +3 dB, দূরত্ব দ্বিগুণ হলে −6 dB।

EX: Decibel calculations
1. A 2 W sound source (isotropic): at r = 10 m, I = 2 ÷ (4π × 100) = 1.59 × 10⁻³ W/m². β = 10 log₁₀(1.59 × 10⁻³ ÷ 10⁻¹²) = 10 × 9.20 = **92.0 dB**.
2. How far away is it just 60 dB (I = 10⁻⁶)? r = √(P ÷ (4π I)) = √(2 ÷ (4π × 10⁻⁶)) = √159,155 = **399 m**.
3. Two equal 70 dB sources together: intensity doubles, so 70 + 3 = **73 dB**. Ten equal sources: 70 + 10 = **80 dB**.
/EX

### The Doppler effect
When the source and the observer move relative to each other, the observed frequency changes. With sound speed v, source speed vₛ and observer speed vₒ (toward each other is positive):

EQ: f′ = f × (v + vₒ) ÷ (v − vₛ)

- **Source approaching** a stationary observer: waves bunch up, f′ = f v ÷ (v − vₛ), higher.
- **Source receding:** f′ = f v ÷ (v + vₛ), lower.
- **Observer approaching** a stationary source: f′ = f (v + vₒ) ÷ v.

EX: A passing siren
1. An ambulance siren at 700 Hz moves at 25 m/s (v = 343 m/s). Approaching: f′ = 700 × 343 ÷ 318 = **755 Hz**. Receding: f′ = 700 × 343 ÷ 368 = **652 Hz**.
2. The pitch drops by a ratio 755 ÷ 652 = 1.157, about **2.5 semitones**, the familiar "neeeow" as it passes.
3. An observer walking toward a stationary 500 Hz source at 20 m/s hears 500 × 363 ÷ 343 = **529 Hz**.

### Radar guns and sonic booms
**Radar gun.** The wave reflects from a moving car, so the Doppler shift happens twice: Δf ≈ 2 f v ÷ c for v much less than c (a first-order Taylor result, from today's math). For f = 24 GHz and v = 30 m/s: Δf = 2 × 24 × 10⁹ × 30 ÷ 3 × 10⁸ = **4800 Hz**, easy to measure.

**Sonic boom.** If vₛ exceeds v, the waves pile up into a cone of half-angle θ with sin θ = v ÷ vₛ (the Mach number M = vₛ ÷ v is its inverse). A jet at Mach 2 has sin θ = 1/2, θ = **30°**.

TRAP: In the Doppler formula, the **signs** matter: use + for motion that brings source and observer closer (so f′ rises). The speeds are measured relative to the **medium** (the air), not to each other. And intensity ratio and decibels use the logarithm: do not add intensities in dB (70 dB + 70 dB is 73 dB, not 140 dB).
