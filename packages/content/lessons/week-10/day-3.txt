# Power series. Then: superposition, interference and beats
lead: A power series is an "infinitely long polynomial", and it is the tool that turns hard functions into easy ones. Today you learn where a power series works (its radius of convergence) and how to differentiate and integrate it term by term. In physics, you add waves together: they reinforce, cancel, and, when their frequencies are slightly different, produce the throbbing sound called beats.

BN: ঘাত ধারা (power series) হলো "অসীম দৈর্ঘ্যের বহুপদী", আর এটাই কঠিন ফাংশনকে সহজ করার হাতিয়ার। আজ শিখবে ঘাত ধারা কোথায় কাজ করে (অভিসারিতার ব্যাসার্ধ) এবং কীভাবে পদে পদে অন্তরকলন ও সমাকলন করতে হয়। পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে তরঙ্গ যোগ করবে: তারা বাড়ে, কাটে, আর কম্পাঙ্ক সামান্য আলাদা হলে স্পন্দ (beats) নামের ধপধপ শব্দ তৈরি করে।

### Polynomials with infinitely many terms
A **power series** about x = a is

EQ: Σ from n = 0 of cₙ (x − a)ⁿ = c₀ + c₁ (x − a) + c₂ (x − a)² + …

For each x it is an ordinary series, which may or may not converge. The set of x where it converges is an **interval** centred at a. Its half-width is the **radius of convergence R**: converges for abs(x − a) < R, diverges for abs(x − a) > R, and the two endpoints must be checked separately.

**Finding R:** apply the ratio test to the terms: L = lim abs(cₙ₊₁ ÷ cₙ) × abs(x − a), and require L < 1.

BN: ঘাত ধারা Σ cₙ(x − a)ⁿ কেন্দ্র a ঘিরে একটা ব্যবধানে অভিসারী। ব্যবধানের অর্ধেক-প্রস্থ হলো অভিসারিতার ব্যাসার্ধ R: |x − a| < R এ অভিসারী, |x − a| > R এ অপসারী, আর দুই প্রান্ত আলাদাভাবে পরীক্ষা করতে হয়। R বের করতে অনুপাত পরীক্ষা ব্যবহার করো।

EX: Radius and interval
1. Σ xⁿ: ratio = abs(x), so R = **1**. At x = ±1 the terms do not tend to 0. Interval **(−1, 1)**, and the sum is 1 ÷ (1 − x).
2. Σ xⁿ ÷ n!: ratio = abs(x) ÷ (n + 1) → 0. R = **∞**: converges for every x.
3. Σ n! xⁿ: ratio = (n + 1) abs(x) → ∞ for x ≠ 0. R = **0**: converges only at x = 0.
4. Σ xⁿ ÷ n: R = 1. At x = 1 it is the harmonic series (diverges). At x = −1 it is the alternating harmonic series (converges). Interval **[−1, 1)**.
5. Σ xⁿ ÷ n²: R = 1. Both endpoints give convergent series (p = 2, and absolutely convergent alternating). Interval **[−1, 1]**.
6. Σ (x − 2)ⁿ ÷ (n 3ⁿ): ratio → abs(x − 2) ÷ 3, so R = **3**, centre 2. At x = 5: harmonic, diverges. At x = −1: alternating harmonic, converges. Interval **[−1, 5)**.
/EX

### Calculus with power series
Inside the interval of convergence you may **differentiate and integrate term by term**, and the radius stays the same.

EX: New series from old
1. Start from 1 ÷ (1 − x) = 1 + x + x² + x³ + … (abs(x) < 1). Differentiate: **1 ÷ (1 − x)² = 1 + 2x + 3x² + 4x³ + …** = Σ (n + 1) xⁿ.
2. Replace x by −x: 1 ÷ (1 + x) = 1 − x + x² − x³ + …. Integrate: **ln(1 + x) = x − x²/2 + x³/3 − x⁴/4 + …**.
3. Replace x by −x²: 1 ÷ (1 + x²) = 1 − x² + x⁴ − …. Integrate: **arctan x = x − x³/3 + x⁵/5 − …**. At x = 1 this gives **π/4 = 1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − 1/7 + …** (the Leibniz series).
4. Differentiate Σ xⁿ ÷ n!: you get the same series, so d/dx eˣ = eˣ ✓.
/EX

EX: Using them
1. e^0.5 ≈ 1 + 0.5 + 0.125 + 0.020833 + 0.002604 + 0.000260 = **1.648698** (the truth is 1.648721).
2. ln(1.2) ≈ 0.2 − 0.02 + 0.002667 − 0.0004 = **0.18227** (truth 0.18232). The error is at most the next term, 0.2⁵ ÷ 5 = 0.000064 ✓.
3. arctan 0.5 ≈ 0.5 − 0.04167 + 0.00625 − 0.00112 + 0.00022 = **0.46368** (truth 0.46365).
4. Σ n ÷ 2ⁿ: Σ n xⁿ = x ÷ (1 − x)² (multiply the derivative above by x). At x = 1/2: 0.5 ÷ 0.25 = **2**.
/EX

BN: ব্যবধানের ভেতরে পদে পদে অন্তরকলন ও সমাকলন করা যায়, ব্যাসার্ধ একই থাকে। 1/(1 − x) = Σxⁿ থেকে: অন্তরকলনে 1/(1 − x)² = Σ(n + 1)xⁿ; −x বসিয়ে সমাকলনে ln(1 + x) = x − x²/2 + x³/3 − …; −x² বসিয়ে সমাকলনে arctan x = x − x³/3 + x⁵/5 − …, x = 1 এ π/4 = 1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − …।

TRAP: Always test the **endpoints** separately: the ratio test says nothing there. Term-by-term calculus keeps the same R but may change the endpoint behaviour (for example, the endpoint can start or stop converging). And when you replace x by something (like −x²), the interval changes with it: 1 ÷ (1 + x²) works only for abs(x) < 1.

### The principle of superposition
When two waves meet in the same place, the displacement is the **sum** of the individual displacements. (The wave equation is linear, as you saw on Day 1.) After they pass, each continues as if nothing happened.

For two waves of the same frequency and amplitude A₀ that differ in phase by Δφ:

EQ: A = 2 A₀ abs(cos(Δφ ÷ 2))     and the intensity is proportional to A²

- **Constructive:** Δφ = 0, 2π, 4π, … gives A = 2A₀ (intensity 4 times one wave).
- **Destructive:** Δφ = π, 3π, … gives A = 0.

When the phase difference comes from a **path difference** d, one wavelength is 2π of phase: Δφ = 2π d ÷ λ.

EQ: Constructive: d = m λ     Destructive: d = (m + ½) λ     (m = 0, 1, 2, …)

BN: দুই তরঙ্গ একই জায়গায় মিললে সরণ যোগ হয় (সমীকরণ রৈখিক)। সমান কম্পাঙ্ক ও বিস্তারে দশা-পার্থক্য Δφ হলে A = 2A₀|cos(Δφ/2)|। গঠনমূলক (constructive): পথ-পার্থক্য d = mλ; ধ্বংসাত্মক (destructive): d = (m + ½)λ। পথ-পার্থক্য d হলে দশা-পার্থক্য Δφ = 2πd/λ।

EX: Two loudspeakers
1. Two speakers play the same note at 343 Hz (λ = 1 m). A listener is 3.0 m from one and 3.5 m from the other. The path difference is 0.5 m = λ/2: **destructive**, near silence.
2. At 686 Hz (λ = 0.5 m), the same path difference is exactly 1 wavelength: **constructive**, with the intensity four times that of one speaker.
3. If the phase differs by 60°, A = 2 A₀ cos 30° = **1.73 A₀**.
/EX

EX: Noise-cancelling headphones
1. A microphone hears the noise and the speaker plays its mirror image (phase π) to cancel it. A phase error δ of 10° (0.1745 rad) leaves a residual amplitude 2 A₀ sin(δ ÷ 2) = 0.174 A₀.
2. The residual intensity is 0.174² = **0.030** of the noise intensity, a 97% cut. A tiny phase error ruins cancellation for high frequencies, so these headphones work best for steady low-pitched noise like engines.
/EX

EX: Light through two slits
1. Light of λ = 600 nm passes through two slits d = 0.2 mm apart and falls on a screen L = 2 m away. Bright fringes are where d sin θ = m λ. For small angles, y = m λ L ÷ d, so the fringe spacing is Δy = 6 × 10⁻⁷ × 2 ÷ 2 × 10⁻⁴ = **6 mm**.
2. Measuring Δy is how Young showed in 1801 that light is a wave.
/EX

### Beats
Two waves of slightly different frequencies f₁ and f₂ are added. Using sin a + sin b = 2 sin((a + b) ÷ 2) cos((a − b) ÷ 2) (Week 9, Day 2):

EQ: sin(2π f₁ t) + sin(2π f₂ t) = 2 cos(2π ((f₁ − f₂) ÷ 2) t) × sin(2π ((f₁ + f₂) ÷ 2) t)

This is a wave at the **average frequency** whose amplitude swells and fades like a slow cosine. The loudness peaks twice per cycle of that cosine, so the **beat frequency** is

EQ: f_beat = abs(f₁ − f₂)

BN: দুই কাছাকাছি কম্পাঙ্ক f₁, f₂ যোগ করলে 2cos(2π((f₁ − f₂)/2)t) × sin(2π((f₁ + f₂)/2)t) পাই: গড় কম্পাঙ্কের তরঙ্গ, যার বিস্তার ধীরে ফোলে-কমে। স্পন্দ কম্পাঙ্ক = |f₁ − f₂|। পিয়ানো টিউনারের কাজ: ফর্কের সাথে তার মেলাতে স্পন্দ শুনে টান ঠিক করা, স্পন্দ থামলে সুর মিলেছে।

The number of swells per second is abs(f₁ − f₂). | f1:Frequency f₁ (Hz):5:15:0.5:10 ;; f2:Frequency f₂ (Hz):5:15:0.5:11 | abs(f1-f2):Beat frequency (Hz):2 ;; (f1+f2)/2:Carrier frequency (Hz):2 | sin(2*pi*f1*x)+sin(2*pi*f2*x) ;; 2*abs(cos(pi*(f1-f2)*x)) | 0,2 | -2.2,2.2

EX: Tuning a piano
1. A 440 Hz tuning fork and a piano string produce 3 beats per second. The string is at 437 Hz or 443 Hz.
2. Tightening the string raises its pitch. If the beats **slow down** after tightening, the string was flat (437 Hz) and is approaching 440 Hz.
3. To raise a string from 437 Hz to 440 Hz, since f ∝ √T, the tension must rise by (440 ÷ 437)² − 1 = **1.4%**.
/EX

TRAP: **Intensity is proportional to the amplitude squared**, so constructive interference quadruples intensity (not doubles it). Do not confuse the path difference d (a length) with the phase difference (an angle): Δφ = 2π d ÷ λ. And beats need **nearly equal** frequencies: f₁ and f₂ far apart give two separate notes, not a throb.
