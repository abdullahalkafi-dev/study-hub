@section math | 2 · Part A: Limits and derivatives (14 marks, about 55 min) | no calculator

LVL: Part A | Questions 1 to 13
P: (1 mark) Find lim (x → 3) (x² − 9) ÷ (x − 3). => (x + 3) → **6**.
P: (1 mark) Find lim (x → 0) sin 3x ÷ (2x). => (3/2) × (sin 3x ÷ 3x) → **3/2**.
P: (1 mark) Find lim (x → ∞) (4x² + 1) ÷ (2x² − x). => Divide by x²: (4 + 1/x²) ÷ (2 − 1/x) → **2**.
P: (1 mark) Find lim (x → 4) (√x − 2) ÷ (x − 4). => Multiply by the conjugate: 1 ÷ (√x + 2) → **1/4**.
P: (1 mark) What happens to 1 ÷ (x − 1) as x → 1⁺ and as x → 1⁻? Does the two-sided limit exist? => From the right it grows to +∞, from the left it falls to −∞. The two-sided limit **does not exist**.
P: (1 mark) Find k so that f(x) = x² + k for x < 1 and f(x) = 5x − 1 for x ≥ 1 is continuous at x = 1. => Right value: 4. Left limit: 1 + k. So 1 + k = 4 and **k = 3**.
P: (1 mark) Show that x³ − 2x − 1 = 0 has a root in (1, 2). => f(x) = x³ − 2x − 1 is continuous. f(1) = −2 < 0 and f(2) = 3 > 0, so by the intermediate value theorem there is a root between 1 and 2. ∎
P: (1 mark) Use the definition of the derivative to find f′(x) for f(x) = x² + 3x. => ((x + h)² + 3(x + h) − x² − 3x) ÷ h = (2xh + h² + 3h) ÷ h = 2x + h + 3 → **2x + 3**.
P: (1 mark) Differentiate 5x³ − 4√x + 2/x. => 15x² − 4 × 1/(2√x) − 2/x² = **15x² − 2/√x − 2/x²**.
P: (1 mark) Find the equation of the tangent to y = x² − 2x at x = 3. => f(3) = 3 and f′(x) = 2x − 2, so f′(3) = 4. y − 3 = 4 (x − 3), so **y = 4x − 9**.
P: (1 mark) Differentiate 3 sin x + e^(2x). => **3 cos x + 2 e^(2x)**.
P: (1 mark) A particle's position is s(t) = t³ − 6t² + 9t. Find its velocity and the times when it is at rest. => v = 3t² − 12t + 9 = 3 (t − 1)(t − 3). At rest when **t = 1 and t = 3**.
P: (2 marks) Find the minimum value of f(x) = x² − 6x + 11 using the derivative, and check by completing the square. => f′ = 2x − 6 = 0 gives x = 3, and f(3) = 9 − 18 + 11 = **2**. Completing the square: (x − 3)² + 2 ≥ 2, with equality at x = 3 ✓.

@section physics | 3 · Part B: Work, energy and power (6 marks, about 35 min) | calculator allowed

LVL: Part B | Questions 14 to 19
P: (1 mark) A 50 N force acts at 37° above the horizontal and moves a crate 10 m horizontally. Find the work done by the force. => 50 × 10 × cos 37° = 500 × 0.7986 = **399 J**.
P: (1 mark) How much work must be done to speed a 1000 kg car from 15 m/s to 25 m/s? => ½ × 1000 × (625 − 225) = **200,000 J**.
P: (1 mark) A block is released from rest at a height of 3 m on a smooth ramp. Find its speed at the bottom. => √(2 × 9.8 × 3) = √58.8 = **7.67 m/s**.
P: (1 mark) A spring with k = 250 N/m is compressed 0.12 m. Find the energy stored and the launch speed of a 0.1 kg block (smooth surface). => ½ × 250 × 0.0144 = **1.8 J**. v = √(2 × 1.8 ÷ 0.1) = **6 m/s**.
P: (1 mark) A crane lifts 400 kg through 15 m in 25 s. Find the power. => 400 × 9.8 × 15 ÷ 25 = **2352 W**.
P: (1 mark) A motor is 75% efficient and takes in 1.2 kW. What force can it exert on a load moving at a steady 2 m/s? => Useful power = 0.75 × 1200 = 900 W, so F = 900 ÷ 2 = **450 N**.

### Challenges of the week
LVL: Challenge | A continuity puzzle
P: Let f be continuous on [0, 1] with f(0) = f(1). Show that there is some x in [0, 1/2] with f(x) = f(x + 1/2). (In words: on any closed loop trip that returns to its starting height, there are two points half a trip apart at the same height.) => Define g(x) = f(x + 1/2) − f(x) on [0, 1/2]. Then g(0) = f(1/2) − f(0) and g(1/2) = f(1) − f(1/2) = f(0) − f(1/2) = −g(0). So g takes opposite signs at the two ends (or is zero at both). By the intermediate value theorem, g(x) = 0 for some x, which means **f(x) = f(x + 1/2)** ∎.

LVL: Challenge | The cost of a climb
P: A 70 kg hiker climbs 800 m. The human body is about 25% efficient at turning food energy into mechanical work. How much food energy is needed, in kilocalories (1 kcal = 4184 J)? How long would a 100 W lamp need to run on the same energy? => Work against gravity: 70 × 9.8 × 800 = 548,800 J. Food energy = 548,800 ÷ 0.25 = 2,195,200 J = **525 kcal**. The lamp would run 2,195,200 ÷ 100 = 21,952 s = **6.1 hours**.
