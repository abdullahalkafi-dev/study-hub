@section math | 2 · Math: the derivative | 60 min

### From average rate to instantaneous rate
For a function y = f(x), the **average rate of change** between x and x + h is the slope of the **secant line** through two points of the graph:

secant slope = (f(x + h) − f(x)) ÷ h

As h shrinks, the second point slides toward the first and the secant line turns into the **tangent line**. The limit of the secant slopes is the slope of the tangent, the **derivative**:

EQ: f′(x) = lim (h → 0) (f(x + h) − f(x)) ÷ h

Other notations: dy/dx (Leibniz), or df/dx. Read dy/dx as "the rate of change of y with respect to x". The derivative at a particular point a is written f′(a).

Two meanings, one number:
- **Geometric:** the slope of the tangent line to the graph at that point.
- **Physical:** the instantaneous rate of change. If s(t) is position, then s′(t) is velocity. If v(t) is velocity, then v′(t) is acceleration.

BN: সেকান্ট (ছেদক) রেখার ঢাল = (f(x + h) − f(x))/h। h → 0 হলে সেকান্ট হয়ে যায় স্পর্শক। এই লিমিটই f′(x)। দুটি অর্থ: জ্যামিতিক: স্পর্শকের ঢাল; ভৌত: তাৎক্ষণিক পরিবর্তনের হার। অবস্থানের অন্তরকলজ = বেগ, বেগের অন্তরকলজ = ত্বরণ।

### Computing from the definition
EX: f(x) = x²
1. (f(x + h) − f(x)) ÷ h = (x² + 2xh + h² − x²) ÷ h = 2x + h.
2. As h → 0: **f′(x) = 2x**.
/EX

EX: f(x) = x³
1. (x + h)³ = x³ + 3x²h + 3xh² + h³. The difference quotient is (3x²h + 3xh² + h³) ÷ h = 3x² + 3xh + h².
2. As h → 0: **f′(x) = 3x²**.
/EX

EX: f(x) = 1 ÷ x
1. (1/(x + h) − 1/x) ÷ h = ((x − (x + h)) ÷ (x (x + h))) ÷ h = −h ÷ (x (x + h) h) = −1 ÷ (x (x + h)).
2. As h → 0: **f′(x) = −1/x²**.
/EX

EX: f(x) = √x
1. (√(x + h) − √x) ÷ h. Multiply by the conjugate: ((x + h) − x) ÷ (h (√(x + h) + √x)) = 1 ÷ (√(x + h) + √x).
2. As h → 0: **f′(x) = 1 ÷ (2√x)**.
/EX

EX: A general quadratic
1. f(x) = 3x² − 2x. The difference quotient is (3(x + h)² − 2(x + h) − 3x² + 2x) ÷ h = (6xh + 3h² − 2h) ÷ h = 6x + 3h − 2.
2. As h → 0: **f′(x) = 6x − 2**.
/EX

### Patterns and the power rule
Look at the results: x² → 2x, x³ → 3x², x⁻¹ → −x⁻², x^(1/2) → ½ x^(−1/2). They all follow one rule.

EQ: d/dx (xⁿ) = n xⁿ⁻¹     d/dx (c) = 0     (c f)′ = c f′     (f + g)′ = f′ + g′

**Why for positive whole n:** (x + h)ⁿ = xⁿ + n xⁿ⁻¹ h + (terms with h² and higher). Subtract xⁿ, divide by h: n xⁿ⁻¹ + (terms with h). Then h → 0. ∎ The rule holds for every real n, as the examples with 1/x and √x suggest.

PREDICT: Before the next example, guess the derivative of x⁴ from the pattern x² → 2x and x³ → 3x². => The power rule predicts 4x³. Check with the binomial expansion: (x + h)⁴ = x⁴ + 4x³h + terms with h², so the quotient tends to 4x³.

EX: Differentiate term by term
1. f(x) = 4x⁵ − 3x² + 7x − 9. f′(x) = 20x⁴ − 6x + 7.
2. g(x) = √x + 2/x³ = x^(1/2) + 2x⁻³. g′(x) = ½ x^(−1/2) − 6x⁻⁴ = 1 ÷ (2√x) − 6 ÷ x⁴.
/EX

### Tangent lines
The tangent line at x = a has slope f′(a) and passes through (a, f(a)): **y − f(a) = f′(a)(x − a)**.

EX: A tangent line
1. f(x) = x² at x = 3. f(3) = 9 and f′(3) = 6.
2. y − 9 = 6 (x − 3), so **y = 6x − 9**.
/EX

FUNC: A curve and its tangent | x | y | x**2 ;; 6*x-9 | -1,5 | -3,16 | - | The curve y = x² and its tangent line at the point (3, 9). The line has slope 6, the derivative of x² at x = 3, and touches the curve at that point.

EX: Where is the tangent horizontal?
1. f(x) = x³ − 3x. f′(x) = 3x² − 3 = 0 gives x² = 1, so **x = ±1**.
2. The points are (1, −2) and (−1, 2): the bottom of a dip and the top of a hill. This is how derivatives find maxima and minima, the topic of Week 7.
/EX

### Velocity and acceleration from calculus
EX: A moving object
1. A car's position is s(t) = t² + 3t. Then v(t) = s′(t) = 2t + 3, so at t = 4 the speed is **11 m/s** (matching Monday's limit).
2. The acceleration is v′(t) = **2 m/s²**, constant.
3. A falling stone: s = 5t² gives v = 10t and a = 10, so at t = 2 the speed is **20 m/s**, as on Monday.
/EX

### When a derivative fails to exist
A function is **differentiable** at a if the limit above exists. It fails at:
- a **corner**, like |x| at 0: the left slope is −1 and the right slope is +1;
- a **vertical tangent**, like the cube root of x at 0, where the slope blows up;
- a **discontinuity** of any kind.

**Differentiable implies continuous.** The reverse is false: |x| is continuous at 0 but not differentiable.

TRAP: f′(a) means "evaluate the derivative function at a". It is **not** the derivative of the number f(a) (which would be 0). Also, do not forget the "h" in the denominator: the difference quotient is a ratio. And the derivative of a **product** is not the product of the derivatives (the product rule arrives in Week 6).

@section physics | 3 · Physics: power, efficiency and the rooftop tank | 30 min

### Power
**Power** is the rate of doing work, or of using energy.

EQ: P = W ÷ t     unit: watt (W) = joule per second

If work is done at a changing rate, P = dW/dt, a derivative. And since W = F d, for a force along the motion:

EQ: P = F v

(because d/dt of F × distance is F × velocity). Other units: 1 kilowatt = 1000 W, 1 horsepower ≈ 746 W, and the energy unit used on electricity bills is the **kilowatt-hour**: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.

BN: ক্ষমতা (power) = কাজের হার, P = W/t, একক ওয়াট। গতির দিকে বল থাকলে P = Fv। বিদ্যুৎ বিলের একক kWh: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J। ক্ষমতা আসলে কাজের সময়-সাপেক্ষে অন্তরকলজ: P = dW/dt।

EX: Power of lifting
1. A person lifts an 80 kg load 10 m in 20 s. Work = 80 × 9.8 × 10 = 7840 J. Power = 7840 ÷ 20 = **392 W**.
/EX

EX: A car at cruising speed
1. A car moves at 25 m/s against a total resistance of 600 N. P = F v = 600 × 25 = **15,000 W = 15 kW** (about 20 horsepower).
/EX

EX: A car on a hill
1. A 1200 kg car climbs a 5° slope at a steady 20 m/s. The air and rolling resistance is 500 N.
2. The driving force must balance resistance and the pull of gravity along the slope: F = 500 + 1200 × 9.8 × sin 5° = 500 + 1025 = 1525 N.
3. P = 1525 × 20 = **30.5 kW**, twice as much as on level ground at the same speed.
/EX

### Efficiency
No machine turns all its input into useful output.

EQ: efficiency = useful output ÷ total input × 100%

A motor that is 70% efficient and takes in 2 kW of electrical power gives 1.4 kW of useful mechanical power. The rest becomes heat.

EX: Electricity bill
1. A 1.5 kW heater runs for 4 hours. Energy = 1.5 × 4 = **6 kWh** = 6 × 3.6 × 10⁶ = 21.6 MJ.
2. At a price of 0.15 per kWh the cost is **0.90**.
/EX

### Top speed: why more power gives only a little more speed
Air drag grows as v² (for a car, F_drag ≈ k v²), so at top speed the power equals drag × speed:

P = k v³, so **v = (P ÷ k)^(1/3)**.

EX: Top speed
1. Take k = 0.4 N·s²/m² and P = 60 kW: v = (150,000)^(1/3) = **53 m/s** (about 191 km/h).
2. Double the power to 120 kW: v = (300,000)^(1/3) = 66.9 m/s, an increase of only **26%** (2^(1/3) = 1.26). This is why very fast cars need enormous engines.
/EX

### The rooftop water tank (this week's story)
EX: Pumping water up
1. A pump lifts 2000 litres (2000 kg) of water to a rooftop tank 30 m above the ground in 20 minutes. The pump system is 60% efficient.
2. Energy to raise the water: m g h = 2000 × 9.8 × 30 = **588,000 J**.
3. Useful power over 1200 s: 588,000 ÷ 1200 = **490 W**.
4. Electrical power needed: 490 ÷ 0.6 = **817 W**.
5. Electrical energy per fill: 817 W × (1/3 hour) = **0.272 kWh**. At 0.15 per kWh, that is about **0.04** per fill.
6. If the tank is moved to 60 m (a taller building), the energy doubles. The height matters linearly, while speed would matter as a square.
/EX

TRAP: Power and energy are different. A 100 W bulb used for one hour is 0.1 kWh. "kW" is power, "kWh" is energy. Also, P = F v uses the force **along** the velocity and holds at each instant.
